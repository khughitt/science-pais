# Research Plan

## Direction

Build a literature-grounded model of post-acute infection syndromes (PAIS) as failed recovery of immune and physiological homeostasis after acute infection, with the goal of testing whether long COVID, ME/CFS, post-treatment Lyme disease syndrome, post-dengue/Q-fever fatigue, and related syndromes share a common pathophysiology. The near-term work is synthesis: map the mechanistic and clinical landscape of each post-infectious syndrome, identify the candidate mechanisms with the strongest evidence, and determine which cross-syndrome claims are grounded enough to operationalize as hypotheses or measurements. This connects to the immune-mechanism frame in `health-immunity` and the multiscale-homeostasis frame in `health-meta`.

## Major Workstreams

1. **Long COVID core.** Map the mechanisms (immune dysregulation, antigen persistence, thromboinflammation), organ-specific sequelae (pulmonary, cardiovascular, neurological, rheumatic, gastrointestinal), subphenotyping, epidemiology, and long-term outcomes; identify the mechanistic claims with the best longitudinal/controlled support.
2. **ME/CFS and overlap.** Survey ME/CFS viral-origin and immune/proteomic-signature evidence and characterize its overlap with long COVID at the symptom and biomarker level.
3. **Non-COVID post-infectious syndromes.** Synthesize PTLDS, post-dengue, post-Q-fever, and post-SARS literature to determine what generalizes across triggering pathogens and what is trigger-specific.
4. **Cross-syndrome synthesis.** Test the "shared PAIS failure mode" hypothesis: assemble the recurring candidate mechanisms (persistent immune activation, antigen/pathogen persistence, autoimmunity, viral reactivation, dysautonomia, metabolic dysfunction) and assess which are convergent across syndromes vs. trigger-specific.
5. **Connect to peers.** Draw immune-mechanism grounding from `health-immunity` (and share bridging papers via commons); escalate homeostasis-frame findings to `health-meta`; flag the PAIS family to `pan-disease` as a case where disease labels may obscure shared biology.

## Decision Gates

- Prioritize computational analysis only after the literature map is sufficient to identify specific, measurable post-infectious state variables worth modeling (e.g. candidate biomarker panels, subphenotype definitions).
- Hold mechanistic claims at their evidence level; do not propagate a single unifying mechanism as established, and require explicit case-definition and control-design labels on epidemiological claims.
- Before adding disease-specific analyses, confirm with `pan-disease` whether the disease label or a cross-syndrome post-infectious phenotype is the right unit of analysis.

## Strategic Risks

- PAIS mechanism and causality are actively contested and fast-moving; the project must keep evidence-level distinctions strict or it will conflate promising hypotheses (microclots, tissue antigen persistence) with established mechanism.
- Case definitions and cohort composition vary enormously; cross-study synthesis risks comparing non-comparable populations unless definitions are tracked.
- Post-infectious phenotypes are highly covariate-sensitive (acute severity, age, sex, timing, prior immunity); apparent signals may reflect confounders.
- The literature is large, heterogeneous, and partly low-quality (uncontrolled, self-reported); synthesis without a sharp cross-syndrome question risks becoming an undifferentiated review rather than a hypothesis-driven inquiry.
