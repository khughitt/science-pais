# Search: pediatric long COVID and MIS-C

## Search Focus

`task:t009` asked for pediatric PASC phenotype, MIS-C, and how pediatric post-infectious illness compares
with adult PAIS. This pass focused on durable cohort/synthesis anchors rather than single-site clinic
case series:

1. pediatric long-COVID symptom definitions and age-stratified phenotype;
2. longitudinal persistence and impairment, including the CLoCk cohort;
3. reinfection as a pediatric PASC risk amplifier;
4. MIS-C as a post-infectious but clinically distinct comparator.

## Query Set

Searches combined local corpus review with PubMed/publisher/RECOVER checks for:

- "pediatric long COVID RECOVER symptoms index children adolescents";
- "early childhood long COVID RECOVER JAMA Pediatrics";
- "children adolescents long COVID reinfection RECOVER-EHR";
- "MIS-C six-month outcomes MUSIC study";
- "pediatric long COVID systematic review meta-analysis".

## Search Verdict

**[+] Pediatric long COVID is real but developmentally staged; [~] MIS-C is adjacent rather than the same
chronic PAIS phenotype.**

The strongest pediatric PASC evidence now comes from RECOVER and CLoCk rather than early uncontrolled
prevalence reports. RECOVER shows that symptom combinations differ by age: infants/toddlers,
preschool-aged children, school-age children, and adolescents require separate research indices. CLoCk
shows why broad pediatric symptom definitions can overcount unless impairment and longitudinal
persistence are considered. RECOVER-EHR shows reinfection increases pediatric PASC-coded outcomes during
Omicron.

MIS-C belongs in the PAIS neighborhood because it is post-infectious and immune-mediated, but it should
not be folded into chronic long-COVID/ME-CFS-like PAIS. MUSIC shows severe acute hyperinflammation with
generally rapid cardiovascular and global recovery by 6 months, making MIS-C a useful contrast case for
"recovering post-infectious inflammation" rather than a typical chronic failed-recovery attractor.

## Follow-up

The high-yield next study is not another symptom prevalence report. It is a pediatric longitudinal
mechanism cohort that stratifies by age/developmental stage, applies impairment-aware PASC definitions,
captures reinfection/vaccination timing, and includes orthostatic/autonomic, immune, and metabolic
endpoints alongside parent/proxy versus child self-report.
