# Evidence Line: Silva2024 (MY-LC) testosterone-symptom-burden association weakly supports the stage threshold

## What this line shows

In the Mount Sinai–Yale MY-LC cohort (n≈165), `paper:Silva2024` finds testosterone **associated with lower long-COVID symptom burden across both sexes** (after accounting for testosterone, sex ceased to predict burden). A gonadal-steroid → recovery gradient is consistent with `proposition:0001`'s claim that hormonal/reproductive context modulates the failed-recovery threshold [@Silva2024].

## Why it is independent

A non-UK-Biobank clinical immune-endocrine cohort, author-independent of the UKB SHBG literature and of the Alberta cohort (`evidence-line:0003`); `independence_group: my-lc`.

## Caveats / scope

`proxy_support`, weak: **cross-sectional**, so reverse-causation is unresolved — the same datum supports `proposition:0003` (LC suppressing the HPG axis) equally well (see `evidence-line:0008`). It measures gonadal steroids, not reproductive *stage*, and does not establish a pre-infection causal direction.
