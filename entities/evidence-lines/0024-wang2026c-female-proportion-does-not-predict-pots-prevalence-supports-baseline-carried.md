# Evidence Line: Wang2026c — female cohort proportion does not predict PASC POTS/OH prevalence

## What this line shows

Wang2026c pools 21 PASC orthostatic-intolerance studies (n=2,916, 74.9% female; POTS 36.2%, OH 18.6%, OI 70.6%) and runs a **meta-regression** on between-study predictors of POTS/OH prevalence. The **proportion female does not significantly predict** POTS (P=0.083) or OH (P=0.959) prevalence, whereas **younger age does** (POTS P<0.001, R²=0.664; OH P=0.038), persisting in multivariable models. If post-infectious dysautonomia *amplified* the female burden, female-heavier cohorts should show more POTS; they do not — age, not femaleness, drives between-cohort variation. This is the strongest single support for `proposition:0009`'s baseline-carried reading [@Wang2026c].

## Why it is independent

A study-level meta-regression across 21 cohorts, methodologically distinct from the within-cohort Kwan2022 incidence design (`evidence-line:0019`) and the single-cohort Eldokla2022 burden null (`evidence-line:0020`); its own `independence_group: wang2026c-pasc-oi-meta`.

## Caveats / scope

`proxy_support`, moderate: the POTS female-proportion null is **marginal and underpowered** (P=0.083, only 15 studies, I²=98%) — a *moderate* sex effect cannot be excluded, though the OH null (P=0.959) is cleaner and the age effect is strong and consistent [@Wang2026c]. This is an **ecological** (study-level) regression, so it cannot rule out an individual-level sex interaction (ecological inference caveat); severe case-definition heterogeneity and cross-sectional primaries limit it. Direction-consistent and the best-powered of the three lines on the *amplification* question, but not decisive on its own.
