# Evidence Line: Geng2024 (STOP-PASC) — null NMV/r RCT in established long COVID

## What this line shows

STOP-PASC, the first placebo-controlled RCT of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir (NMV/r, 15 days) for *established*
PASC (n=155, median 17.5 months post-infection), was **null** on its primary 6-symptom Likert composite at
10 weeks and on all PROMIS secondaries (physical P=.66, fatigue P=.79, dyspnea P=.70, cognition P=.98),
and was stopped early for futility. If residual replicating antigen drove symptoms and NMV/r cleared it,
benefit was expected; none appeared. This **weakly disputes** `proposition:0020` (antigen clearance
rescues established-disease symptoms) [@Geng2024].

## Why it is independent

`independence: independent` — Stanford single-center cohort, distinct from the PAX-LC (`0054`) and
outSMART-LC (`0055`) cohorts. NMV/r and AER002 are also mechanistically distinct clearance modalities
(Mpro inhibitor vs neutralizing mAb).

## Caveats / scope

**Weak**, because the trial never demonstrated **antigen target-engagement**: *no participant had
detectable baseline stool SARS-CoV-2 RNA*, there was no antigen-positive enrichment, and tissue
compartments were not assayed — so the treated population may not have carried the antigen the hypothesis
requires. Further bounded by: 15-day course (possibly too short / poor tissue penetration); protracted,
~99% vaccinated cohort; partial unmasking via dysgeusia (61.8% vs 7.5%); long COVID only. Per
`discussion:0003`, this null **does not refute antigen persistence** (`hypothesis:0002`) — a drug that
did not demonstrably clear antigen cannot test whether clearing antigen helps. Consistent with the
`proposition:0021` fixed-risk-factor reconciliation [@Geng2024].
