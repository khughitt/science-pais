# Evidence Line: Bhattacharjee2026 (PAX-LC immunologic substudy) — the target-engagement failure

## What this line shows

The PAX-LC immunologic substudy (n=82; 37 NMV/r, 45 PBO/r) measured what the antiviral actually *did*:
over the 15-day course, NMV/r changed **neither circulating SARS-CoV-2 Spike/S1 antigen** (S1 unchanged
in the NMV/r arm, p=0.958), **nor anti-Spike antibody, nor PBMC immune subsets**. The only correlate of
self-reported improvement — in *both* arms — was falling RANTES/CCL5. This is the decisive companion to
the clinical nulls: it directly demonstrates that 15-day NMV/r **does not engage the antigen target**,
which is why STOP-PASC (`evidence-line:0053`) and PAX-LC's own null primary endpoint cannot be read as testing
`proposition:0020`. Coded `disputes` (it weighs weakly against the reversible-target reading) with
`evidence_role: model_criticism` — its real content is to **disarm the other nulls as evidence against
the mechanism**.

## Why it is independent

`independence: independent` — PAX-LC is a separate decentralised RCT (28 US states) from STOP-PASC
(Stanford) and outSMART-LC (UCSF). The clinical primary endpoint (PROMIS-29 PHSS, reported by Krumholz et
al.) was null; this is the pre-specified mechanistic substudy.

## Caveats / scope

**Weak** as a dispute of `proposition:0020` precisely because its message is "the antecedent (antigen
cleared) was never satisfied." Bounded by: n=82, underpowered for immune subgroups; no ritonavir-only
control (PBO/r contains ritonavir, confounding the shared cytokine shifts); Day-28 readout shorter than
IgG half-life; ~45–55% of participants had no detectable baseline antigen (no enrichment); preprint, not
peer-reviewed; long COVID only. The NMV/r-fails-to-clear-Spike result actually *supports a tissue-reservoir
/ non-replicating-antigen* model (`hypothesis:0002`) over active bloodstream replication, and reinforces
that null NMV/r trials **must not** be cited against antigen persistence.
