# Evidence Line: ME/CFS butyrate-producing capacity deficit

## What this line shows

Guo2023 profiles 106 ME/CFS cases and 91 healthy controls with fecal shotgun metagenomics, functional
metagenomics, qPCR, fecal SCFA metabolomics, network analysis, and clinical phenotyping. ME/CFS cases show
reduced *Faecalibacterium prausnitzii* and *Eubacterium rectale*, confirmed deficient butyrate-producing
capacity, and an inverse relationship between *F. prausnitzii* abundance and fatigue severity.

Xiong2023 adds a duration-stratified host-microbiome view: short-term ME/CFS shows stronger microbial
dysbiosis, while long-term ME/CFS can have more severe clinical/metabolic abnormalities despite partial
microbiome-composition normalization. It also reports reduced microbial butyrate biosynthesis and lower
plasma butyrate, bile acids, and benzoate.

Together, these papers support the ME/CFS leg of `proposition:0031`: gut dysbiosis and reduced
SCFA/butyrate capacity are not unique to SARS-CoV-2 long COVID.

## Why it is independent

`independence: independent` relative to the long-COVID CUHK line and the SIM01 RCT. The two ME/CFS papers
are consolidated as one evidence line because they appeared as paired Cell Host & Microbe microbiome
studies in the same moment and address the same ME/CFS gut-axis question, but they use distinct cohort
structures and complementary assays.

## Caveats / scope

**Moderate** for recurrence of the gut-butyrate axis in ME/CFS; not causal. Diet, IBS/GI comorbidity,
illness duration, reduced activity, medication/supplement use, and geographic microbiome variation remain
important. Xiong2023 specifically warns against reading stool dysbiosis as a universal maintenance driver
for established long-duration disease.
