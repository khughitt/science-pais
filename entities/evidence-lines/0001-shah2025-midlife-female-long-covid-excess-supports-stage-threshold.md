# Evidence Line: Shah2025 midlife female long-COVID excess weakly supports a reproductive-stage threshold

## What this line shows

`paper:Shah2025` establishes female sex as a reproducible long-COVID risk factor with the clearest excess in a **midlife age band** — the band where the menopausal transition concentrates. A midlife-peaked female excess is the pattern a reproductive-stage threshold shift predicts, so the observation is weakly consistent with `proposition:0001`.

## Why it is independent

A population-scale risk-factor analysis distinct from the hormone-cohort lines (`evidence-line:0002`, `evidence-line:0003`); its own `independence_group: shah2025-lc-risk`.

## Caveats / scope

`proxy_support`, weak: a midlife age band is a coarse proxy for reproductive stage and does **not** isolate perimenopause from chronological age, pregnancy, comorbidity, or ascertainment — exactly the decomposition `proposition:0004` demands. Consistency with the prediction is not a direct test of it.

**Refinement (2026-06-22, `task:t018` / `interpretation:0003`): the support is for an age-banded effect, NOT a menopausal-status mechanism.** Shah2025 is the only PAIS study that breaks the age/menopause collinearity *within* a band, and on that finer cut the midlife female excess is **near-identical** in menopausal (RR ≈ 1.42, 95% CI 0.99–2.03) versus nonmenopausal (RR ≈ 1.45, 1.15–1.83) women at ages 40–54. So the 40–54 peak this line reads as supporting `proposition:0001` is **age-linked but not attributable to menopausal status per se** — it `disputes` a *menopause-specific* reading of the threshold while leaving an age/immunosenescence threshold intact. This line's weak support therefore stands only for the age-banded form of `proposition:0001`; it should **not** be cited as evidence that the menopausal transition specifically drives the excess.
