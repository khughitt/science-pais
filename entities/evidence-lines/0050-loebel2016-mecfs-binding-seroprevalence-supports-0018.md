# Evidence Line: Loebel2016 binding-ELISA β2/M3/M4 seroprevalence in ME/CFS

## What this line shows

Loebel2016 (the **foundational** functional-autoantibody-thread paper for ME/CFS, Scheibenbogen group)
reported elevated **β2-adrenergic and M3/M4-muscarinic receptor antibodies** in a subset of ME/CFS
patients vs controls (β2/M-receptor antibody elevation in ~29.5% of patients), and antibody levels
declined in rituximab responders in a linked sub-cohort — establishing that an anti-GPCR autoantibody
*repertoire against the canonical β-adrenergic/muscarinic targets* exists in ME/CFS. This anchors the
*existence* premise of `proposition:0018` for the ME/CFS trigger.

## Why it is independent

`independence: independent` — distinct Berlin ME/CFS cohort and a CellTrend binding-ELISA platform,
sharing no data with the POTS (`0049`/`0051`) or long-COVID (`0052`) lines.

## Caveats / scope

**Weak**, and the weakness is load-bearing: (1) the assay is a **binding ELISA (CellTrend), not a
functional/receptor-activation bioassay** — it shows antibodies *bind*, not that they *agonize/antagonize*
the receptor, so it cannot establish pathogenicity; (2) it is therefore **directly exposed to the
Hall2022 specificity critique** (`evidence-line:0051`) that the same commercial ELISA scores ~all POTS
*and* ~all healthy controls positive; (3) healthy-control seropositivity rates and per-receptor effect
sizes were not fully resolvable from the accessible text (`[UNVERIFIED]` in `paper:Loebel2016`); (4)
CellTrend/Heidecke COI on the titer thresholds; (5) **no small-fiber correlate** — autonomic/symptomatic
endpoints only. A seroprevalence brick, not a pathogenicity demonstration.
