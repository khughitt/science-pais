# Evidence Line: Appelman2024 long-COVID muscle OXPHOS worsening after PEM

## What this line shows

Appelman2024 (n = 25 long COVID vs 21 recovered controls; PEM a **required** inclusion criterion; before/after exercise-biopsy design that deliberately provoked PEM) shows that long-COVID PEM has a real, objective tissue correlate: reduced mitochondrial **oxidative-phosphorylation (OXPHOS) capacity**, a glycolytic fiber-type shift, amyloid-containing deposits, and myopathic features — abnormalities that **worsen the day after** provoked PEM, with deconditioning ruled out by matched confirmed-maximal effort. Crucially for `proposition:0011`, this objective long-COVID lesion sits at the **peripheral-muscle** endpoint, not at the whole-body gas-exchange endpoint where the ME/CFS signature lives (`evidence-line:0026`) and where long COVID was null (`evidence-line:0027`). It is what makes the divergence *endpoint-specific relocation* rather than *absence of any objective basis*.

## Why it is independent

Distinct cohort and assay (skeletal-muscle biopsy + respirometry, not whole-body CPET) from the other two lines; its own `independence_group: appelman2024-lc-muscle-biopsy`. It is the line that prevents the proposition from being misread as "long-COVID PEM lacks an objective basis."

## Caveats / scope

`proxy_support`, moderate: a within-subject provocation design (not a PEM-positive vs PEM-negative between-group contrast), so it speaks to *where the long-COVID lesion is* rather than to PEM-status stratification per se; muscle biopsy and whole-body CPET are non-commensurable endpoints, so "relocation" is an inference across assays, not a head-to-head measurement; and the primary lesion (mitochondrial vs vascular vs immune) is left open by the authors. It anchors the long-COVID *positive* leg of the three-cohort convergence in `proposition:0011`.
