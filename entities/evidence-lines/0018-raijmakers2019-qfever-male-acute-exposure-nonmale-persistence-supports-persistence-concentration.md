# Evidence Line: Q-fever male-skewed acute exposure but non-male-skewed QFS persistence supports persistence-concentration

## What this line shows

Acute Q-fever exposure is **male**-skewed (occupational/farming exposure to *Coxiella burnetii*), yet Q-fever fatigue syndrome (QFS) persistence is **not** male-skewed (`dataset:dutch-qfever-qfs-cohort`, the Dutch QFS cohorts; the sex-gradient framing is recorded in that dataset note, with `paper:Raijmakers2019` as a within-cohort QFS source). A trigger whose acute phase over-samples men but whose persistent-fatigue phase loses that male skew is the third instance of the acute→post-acute sex reversal `proposition:0008` predicts.

## Why it is independent

A third, mechanistically distinct trigger (Q-fever / *Coxiella*) and study design (a natural experiment in an occupationally-exposed population) from the COVID and dengue meta lines. Its own `independence_group: dutch-qfs-natural-experiment`.

## Caveats / scope

`proxy_support`, **weak**: this is *directional/qualitative only* — there is no published female OR for QFS persistence, so it cannot quantify the reversal, only show its sign. The acute male skew is an *exposure* artefact (occupation), not an acute *severity* female-protection, so the analogy to COVID/dengue is partial. Direction-consistent, not a quantitative anchor.
