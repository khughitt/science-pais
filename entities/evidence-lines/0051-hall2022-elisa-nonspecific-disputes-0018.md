# Evidence Line: Hall2022 — standard-methodology ELISA shows GPCR autoantibodies are non-specific in POTS

## What this line shows

Hall2022 (Circulation) re-measured cardiovascular **GPCR autoantibodies by standard binding ELISA** in
POTS vs healthy controls and found them **non-discriminating**: at the manufacturer threshold **98.3% of
POTS and 100% of controls** were "seropositive" for α1-AR (p = 0.5); across 11 receptors there was **no
significant POTS-vs-control difference** at either the manufacturer or a control-derived (mean+2SD)
threshold, and the best single-receptor diagnostic C-statistic was ~0.58 (panel 0.60–0.65). The
interpretation: **ELISA seropositivity reflects ubiquitous low-titer natural GPCR antibodies and
non-specific binding, not disease-specific autoimmunity**. This directly disputes the *binding-ELISA*
evidentiary basis of `proposition:0018` [@Hall2022].

## Why it is independent

`independence: independent` — a separate POTS cohort run specifically as a methodological replication;
shares no data with the other three lines. It is the disconfirming counterweight to the binding-ELISA
support line `0050` (Loebel) and stands against the existence-premise those binding studies assert.

## Caveats / scope

`dispute_scope: mechanism` and **moderate, deliberately not strong** — the scope boundary is the whole
point: Hall2022 critiques **binding ELISA only**. It does **not** test functional/receptor-activation
bioassays, so it does **not** refute the *functional*-assay support (`evidence-line:0049`, Kharraziha's
FRET α1-AR↔OHQ correlation), which is precisely the rebuttal the functional-autoantibody camp makes.
Thus it guts the binding-seroprevalence evidence (`0050`) without touching the functional-activity
evidence (`0049`). Net effect on `0018`: it removes the cheap/common binding-ELISA "support" from the
ledger and raises the bar to functional or interventional designs, but does not by itself falsify the
proposition. Also POTS, not PAIS, and no small-fiber readout.
