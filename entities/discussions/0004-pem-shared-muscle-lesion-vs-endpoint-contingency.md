# Hypothesis Comparison: cross-trigger PEM — one shared muscle lesion vs endpoint-contingent objective correlate

## Hypotheses Compared

This is a deliberately asymmetric pairing: a **mechanistic localization+causation** hypothesis
against a **meta-level structural claim about measurement and inference**. They are not two rival
mechanisms for one phenomenon. The value of the comparison is in finding the *one narrow place they
actually collide* and the *single experiment that adjudicates it*.

### H0006 — Skeletal-muscle ischemic-mitochondrial lesion as the primary PEM substrate

Organizing conjecture: PEM is the clinical signature of a **muscle-localized** vicious cycle —
microvascular hypoperfusion → ionic dysregulation (Na⁺ → reverse-mode Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchange → Ca²⁺
overload) → mitochondrial damage → worse perfusion (Scheibenbogen2024's "acquired ischemic
mitochondrial myopathy"). Core propositions: **P1** (deficit localized to skeletal muscle, not
CNS/circulating cells), **P2** (ischemic/hypoperfusion cause is upstream), **P3** (ionic mediator),
**P4** (self-perpetuation). The load-bearing auxiliary for *this* comparison is **A1: the same muscle
lesion underlies PEM in post-COVID and ME/CFS** — an explicit cross-trigger *sharing* claim, a
tissue-specific instance of `hypothesis:0001`. Belief: **speculative** (single-group AIMM model,
single-study ²³Na-MRI anchor, disclosed COI; strongest independent support — Appelman2024, Joseph2023 —
confirms a provoked peripheral lesion but not the ionic cascade).

### H0008-M3 — `proposition:0029`: a PAIS phenotype's objective correlate is endpoint- and trigger-specific

Organizing claim (a `structural_claim`, not a mechanism): **even among objective endpoints**, the
*choice* of endpoint can manufacture or hide an apparent cross-trigger shared mechanism. M3 is the
least-supported member of the h0008 bundle (**fragile**, single instance). Its single instance *is* the
PEM case, carried by the **supported** empirical regularity `proposition:0011`: PEM is shared as a
*symptom label*, but its objective correlate is not one shared failure mode — the ME/CFS whole-body
2-day-CPET decrement (Keller2014: 13.8% day-2 VO₂peak fall) does **not** transfer to PEM-enriched long
COVID (Gattoni2025: whole-body null, n=15), while long COVID nonetheless shows a real post-exertional
lesion at the **muscle** endpoint (Appelman2024: worsened OXPHOS after provoked PEM). Same symptom,
different measurable lesion and measurement channel [@Keller2014; @Gattoni2025; @Appelman2024].

## Proposition-Level Comparison

The two hypotheses agree more than the "head-to-head" framing suggests, and disagree on exactly one
axis.

**Where they ALIGN (and both draw on the same evidence):**

- **Locus is peripheral/muscle, not central.** H0006-P1 localizes the deficit to skeletal muscle;
  `proposition:0011` is the *relocated-positive* observation that the long-COVID objective lesion sits
  at the muscle endpoint (Appelman2024), not the whole-body one. **Appelman2024 supports both.** Both
  also push back on Walitt2024's central "effort-preference" framing.
- **Whole-body CPET is not the universal PEM signature.** H0006 treats the muscle lesion as primary and
  the whole-body CPET decrement as a *downstream readout*; M3 treats the whole-body endpoint as one
  endpoint among several whose positivity is trigger-specific. Neither treats Keller2014's ME/CFS
  whole-body decrement as the cross-trigger gold standard.

**Where they genuinely COLLIDE — the one axis that matters:**

- H0006-**A1** asserts the *same* muscle lesion is **shared** across ME/CFS and long COVID. M3 asserts
  that cross-trigger *sameness* is **endpoint-contingent** — that picking the muscle endpoint vs the
  whole-body endpoint can decide whether the two triggers look like one mechanism or two. A1 says
  "one lesion, shared"; M3 says "whether it looks shared depends on where you measure." These cannot
  both be unconditionally true: if A1 holds, the shared muscle lesion should appear at the muscle
  endpoint **regardless** of the whole-body discordance, which would make the cross-trigger divergence
  an artifact of endpoint *selection* (the wrong endpoint hid a real shared lesion) rather than
  evidence of genuinely different mechanisms.

Critically, M3 is **not a rival to H0006 as a whole** — it is a *constraint on A1*. H0006's P1–P4 could
be entirely correct **for long COVID specifically** and M3 still hold, because the ME/CFS
instantiation might run dominantly through a different endpoint. The collision is narrow but real.

Belief-state asymmetry (from `science belief profile`): `proposition:0011` is **supported** (three
independent cohorts: a positive reference, a negative transfer, a relocated positive); `proposition:0029`
(M3, the *generalization* of that single instance to a structural rule) is **fragile**; `hypothesis:0006`
is **speculative**; `hypothesis:0001` (the shared-attractor parent of A1) is **speculative**. So on the
collision axis, the better-evidenced side is currently M3's empirical base (`prop:0011`) — but **only at
the whole-body endpoint**. At the muscle endpoint, the sole datum (Appelman2024) supports H0006's
localization and is *silent* on cross-trigger sharing because no ME/CFS muscle arm has been run in the
same frame.

## Discriminating Predictions

The hypotheses diverge on a single observable: **does ME/CFS exhibit the same post-exertional muscle
OXPHOS lesion that long COVID shows (Appelman2024), under a harmonized protocol?**

- **H0006-A1 predicts:** YES — both triggers converge at the muscle endpoint. The whole-body CPET
  discordance (Keller2014 vs Gattoni2025) is then revealed as endpoint *mis-selection*: the muscle
  lesion was there in both all along; the whole-body assay simply has different sensitivity per trigger.
  (Note this outcome is *itself* an endpoint-contingency observation — but it reads "wrong endpoint hid a
  **real shared** lesion," rescuing A1.)
- **M3 predicts:** the same/different verdict **flips with endpoint and does not resolve to one shared
  mechanism** — e.g. ME/CFS stays whole-body-dominant while long COVID stays muscle-dominant, or the
  muscle-OXPHOS phenotypes differ in kind (fiber-type shift, Complex-II selectivity) between triggers.
  Cross-trigger "sameness" remains an artifact of which endpoint is privileged.

Subtle but important: a YES outcome **confirms A1 and simultaneously sharpens M3's framing** rather than
killing M3 — it would show endpoint choice *can* hide a shared mechanism (M3's "hide" limb) while
denying its "manufacture a fake shared mechanism" limb. A NO/flip outcome **confirms M3's single
instance within a clean within-design comparison** (upgrading it from between-study-confounded to
within-design) and **weakens A1**.

## Discriminating Evidence Needed

**The single highest-value experiment: a harmonized, multi-endpoint, cross-trigger PEM study.** One
protocol, one site, applied to **both** ME/CFS and long COVID (plus recovered/deconditioned controls),
measuring at *both* endpoints in the *same* subjects:

1. Whole-body **two-day CPET** (peripheral-vs-central O₂-extraction decomposition, per H0006's
   prediction (i)); and
2. **Pre / immediately-post / 24–48 h post** exercise **muscle biopsy** — mitochondrial OXPHOS,
   fiber-type, and ideally ionic markers (intracellular Na⁺/Ca²⁺) to also touch P3.

This design adjudicates A1 vs M3 directly (the cross-trigger muscle-endpoint cell), and it is
**simultaneously**:

- H0006's **promotion criterion #2** (peripheral O₂ extraction limits day-2 CPET in ≥2 independent
  cohorts) plus the missing ME/CFS muscle arm;
- the **within-cohort harmonized design** that `proposition:0011`, `question:0015`, and `task:t025`
  all flag as *not existing* in the literature;
- a partial test of M3's "objective ⇒ survives" caution from the t055 audit.

**Key finding for the project's task queue:** *both* candidate hypotheses are blocked on the **same**
missing experiment. h0006 cannot promote without the independent cross-trigger muscle confirmation;
M3 cannot rise above fragile on this instance without the harmonized within-design comparison. This is
a data-gated convergence — no public dataset currently supplies the paired multi-endpoint cross-trigger
design (the t025/t044 sweeps confirmed its non-existence as of mid-2026).

A cheaper interim probe has now run (`task:t056`, `interpretation:0019`): ME/CFS muscle-endpoint data
do exist (Jones2012/Wong1992 31P-MRS, Brown2015 contraction-stimulated muscle cells, Bizjak2024 resting
muscle biopsy). They move the A1-vs-M3 needle from "absent ME/CFS muscle cell" to "non-harmonized
ME/CFS muscle cell": muscle localization is more plausible, but Appelman-type cross-trigger equivalence
is still untested.

## Rival-Model Packet

Three bounded readings of the cross-trigger PEM evidence:

- **Shared-muscle-lesion (A1).** One muscle ischemic-mitochondrial lesion underlies PEM in both
  triggers; endpoint discordance is measurement sensitivity. *Most distinguishing observation:* ME/CFS
  shows Appelman2024-type post-exertional muscle OXPHOS decline under a harmonized protocol.
- **Endpoint-contingent / no-single-mechanism (M3).** Cross-trigger sameness is an artifact of endpoint
  selection; the triggers have genuinely different dominant lesions. *Most distinguishing observation:*
  the same/different verdict flips with endpoint and the muscle-endpoint phenotypes differ in kind
  between triggers.
- **Underpowered-null / premature-divergence (the deflationary third).** The apparent divergence is
  driven by Gattoni2025's weak null (n=15, deconditioned, retrospective PEM); at adequate N with
  provocation-confirmed PEM the whole-body signatures might converge after all. *Most distinguishing
  observation:* an adequately-powered long-COVID 2-day-CPET reproduces the ME/CFS day-2 decrement.

`current_working_model`: **unset.** The evidence does not currently favor one — `prop:0011` is supported
but now contested by the t056 ME/CFS muscle-bioenergetics body; the muscle endpoint is positive in both
trigger families at a broad level, but not harmonized at the Appelman-type OXPHOS/SDH biopsy endpoint,
and the deflationary reading remains live because the load-bearing long-COVID whole-body null is weak.

## Current Verdict

**Contested and insufficiently resolved on the collision axis; substantially complementary elsewhere.**

- On the narrow A1-vs-M3 axis, **M3's empirical base (`proposition:0011`) remains supported but is now
  contested**. The t056 body shows ME/CFS muscle bioenergetic abnormalities exist (`proposition:0030`),
  so the old "whole-body-only ME/CFS vs muscle-only long COVID" dichotomy is too clean. The muscle
  endpoint, where the collision is actually decided, now has positive evidence on both sides at a broad
  level, but still no harmonized Appelman-type cross-trigger datum.
- M3 as a **general structural rule** (`prop:0029`) remains **fragile**: it generalizes from a single
  phenotype (PEM), and that single instance is currently a between-study contrast carrying the very
  ascertainment confound M3 exists to police. It needs either the harmonized within-design test (to
  clean up the PEM instance) or a *second phenotype* (the SFN cross-trigger-convergence claim,
  `proposition:0017`, currently UNTESTED) to become a regularity.
- H0006 remains **speculative** primarily for reasons *orthogonal* to this comparison (single-group
  AIMM model, unreplicated ²³Na anchor, unconfirmed ionic cascade). This comparison does not move
  H0006's overall grade; it isolates A1 as the proposition most exposed to M3.

Neither side "wins." The honest state is that **one missing experiment gates both**, and until it runs,
the defensible position is: long-COVID PEM has a real muscle-endpoint lesion and ME/CFS has independent
muscle-bioenergetic abnormalities (shared *localization* with H0006), but cross-trigger *sameness of
mechanism* is unproven and currently endpoint-contingent.

## Synthesis Or Coexistence

These hypotheses are **complementary at different levels**, not mutually exclusive as wholes:

- H0006 is a candidate **mechanism** (muscle ischemic-ionic-mitochondrial cascade). M3 is a
  **meta-constraint on inference** (don't read "one shared mechanism" off symptom-sharing without
  pinning the endpoint). M3 can be true *and* H0006's mechanism can be the correct account of the
  long-COVID muscle endpoint.
- The productive synthesis: **M3 is a guardrail on H0006's A1**, not a competitor. It converts A1 from
  an assumption ("the muscle lesion is shared") into a *testable, endpoint-specified* claim ("ME/CFS
  and long COVID share the muscle-OXPHOS-post-provocation lesion under a harmonized protocol"). That
  reframing is the concrete gain from running the comparison: A1 should be re-stated with its endpoint
  bound, and h0006's promotion criterion should explicitly require the **muscle-endpoint cross-trigger**
  confirmation, not merely "an independent group."
- Both feed `hypothesis:0001` (shared attractor): `prop:0011` already constrains it ("if shared, not
  uniform at the objective bioenergetic level"). This comparison sharpens that to: the attractor's
  *shared* expression, if any, must be demonstrated **at a fixed endpoint across triggers**, not
  inferred from convergent symptoms.

## Focus

Whether the cross-trigger PEM "shared mechanism" is one real shared muscle lesion (H0006-A1) or an
endpoint-selection artifact (H0008-M3). The two are not whole-hypothesis rivals: M3 is a constraint on
A1, and they agree that objective muscle abnormalities exist. The live question is the single cell of
evidence that does not yet exist — ME/CFS post-exertional muscle OXPHOS/SDH under a harmonized
Appelman-like protocol.

## Current Position

Insufficiently resolved, leaning skeptical on A1's *unconditional* sharing claim. `proposition:0011`
(M3's empirical base) is **supported but contested**; at the muscle endpoint, Appelman2024 and the t056
ME/CFS body back H0006's *localization* while still saying too little about cross-trigger same-lesion
equivalence. H0006 stays **speculative** for reasons orthogonal to this comparison; M3 as a general rule
stays **fragile** (single phenotype, between-study instance). No working model is favored.

## Critical Analysis

The strongest move the comparison makes is reclassifying M3 from "rival" to "guardrail": it converts
A1 from an assumption into an endpoint-specified, testable claim. The load-bearing weakness on the M3
side is that its single PEM instance is a between-study contrast (Keller2014 vs Gattoni2025 vs
Appelman2024 — different cohorts, case definitions, protocols), carrying the exact ascertainment
confound M3 exists to police; the Gattoni2025 null is also weak (n=15, 40% deconditioned) [@Gattoni2025]. The
load-bearing weakness on the H0006 side is that A1's sharing claim has *no* harmonized Appelman-type
cross-trigger muscle datum at all. The deflationary third reading (premature divergence driven by an underpowered
null) remains live and must not be dismissed.

## Evidence Needed

A harmonized, multi-endpoint, cross-trigger PEM study (one protocol/site; ME/CFS + long COVID +
recovered/deconditioned controls; whole-body 2-day CPET with peripheral-vs-central O₂-extraction
decomposition **and** pre/immediately-post/24–48 h muscle biopsy for OXPHOS, fiber-type, and ionic
markers). This single design adjudicates A1 vs M3, is H0006's promotion criterion #2 plus the missing
ME/CFS muscle arm, and is the within-cohort harmonized comparison `question:0015`/`task:t025` flag as
non-existent. The cheaper interim has now been run (`task:t056`): ME/CFS muscle-bioenergetic data exist,
but are not endpoint-equivalent to Appelman2024.
