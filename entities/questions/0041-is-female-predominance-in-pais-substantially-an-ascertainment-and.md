# Is female predominance in PAIS substantially an ascertainment and healthcare-seeking artifact?

## Summary

The strong, consistent female predominance reported across PAIS and adjacent syndromes (long COVID,
ME/CFS, fibromyalgia) is usually read as a clue to sex-specific biology (autoantibody load, X-linked
immune-gene dosage, sex-hormone immunomodulation) — the framing of `question:0007`. This question asks
the deflationary counterpart: **how much of that female skew is generated by differential ascertainment
and healthcare-seeking** — women being more likely to present, be surveyed, self-refer to symptom
cohorts, and be diagnostically coded — rather than by a genuine biological excess. It is the
sex-specific application of `hypothesis:0008` (measurement/ascertainment bias shapes apparent PAIS
group differences). The sharp, testable form: **the sex ratio should shrink toward parity when
ascertainment is unbiased (community sampling, not referral) and when endpoints are objective rather
than self-reported.** The best current answer, on the evidence below, is that ascertainment **inflates
and reshapes** the female excess substantially but does **not manufacture** it — a real, smaller female
predominance survives unbiased sampling.

## Why It Matters

- **Decision it affects:** whether the project should spend mechanistic effort hunting a *biological*
  cause for the *magnitude* of female predominance, or first discount that magnitude for ascertainment.
  Over-reading a partly-artifactual sex ratio would misdirect the sex-difference mechanism program
  (`question:0007`, `hypothesis:0005`) and inflate sex as an effect modifier in every downstream
  stratified analysis.
- **Risk if unanswered:** cross-study sex ratios (which range from near-parity to >20:1 depending on
  sampling frame) get pooled and interpreted as biology, baking an ascertainment artifact into the
  project's covariate model and into any sex-stratified mechanism claim.

## Current Evidence

**Supporting the artifact reading (ascertainment inflates the skew):**

- **Fibromyalgia — the cleanest demonstration, but a *non-infectious read-across* (`cite:Wolfe2018`,
  `cite:Walitt2015`; D-003).** In referral/selection-biased clinical samples fibromyalgia is **95.3%
  female** (~20:1), but under *unbiased* ascertainment (self-report 2016 criteria applied to unselected
  cohorts) the female proportion falls to **≤60%** (58.7% in an RA cohort; 59.2% in a German
  general-population sample — i.e. ~1.4:1). Survey-based US community prevalence (2012 NHIS) gives women
  2.38% vs men 1.06%, ~2.2:1. The sampling frame alone moves the sex ratio from ~20:1 to ~1.4–2.2:1 —
  direct proof that referral/ascertainment *can* inflate a female skew by roughly an order of magnitude.
  **Per D-003 this is fibromyalgia, a non-infectious syndrome held as boundary-monitor / read-across, not
  a PAIS case:** it demonstrates the *mechanism is possible*, but it is not evidence that the same
  inflation operates at the same magnitude in the infection-triggered syndromes, and it cannot count as
  independent cross-trigger support. Treat it as a non-infectious read-across of the ascertainment
  mechanism.
- **ME/CFS — diagnostic inequality is real, but not shown to *produce the sex ratio*
  (`cite:Samms2025`, `cite:Jason1999`).** English primary-care records give a recorded F:M ratio of
  **3.88:1** (peaking ~6:1 mid-life). `cite:Samms2025` demonstrates strong diagnostic inequality — by
  **age, ethnicity, deprivation, and geography** (176 GP practices recording *zero* diagnoses,
  concentrated in deprived areas) — establishing that ascertainment shapes *who* gets an ME diagnosis.
  **Important scope limit:** it shows unequal access along those axes; it does **not** show that the
  female:male ratio *itself* is generated by sex-differential ascertainment (it does not decompose the
  sex ratio into access vs biology). The classic community-based CFS study (`cite:Jason1999`) overturned
  the earlier clinic-derived "white, middle-class women" picture — finding high prevalence among women
  *and* minority / lower-education groups — so the clinic sex-and-class *profile* was partly an access
  artifact, but a female excess remained in the community sample.
- **Long COVID — the female excess rests on self-report (`cite:Cohen2023`, `cite:Shah2025`).**
  The widely cited female excess (`cite:Shah2025`: female RR 1.31) rests on a *self-reported* symptom
  estimand. `cite:Cohen2023` found women had 1.76× higher odds of self-reported long COVID but were
  *slightly less* likely than men to report activity limitations. **Scope limit:** the
  activity-limitation endpoint is itself **self-reported and conditioned on already reporting long
  COVID** — so it cannot identify an ascertainment mechanism, and it is *not* an objective-endpoint
  incidence comparison. It shows the sex contrast differs *between two self-reported measures*, which is
  suggestive that the crude excess is measurement-sensitive, but it does not demonstrate attenuation on an
  objective endpoint.

**Evidence that keeps a biological reading open (but does not confirm the residual is biological):**

- **A residual excess survives unbiased ascertainment.** Even in community/survey samples the female
  predominance does not vanish — community fibromyalgia stays ~1.4–2.2:1, community CFS remains
  female-skewed. Ascertainment reshapes and inflates; it does not fully explain the excess. **This does
  not, however, establish that the residual is *biological*** — a residual can also reflect
  sex-differential symptom *reporting* that unbiased sampling frames do not remove.
- **Sex-difference immune biology exists in principle (`cite:Klein2016`, `cite:Silva2024`).** There are
  well-established general sex differences in immunity (biallelic escape of X-linked immune genes,
  sex-hormone immunomodulation, stronger female antibody/T-cell responses, higher female autoimmune
  susceptibility; `cite:Klein2016`), and long-COVID-specific immune-endocrine correlates have been
  reported (lower testosterone associating with higher symptom burden; more exhausted T cells; higher
  latent-herpesvirus antibody reactivity in women; `cite:Silva2024`, preprint — confirm peer-reviewed
  status before hardening). **Scope limit:** these show a biological mechanism is *available* to explain a
  female excess; they do **not** demonstrate that the *residual PAIS excess (after ascertainment) is
  attributable to that biology* rather than to residual differential reporting. The composition of the
  residual is the open question.

## Thoughts

- **Best current interpretation:** ascertainment/healthcare-seeking **plausibly inflates the observed
  female excess** — demonstrated cleanly as a *mechanism* in fibromyalgia (a non-infectious read-across,
  D-003: ~20:1 → ~1.4–2.2:1 across sampling frames) and consistent with documented diagnostic inequality
  in ME/CFS. **But the biological-versus-reporting composition of the *residual* excess remains
  unresolved.** A residual female predominance survives unbiased sampling, yet the available evidence
  does not identify whether that residual is genuine sex-difference biology or sex-differential symptom
  reporting that unbiased frames do not remove. So the honest verdict is *not* "real sex-difference
  biology persists" — it is that ascertainment inflates the excess by an unknown fraction and the nature
  of the remainder is open. Note too that the strongest inflation evidence is non-infectious read-across;
  direct, infection-triggered evidence that ascertainment inflates the *sex ratio specifically* is
  thinner.
- **Major remaining uncertainty:** the specific prediction that the long-COVID female skew shrinks toward
  parity under *objective biomarker/imaging* endpoints is only partially supported — attenuation is shown
  for a *functional* endpoint (`cite:Cohen2023`), but a clean objective-endpoint sex-ratio study is
  **[UNVERIFIED / missing]**. This is the discriminating test the project would need to commission or
  find. Second uncertainty: how much of the residual excess is sex-differential *healthcare-seeking* vs
  genuine sex-differential *biology*, which unbiased ascertainment alone does not separate.
- **Priority:** P3 — a calibration/discounting question rather than a mechanism discovery; its main value
  is preventing over-reading of sex ratios in `question:0007` / `hypothesis:0005` and in stratified
  analyses.
