# Is female predominance in PAIS substantially an ascertainment and healthcare-seeking artifact?

## Summary

The strong, consistent female predominance reported across PAIS and adjacent syndromes (long COVID,
ME/CFS, fibromyalgia) is usually read as a clue to sex-specific biology (autoantibody load, X-linked
immune-gene dosage, sex-hormone immunomodulation) — the framing of `question:0007`. This question asks
the deflationary counterpart: **how much of that female skew is generated by differential ascertainment
and healthcare-seeking** — women being more likely to present, be surveyed, self-refer to symptom
cohorts, and be diagnostically coded — rather than by a genuine biological excess. It is the
sex-specific application of `hypothesis:0008` (measurement/ascertainment bias shapes apparent PAIS
group differences). The sharp, testable form: **the sex ratio should shrink toward parity when
ascertainment is unbiased (community sampling, not referral) and when endpoints are objective rather
than self-reported.** The best current answer, on the evidence below, is that ascertainment **inflates
and reshapes** the female excess substantially but does **not manufacture** it — a real, smaller female
predominance survives unbiased sampling.

## Why It Matters

- **Decision it affects:** whether the project should spend mechanistic effort hunting a *biological*
  cause for the *magnitude* of female predominance, or first discount that magnitude for ascertainment.
  Over-reading a partly-artifactual sex ratio would misdirect the sex-difference mechanism program
  (`question:0007`, `hypothesis:0005`) and inflate sex as an effect modifier in every downstream
  stratified analysis.
- **Risk if unanswered:** cross-study sex ratios (which range from near-parity to >20:1 depending on
  sampling frame) get pooled and interpreted as biology, baking an ascertainment artifact into the
  project's covariate model and into any sex-stratified mechanism claim.

## Current Evidence

**Supporting the artifact reading (ascertainment inflates the skew):**

- **Fibromyalgia — the cleanest demonstration (`cite:Wolfe2018`, `cite:Walitt2015`).** In
  referral/selection-biased clinical samples fibromyalgia is **95.3% female** (~20:1), but under
  *unbiased* ascertainment (self-report 2016 criteria applied to unselected cohorts) the female
  proportion falls to **≤60%** (58.7% in an RA cohort; 59.2% in a German general-population sample —
  i.e. ~1.4:1). Survey-based US community prevalence (2012 NHIS) gives women 2.38% vs men 1.06%, ~2.2:1.
  The sampling frame alone moves the sex ratio from ~20:1 to ~1.4–2.2:1. This is direct proof that
  referral/ascertainment can inflate a female skew by roughly an order of magnitude in a PAIS-adjacent
  syndrome.
- **ME/CFS — access and coding disparities (`cite:Samms2025`, `cite:Jason1999`).** English primary-care
  records give a recorded F:M ratio of **3.88:1** (peaking ~6:1 mid-life), but the same analysis finds a
  strong deprivation gradient and 176 GP practices recording *zero* diagnoses, concentrated in deprived
  areas — read by the authors as unequal healthcare access / under-ascertainment, not biology. The
  classic community-based CFS study (`cite:Jason1999`) overturned the earlier clinic-derived
  "white, middle-class women" picture, finding the highest prevalence among women *and* minority /
  lower-education groups — i.e. the clinic sex-and-class profile was itself an access artifact.
- **Long COVID — the female excess concentrates in self-report (`cite:Cohen2023`, `cite:Shah2025`).**
  The widely cited female excess (`cite:Shah2025`: female RR 1.31) rests on a *self-reported* symptom
  estimand. When the endpoint is more functional, the disparity attenuates: `cite:Cohen2023` found women
  had 1.76× higher odds of self-reported long COVID but were *slightly less* likely than men to report
  activity limitations — the sex gap nearly evaporates on the functional endpoint.

**Conflicting evidence (the female excess is at least partly real biology):**

- **A genuine excess survives unbiased ascertainment.** Even in community/survey samples the female
  predominance does not vanish — fibromyalgia stays ~1.4–2.2:1, community CFS remains female-skewed.
  Ascertainment reshapes and inflates; it does not fully explain.
- **Mechanistic sex-difference biology is real (`cite:Klein2016`, `cite:Silva2024`).** There are
  well-established sex differences in immunity (biallelic escape of X-linked immune genes,
  sex-hormone immunomodulation, stronger female antibody/T-cell responses, higher female autoimmune
  susceptibility; `cite:Klein2016`). Long-COVID-specific immune-endocrine correlates (lower testosterone
  associating with higher symptom burden; more exhausted T cells; higher latent-herpesvirus antibody
  reactivity in women) provide a concrete biological substrate (`cite:Silva2024`; note: preprint —
  confirm peer-reviewed status before hardening).

## Thoughts

- **Best current interpretation:** female predominance in PAIS is **inflated and reshaped by
  ascertainment/healthcare-seeking, not substantially manufactured by it.** The defensible quantitative
  prior is that unbiased sampling roughly halves-to-quarters the crude clinic sex ratio, leaving a real
  ~1.3–2:1 excess with a plausible biological basis. The strong form of the question ("substantially an
  artifact") is *supported for the excess magnitude* but *refuted for the existence* of a female excess.
- **Major remaining uncertainty:** the specific prediction that the long-COVID female skew shrinks toward
  parity under *objective biomarker/imaging* endpoints is only partially supported — attenuation is shown
  for a *functional* endpoint (`cite:Cohen2023`), but a clean objective-endpoint sex-ratio study is
  **[UNVERIFIED / missing]**. This is the discriminating test the project would need to commission or
  find. Second uncertainty: how much of the residual excess is sex-differential *healthcare-seeking* vs
  genuine sex-differential *biology*, which unbiased ascertainment alone does not separate.
- **Priority:** P3 — a calibration/discounting question rather than a mechanism discovery; its main value
  is preventing over-reading of sex ratios in `question:0007` / `hypothesis:0005` and in stratified
  analyses.
