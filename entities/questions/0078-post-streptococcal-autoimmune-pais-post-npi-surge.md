# Does the post-NPI S. pyogenes surge elevate pediatric post-streptococcal PAIS (PANDAS/PANS, ARF, reactive arthritis) burden beyond standard iGAS surveillance?

## Summary

Furgier et al. (2026) document a +628% post-NPI surge in *S. pyogenes*-attributed pediatric mastoiditis in France that outlasted the general iGAS wave and was most pronounced in children under 5 — consistent with an NPI-era immunity gap in those who had their first GAS exposures compressed into the post-NPI window. *S. pyogenes* is the causative agent of well-characterized post-streptococcal autoimmune syndromes: acute rheumatic fever (ARF), post-streptococcal reactive arthritis, PANDAS/PANS, and Sydenham's chorea. If the post-NPI GAS surge was accompanied by elevated post-streptococcal autoimmune sequelae, this would represent a pediatric-specific PAIS pathway not currently captured by iGAS surveillance, which focuses on acute-phase severity (invasive disease, mortality) rather than chronic post-infectious morbidity.

## Why It Matters

- **Decision this question affects:** whether post-streptococcal conditions should be added to the PAIS syndrome inventory as a pediatric arm with a distinct trigger (GAS) and distinct autoimmune mechanism (anti-GAS antibody cross-reactivity with neuronal and cardiac antigens)
- **Risk if unanswered:** the immunity-debt frame in the project is currently analyzed through the lens of SARS-CoV-2/long COVID and ME/CFS; if post-pandemic GAS exposure creates a pediatric PAIS cohort via post-streptococcal autoimmunity, the project's scope would be systematically missing a large, epidemiologically visible group
- **Surveillance gap:** standard iGAS surveillance captures acute invasive disease but not chronic post-streptococcal sequelae (PANDAS diagnoses, ARF incidence, rheumatology referrals) — a possible hidden signal in the same time-series window

## Current Evidence

**Supporting:**
- Post-NPI S. pyogenes mastoiditis: +628% above expected trend in France, concentrated in children under 5 (Furgier et al. 2026), demonstrating a real surge in first GAS exposures in a naïve cohort
- iGAS global resurgence in 2022–2023 post-NPI documented across multiple countries (Vieira et al. 2024, M1UK lineage; UK UKHSA surveillance)
- Post-streptococcal autoimmune mechanisms are well established: ASO titer elevation, anti-neuronal antibodies (anti-D1R, anti-lysoganglioside), molecular mimicry with cardiac antigens
- ARF incidence is known to vary with GAS epidemic intensity and exposure history

**Conflicting / gaps:**
- No study to date has directly linked the post-NPI iGAS wave to elevated ARF, PANDAS/PANS, or reactive arthritis incidence using time-series methods comparable to Furgier et al.
- PANDAS/PANS remain diagnostically contested, making population surveillance difficult
- The dominant post-NPI GAS lineage in France is not characterized — M1UK and other high-virulence lineages differ in their rheumatogenic potential

## Thoughts

- **Best current interpretation:** the post-NPI iGAS surge almost certainly produced elevated rates of post-streptococcal autoimmune conditions in countries with high iGAS rebound; the question is whether this is detectable in ICD-coded administrative data or rheumatology/neurology registries, and whether its magnitude is clinically significant relative to the iGAS wave itself
- **Major uncertainty:** the ratio of post-streptococcal PAIS to acute GAS infections may not have changed even if both rose proportionally — what would matter is whether the immunity-gap cohort (first-exposure children) has a higher post-streptococcal autoimmune conversion rate than cohorts with prior immunity
