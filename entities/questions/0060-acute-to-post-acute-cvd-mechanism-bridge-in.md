# Which acute influenza-to-CVD mechanisms extend into post-acute cardiovascular sequelae, and by what pathway?

## Summary

Skaarup 2023 catalogues the acute-phase mechanisms by which influenza infection elevates
cardiovascular risk: direct endovascular plaque destabilisation, ectopic-trypsin-mediated
myocardial entry, hypercoagulability, adrenergic biomechanical stress, and hypoxaemia-driven
Type 2 MI. These mechanisms are well-characterised for the acute window (days to ~4 weeks).
The open question is whether any of these pathways persist or transition into post-acute
cardiovascular sequelae — sustained vascular inflammation, durable prothrombotic state,
fibrotic myocardial remodelling, or accelerated atherosclerosis — and if so, which pathway
mediates the transition and over what timescale.

## Why It Matters

- Determines whether the influenza→CVD literature is a usable mechanistic template for
  SARS-CoV-2→post-acute CVD burden (i.e. whether "long flu CVD" and "long COVID CVD" share
  pathways, not just acute triggers).
- Without an acute-to-post-acute bridge, influenza remains only an acute-phase comparator and
  cannot anchor the PAIS vascular-persistence hypotheses (h0002, h0014).
- Risk if unanswered: the field may over-extrapolate acute-phase mechanisms to explain
  months-long CVD sequelae without the causal warrant to do so.

## Current Evidence

- **Acute-phase documented:** hypercoagulability, plaque destabilisation, direct myocardial
  infection, and Type 2 MI risk are well-established for influenza (Skaarup 2023; Kwong NEJM
  2018; Musher NEJM 2019).
- **Post-acute gap:** Skaarup 2023 explicitly does not address post-acute CVD sequelae; the
  review is scoped to the acute window. Evidence for sustained post-influenza CVD burden beyond
  4–8 weeks is sparse and largely absent from the reviewed literature.
- **SARS-CoV-2 comparator:** elevated CVD risk persisting >1 year post-acute COVID-19 is
  documented (Xie & Al-Aly 2022); the mechanistic overlap with acute-phase pathways is debated.
- **Myocardial fibrosis:** animal models (Kenney 2022) demonstrate fibrosis after direct
  cardiac influenza replication; whether this persists and contributes to long-term cardiac
  dysfunction in humans is unknown [UNVERIFIED].

## Thoughts

- The best current interpretation is that most acute influenza→CVD risk resolves with infection
  clearance; the longer-term CVD burden is under-studied for influenza compared to COVID-19.
- If the vascular antigen-persistence mechanism (influenza RNA in arterial walls; Haidari 2010 [@Haidari2010])
  is real and durable, it could sustain low-grade plaque-destabilising inflammation post-acutely —
  this is the most plausible bridge candidate.
- The major uncertainty is the persistence half-life of influenza material in vascular tissue
  and whether it crosses the threshold for sustained immune activation.
