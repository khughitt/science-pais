# What mechanism underlies the consistent female predominance of post-infectious fatigue across PAIS, and why does it track somatic fatigue but not post-infectious depression?

## Summary

Female sex is the most consistent predictor of PAIS across triggers (Choutka2022), with near-identical female odds for post-dengue fatigue (OR ~1.65-1.69; Hertanti2025, Conde2026) and sharply longer recovery for older females in long COVID (Gusinow2026). Strikingly, the female excess tracks somatic post-infectious *fatigue* but not post-infectious *depression* (Conde2026). This question asks what mechanism underlies the female predominance and what the fatigue/depression dissociation implies about PAIS neuroimmune subtypes.

## Why It Matters

- A correct mechanism (e.g. estrogen-driven B-cell/autoimmune amplification vs sex-differentiated immune-regulatory resilience) would identify a modifiable risk axis and inform sex-stratified trial design.
- If unanswered, the single most reproducible PAIS risk factor remains unexplained, and the project's covariate-modeling guidance (sex, age, hormonal state) lacks a mechanistic anchor.

## Current Evidence

- Supporting: Hertanti2025 and Conde2026 independently report female OR ~1.65-1.69 for post-dengue fatigue but a null for post-dengue depression; Gusinow2026 finds ~4x longer recovery in older females vs young males, attributed to sex-differentiated immune regulation independent of acute severity; Ganesh2022 reports IL-6 elevation more common in women.
- Conflicting / cautionary: healthcare-seeking and ascertainment differences could inflate apparent female predominance (Zhang2022 raises this for PASC subphenotypes); estrogen-amplification is a hypothesis, not yet demonstrated mechanistically in PAIS.

## Thoughts

- Best current interpretation: a sex-differentiated immune-regulatory setpoint (plausibly estrogen-modulated B-cell/autoimmune tone) lowers the threshold for failed resolution; the fatigue-vs-depression dissociation suggests sex-linked immune dysregulation selectively drives the somatic-fatigue arm rather than neuropsychiatric outcomes.
- Major uncertainty: whether the driver is hormonal (estrogen), X-linked immune-gene dosage, or sex differences in autoimmunity propensity — and how much is artefactual ascertainment.

## Connections to Project

- Related hypotheses: `hypothesis:0001-shared-dysregulated-attractor` (host-predisposition seeding); informs the covariate model for all PAIS analyses.
- Required data or analyses: sex- and hormone-status-stratified immune profiling across PAIS; mediation analysis of sex → autoantibody/IL-6 → fatigue vs depression; correction for care-seeking bias.
- Priority level: P2 — high cross-PAIS leverage, mechanistically underdetermined.
