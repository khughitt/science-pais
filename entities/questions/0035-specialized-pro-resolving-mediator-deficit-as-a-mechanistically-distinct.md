# Specialized pro-resolving mediator deficit as a mechanistically distinct PAIS endotype

## Summary

Is a **failure of the active, SPM-driven inflammation-*resolution* program** — the lipid-mediator
class-switch to resolvins, protectins, maresins, and lipoxins that normally terminates inflammation — a
mechanistically distinct PAIS endotype, separable from persistent pro-inflammatory *drive*? The
wound-healing-derived prediction: such an endotype would be identifiable by a lipid-mediator metabolomic
signature and would respond preferentially to **SPM precursors/analogs** (ω-3-derived pro-resolving
mediators) rather than to anti-inflammatories/immunosuppression.

## Why It Matters

- **Decision it affects:** whether to pursue **pro-resolution** therapeutics and a lipid-mediator
  stratifier — a drug class orthogonal to immunosuppression — for a resolution-failure subset.
- **Risk if unanswered:** if resolution-failure is real but unmeasured, anti-inflammatory trials will keep
  failing in exactly the subgroup whose problem is a failed *switch-off*, not excess *drive* — and the
  failures will be misread as "inflammation isn't involved" rather than "the wrong node was targeted."

## Current Evidence

- **Supporting — indirect, acute-severity only:** Archambault et al. 2021 (FASEB J) [@Archambault2021], a
  bronchoalveolar-lavage lipidomic study of mechanically-ventilated COVID-19 patients (n=33 vs 25
  controls), documented a **"lipid mediator storm"** in which SPMs (D-series resolvins, lipoxin A4, PDX)
  are *elevated* but **co-occur** with massive pro-inflammatory eicosanoids (thromboxane, leukotrienes,
  prostaglandins) — a pattern the authors read as **"resolution has not fully been engaged."** This is the
  nearest published evidence of a resolution class-switch *failure* in the COVID cascade.
- **Framework backbone:** the Serhan resolution-of-inflammation / lipid-mediator class-switch model — SPMs
  actively drive resolution; failure of the switch yields chronic, non-resolving inflammation. (General
  resolution biology; see scope note below.)
- **Conflicting / limiting — the empirical gap is the headline:** there is **no direct SPM or
  lipid-mediator measurement in any PAIS cohort** (long COVID / ME-CFS / post-infectious). Critically,
  Archambault2021 shows SPMs *elevated* (not deficient) in acute severe COVID — so a literal **"SPM
  deficit"** framing is **not** supported by the available data. The defensible claim is
  **resolution-class-switch failure / inadequate SPM response relative to pro-inflammatory load**; the
  further leap that this leaves a *chronic residual resolution incompetence in PAIS* is `[SPECULATION]`.
  Evidence is also severity-bounded (ventilated ICU patients) and acute (no post-acute follow-up).

## Thoughts

- **Best current interpretation:** resolution-failure is a mechanistically coherent, therapeutically
  distinct candidate endotype — but it currently rests on framework-import plus one acute-severe-COVID
  lipidomics paper. The endotype should be **reframed from "SPM deficit" to "resolution class-switch
  failure / SPM-response inadequacy,"** which is what the data actually support.
- **Major uncertainty:** whether *any* measurable SPM/lipid-mediator abnormality persists into PAIS — the
  entire empirical basis is missing. A long-COVID / ME-CFS **lipid-mediator metabolomics** study
  (plasma/serum, cases vs full-recovery controls) is the obvious and decisive next dataset.
