# Do PTLDS, long COVID, and ME/CFS share a non-length-dependent autonomic small-fiber neuropathy substrate that distinguishes them from primary dysautonomia?

## Summary

Dysautonomia (especially POTS) and small-fiber neuropathy (SFN) recur across long COVID, post-vaccine PASC, PTLDS, and ME/CFS. Several authors propose a *non-length-dependent, autonomic* SFN — targeting dorsal root ganglia — as a shared peripheral substrate (a "final common pathway") for autonomic symptoms and exercise intolerance. This question asks whether that substrate is genuinely shared and whether it distinguishes PAIS dysautonomia from primary (idiopathic) dysautonomia.

## Why It Matters

- Decides whether a single diagnostic instrument (standardized skin-biopsy SFN typing + autonomic testing) can phenotype dysautonomia across PAIS, and whether immunomodulatory therapy aimed at autoimmune SFN is rational across syndromes.
- If unanswered, "POTS" risks being treated as one entity when it has plural substrates (mast-cell-mediated, structural-autoimmune, central), each needing different therapy.

## Current Evidence

- Supporting: Oaklander2022 provides the earliest prospective long-COVID referral series using paired distal+proximal skin biopsies (63% distal / ~50% proximal abnormal), demonstrating that proximal involvement commonly accompanies distal SFN — consistent with a non-length-dependent pattern, though per-patient NLD classification is not formally reported; Adler2024 proposes non-length-dependent autonomic SFN as a final common pathway across PTLDS/PASC/ME-CFS; Limongelli2026 finds an autoimmune-pattern (non-length-dependent in ~33%) SFN in post-vaccine PASC with an SLE/Crohn's/RA-like innate-like T-cell profile; Bateman2023 documents SFN in up to 50% of FM and 30% of ME/CFS iCPET patients; **Joseph2021** (ingested 2026-06-24) provides the largest primary skin-biopsy IENFD study in ME/CFS specifically (n=160, NAM criteria, 31% lower-leg SFN confirmed by PGP9.5 biopsy) — directly fills the ME/CFS skin-biopsy gap for P4, though the distal-only biopsy protocol means the non-length-dependent pattern (P2) is not assessable.
- Conflicting / cautionary: Finsterer2022 argues post-COVID dysautonomia is mechanistically heterogeneous (SFN vs GBS-residual vs central HPA), not uniformly SFN; Salvucci2023 shows a reversible mast-cell-mediated dysautonomia subtype that is not structural neuropathy.

## Thoughts

- Best current interpretation: SFN is a real and recurrent substrate, but dysautonomia is plural; a shared *non-length-dependent autoimmune* SFN subtype likely exists across PAIS but coexists with non-neuropathic (mast-cell, central) substrates.
- Major uncertainty: no cross-syndrome study uses a standardized skin-biopsy protocol with primary-dysautonomia controls; comparator arms (asymptomatic, idiopathic POTS) are missing.
