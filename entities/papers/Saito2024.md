# Metabolomic and immune alterations in long COVID patients with chronic fatigue syndrome

- **Authors:** Suguru Saito, Shima Shahbaz, Xian Luo, Mohammed Osman, Desiree Redmond, Jan Willem Cohen Tervaert, Liang Li, and Shokrollah Elahi
- **Year:** 2024
- **Journal:** Frontiers in Immunology
- **DOI/URL:** https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2024.1341843
- **BibTeX key:** Saito2024
- **Source:** Full-text PDF (papers/pdfs/2024_Saito_metabolomic-immune-alterations-long-covid-cfs.pdf), read 2026-06-20.

## Key Contribution

This study provides a paired plasma metabolomic and immune biomarker profile of long COVID (LC) patients who specifically met ME/CFS diagnostic criteria, comparing them to age-sex-matched recovered SARS-CoV-2 individuals (R), acute hospitalized COVID-19 patients (A), and unexposed healthy controls (HC) recruited ~12 months post acute infection. The central finding is that LC-with-ME/CFS patients exhibit persistent, broad metabolomic abnormalities — spanning at least 18 altered pathways — alongside elevated pro-inflammatory cytokines and markedly reduced plasma ATP, establishing a signature of sustained immune activation coupled with apparent bioenergetic failure that overlaps ME/CFS literature. A key clinically actionable observation is that sarcosine and serine concentrations are specifically reduced in LC patients and inversely correlate with depression, anxiety, and cognitive dysfunction scores, suggesting potential therapeutic targets.

## Methods

**Design:** Single-center (University of Alberta Hospital), cross-sectional observational cohort study. PCR-confirmed SARS-CoV-2 Wuhan-strain infection; all LC and R participants recruited ~12 months post acute illness onset.

**Cohorts (N = 75 total):**
- LC: n = 30 (80% female; 40% aged <49, 36.6% aged 50–60, 23.3% aged >61); met CDC/WHO + DePaul Symptom Questionnaire ME/CFS criteria. LC patients who did not meet ME/CFS criteria were excluded.
- R (recovered, no LC symptoms): n = 15
- A (acute, ICU-admitted COVID-19): n = 15
- HC (SARS-CoV-2 unexposed, seronegative, pre-pandemic recruited): n = 15

**Metabolomics:** Untargeted plasma metabolomics via Chemical Isotope Labeling (CIL) LC-MS (Agilent 1290 / Bruker Impact II QTOF). 2,584 metabolites detected across 75 plasma samples. Multivariate analysis: PLS-DA; univariate: volcano plots (fold-change >1.5, p < 0.05); pathway enrichment via MetaboAnalyst 5.0.

**Immune and cytokine profiling:** V-PLEX multiplex cytokine/chemokine panel (Meso Scale Discovery) for IL-1α, IL-6, TNF-α, IP-10, IL-13, IL-1β, CRP, SAA, Flt-1. Soluble CD14 (sCD14) by ELISA (R&D Systems). Anti-CaSR (calcium-sensing receptor) antibody levels (EAGLE Biosciences). Plasma ATP by ATPlite luminescence assay.

**Clinical assessments:** 7-questionnaire battery including DePaul Symptom Questionnaire (DSQ) for ME/CFS, FACIT fatigue scale, FACIT-Dyspnea, Fibromyalgia (FM) criteria, Cognitive Failure Questionnaire (CFQ), Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI), Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS). Correlation analyses used two-tailed Spearman correlation; group comparisons via Kruskal-Wallis / Mann-Whitney U with Dunn's post hoc; p < 0.05 threshold.

**Case definition:** ME/CFS criteria per CDC and WHO long-COVID guidelines; validated by DSQ scoring frequency ≥2 and severity ≥2 on at least one symptom per category across 6 symptom domains (fatigue, malaise, sleep, pain, cognitive/neurological, autonomic/neuroendocrine/immune). LC patients not meeting ME/CFS criteria were excluded — this is an enriched ME/CFS-positive LC cohort, not a general LC cohort.

## Key Findings

**Metabolomic landscape:**
- LC patients show a clearly distinct plasma metabolomic profile from both R and HC groups by PLS-DA; R group sits between HC and LC, suggesting a partial or prolonged recovery trajectory.
- 18 metabolic pathways are significantly altered in LC vs HC, including: glycine/serine/threonine, alanine/aspartate/glutamate, arginine biosynthesis, arginine/proline, histidine, beta-alanine, tryptophan, cysteine/methionine, pantothenate/CoA biosynthesis, pyrimidine, purine, glutathione, nicotinamide, sphingolipid, porphyrin/chlorophyll, glyoxylate/dicarboxylate, and aminoacyl-tRNA biosynthesis.
- 15 of those 18 pathways are also altered in LC vs R, with the 3 differentially altered only vs HC being glutamine/nitrogen/butanoate metabolism; cysteine/methionine and glutathione metabolism are additionally altered vs R but not HC.

**Specific depleted metabolites in LC (vs HC and/or R):**
- Sarcosine, serine, aspartate, asparagine, glutamine, histidine, N-formimino-glutamate, ethanolamine phosphate, uracil, arginine, dehydroalanine, thymine, porphobilinogen (decreased vs HC).
- Aspartate, asparagine, glutamine, histidine, N-formimino-glutamate, sarcosine, ethanolamine phosphate (decreased vs R).

**Specific elevated metabolites in LC:**
- 5-Aminolevulinate, cystatho, putrescine, 4-aminobutyraldehyde, kynurenine, serotonin, Formyl-5-hydroxykynurenamine, 5-Hydroxykynurenamine, 2-Aminomuconate, xanthine, 4-Aminolevulinate (increased vs HC).
- 4-Aminobutanoate, 4-aminobutyraldehyde, 3-hydroxyanthranilate, porphobilinogen (increased vs R).

**ATP — bioenergetic signal:**
- Plasma ATP is substantially reduced in LC patients relative to both HC and R groups (Kruskal-Wallis, p significant). Notably R patients show a significant *elevation* in plasma ATP compared to HC — suggesting a rebound during recovery that does not occur in LC. This is interpreted as evidence for bioenergetic dysfunction in the LC-with-ME/CFS phenotype and draws a parallel to mitochondrial impairment described in ME/CFS.

**Pro-inflammatory cytokines and immune markers (LC vs R and/or HC):**
- Elevated: IL-1α, IL-6, TNF-α, IP-10 (CXCL10), CRP, SAA (all significantly elevated vs HC and/or R).
- Not significantly different: IL-13, IL-1β (between groups).
- Flt-1 (sVEGFR-1): significantly elevated in LC vs HC (not vs R), suggesting vascular/endothelial involvement.
- sCD14: significantly elevated in LC vs both R and HC — marker of monocyte/macrophage activation indicative of ongoing innate immune activation.
- Anti-CaSR antibody: significantly elevated in LC compared to both HC and R (p = 0.0001), pointing toward an autoimmune component targeting the calcium-sensing receptor.
- Soluble CaSR: elevated in acute COVID patients but not LC, distinguishing acute-phase shedding from LC autoantibody response.

**Sarcosine and serine — symptom correlations:**
- Serine levels inversely correlate with anxiety score (R² = 0.20, p = 0.028) and depression score (R² = 0.317, p = 0.0018) in LC patients.
- Sarcosine levels inversely correlate with cognitive failure score (R² = 0.33, p = 0.004) and depression score (R² = 0.27, p = 0.01) in LC patients.
- No significant association found between serotonin, tryptophan, aspartate, or kynurenine levels and neuropsychiatric symptom scores.

**Sex disparity:** ~80% of LC patients in this cohort are female, consistent with broader LC epidemiology and the female predominance in ME/CFS.

**Recovered group metabolomics:** R individuals also exhibit altered metabolomic profiles at 12 months (~15 altered metabolic pathways vs HC), suggesting that metabolic recovery from SARS-CoV-2 is prolonged even without ongoing LC symptoms.

## Relevance

**Bioenergetic-PEM question (question:0011-mitochondrial-basis-of-pem):** The finding of significantly reduced plasma ATP in LC-with-ME/CFS patients is one of the paper's most directly relevant results. The authors explicitly parallel this to mitochondrial dysfunction reported in idiopathic ME/CFS (citing studies of circulating lymphocytes), and interpret low ATP as consistent with impaired mitochondrial biogenesis and/or activity. TCA cycle intermediates (fumarate, succinate, malate) were not significantly different between groups — the authors acknowledge this as a limitation and suggest it reflects tissue-specific rather than circulating ATP deficits. This is weaker evidence for mitochondrial mechanism than direct mitochondrial function assays but provides an accessible blood biomarker signal consistent with the hypothesis.

**LC↔ME/CFS convergence (topic:mecfs-long-covid-convergence):** The study was explicitly designed to enrich for the ME/CFS phenotype within LC, and the metabolomic alterations found — reduced glutamine/nitrogen metabolism, amino acid imbalances, elevated kynurenine pathway metabolites, low sarcosine/serine — echo metabolic signatures in idiopathic ME/CFS such as those reported in Naviaux2016 (metabolic features of chronic fatigue syndrome). In particular, Naviaux2016 identified reduced purines and disturbed amino acid metabolism in ME/CFS; this paper finds overlapping perturbations in LC-with-ME/CFS, supporting the hypothesis that SARS-CoV-2-triggered LC converges on the same dysregulated metabolic attractor as ME/CFS (hypothesis:0001-shared-dysregulated-attractor).

**Immune dysregulation (topic:long-covid-immune-dysregulation, hypothesis:0003-immune-exhaustion-feedback):** Elevated sCD14 (monocyte activation marker), pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1α, IL-6, TNF-α, IP-10), and anti-CaSR autoantibodies at 12 months post-infection indicate that immune dysregulation — including innate immune activation and an autoimmune component — persists well into the chronic phase. This supports the persistent immune activation arm of hypothesis:0003. The authors propose that impaired antiviral response may enable antigen persistence, perpetuating chronic inflammation.

**Biomarkers (topic:biomarkers-and-objective-endpoints):** sCD14, anti-CaSR antibody titer, plasma ATP, sarcosine, and serine emerge as candidate objective biomarkers distinguishing LC-with-ME/CFS from recovered and healthy individuals. The inverse correlations of sarcosine and serine with neuropsychiatric scores are particularly noteworthy as they link an objective metabolomic measure to subjective symptom dimensions.

**Shared failure mode (topic:shared-failure-mode-across-pais):** Persistent metabolomic abnormalities even in the asymptomatic R group at 12 months suggests a broader post-SARS-CoV-2 metabolic sequela that does not require frank LC; LC represents the extreme of a continuum of disturbed recovery.

## Limitations

- **Small sample size:** n = 30 LC, n = 15 per comparison group; single-center (University of Alberta). Limits statistical power and generalizability; imbalanced group sizes noted by authors.
- **Enriched ME/CFS cohort design:** By excluding LC patients not meeting ME/CFS criteria, findings apply specifically to the LC-with-ME/CFS phenotype and cannot be generalized to the broader heterogeneous LC population. This is a strength for mechanistic focus but limits prevalence estimates.
- **Cross-sectional, no longitudinal sampling:** Single plasma draw per participant; cannot track intra-individual trajectory or establish temporal causality. Longitudinal metabolomic sampling would be needed to map the recovery arc.
- **Wuhan-strain only:** All participants infected with Wuhan strain; differential immune responses to Delta, Omicron, and other variants are not captured.
- **No lipid panel:** Metabolomic analysis did not include lipids, leaving lipid metabolism (an important energy substrate and membrane component pathway) uncharacterized. This is a major gap given known lipid dysregulation in ME/CFS.
- **Plasma ATP is systemic, not tissue-specific:** TCA cycle intermediates were not significantly altered; tissue-level (e.g., muscle, PBMC) ATP and mitochondrial function measurements would be more mechanistically informative.
- **Vaccine-naïve cohort:** Both LC and R participants had not received SARS-CoV-2 vaccines, limiting applicability to vaccinated populations where metabolic and immune responses may differ.
- **Female-skewed LC cohort (80%):** Sex was not adequately powered as a biological variable; sex-stratified analyses were not feasible. This limits interpretability across sexes.
- **Descriptive metabolomics:** Authors acknowledge analyses are descriptive (no causal inference or intervention data). Correlations of sarcosine/serine with symptom scores are cross-sectional associations, not evidence of causal direction.
- **No validation cohort:** Single discovery cohort; findings require replication in independent, larger, ideally longitudinal cohorts.

## Model / Tool Availability

No model, software tool, or processed dataset was released as a reusable artifact. The original metabolomics data are described as included in Supplementary Material; further inquiries directed to the corresponding author (elahi@ualberta.ca). ## Follow-up

**Papers to read next:**
- Naviaux2016 (already in project): compare the specific depleted metabolite lists between idiopathic ME/CFS and LC-ME/CFS in Saito2024 — how much overlap exists at the individual-metabolite level vs pathway level?
- Peppercorn2023 (already in project): cross-reference immune biomarker profiles with this study's cytokine findings.
- Appelman2024 (already in project): compare mitochondrial function findings in muscle with the plasma ATP reduction here.
- Fraser et al. 2020 (cited as ref 35 in paper): plasma metabolomics of critically ill COVID-19 patients — acute-phase comparison context.
- Kovarik et al. 2023 (cited as ref 73): multi-omics anti-inflammatory immune signature in long COVID (3-month cohort) — contrast with this 12-month ME/CFS-enriched study.

**Questions this raises:**
- Does the reduced plasma ATP in LC-with-ME/CFS correlate with direct measures of mitochondrial respiratory capacity in PBMCs or skeletal muscle (cf. question:0011)?
- Is the sarcosine/serine depletion a consequence of immune-mediated amino acid catabolism (e.g., IDO/TDO shunting tryptophan toward kynurenine), or does it reflect a separate synthetic deficit?
- Does anti-CaSR autoantibody titer correlate with symptom severity or specific phenotypic clusters (e.g., dysautonomia, cognitive dysfunction) within LC-with-ME/CFS?
- How do the 15 metabolic pathways altered in both LC vs HC and LC vs R compare to those altered in idiopathic ME/CFS cohorts (e.g., Naviaux2016) — a systematic metabolite-overlap analysis would quantify convergence?
- Given the 80% female composition of the LC cohort, are the sarcosine and serine associations sex-specific or robust across sexes?
