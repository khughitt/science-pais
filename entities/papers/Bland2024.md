# Profiles of objective and subjective cognitive function in Post-COVID Syndrome, COVID-19 recovered, and COVID-19 naive individuals

- **Authors:** A. R. Bland, M. Barraclough, W. R. Trender, M. A. Mehta, P. J. Hellyer, A. Hampshire, I. K. Penner, R. Elliott, S. Harenwall
- **Year:** 2024
- **Journal:** Scientific Reports, 14:13368
- **DOI/URL:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-62050-x
- **BibTeX key:** Bland2024
- **Source:** Full-text PDF via Unpaywall OA (read 2026-06-22)

## Key Contribution

Cross-sectional study (n=162) directly comparing objective and subjective cognitive function across three groups — Post-COVID Syndrome (PCS, n=50), Recovered-COVID (n=59), and No-COVID (n=53) — using both a battery of online neurocognitive tasks (Cognitron) and self-report questionnaires. The key finding is a near-zero, non-significant correlation between objective and subjective cognition (r=−0.07, p=0.161), establishing that self-reported "brain fog" does not track objectively measured cognitive performance. Subjective complaint is statistically explained by fatigue and perceived stress rather than COVID-19 status, while the objective deficit is COVID-exposure-linked and persists even in fully recovered individuals.

## Methods

**Design:** Cross-sectional; online recruitment via Academic Prolific. Participants completed questionnaires then were redirected to Cognitron tasks remotely on phone, tablet, or computer.

**Inclusion criteria:** PCS group required new or ongoing symptoms 12+ weeks after acute COVID-19 infection (NICE 2020 definition). Exclusion: prior/current clinically diagnosed psychiatric or neurological disorder; mood-altering medication at time of study; individuals with anxiety or depression diagnosis were excluded to control for confounders (notable caveat — see Limitations).

**Groups:**
- Post-COVID Syndrome (PC): n=50; mean age 41.66; 58% female; 96% PCR/LFT confirmed; mean 10.48 months since diagnosis; 18% hospitalized
- Recovered-COVID (RC): n=59; mean age 38.12; 62% female; 89.7% PCR/LFT confirmed; mean 12.83 months since diagnosis; 10% hospitalized
- No-COVID (NC): n=53; mean age 41.98; 47% female; never COVID-positive

**Subjective cognition:** Cognitive Failures Questionnaire (CFQ), 25-item self-report of everyday memory, distractibility, forgetfulness, and false triggering in past 2 weeks (α=0.94).

**Objective cognition:** Cognitron battery — Block Rearrange (spatial problem solving), Tower of London (spatial planning/executive function), Spatial Span (working memory), Target Detection (spatio-visual attention), Object Memory — Immediate and Delayed (episodic memory). A global index score (cogFactor) was derived via PCA.

**Covariates:** Fatigue Scale for Motor and Cognitive Functions (FSMC, α=0.96); Perceived Stress Scale 10-item (PSS-10, α=0.93); Post-COVID symptom and impact tool.

**Statistics:** MANCOVA with COVID group as between-subjects factor; subjective and objective cognition as dependent variables; age and education as covariates; fatigue and stress entered as additional covariates in follow-up models. Bonferroni correction applied. Pillai's trace statistic used.

## Key Findings

**Objective-subjective dissociation (load-bearing result):**
- Objective and subjective cognitive function were **not significantly correlated** across the full sample (r=−0.07, p=0.161).
- This is the central dissociation finding: "brain fog" self-reports do not reflect objectively measured cognitive performance.

**Subjective cognition driven by fatigue and stress, not COVID status:**
- MANCOVA (without fatigue/stress covariates): PCS reported significantly more subjective cognitive difficulties than Recovered-COVID (p=0.006, CI=2.29, 18.09) and No-COVID (p<0.001, CI=3.75, 19.72) groups.
- When fatigue and stress were added as covariates, the main effect of COVID group on **subjective** cognition became non-significant (F(2,154)=0.56, p=0.575, η²=0.01).
- Fatigue showed a significant effect on subjective cognition (F(2,154)=36.61, p<0.001, η²=0.32); stress also significant (F(2,154)=4.62, p=0.033, η²=0.03).
- Subjective cognition correlated with general Post-COVID symptoms (r=0.407, p=0.002) but not with neurological symptom count.

**Objective cognition linked to COVID exposure, not fatigue:**
- MANCOVA: significant main effect of COVID group on **objective** cognition (F(2,156)=4.47, p=0.013, η²=0.05).
- Both PCS (p=0.038) and Recovered-COVID (p=0.026) performed significantly worse than No-COVID participants on the global cogFactor.
- When fatigue and stress were added: the COVID group effect on objective cognition **remained significant** (F(2,154)=4.61, p=0.011, η²=0.06) — objective deficit is not attributable to fatigue or stress.
- Stress had a modest but significant effect on objective cognition (F(2,154)=4.75, p=0.031, η²=0.03); fatigue did not (F(2,154)=0.98, p=0.325, η²=0.01).
- The most marked objective deficits were in Object Memory (Immediate: PC<NC***, RC<NC**; Delayed: PC<NC**, RC<NC**) and spatial span (PC<NC*).

**Time since infection and objective recovery:**
- Objective cognition improved with time since infection in **Recovered-COVID only** (r=0.26, p=0.048), not in PCS (r=0.12, p=0.390).
- No improvement in subjective cognition over time (F(1,155)=0.158, p=0.692, η²=0.002).

**Neurological symptom burden:**
- Objective cognitive function correlated negatively with neurological symptom count in PCS (r=−0.265, p=0.034), controlling for age and education — more neurological symptoms predicted worse objective performance.
- Neurological symptom count excluded items directly referencing brain fog/memory/concentration/word-finding to avoid circularity.

**Clinical and demographic covariates:**
- PCR confirmation and hospitalization did not significantly affect either subjective or objective cognition.
- Groups were well-matched on age, sex, and education.

## Relevance

**Core contribution to PAIS cognitive subphenotype framing:**

This paper provides a clean, three-arm cross-sectional test of the subjective/objective cognitive dissociation in Post-COVID Syndrome. The near-zero correlation (r=−0.07) between CFQ and Cognitron global score establishes that subjective complaint ("brain fog") and objectively measured cognitive performance are empirically distinct constructs in this population.

The mechanistic implication is important: subjective complaint operates through fatigue and perceived stress pathways (explaining its association with general Post-COVID symptoms), while objective deficits reflect COVID exposure itself and track neurological symptom burden (not fatigue). This dissociation aligns with comparable findings in ME/CFS (Cockshell & Mathias 2014), post-Q-fever (Duits et al.), and ICU survivors (Brück et al.) — suggesting a cross-PAIS pattern rather than a COVID-specific artifact.

**Relevance to task:t018** (compare female/reproductive-stage excess across PAIS subphenotypes): if the subjective cognitive complaint in PAIS is fatigue-mediated rather than COVID-driven, female-predominant PAIS subphenotypes may show elevated "brain fog" complaint partly through the fatigue pathway, independent of any objective cognitive exposure effect. This would be a confound in subphenotype analyses that rely on self-reported cognitive difficulty as the outcome.

**Relevance to question:0007** (mechanism of female predominance in PAIS): PCS group was 58% female, RC group 62% female vs NC 47% female — the female predominance is visible but not the focus of this paper and no sex-stratified analyses were reported. Subjective cognition being fatigue-driven is directly relevant to the fatigue/depression dissociation discussed in q0007: if subjective cognition tracks fatigue (which has female predominance), then "brain fog" complaints will systematically co-vary with the female-excess fatigue phenotype even when objective cognitive deficits are COVID-status-linked and sex-neutral.

**Corroborates dissociation independently of:**
- Cheetham2023 (large REACT cohort, objective/subjective dissociation)
- Delgado-Alonso2023 (neuropsychological profiling in long COVID)

## Limitations

1. **Cross-sectional design:** Cannot establish causal direction; fatigue explaining subjective cognition may reflect common underlying cause (e.g. neuroinflammation driving both) rather than a unidirectional mediation.

2. **Exclusion of depression/anxiety diagnoses:** Deliberately excluded participants with clinically diagnosed depression or anxiety to control confounders — but PCS patients may have *acquired* depression as a consequence of COVID-19. This potentially underestimates the psychological drivers of subjective cognition in real-world PCS and creates a sample that does not represent the broader PCS population.

3. **Modest sample size (n≈50 per group):** Adequate for primary MANCOVA contrasts but insufficient to detect smaller subgroup effects (e.g. sex-stratified objective/subjective dissociation). Eighteen percent of both PC and RC groups had more than one COVID infection, further complicating exposure homogeneity.

4. **Self-reported COVID-19 status:** Primarily PCR/LFT confirmation (96% PC, 89.7% RC) but some participants lacked test records at time of acute infection — introduces possible misclassification, though proportions are small.

5. **Online/unsupervised cognitive testing:** Cognitron tasks validated for remote use, but environmental variability (noise, interruptions, device differences) is uncontrolled and could add noise to objective scores.

6. **Single time point per participant:** No within-person pre-COVID baseline; cannot disentangle pre-existing cognitive differences from infection-acquired ones.

7. **No sex-stratified analysis reported:** Female proportions differ across groups (PC 58%, RC 62%, NC 47%) but no sex-stratified cognitive analyses were conducted; the sex distribution confound on fatigue-driven subjective complaint cannot be evaluated from this paper.

8. **Fatigue scale (FSMC) designed for MS:** Originally validated in Multiple Sclerosis; adapted use in post-COVID is reasonable but the scale's factor structure and discriminant validity for separating somatic from cognitive fatigue in PCS warrants caution.

## Follow-up

- **Cheetham2023** and **Delgado-Alonso2023** provide independent replication of the subjective/objective dissociation — triangulate their effect sizes and sample characteristics for convergence assessment.
- **Sex-stratified reanalysis or meta-analysis:** None of the three dissociation papers (Bland, Cheetham, Delgado-Alonso) reports sex-stratified cognitive outcomes; an open question is whether the objective deficit is equal across sexes while the subjective complaint (fatigue-mediated) is female-skewed.
- **Fatigue as mediator vs confounder:** A causal mediation analysis with fatigue as mediator between COVID status and subjective cognition would test the mechanism more cleanly than the covariate-addition approach used here.
- **Longitudinal extension:** Given that objective recovery is significant in RC but not PCS over ~10-13 months, a prospective cohort study comparing objective trajectory in PCS who later remit vs persist would directly test whether sustained neurological symptom burden (r=−0.265) is the mechanism of the plateau.
