# Chronic sequelae complicate convalescence from both dengue and acute viral respiratory illness

- **Authors:** Shirin Kalimuddin, Yii Ean Teh, Liang En Wee, Shay Paintal, Ram Sasisekharan, Jenny G. Low, Sujata K. Sheth, Eng Eong Ooi
- **Year:** 2022
- **Journal:** PLOS Neglected Tropical Diseases, vol. 16, no. 8, e0010724
- **DOI:** 10.1371/journal.pntd.0010724
- **BibTeX key:** Kalimuddin2022
- **Source:** PDF

## Key Contribution

This prospective cohort study provides controlled, quantitative evidence that chronic post-infectious sequelae — fatigue and CNS-related symptoms persisting ≥3 months — are common after both dengue (18.0% of participants) and non-SARS-CoV-2 acute viral respiratory infections (ARI; 14.6%), establishing that this failure of homeostatic recovery is not unique to SARS-CoV-2/long COVID. Crucially, patients with chronic sequelae had significantly lower SF-12v2 Mental Component Summary (MCS) scores at month 3 compared to those who recovered (median 47.7 vs. 56.0, p<0.001 combined cohorts), demonstrating functional impairment beyond self-reported symptoms. The study also validated MAIDEN, a mobile-phone-based remote data collection platform, as an effective and scalable tool for longitudinal post-infection surveillance.

## Methods

**Design:** Prospective observational cohort study, Singapore, September 2017–March 2019 (pre-COVID-19 pandemic). Two parallel cohorts followed up to 12 months post-enrolment.

**Participants:** 209 patients enrolled (48 dengue, 161 ARI) from inpatient wards, emergency departments, and staff healthcare clinics at two Singapore hospitals. Dengue diagnosis required confirmed NS1 antigen and/or DENV PCR. ARI diagnosis required compatible symptoms plus respiratory virus multiplex PCR (Anyplex II RV16 panel, detecting influenza A/B, RSV A/B, parainfluenza 1–4, metapneumovirus, rhinovirus, OC43, 229E, NL63, adenovirus, enterovirus, bocavirus 1–4). Exclusion criteria included active malignancy, neurological/rheumatological/psychiatric conditions, concomitant bacterial infection, immunosuppressive therapy, and chronic viral infection (HIV, hepatitis B/C).

**Data collection platform:** MAIDEN (Mobile-phone Application for Information collection in Dengue), a purpose-built smartphone app. Patients entered symptom data daily (days 1–7), weekly (weeks 2–8), and monthly (month 3 onward). All data uploaded to secure cloud and downloaded to REDCap. Only the first study visit required in-person attendance.

**Symptom assessment:** Five symptoms tracked: fatigue, somnolence, headache, concentration impairment, memory impairment. Mental health assessed using the validated SF-12v2 health survey; Mental Component Summary (MCS) scores calculated using Optum PRO CoRE software (population average = 50; below 50 = worse than average).

**Case definition:** "Chronic sequelae" = any of the five symptoms persisting ≥3 months post-enrolment. Symptoms considered resolved if absent for ≥2 consecutive months.

**Statistics:** Fisher's exact test (categorical), Mann-Whitney test (continuous), Kaplan-Meier curves for time-to-symptom-resolution, GraphPad Prism 9.2.0.

**Cohort characteristics:** Dengue cohort was 63% male, 77.1% hospitalised; ARI cohort was 29% male (p<0.001), 18.1% hospitalised (p<0.001). Median age 37 years (dengue) vs. 34 years (ARI). Comorbidities (diabetes, hypertension, ischemic heart disease) balanced and low-prevalence in both groups.

## Key Findings

**Acute symptom burden (week 1):**
- Nearly all patients reported at least one CNS-related symptom within the first week: 95.8% of dengue patients and 94.4% of ARI patients.
- Fatigue and somnolence were most prevalent in dengue; lethargy and headache were most common in ARI.

**Chronic sequelae prevalence (≥3 months follow-up):**
- Dengue cohort (n=39 with ≥3 months follow-up): 18.0% met the chronic sequelae definition (7/39).
- ARI cohort (n=158): 14.6% (23/158); rate not statistically different from dengue (p=0.62).
- Most common chronic symptoms in dengue: fatigue, concentration impairment, memory impairment (each 10.3%).
- Most common chronic symptoms in ARI: fatigue (9.5%), headache (8.2%), somnolence and concentration impairment (each 6.3%).

**Temporal dynamics (Kaplan-Meier):**
- The majority of dengue patients resolved fatigue and CNS symptoms within the first weeks of convalescence. However, a plateau in symptom persistence was observed, with a subset remaining symptomatic through month 12.
- ARI patients showed a similar biphasic pattern: rapid early resolution with a persistent tail through 12 months.

**Mental health impact (SF-12v2 MCS at month 3):**
- Combined cohorts: median MCS 47.7 (chronic sequelae) vs. 56.0 (resolved), p<0.001.
- ARI cohort alone: median MCS 46.6 vs. 56.0, p<0.01.
- Dengue cohort alone: median MCS 50.0 vs. 55.7, p=0.20 (non-significant, likely underpowered; n=7 chronic sequelae cases).

**No predictive demographic factors identified:**
- No statistically significant difference in age, gender distribution, or hospitalisation status between patients with and without chronic sequelae in either cohort (Table 3), suggesting sequelae risk is not readily predicted by standard clinical variables.

**Viral etiology in ARI (n=29 with confirmed pathogen):**
- Influenza A/B: 48.3%; Rhinovirus: 20.7%; Adenovirus: 13.8%; OC43: 13.8%; Parainfluenza: 10.3%.

**Mechanistic discussion (authors):**
- Authors propose that aberrant innate immune activation may cause chronic inflammation, long-term tissue damage, and immune exhaustion, driving chronic sequelae. They cite associations between FcγRIIa (FcγRIIa-131HH) gene polymorphism, autoimmune markers, and symptom persistence in dengue (Garcia et al. 2011). They draw explicit parallels with proposed long COVID mechanisms: chronic inflammation, antigen persistence, latent virus reactivation, and autoantibody formation.
- Authors also note prior work showing T-cell exhaustion gene expression at baseline correlating with post-vaccination fatigue, suggesting a shared immune dysregulation substrate.

## Relevance

This paper directly addresses the project research question (research-question:post-acute-infection-syndromes) by providing prospective epidemiological evidence that failed homeostatic recovery after acute infection is a general phenomenon extending across multiple viral pathogens, not an idiosyncrasy of SARS-CoV-2. Key connections to the PAIS frame:

1. **Failed homeostatic recovery as a shared failure mode:** 14–18% of patients with dengue or common respiratory viruses (influenza, rhinovirus, adenovirus) fail to recover fully within 3 months — the same symptomatic phenotype (fatigue, cognitive impairment) seen in long COVID and ME/CFS. The Kaplan-Meier plateau structure is classic for a bimodal recovery population: most recover, but a subset enters a persistent dysregulated state.

2. **Functional impairment:** The MCS finding (median 47.7 vs. 56.0, p<0.001) establishes that self-reported persistence translates to measurable functional mental health deficits — not merely subjective complaint. This aligns with the project's interest in how PAIS impairs normal physiology and quality of life.

3. **Cross-pathogen prevalence data:** This is one of few prospective studies to compare post-dengue and post-ARI chronic sequelae in parallel with identical methodology, extending the comparative PAIS evidence base. It complements Choutka2022's narrative cross-pathogen review with original quantitative data.

4. **Absence of clinical predictors:** The failure to identify age, sex, or hospitalisation severity as predictors of chronic sequelae echoes long COVID literature and supports the hypothesis that PAIS susceptibility involves host immunogenetics or microenvironmental factors not captured by standard demographics.

5. **Immune exhaustion hypothesis:** The authors' reference to T-cell exhaustion as a potential driver directly connects to immune-homeostasis dysregulation models central to this project and to the immunity peer project (~/d/health/processes/immunity).

## Limitations

- **Small dengue cohort:** Only 39 patients with ≥3 months follow-up; the chronic sequelae subgroup in dengue was just 7 patients, giving insufficient power to detect differences in MCS or demographic predictors.
- **ARI cohort not tested for dengue:** Co-infection with DENV in ARI patients could not be excluded (Singapore is dengue-endemic), potentially misclassifying some ARI chronic sequelae.
- **Imbalanced hospitalisation rates:** Dengue patients were hospitalised at much higher rates (77.1% vs. 18.1%), precluding head-to-head comparison between cohorts. The study explicitly avoided this comparison.
- **Self-reported symptom outcomes:** All symptom data were self-reported via MAIDEN; no objective biomarker or neuropsychological testing was performed. No blood, immunological, or imaging data were collected to explore mechanisms.
- **No untreated control group:** No healthy control cohort was enrolled for comparison of MCS baseline, so MCS differences between chronic-sequelae and recovered patients cannot be fully attributed to post-infection state vs. pre-existing differences.
- **Pathogen confirmation in ARI limited:** Only 29/161 ARI patients had confirmed viral etiology; the majority were enrolled on clinical criteria alone.
- **Pre-COVID cohort:** Recruitment ended March 2019; the study population and healthcare context are pre-pandemic Singapore, which may limit generalizability to broader or post-pandemic settings.
- **No mechanistic data:** The study is entirely descriptive/epidemiological; mechanistic hypotheses are speculative based on cited literature, not from this study's data.

## Model / Tool Availability

**MAIDEN (Mobile-phone Application for Information collection in Dengue):** A purpose-built smartphone application for remote, longitudinal symptom data collection in acute viral infections. Not described as publicly released. Data collected via MAIDEN was stored on a secure cloud server and downloaded to REDCap. Contact: Singapore General Hospital research office (research.office@sgh.com.sg) for data access.

No computational models, trained classifiers, or publicly accessible datasets were released from this study.

## Follow-up

- **Mechanistic follow-up:** This study calls explicitly for research into the pathophysiology of post-dengue and post-ARI chronic sequelae — particularly whether T-cell exhaustion, FcγRIIa polymorphism, and autoimmune markers are causal. Follow-up studies with immunological profiling of participants at baseline vs. chronic-sequelae onset would be informative.
