# Core features and inherent diversity of post-acute infection syndromes

- **Authors:** Alain Trautmann
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal:** Frontiers in Immunology
- **DOI:** 10.3389/fimmu.2025.1509131
- **BibTeX key:** Trautmann2025
- **Source:** PDF

## Key Contribution

This narrative review proposes a unifying theoretical framework for post-acute infection syndromes (PAIS) — long COVID, ME/CFS, and long Lyme/PTLDS — built around two pillars: (1) a set of individual predispositions that seed self-sustained pathological vicious circles after a poorly resolved infection; and (2) a fundamentally broken interoceptive feedback loop between the brain and periphery that maintains the dyshomeostatic state. The central thesis is that PAIS should be understood not as a single disease entity but as a family of self-assembling "building blocks" (pathological vicious circles) that combine in different proportions across individuals to produce both shared core symptoms and striking clinical diversity.

## Methods

This is a single-author narrative/theoretical review (Frontiers in Immunology, review article type). No original cohort data are generated. The author synthesizes published experimental and clinical literature across immunology, neuroscience, mitochondrial biology, microbiology, and interoception research, covering long COVID, ME/CFS, and long Lyme/PTLDS. The review explicitly draws on data from patient surveys, brain imaging studies (PET, MRI), clinical exercise testing (CPET, 10-min NASA Lean Test), proteomics, and metabolomics described in the cited primary literature. Table 1 was constructed from clinical expert input and published case series.

## Key Findings

**Shared core symptom cluster:** Fatigue, brain fog (impaired memory/attention), myalgia, unrefreshing sleep, and headaches are rated very frequent (>50%) in all three PAIS (long COVID, ME/CFS, long Lyme/PTLDS; Table 1). Pathogen-specific features (e.g., anosmia in long COVID, arthritis in long Lyme) are relatively few.

**PAIS may be triggered by diverse pathogens:** Table 2 enumerates at least 13 viral and 1 bacterial species with documented post-acute sequelae, including SARS-CoV-2, SARS, Ebola, dengue, polio, enteroviruses, Chikungunya, West Nile virus, H1N1 influenza, EBV, and *Borrelia*. Pathogen-specific tropism determines some tissue-level persistence sites (e.g., SARS-CoV-2 in gut, brain, heart, lymphoid tissue; *Borrelia* in brain/synovial fluid).

**Four self-sustained inflammatory loops (Figure 3):**
1. T cell / B cell / monocyte-macrophage triangle: activated Th1 T cells → IFN-γ → monocyte/macrophage activation → antigen presentation → further T and B cell activation.
2. Microglial self-sustaining neuroinflammatory loop: peripheral inflammatory cytokines cross a damaged blood–brain barrier, activate microglia → more pro-inflammatory cytokines → further microglial activation.
3. Mitochondrial ROS loop: excessive ROS damages mitochondria → reduced ATP, more ROS → impaired oxidative phosphorylation, inflammation inhibits mitochondrial respiration.
4. Bacterial translocation / gut-dysbiosis loop: inflamed, damaged gut mucosa → microbial and LPS translocation → systemic inflammation amplified by dysbiosis.

**Individual predispositions as building blocks:** The author catalogs at least six heritable/acquired individual frailties that each seed one or more of the above loops: weak immune responsiveness (fails to clear pathogen), excessive immune/inflammatory responsiveness, propensity to autoimmunity, propensity to microclot formation, mitochondrial dysfunction, and psychic scars / toxic lifestyle / immunological scars (Figure 3, Figure 4A). These predispositions are heterogeneous and mostly sub-clinical before triggering.

**Interoception as the central organizer of PAIS:** The author frames PAIS as a failure of interoception — the brain's capacity to sense, integrate, and respond to internal body states. Key anatomical nodes: nucleus tractus solitarius (NTS), parabrachial nucleus (PBN), paraventricular nucleus (PVN) of hypothalamus, amygdala, insular cortex (Figure 2). The dysfunctional brain-body dialog explains fatigue, dysautonomia, HPA axis dysfunction, and why PAIS symptoms cluster around the autonomic-neuroendocrine interface.

**Dysautonomia and PEM:** ANS dysfunction (impaired vagal/parasympathetic activity) manifests as POTS (postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome) or orthostatic hypotension. Post-exertional malaise (PEM) is linked to impaired muscle repair (plasma hsp70 approximately 4× lower in ME/CFS patients than healthy controls; after maximal exercise hsp70 increases >50% in HC but decreases 15–30% in ME/CFS patients). VO₂max in long COVID patients is 30–45% lower than healthy controls on CPET.

**HPA axis dysfunction:** The cortisol awakening response (net cortisol increase in the first hour post-waking) is markedly blunted in severe ME/CFS, post-TBI, and PTSD patients. Chronic HPA stimulation desensitizes the axis and converts its normally anti-inflammatory acute response into a pro-inflammatory state.

**Tryptophan–kynurenine pathway (TKP) metabolic disruption:** Inflammation (IFN-γ) upregulates IDO, diverting tryptophan away from serotonin synthesis toward kynurenine/quinolinic acid. Low plasma tryptophan and low TKR (tryptophan/kynurenine ratio) have been reported in both long COVID and ME/CFS. Quinolinic acid is neurotoxic; kynurenic acid is anti-inflammatory but reduces serotonin.

**Gut microbiota and intestinal permeability:** Elevated plasma zonulin (a marker of tight-junction opening) persists in long COVID patients. Fungal translocation from gut to blood is elevated in post-acute SARS-CoV-2 sequelae (NCT05747534 trial testing lazarotide, a paracellular permeability inhibitor, currently enrolling). Dysbiosis (reduced butyrate-producing bacteria) is documented in long COVID, ME/CFS, and long Lyme.

**Waddington epigenetic landscape analogy (Figure 4B):** Health and PAIS are modeled as two basins of attraction. Infection can push the organism over the ridge into the PAIS basin. The depth and shape of the PAIS basin is determined by individual building-block combinations, explaining why single-target treatments tend to fail and multi-component interventions are needed.

**Composite PAIS subtyping proposed:** The author recommends profiling patients along four axes: (1) inflammatory biomarkers (IL-6, TNF-α, IL-1β, IFN-α, neutrophil/NET markers); (2) zonulin, cortisol awakening response, tryptophan, kynurenine; (3) questionnaire-based symptom severity; (4) functional tests (HRV, VO₂max, orthostatic tolerance, cognitive tests). DNA methylation-based clustering of PBMCs has already yielded unambiguous ME/CFS vs. HC separation in published studies.

## Relevance

This review maps directly onto the project research question (research-question:post-acute-infection-syndromes) and its PAIS-as-failed-homeostatic-recovery frame on multiple dimensions:

1. **Shared failure mode:** Trautmann's "building block" model is an explicit mechanistic articulation of the project's core hypothesis that PAIS syndromes represent a convergent failure of post-infectious homeostatic recovery rather than disease-specific pathologies. The four self-sustained inflammatory loops operationalize exactly what "persistent immune activation" and "failure to restore baseline" mean.

2. **Interoception as the homeostatic feedback system:** The interoception framework directly addresses *why* homeostasis fails to re-establish: the brain's interoceptive sensing apparatus is disrupted, so the normal negative-feedback signals that would terminate inflammatory responses and restore autonomic/endocrine baselines are blunted or misinterpreted. This provides a neuroimmunological mechanism linking immune dysregulation to dysautonomia, fatigue, and brain fog under a single theoretical umbrella.

3. **Cross-PAIS evidence:** By explicitly comparing long COVID, ME/CFS, and long Lyme/PTLDS, the paper supports the project framing that these are instances of a common failure mode. The shared core symptom cluster (Table 1) and the overlapping pathogen list (Table 2) are directly usable as evidence for cross-PAIS convergence.

4. **Individual terrain heterogeneity:** The predisposition-as-building-block model explains why cohort-level analyses often fail to find consistent biomarkers: different patients assemble different subsets of loops. This has practical implications for the project's computational analysis plans.

5. **Antigen/pathogen persistence:** Trautmann gives an up-to-date synthesis of viral reservoir evidence (gut, brain, cardiac, lymphoid tissues for SARS-CoV-2; B cells for EBV; synovial fluid for *Borrelia*), directly connecting to the project's antigen-persistence axis.

## Limitations

1. **Single-author narrative review with no systematic search:** No PRISMA or equivalent protocol. Evidence synthesis reflects one expert's reading of a large heterogeneous literature; selection biases may favor the interoception/autonomic framing.

2. **Mechanistic claims largely correlative:** The four proposed self-sustained loops are plausible and well-supported by mechanistic analogy (e.g., from MS, autoimmune diseases), but causal directionality within each loop has rarely been established in PAIS patients specifically. The review itself acknowledges this (e.g., "correlation does not imply causal relationship" for mitochondrial dysfunction/fatigue severity).

3. **ME/CFS causation remains ambiguous:** The review notes that no unique, identified pathogen is responsible for ME/CFS triggering; the epidemiological outbreaks described are historical and lacked rigorous case definition verification.

4. **Interoception framework applied by extrapolation:** While the interoception literature is rich in rodent models and neuroimaging studies, the claim that "failed interoception" is *the* central mechanism of PAIS is a theoretical synthesis; direct measurement of interoceptive accuracy in PAIS patients is sparse.

5. **Sex and age covariates:** The review mentions sex differences in IFN-α responses (women stronger acute response, poorer resolution), but does not systematically model age, sex, or infection-history covariates across the evidence it synthesizes.

6. **No meta-analytic statistics:** All effect sizes (VO₂max 30–45% lower in long COVID; hsp70 4× lower in ME/CFS) are drawn from individual cited studies, not pooled analyses; generalizability is unclear.

7. **Vaccine-induced PAIS / PASC distinction:** The review does not systematically address vaccine-associated adverse events as a potential PAIS trigger, which is a contested adjacent area the project tracks.

## Model / Tool Availability

No software, computational model, or dataset is released with this paper. The composite profiling strategy proposed (combining inflammatory biomarkers, TKP markers, cortisol awakening response, questionnaires, HRV, VO₂max, orthostatic tolerance, cognitive tests) is a conceptual recommendation for future clinical trial design, not an implemented tool.

## Follow-up

- **Cited paper to prioritize:** Griffin's PLoS Biol paper on why viral RNA persists after recovery (Trautmann ref 71, 2021) — directly addresses antigen persistence mechanism.
- **Clinical trial to monitor:** NCT05747534 (lazarotide for intestinal permeability in long COVID children/young adults) — tests a key node in the gut-dysbiosis loop.
- **Waddington landscape formalization:** Goldstein's paper on the extended autonomic system, dysautonomia, and COVID-19 (Trautmann ref 325, 2020) — may provide a more quantitative attractor-state model.
- **Key question raised:** What empirical evidence exists that the four self-sustained loops can be simultaneously active in the same patient, and do their co-occurrence patterns stratify PAIS severity or subtype? This is testable with the composite profiling strategy the review proposes.
- **Epigenetic PBMC clustering paper:** The DNA methylation-based PBMC clustering study by Nikesjo and colleagues (Trautmann ref 328, Clin Epigenet 2022) on PAIS subtyping — potentially high-value computational anchor for project analyses.
