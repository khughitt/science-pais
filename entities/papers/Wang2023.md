# WASF3 disrupts mitochondrial respiration and may mediate exercise intolerance in myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome

- **Authors:** Ping-yuan Wang, Jin Ma, Young-Chae Kim, Annie Y. Son, Abu Mohammad Syed, Chengyu Liu, Mateus P. Mori, Rebecca D. Huffstutler, JoEllyn L. Stolinski, S. Lalith Talagala, Ju-Gyeong Kang, Brian T. Walitt, Avindra Nath, Paul M. Hwang
- **Year:** 2023
- **Journal:** Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (PNAS), vol. 120, no. 34, e2302738120
- **DOI:** 10.1073/pnas.2302738120
- **PMCID:** PMC10450651
- **BibTeX key:** Wang2023
- **Source:** Europe PMC full text (XML; no PDF copy retrieved — OA available at PMC)

## Key Contribution

This paper identifies **WASF3 as a direct molecular mediator of bioenergetic deficiency in ME/CFS**, providing the first named candidate molecule for the exercise intolerance and postexertional malaise that define the syndrome. The discovery originates from a single index patient and is extended mechanistically through cell culture and a transgenic mouse model, then corroborated in a cohort of ME/CFS patient skeletal muscle biopsies.

The central mechanism is: **ER stress → elevated WASF3 protein → WASF3 localizes to mitochondria and physically interacts with Complex III (CIII) of the respiratory chain → impairs assembly of the CIII2+IV supercomplex → reduced Complex IV (cytochrome c oxidase) activity and overall oxygen consumption → decreased ATP synthesis capacity → exercise intolerance/PEM**.

Critically, the authors show that pharmacologic reduction of ER stress decreases WASF3 and rescues supercomplex formation and respiration in the patient's cells, establishing a potentially targetable pathway.

## Methods

**Index patient and family control.** Patient S1: 38-year-old woman with Li-Fraumeni syndrome (germline TP53 P152L mutation) and lifelong severe fatigue beginning after infectious mononucleosis at age 16; previously evaluated for mitochondrial disease (mitochondrial genomic DNA sequencing, skeletal muscle biopsy — all non-diagnostic). Patient S2: S1's 34-year-old unaffected male sibling carrying the identical TP53 mutation — matched genetic control.

**In vivo mitochondrial function.** Phosphocreatine (PCr) recovery kinetics after submaximal foot exercise measured by 31P-magnetic resonance spectroscopy (31P-MRS) at 3 T MRI. PCr recovery time constant (Tc) calculated by monoexponential fitting. Tested twice in S1 (months apart) and once in S2 for reproducibility.

**Cell models.** Primary skin fibroblasts from S1 and S2; human primary myoblasts; mouse C2C12 myoblasts. Oxygen consumption rate (OCR) measured with Seahorse XF24 Analyzer (inhibitors: rotenone, oligomycin, FCCP). WASF3 knocked down via shRNA lentivirus; WASF3 overexpressed via cDNA transfection/transduction.

**WASF3 transgenic mice.** Human BAC clone (~180 kb, including ~16 kb promoter) microinjected into B6D2F1/J zygotes; two independent transgenic lines (WASF3 Tg). Male mice ~10 weeks used for treadmill endurance (graded protocol to exhaustion), grip strength, blood lactate, glycogen, and ex vivo soleus muscle OCR/ECAR (Seahorse).

**Supercomplex analysis.** Blue native polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (BN-PAGE) of digitonin-solubilized mitochondria; in-gel activity assays for CI (NADH/NBT) and CIV (cytochrome c/DAB); immunoblotting with CIII Rieske FeS (UQCRFS1) and MTCO1/MTCO2 antibodies.

**Proximity biotinylation.** Full-length and C-terminus-truncated WASF3 (residues 1–332 vs. 1–499) fused to biotin ligase BirA; streptavidin pull-down assessed WASF3 proximity to CIII subunits UQCRC1 and UQCRFS1.

**ME/CFS patient cohort biopsy samples.** Deidentified skeletal muscle biopsies from healthy volunteers (n = 10) and ME/CFS patients (n = 14) obtained under ClinicalTrials.gov NCT02669212. Immunoblotted for WASF3, MTCO1, COX17, PERK, BiP, GAPDH, LONP1.

**ER stress pharmacology.** ER stress inducers: thapsigargin (100 nM) and tunicamycin (1 µg/mL) in human myoblasts; LPS (5 mg/kg i.p.) in C57BL/6 mice. ER stress inhibitors in patient S1 fibroblasts: tauroursodeoxycholic acid (TUDCA, 100 µM) and salubrinal (10 µM, a PP1 inhibitor stabilizing p-eIF2α).

**Statistical analysis.** Unpaired two-tailed Student's t-test; one-way ANOVA where noted. GraphPad Prism v7.

## Key Findings

**Index patient — in vivo mitochondrial failure.** Patient S1's tibialis anterior PCr Tc was 80 s (reproduced on repeat testing); historical healthy-volunteer range 37–40 s; sibling control S2 (same TP53 mutation) Tc = 30 s. S1's primary fibroblasts showed lower OCR and reduced CIV subunit proteins (MTCO1, MTCO2 ~34% lower) compared with S2.

**WASF3 elevated in S1.** WASF3 protein was ~40% higher in S1 fibroblasts than S2; co-occurring with activated p38 MAPK phosphorylation and Ser46-phosphorylated p53 (downstream of WASF3/p38 signaling).

**WASF3 knockdown rescues respiration.** shRNA knockdown of WASF3 in S1 fibroblasts significantly increased spare respiratory capacity (SRC) to the level of S2 controls, and restored CIV subunit levels. The same rescue was replicated in human myoblasts with or without TP53 mutation and in control fibroblasts — demonstrating the effect is not TP53-specific.

**WASF3 overexpression reproduces the defect.** Stably transfected WASF3 in C2C12 myoblasts decreased respiration and reduced CIV subunit levels while increasing other complex subunits (interpreted as a compensatory mitochondrial biogenesis response via PGC1α induction). Overexpression also activated p38 phosphorylation — these signaling changes were downstream of mitochondrial disruption, not upstream.

**WASF3 transgenic mice.** WASF3 Tg mice (~3-fold WASF3 overexpression in skeletal muscle; levels in heart, brain, liver not markedly elevated):
- Soleus OCR:ECAR ratio significantly lower than wild-type (shifted toward glycolysis).
- Blood lactate elevated at rest — paralleling the elevated resting lactate reported in ME/CFS patients.
- Skeletal muscle glycogen significantly lower (increased glucose utilization compensating for mitochondrial insufficiency).
- Body weight and appearance normal.
- Treadmill endurance **~50% reduced** compared to wild-type littermates.
- Four-paw grip strength unchanged — isolated effect on endurance/aerobic capacity, not contractile force.
- EM of myofilaments showed normal sarcomere structure — the exercise defect is metabolic, not structural.

**WASF3 localizes to mitochondria and disrupts CIII2+IV supercomplex assembly.** Subcellular fractionation confirmed WASF3 enrichment in mitochondria (with a faster-migrating band suggesting mitochondria-specific phosphorylation). BN-PAGE showed that WASF3 overexpression specifically reduced the ~720 kDa SC CIII2+IV band (UQCRFS1 and in-gel CIV DAB staining) with ~50% reduction in CIV supercomplex activity; CI band (NBT) was unaffected. Pre-incubation of mitochondrial lysate with WASF3 antibody mimicked this selective CIII2+IV disruption. Proximity biotinylation showed WASF3's N-terminal domain (residues 1–332) is sufficient for proximity to CIII subunits UQCRC1 and UQCRFS1.

**Elevated WASF3 in ME/CFS patient biopsies.** Blinded skeletal muscle biopsies (10 healthy, 14 ME/CFS) showed significantly higher WASF3 and PERK (ER stress marker), lower BiP (ER chaperone), and reciprocally lower MTCO1 and COX17 in ME/CFS. The levels of CIV subunits (MTCO1, COX17) were significantly negatively correlated with WASF3 (r = −0.55, p = 0.005; r = −0.59, p = 0.003) and PERK (r = −0.49, p = 0.01; r = −0.47, p = 0.02). The ME/CFS samples showed "ER stress response failure" — elevated PERK but paradoxically low inhibitory eIF2α phosphorylation, and low BiP.

**ER stress drives WASF3 posttranslationally.** Thapsigargin and tunicamycin increased WASF3 protein (but not mRNA) in human myoblasts, with reciprocal MTCO1 decrease. LPS in mice similarly elevated WASF3 in skeletal muscle. Salubrinal (eIF2α dephosphorylation inhibitor) reduced PERK and WASF3 in S1 cells, restored CIII2+IV supercomplex assembly (confirmed by BN-PAGE), and improved OCR dose-dependently.

## Relevance

This paper is one of the most mechanistically explicit reports in the ME/CFS literature, offering a **tractable molecular target (WASF3) for the cardinal symptom of exercise intolerance**. It is directly relevant to this project on several levels:

1. **Candidate primary mitochondrial lesion for PEM.** The WASF3-CIII interaction provides a molecularly defined step at which a plausible upstream trigger (ER stress) disrupts oxidative phosphorylation in skeletal muscle. Unlike the many correlational mitochondrial studies in ME/CFS, this paper demonstrates causality in both directions: WASF3 knockdown rescues function; WASF3 overexpression reproduces the defect; transgenic mice recapitulate exercise intolerance.

2. **Upstream signal: ER stress.** ER stress provides a mechanistic bridge from post-infectious/inflammatory perturbations (including viral infections and LPS) to the bioenergetic phenotype. The authors note that SARS-CoV-2 persistence and viral ER stress induction could drive the same pathway in long COVID — directly linking this finding to the PAIS frame.

3. **Corroboration in an ME/CFS cohort.** The biopsy data (NCT02669212, n = 24) place WASF3 elevation in the biologically characterized post-infectious ME/CFS context, not just in an individual LFS case. The negative correlation between WASF3/PERK and CIV subunits is quantitatively consistent across the cohort.

4. **Potential for a metabolic shift marker.** The mouse data recapitulate the elevated resting blood lactate and reduced glycogen observed in ME/CFS patients — a glycolytic shift that could serve as a measurable phenotype bridging to exercise-challenge studies (CPET).

5. **Partial mechanistic connection to inflammation.** The ER stress failure state described in ME/CFS biopsies (high PERK, low BiP, paradoxically low p-eIF2α) echoes features of chronic inflammatory and post-viral conditions, reinforcing the "failed resolution" frame of hypothesis:0001.

## Project Framework Mapping

- **hypothesis:0001 (shared-dysregulated-attractor):** WASF3-mediated bioenergetic failure represents a concrete instance of the "metabolic/mitochondrial impairment" node in the attractor loop. ER stress as the upstream driver could be seeded by persistent viral antigen, microbial translocation (LPS), or immune activation — consistent with the proposition that multiple triggers converge on shared physiological failure modes.
- **question:0011 (mitochondrial-basis-of-pem):** This paper is the strongest direct evidence addressing question:0011: it identifies a specific molecular lesion (WASF3 disruption of CIII2+IV) that causally reduces aerobic ATP production in the relevant tissue (skeletal muscle), with in vivo exercise phenotype confirmation in mice and correlational support in an ME/CFS biopsy cohort. The open question is whether the same WASF3/ER-stress axis is active in PAIS triggered by pathogens other than those historically linked to ME/CFS.
- **Direction-of-effect tension (hypometabolic vs. hyperactivated):** Wang2023 sits at the "compensatory/disrupted-respiration" pole — the primary lesion is a block in mitochondrial electron transfer causing deficient oxidative phosphorylation and compensatory glycolytic upregulation. This is not a hypermetabolic/hyperactivated state but rather a structural failure of energy production under demand. It partially resolves the tension by identifying a specific mechanism rather than a direction label: the failure of supercomplex assembly explains why the muscle cannot meet energy demand during exertion.

## Limitations / Caveats

1. **Single index patient as discovery vehicle.** The initial characterization derives from one patient (S1) with a rare cancer syndrome (Li-Fraumeni / germline TP53 mutation). While the authors use S2 (same TP53 mutation, no fatigue) as an elegant genetic control, the LFS context may introduce confounders. TP53 mutations affect mitochondrial function and the WASF3 is elevated in the setting of a disease not representative of the general ME/CFS population.

2. **Small ME/CFS biopsy cohort.** n = 10 healthy controls and n = 14 ME/CFS patients for the biopsy immunoblot data; correlations are significant but the small N warrants replication. The ME/CFS patients were recruited under NCT02669212 (post-infectious ME/CFS comprehensive phenotyping), but the paper does not report case definition, disease duration, sex breakdown, or illness severity for this subset.

3. **Mouse model overexpresses WASF3 ~3-fold vs. ~40% in human samples.** The authors acknowledge that Tg mice express roughly threefold more WASF3 in skeletal muscle than the patient S1 cells and ME/CFS biopsies, yet the magnitude of downstream CIV and respiration reductions is similar — interpreted as saturation kinetics. This discrepancy between expression and effect requires clarification for dose-response understanding.

4. **ER stress upstream cause not established.** The paper demonstrates that ER stress inducers raise WASF3 and that reducing ER stress rescues supercomplex/respiration, but does not establish *why* ER stress is elevated in ME/CFS patients. The "ER stress response failure" observed (elevated PERK, low BiP, paradoxically low p-eIF2α) is suggestive of a dysregulated unfolded protein response, but the primary driver remains unknown.

5. **No functional exertion challenge in ME/CFS cohort.** The study does not include a 2-day CPET or exercise-challenge protocol in the ME/CFS biopsy cohort, so the direct link between WASF3 elevation, supercomplex disruption, and the provoked PEM state remains inferential.

6. **Cross-PAIS generalizability unproven.** Cohort limited to ME/CFS patients (post-infectious but not further specified by trigger). Whether WASF3 elevation and the ER stress axis operate similarly in long COVID, PTLDS, or other PAIS is speculative, though the authors note SARS-CoV-2 can induce ER stress.

7. **Correlation vs. causality in the biopsy cohort.** The negative WASF3-CIV correlation in patient biopsies is observational; the causal direction shown in cell/mouse systems is not directly proven in the human cohort.

## Follow-up

- Confirm whether WASF3 expression and ER stress markers (PERK, BiP, p-eIF2α) are elevated in long COVID skeletal muscle biopsies relative to recovered COVID and healthy controls — the most direct test of cross-PAIS applicability.
- Assess WASF3/CIII2+IV status pre- and post-standardized exercise challenge (two-day CPET) in ME/CFS, long COVID, and recovered controls, to directly link the molecular lesion to the provoked PEM state (connects to question:0011).
- Test whether salubrinal or other ER stress modulators (TUDCA, ISR-targeting compounds) improve exercise capacity in ME/CFS in a controlled trial — the paper's pharmacology section provides cell-level proof of concept.
- Examine whether viral persistence or immune-mediated ER stress (e.g. via interferon signaling, unfolded viral proteins) quantitatively predicts WASF3 levels across PAIS cohorts.
- Consider whether the elevated resting blood lactate and reduced glycogen (measurable in mice and clinically accessible in humans) could serve as indirect WASF3-pathway activity markers for stratification or response monitoring.
