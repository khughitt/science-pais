## Key Contribution

This large-scale retrospective cohort study from the US Veterans Affairs (VA) healthcare system is the first comprehensive 3-year follow-up of post-acute sequelae of COVID-19 (PASC), tracking 135,161 SARS-CoV-2-infected individuals and 5,206,835 controls across 80 prespecified outcomes in 10 organ systems. The central finding is that while PASC risks decline over time in non-hospitalized individuals (with death risk normalizing after year 1), substantial residual morbidity and mortality persist in the third year, particularly among those hospitalized during the acute phase — who carry 29% elevated death risk and 90.0 DALYs per 1,000 persons in year 3 alone. The study provides the longest prospective epidemiological characterization of PASC to date, demonstrating that post-infectious homeostatic failure is not fully resolved at 2 years and that acute illness severity is the dominant predictor of long-term persistence.

## Methods

**Study design:** Retrospective observational cohort using VA Corporate Data Warehouse electronic health records. Enrolled participants positive for SARS-CoV-2 between March and December 2020 (pre-vaccine era, ancestral variant), ensuring full 3-year follow-up for all participants.

**Cohorts:**
- Non-hospitalized COVID-19: n = 114,864 (88.0% male, mean age ~60)
- Hospitalized COVID-19: n = 20,297 (94.2% male, mean age ~65)
- Control (no COVID-19 evidence): n = 5,206,835

**Follow-up:** All participants followed for exactly 3 years; total person-years: 344,592 (non-hospitalized), 60,891 (hospitalized), 15,620,505 (controls).

**Outcomes:** 80 prespecified incident outcomes spanning 10 organ systems (cardiovascular, coagulation/hematologic, fatigue, gastrointestinal, kidney, mental health, metabolic, musculoskeletal, neurologic, pulmonary); death assessed separately. Outcomes evaluated year-by-year (years 1, 2, 3) and cumulatively at 3 years.

**Burden quantification:** Disability-adjusted life-years (DALYs) computed by applying Global Burden of Disease (GBD) health burden coefficients to cumulative incident outcome rates per 1,000 persons per year.

**Confounding control:** Inverse probability weighting (IPW) based on high-dimensional propensity scores including demographics, comorbidities, medications, laboratory values, and healthcare utilization. Ten sensitivity analyses conducted, including doubly robust models, zero-inflated Poisson models, Fine-Gray competing-risk models, and PASC defined using 73 outcomes instead of 80.

**Statistical models:** Poisson regression for incidence rate ratios (IRRs) and excess burdens; bootstrapped 95% CIs. Negative outcome control (incident neoplasm) confirmed absence of unmeasured confounding.

**Era note:** Enrollment restricted to 2020, predating vaccines and antivirals; results therefore reflect ancestral SARS-CoV-2 outcomes in an unvaccinated population. These do not generalize to vaccinated or antiviral-treated individuals.

## Key Findings

**Mortality:**
- Non-hospitalized: IRR 1.58 (95% CI 1.53–1.62) in year 1; 0.97 (0.94–1.00) in year 2; 1.01 (0.97–1.04) in year 3 — elevated death risk resolves after year 1.
- Hospitalized: IRR 3.17 (3.00–3.33) in year 1; 1.44 (1.34–1.55) in year 2; 1.29 (1.19–1.40) in year 3 — death risk remains significantly elevated in year 3 (excess burden: 8.16 per 1,000 persons).

**PASC incident sequelae — non-hospitalized:**
- Year 1: IRR 1.23 (1.22–1.25); 212.3 sequelae per 1,000 persons.
- Year 2: IRR 1.16 (1.14–1.18); 125.0 per 1,000 persons.
- Year 3: IRR 1.05 (1.03–1.08); 41.2 per 1,000 persons.
- 3-year cumulative: 378.7 (95% CI 356.6–401.1) per 1,000 persons (56.1% year 1, 33.0% year 2, 10.9% year 3).

**PASC incident sequelae — hospitalized:**
- Year 1: IRR 2.82 (2.76–2.89); 1,696.6 per 1,000 persons.
- Year 2: IRR 1.57 (1.49–1.66); 443.3 per 1,000 persons.
- Year 3: IRR 1.34 (1.24–1.45); 252.8 per 1,000 persons.
- 3-year cumulative: 2,391.7 (2,316.0–2,472.3) per 1,000 persons (70.9% year 1, 18.5% year 2, 10.6% year 3).

**PASC DALYs — non-hospitalized:**
- Year 1: 54.3; year 2: 27.3; year 3: 9.6 DALYs per 1,000 persons.
- 3-year cumulative: 91.2 (95% CI 81.6–101.0).

**PASC DALYs — hospitalized:**
- Year 1: 527.1; year 2: 149.6; year 3: 90.0 (95% CI 55.2–124.8) DALYs per 1,000 persons.
- 3-year cumulative: 766.2 (95% CI 731.7–803.3) — 8.4× higher than non-hospitalized.

**Top organ systems by 3-year cumulative sequelae (non-hospitalized):**
- Neurologic: 79.5 per 1,000; cardiovascular: 66.7; pulmonary: 56.9 (3-year cumulative).
- 3-year cumulative DALYs: neurologic 32.2, cardiovascular 16.9, pulmonary 10.5, coagulation/hematologic 9.7, mental 7.2.

**Top organ systems by 3-year cumulative sequelae (hospitalized):**
- Mental: 534.4; cardiovascular: 318.8; neurologic: 282.2; coagulation/hematologic: 274.5; kidney: 197.0 per 1,000.
- 3-year cumulative DALYs: cardiovascular 173.5, mental 156.3, neurologic 138.5, coagulation/hematologic 107.5, kidney 72.4.

**Year-3 organ systems still significantly elevated (hospitalized):**
Seven of 10 systems remained significant in year 3 (all except kidney, metabolic, and musculoskeletal). Notable year-3 contributions: neurologic 14.2, pulmonary 11.1, gastrointestinal 7.4 sequelae per 1,000 persons; cardiovascular 7.2, neurologic 1.5, pulmonary 0.8 DALYs per 1,000.

**Year-3 organ systems still elevated (non-hospitalized):**
Three systems remained significant in year 3: neurologic, pulmonary, and gastrointestinal — contributing 14.2, 11.1, and 7.4 sequelae per 1,000 persons respectively.

**Relative burden across 3 years:**
- Non-hospitalized: Year 1 59.6%, Year 2 29.9%, Year 3 10.5% of total DALYs.
- Hospitalized: Year 1 68.8%, Year 2 19.5%, Year 3 11.7% of total DALYs.

**Negative outcome control:** Incident neoplasm showed neutral associations (IRRs ~1.0 in both groups across all years), confirming the absence of major unmeasured confounding.

## Relevance

This paper is central to the post-acute-infection project's research question (research-question:post-acute-infection-syndromes): *Why do some people fail to recover after acute infection, and what shared mechanisms link the post-acute infection syndromes?*

1. **Quantifies the multi-year horizon of homeostatic failure.** Prior work characterized PASC risk up to 2 years; this study extends to 3 years and shows that the failure of homeostatic recovery after hospitalized COVID-19 is not resolved even at year 3 — 90.0 DALYs per 1,000 persons in year 3 alone, with death risk still 29% elevated. This is direct evidence that post-infectious dysregulation can constitute a stable, multi-year pathological attractor rather than a transient deviation.

2. **Severity of acute illness as the key predictor of PAIS persistence.** Non-hospitalized individuals largely recover by year 2 (death risk normalizes; PASC risk approaches null in most systems by year 3), whereas hospitalized individuals carry persistent excess risk across 7 organ systems in year 3. This supports the PAIS frame: the depth of the initial homeostatic disruption — quantified here by hospitalization — predicts which individuals fail to return to baseline and at what timescale.

3. **Multi-organ failure-mode topology.** The organ systems persisting in year 3 differ by severity: in non-hospitalized individuals, neurologic, pulmonary, and GI systems persist; in hospitalized individuals, cardiovascular, mental, neurologic, coagulation/hematologic, kidney, pulmonary, and GI all remain elevated. This suggests the failure mode is not uniform — in severe disease, the system fails to recover in domains reflecting endothelial/vascular injury, immune dysregulation, and end-organ damage, consistent with the project's hypotheses about thromboinflammation and immune exhaustion as drivers of PAIS.

4. **Temporal trajectory data for model-building.** The year-by-year decomposition of sequelae counts and DALYs provides empirical time series that constrain any dynamical model of post-infectious recovery: the risk trajectory is roughly exponential decay in non-hospitalized individuals (IRR: 1.23 → 1.16 → 1.05 over 3 years) but more persistent in hospitalized individuals (2.82 → 1.57 → 1.34), suggesting qualitatively different recovery dynamics.

5. **DALY magnitude anchors cross-PAIS burden comparisons.** The 91.2 DALYs/1,000 (non-hospitalized, 3 years) and 766.2 DALYs/1,000 (hospitalized, 3 years) establish a COVID-19 reference point against which the project can benchmark other PAIS conditions (ME/CFS, PTLDS, post-dengue) when data become available.

6. **Pre-vaccine era cohort isolates SARS-CoV-2 biology.** Because enrollment was restricted to 2020, the results are not confounded by vaccination or antiviral treatment, making this a cleaner test of the viral infection's intrinsic capacity to trigger persistent homeostatic failure.

## Limitations

1. **VA cohort generalisability:** Population is ~90% male, predominantly older (mean age ~60–65), mostly White, and US veterans. Findings may not generalize to women, younger adults, children, or non-Western populations. Sex differences in PASC biology are well-documented and likely not captured here.

2. **Pre-vaccine era only:** Enrollment in 2020 predates COVID-19 vaccines and antiviral treatments (Paxlovid). The 3-year outcomes do not reflect post-vaccination PASC trajectories and likely overestimate the current burden in vaccinated populations.

3. **Ancestral variant only:** All infections occurred during the ancestral SARS-CoV-2 era. Omicron-era PASC may have a different organ-system profile and shorter risk horizon, limiting extrapolation to current epidemiology.

4. **Residual confounding:** IPW controls observed covariates; unobserved genetic immune variation, socioeconomic factors, prior viral exposures, and microbiome differences cannot be fully balanced.

5. **Administrative outcome ascertainment:** Outcomes derived from ICD codes and lab values. Under-documentation, care-seeking variation, and billing practices introduce measurement error; less severe sequelae may be undercoded.

6. **No mechanistic resolution:** The study is epidemiological; it characterizes *what* persists and *for how long* but cannot identify the biological mechanisms (viral persistence, autoimmunity, immune exhaustion, mitochondrial dysfunction) driving persistence — the central question for the PAIS project.

7. **Control group contamination:** The control group (no VA COVID-19 test) may contain undiagnosed COVID-19 cases, particularly early in 2020 when testing was limited. This would attenuate estimated excess risks.

8. **PASC definition breadth:** The 80-outcome definition of PASC is comprehensive but broad. Alternative narrower definitions (73 outcomes) yielded consistent results in sensitivity analyses, but the operational case definition used here may not correspond to clinical or patient-defined long COVID.

9. **Competing risks not primary model:** The primary analysis used Poisson regression; Fine-Gray competing-risks models were used only as a sensitivity analysis. In a population with high mortality (hospitalized group: ~3× death risk in year 1), competing risks may meaningfully affect PASC incidence estimates if not the primary frame.

## Follow-up

**Papers to read/cross-reference:**
- Bowe B, Xie Y, Al-Aly Z. *Post-acute sequelae of COVID-19 at 2 years* (Nat Med 2023) — this paper's immediate predecessor; extends comparison to 24 months and benchmarks Cai2024's year-3 findings.
- Zhang H et al. *3-year outcomes of discharged survivors of COVID-19 following the SARS-CoV-2 omicron (B.1.1.529) wave in 2022 in China* (Respir Med 2024) — a parallel 3-year follow-up under a different variant; important comparator for ancestral-vs-omicron differences.
- Peluso MJ et al. *Multimodal molecular imaging reveals tissue-based T cell activation and viral RNA persistence for up to 2 years following COVID-19* (medRxiv 2023) — cited as mechanistic complement; viral persistence and T cell activation may explain why hospitalized individuals fail to recover.
- Taquet M et al. *Neurological and psychiatric risk trajectories after SARS-CoV-2 infection* (Lancet Psychiatry 2022) — neuropsychiatric trajectory data at 2 years corroborating persistent neurologic burden found here.

**Questions this raises for the project:**
1. What biological mechanisms explain why hospitalized COVID-19 patients continue to accrue new PASC in years 2–3 at IRR 1.34, whereas non-hospitalized patients approach baseline? Does this reflect ongoing antigen persistence, immune exhaustion, end-organ remodeling, or a distinct chronic-inflammation phenotype?
2. The 8.4× DALY ratio (hospitalized vs. non-hospitalized) is the most striking severity-stratified signal in the PAIS literature — does the same severity-stratified DALY gradient hold for other PAIS-defining infections (Lyme, Q fever, dengue, EBV) or is this a feature of SARS-CoV-2's systemic tropism?
3. Given that risk trajectories differ qualitatively between non-hospitalized (near-resolution by year 3) and hospitalized groups (persistent attractor), do these represent quantitatively different points on a continuum or genuinely distinct biological states with different therapeutic implications?
