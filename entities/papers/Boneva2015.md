# Early menopause and other gynecologic risk indicators for chronic fatigue syndrome in women

- **Authors:** Roumiana S. Boneva, Jin-Mann S. Lin, Elizabeth R. Unger
- **Year:** 2015
- **Journal:** Menopause, 22(8), 826-834
- **DOI:** 10.1097/GME.0000000000000411
- **PMID:** 25647777
- **PMCID:** PMC5745581
- **BibTeX key:** Boneva2015
- **Source:** PubMed/PMC bibliographic record and abstract; full-text interpretation restricted to abstract-visible methods/results for this pass.

## Key Contribution

Boneva et al. test whether gynecologic history differs between women with CFS and healthy controls in a population-based Georgia case-control sample. For this project, the load-bearing contribution is the strongest non-COVID reproductive-stage signal surfaced by `interpretation:0003`: CFS cases had substantially earlier menopause and more gynecologic disorders/surgeries than controls.

The directionality result is mixed rather than decisive. The early-menopause association is compatible with an antecedent reproductive/gynecologic vulnerability, but much of the signal is surgical menopause. In the dated-surgery subset, hysterectomy/oophorectomy occurred before CFS onset in 71%, which weakens a blanket "CFS caused the menopause timing" explanation for that surgical component.

## Methods

- **Design:** population-based case-control subset from Georgia, United States, conducted in 2004-2009.
- **Population:** 84 women with CFS and 73 healthy controls.
- **Ascertainment:** gynecologic history by self-administered questionnaire; odds ratios adjusted for body mass index and other covariates where relevant.
- **Directionality handle:** date of gynecologic surgery and date of CFS onset were available for a subset, allowing a limited temporal check for hysterectomy/oophorectomy.

## Key Findings

- Women with CFS reported more menopausal status than controls (61.9% vs 37.0%; OR 2.37, 95% CI 1.21-4.66).
- Mean age at menopause onset was earlier in CFS than controls (37.6 vs 48.6 years), with an adjusted OR of 1.22 per year earlier menopause (95% CI 1.09-1.36).
- CFS cases reported more excessive menstrual bleeding, intermenstrual bleeding, endometriosis, noncontraceptive hormone use, nonmenstrual pelvic pain, gynecologic surgery, and hysterectomy.
- Hysterectomy and oophorectomy occurred at younger ages in CFS cases; among women with both surgery date and CFS-onset date, surgery preceded CFS onset in 71%.

## Relevance

This paper weakly supports `proposition:0001` because earlier reproductive-stage transition, especially surgical menopause, is observed before CFS onset in part of the sample. It also bears on `proposition:0003`: the dated-surgery subset disputes a simple reverse-causation reading for the surgical component, but it does not rule out illness-driven reproductive-axis perturbation or earlier natural menopause after disease onset.

## Limitations

- CFS is not confirmed as post-infectious in this paper, so the link to PAIS is indirect.
- The design is case-control and retrospective; gynecologic history and CFS onset dates are vulnerable to recall and selection bias.
- The strongest temporal information concerns surgery, not natural menopause; gynecologic disorders leading to surgery may be confounders, mediators, or markers of shared vulnerability.
- The study does not provide pre-infection hormone/stage data, so it cannot adjudicate the main forward-vs-reverse causal question for `hypothesis:0005`.
