# High levels of eicosanoids and docosanoids in the lungs of intubated COVID-19 patients

- **Authors:** Anne-Sophie Archambault, Younes Zaid, Volatiana Rakotoarivelo, Caroline Turcotte, Étienne Doré, Isabelle Dubuc, Cyril Martin, Olivier Flamand, Youssef Amar, Amine Cheikh, Hakima Fares, Amine El Hassani, Youssef Tijani, Andréanne Côté, Michel Laviolette, Éric Boilard, Louis Flamand, Nicolas Flamand
- **Year:** 2021
- **Journal:** The FASEB Journal, 35(6), e21666
- **DOI/URL:** https://doi.org/10.1096/fj.202100540R
- **PMID:** 34033145 | **PMCID:** PMC8206770
- **BibTeX key:** Archambault2021
- **Source:** Full text via Europe PMC (XML, PMC8206770), read 2026-07-07.

> **Scope note for question:0035:** This paper characterises the lipid mediator profile of *acute* severe COVID-19 (intubated patients), not post-acute / long COVID. It is read here as the best available empirical anchor for the SPM-deficit PAIS endotype question because (a) it is the first targeted lipidomic measurement of SPMs in SARS-CoV-2-infected lung tissue, and (b) the finding that pro-inflammatory eicosanoids and SPMs co-exist without completing the class-switch is the closest published evidence for resolution failure in the COVID-19/PAIS pathological cascade. Whether the resolution failure documented here propagates into PAIS-sustaining chronicity is not addressed by this paper.

## Key Contribution

This study provides the first targeted lipidomic profiling of bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) fluid in mechanically ventilated COVID-19 patients, documenting a "lipid mediator storm" that parallels the cytokine storm: massive co-elevation of pro-inflammatory eicosanoids (thromboxane B2, prostaglandins, leukotrienes, HETEs) together with D-series resolvins, lipoxin A4, and PDX (protectin DX). Critically, the co-existence of both pro-inflammatory and pro-resolving mediator classes at the same time point is unexpected under the canonical Serhan class-switch model, in which SPMs succeed and replace the pro-inflammatory phase; the authors conclude that "resolution has not fully been engaged" in acute severe COVID-19. This constitutes the first direct evidence that the lipid mediator class-switch fails in severe SARS-CoV-2 lung disease, providing a biological substrate for the resolution-failure framing of COVID-19 severity and — by extension — a mechanistic precedent for the question:0035 resolution-deficit PAIS endotype hypothesis.

## Methods

- **Design:** Prospective observational study; BAL sampling at single time point (within 2 hours of intubation).
- **Cohort:** 33 mechanically ventilated COVID-19 patients (Cheikh Zaid Hospital, Rabat, Morocco; May–June 2020, pre-remdesivir, pre-dexamethasone); 25 healthy non-smoking controls (Québec City Heart & Lung Institute).
- **Groups:** COVID-19 patients: 58 ± 3 years, 16 F/17 M; healthy controls: 26 ± 1 years, 16 F/9 M — age mismatch is a stated limitation.
- **Lipid mediator panel:** Targeted LC-MS/MS quantitation of >50 analytes including: COX metabolites (TXB2, PGD2, PGE2, 12-HHTrE, PGI2), leukotrienes (LTB4, LTE4, eoxin E4), lipoxygenase metabolites (5-HETE, 12-HETE, 15-HETE, 15-KETE, HEPEs, HODEs), SPMs (RvD1–RvD6 resolvins, PDX, lipoxin A4, maresins 1 and 2), and precursor fatty acids (AA, EPA, DPA, DHA). Note: not all SPMs could be quantitated due to unavailability of appropriate deuterated internal standards.
- **Statistics:** Mann-Whitney non-parametric comparisons; Spearman correlation matrix for inter-lipid and lipid-clinical variable associations.

## Key Findings

**Pro-inflammatory eicosanoids (all markedly elevated vs healthy controls):**
- COX pathway: TXB2 >> PGE2 ~ 12-HHTrE > PGD2 (in order of fold-elevation)
- Leukotrienes: LTB4 and its omega-oxidation metabolites (20-OH-, 20-COOH-LTB4); LTE4; eoxin E4 (EXE4). LTB4 levels comparable to those in ARDS. The LTB4 omega-oxidation ratio (2.84 ± 0.53) suggests impaired LTB4-mediated host defense, consistent with patterns seen in cystic fibrosis and burns.
- 15-LO metabolites: 15-HETE, 15-KETE, 5-HETE, 12-HETE, 15-HEPE, 12-HEPE all elevated; 8-HETE, 11-HETE, 18-HEPE elevated as markers of oxidative burst
- LA-derived: 13-HODE, 13-KODE, 9-HODE (non-enzymatic oxidation marker), 13-HOTrE, 15-HETrE elevated

**Docosanoids and SPMs (elevated, co-existing with pro-inflammatory mediators):**
- Precursor fatty acids DPAn-3 and DHA elevated in COVID-19 BALs
- SPM precursors: 17-HDPA(n-3), 14-HDHA, 17-HDHA all significantly elevated
- SPMs elevated: D-series resolvins (RvD1, RvD2, RvD4, RvD5, RvD6; RvD3 not elevated); PDX; lipoxin A4 (LXA4)
- **Maresins 1 and 2: NOT detected** despite adequate assay sensitivity — absence notable
- E-series: RvE1 (from EPA) not detected
- LXA4, PDX, and RvD4 positively correlated with blood eosinophil counts

**Resolution class-switch status:**
- Pro-inflammatory eicosanoids AND SPMs co-exist simultaneously — unexpected under the class-switch model where SPMs should appear after and replace pro-inflammatory mediators
- Authors explicitly state: "prostaglandins, leukotrienes, and SPMs co-exist and participate in the inflammatory cascade during the acute phase of inflammation/infection in which resolution has not fully been engaged"
- Most lipid mediators correlate with each other regardless of class or biosynthetic pathway, suggesting a global upregulation rather than an orderly class-switch sequence

**Lipid–clinical correlations:**
- Very few significant correlations between BAL lipid levels and standard clinical biomarkers (CRP, D-dimers, LDH, lymphocyte count)
- BAL TXB2 levels did not correlate with plasma TXB2, suggesting the lung lipid environment is compartment-specific and not captured by blood sampling
- LXA4, PDX, RvD4 positively correlated with blood eosinophil counts (suggests eosinophils as a partial SPM source in the lung)

## Relevance

**Direct relevance to question:0035 (SPM-deficit PAIS endotype):**
The paper establishes, for the first time, that the SPM-driven resolution class-switch fails to complete during severe SARS-CoV-2 infection — the pathological context that is the leading cause of long COVID. The key empirical finding is NOT a simple SPM deficit (SPMs are elevated), but rather a co-presence pattern in which the body has mounted an SPM response yet it fails to resolve the pro-inflammatory storm. This "resolution competence failure" is mechanistically distinct from immunosuppression or anti-inflammatory treatment: SPM biosynthesis is engaged, the class-switch is not completing. This is precisely the resolution-biology distinction that question:0035 imports from wound-healing biology (failure to mount an effective SPM class-switch, not merely elevated inflammation). The paper does not address whether this failure mode persists, adapts, or changes character in patients who develop PAIS; that is the critical unstudied gap.

The paper explicitly raises resolution pharmacology (SPM precursors 18-HEPE, 17-HDHA as dietary supplements; aspirin; dexamethasone acting partly via SPM upregulation) as candidate therapeutic modifiers — directly anticipating the therapeutic prediction of question:0035.

**Relevance to question:0010 (vascular microclot subphenotype):**
The dominant pro-inflammatory lipid in the BAL is TXB2, a potent platelet activator and vasoconstrictor; its extreme elevation in COVID-19 lungs provides a mechanistic link between the lipid mediator storm, platelet activation, and the microclot/thromboinflammation phenotype in question:0010. TXA2 receptor activation drives bronchoconstriction, leukocyte activation, and thrombus formation — consistent with the vascular pathology of severe COVID-19 and its PAIS sequelae.

**Relevance to question:0006 (JAK-STAT/IL-6 driver):**
The COX-2 upregulation underlying the prostanoid storm is itself induced by IL-1β and other cytokines already elevated in severe COVID-19; thus the lipid mediator storm is downstream (at least in part) of the cytokine storm. This situates the question:0006 cytokine-driver question and question:0035 SPM-resolution question in a potential causal chain: cytokine storm → COX-2 upregulation → prostanoid/TXB2 storm → ongoing pro-inflammatory amplification → failure of SPM class-switch completion.

**Relevance to question:0022 (immune state displacement — mediator vs co-traveler):**
The finding that SPMs are elevated (not deficient) in acute severe COVID-19, yet fail to resolve inflammation, raises the question of whether elevated SPMs are genuine resolution mediators or are co-travelers of the general lipid biosynthesis upregulation. The authors' observation that most lipid classes correlate with each other (global upregulation) without expected class-switch ordering is consistent with mediator co-traveler dynamics.

**Relevance to hypothesis:0001 (shared dysregulated attractor):**
The lipid mediator storm documented here is a COVID-19-specific finding that may not generalise across PAIS triggers. Whether the same resolution class-switch failure occurs in EBV, Borrelia, or other PAIS triggers is unknown. If resolution-failure is trigger-specific rather than a shared mechanism, it would argue against the SPM-deficit endotype being part of the shared attractor mechanism — instead representing a COVID-specific feature.

## Limitations

**Study design:**
- Single acute time point: BALs obtained within 2 hours of intubation — captures the peak-severity acute phase, not the resolution trajectory. Resolution failure in PAIS occurs over weeks to months; this paper cannot address that timescale.
- Critically ill cohort only: all patients required mechanical ventilation. Whether the resolution class-switch failure is a severity-dependent phenomenon (only in ICU-grade disease) or a general feature of SARS-CoV-2 infection is unknown. Most PAIS cases arise from non-ICU acute illness.
- No longitudinal sampling: no follow-up lipidomics at recovery, post-acute, or chronic PAIS stages. The paper does not tell us whether patients who developed long COVID had distinct lipid profiles at intubation.
- No PAIS outcome data: post-discharge outcomes not reported. No linkage to long COVID or PAIS incidence.
- Age mismatch: healthy controls (26 years) are substantially younger than COVID-19 patients (58 years); age affects baseline lipid mediator profiles, notably PGD2 which is known to increase with aging and worsen coronavirus infections in aged mice.
- BAL protocol difference: healthy controls received one 50 mL bolus; COVID-19 patients received two 50 mL boli — authors note this may slightly underestimate COVID-19 lipid levels but the direction is conservative.
- Incomplete SPM panel: not all SPMs could be quantitated due to unavailability of deuterated internal standards. The presented SPM panel is a fraction of the full SPM family.

**Conceptual limitations for question:0035:**
- This paper shows SPM *elevation* (not deficit) in severe COVID-19 — the "failure" is not insufficient SPM production but incomplete class-switch against an overwhelming pro-inflammatory background.
- The PAIS-specific lipid mediator profile (weeks to months post-infection in patients who develop long COVID vs those who recover) is entirely unstudied in this paper and in the field as of the search date.
- No SPM-specific therapeutic intervention tested here.

Not applicable. No computational model or tool released. LC-MS/MS targeted lipidomics using Shimadzu 8050 triple quadrupole; Strata-X solid phase extraction. Supplementary materials include full lipid transition table (Table S2) and stability validation (Figure S1).

## Follow-up

**Critical gap to fill for question:0035:** A lipidomic study in long COVID patients specifically, with a panel including SPMs, comparing: (a) non-COVID healthy controls, (b) COVID-19-recovered-without-PAIS, and (c) long COVID patients at ≥3 months post-infection. This is the experiment that Archambault 2021 does not do and that would directly test the SPM-deficit PAIS endotype hypothesis. No such published study was identified in the 2026-07-07 literature search.

**Mechanistic follow-up needed:**
- Maresin absence: were maresins (MaR1, MaR2) genuinely absent or below assay threshold? Maresins have potent anti-inflammatory effects and their absence could be meaningful. Needs replication with improved sensitivity.
- Temporal dynamics: are SPMs sustained, declining, or absent in patients who go on to develop long COVID vs those who recover? Longitudinal BAL or blood lipidomic profiling of COVID-19 patients with PAIS outcome tracking would answer this.
- Cross-trigger comparison: do other PAIS triggers (EBV, Borrelia, influenza) show the same class-switch failure pattern? If so, this is a shared mechanism candidate.
