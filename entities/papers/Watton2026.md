## Key Contribution

This review by Watton (independent researcher) and Prusty (University of Würzburg, herpesvirus biology) argues that ME/CFS should be reframed as "a disorder of impaired adaptive capacity within post-infectious disease biology." It tracks the historical arc from early epidemic observations through the rise and subsequent guideline reversal of CBT/GET-based psychosocial frameworks, then assembles twelve lettered mechanistic advances — mitochondria as CDR hubs, abortive herpesvirus reactivation, fibronectin-IgG immune complexes, endothelial state-dependent failure, autophagy/mitophagy disruption, lipid raft dysregulation, haptoglobin proteoform variation, extracellular vesicle signalling, monocyte/T-cell reprogramming, TRPM3 calcium-channel dysfunction, and challenge-revealed pathology — to propose a convergent systems-level interpretation. A central organizing concept is "state-dependent dysfunction": key ME/CFS abnormalities are not constitutive but emerge under physiological stress, which explains why resting investigations are unrevealing and why exercise-challenge paradigms are necessary.

## Methods

This is a **structured narrative synthesis**, not a systematic review. The authors searched PubMed from 1 January 2000 to 30 April 2026, giving priority to primary research from the past six years. Search terms combined: "ME/CFS," "Chronic Fatigue Syndrome," "Long-COVID," "PASC," "immune dysregulation," "mitochondrial dysfunction," "endothelial dysfunction," "metabolomics," "extracellular vesicles," and "Therapeutic." Preference was given to human cohort studies, mechanistic investigations, multi-omic analyses, and studies using physiological challenge paradigms; earlier foundational work was included for context. The review acknowledges heterogeneity of case definitions and cohort dependency. There is no PRISMA flow diagram, no pre-specified inclusion/exclusion criteria beyond the above, and no formal risk-of-bias assessment. Evidence synthesis is narrative and reflects the authors' expert reading of a large heterogeneous literature.

## Key Findings

**Historical narrative — rise and fall of the psychosocial model**

The review documents how the absence of biomarkers in the 1980s–2010s created a vacuum that biopsychosocial frameworks filled, culminating in the PACE trial (2011) and 2007 NICE guidance endorsing CBT and GET. Sustained patient-driven and researcher-driven methodological critique (Freedom of Information tribunal rulings, outcome-switching analyses, peer-reviewed reassessments) ultimately produced the 2021 NICE guideline reversal, which removed GET as a recommended treatment and reframed CBT as supportive rather than curative. The paper uses this history as a case study: the absence of biomedical answers should not be resolved by prematurely adopting convenient explanatory models.

**Twelve mechanistic advances (Sections A–L), all primarily ME/CFS-focused:**

- **(A) Physiological challenge:** Two-day CPET demonstrates reproducible objective impairments in recovery (reduced VO₂, workload, anaerobic threshold on day 2), establishing PEM as state-dependent physiological failure rather than deconditioning. Deep phenotyping cohorts (citing Walitt et al. NIH intramural) integrate multi-modal data within the same patients.

- **(B) Mitochondria as CDR hubs:** Robert Naviaux's "Cell Danger Response" (CDR) reframes mitochondria from passive bioenergetic organelles to active signalling hubs that reprogram metabolism toward defence/repair when danger persists. Naviaux's metabolomic studies in ME/CFS provided the first systems-level evidence of a hypometabolic state across multiple pathways. The review presents "low energy" as an actively maintained cellular condition, not simple ATP shortage.

- **(C) Viral proteins and abortive lytic replication:** Prusty's group's model proposes that herpesvirus dUTPases produced during abortive lytic reactivation (without productive infection or detectable viraemia) can activate innate immune pathways and drive chronic immune stimulation. This resolves the inconsistency between epidemiological herpesvirus links and failure to demonstrate active replication. The authors extend this conceptually to other latent/persistent viruses.

- **(D) Fibronectin-immunoglobulin complexes:** One study (reference [91] in the paper) finds directionally discordant fibronectin biology in ME/CFS: elevated circulating fibronectin in serum but depleted fibronectin within IgG-bound circulating immune complexes (CICs). The authors propose this reflects impaired immune clearance, with elevated circulating fibronectin activating TLR4 and triggering cytokine responses, platelet activation, and mast cell signalling relevant to vascular tone and endothelial stress.

- **(E) Endothelial dysfunction — state-dependent:** Endothelial cells exposed to ME/CFS plasma appear normal under resting conditions but fail to mount adequate metabolic responses under immune, oxidative, or mechanical challenge. This parallels endotheliopathy in Long COVID. Convergence is argued by analogy across the two disease literatures.

- **(F) Autophagy/mitophagy:** Elevated ATG13 (an autophagy initiator) in ME/CFS serum across independent cohorts (Gottschalk et al.) triggers inflammatory signalling in vitro. Impaired mitophagy provides a plausible pathway from infection-triggered immune activation to sustained redox stress and reduced bioenergetic capacity.

- **(G) Lipid raft / SMPDL3B:** Altered SMPDL3B (sphingomyelin phosphodiesterase acid-like 3B) expression modifies lipid raft composition and immune receptor clustering in ME/CFS. This links systems-level lipid abnormalities to immune cell phenotypes.

- **(H) Haptoglobin proteoform variation:** Moreau's group finds that reduced functional haptoglobin quantity and altered proteoform structure permit exaggerated oxidative and vascular stress during exertion, providing a direct biochemical substrate for PEM amplification.

- **(I) Extracellular vesicles (EVs):** EV cargo profiling in ME/CFS reveals altered cytokine signatures, severity-associated immune cell markers, exercise-induced EV changes aligned with PEM, and mitochondrial DNA capable of activating innate immune pathways — a mechanism for propagating local cellular dysfunction systemically.

- **(J) Monocyte/T-cell reprogramming:** Stable transcriptional and metabolic reprogramming of monocytes and T cells in both ME/CFS and Long COVID, with Long COVID monocytes showing altered interferon signalling and inflammatory gene expression that persists after viral clearance.

- **(K) TRPM3 / autoimmunity / calcium:** Functional autoantibodies against β-adrenergic and muscarinic receptors perturb vascular tone and autonomic regulation. TRPM3 calcium-permeable channel dysfunction in NK cells provides a molecular link between altered calcium signalling and impaired immune stress responses. Mast cell activation and exertion-linked tissue calcium accumulation further reinforce this axis.

- **(L) Dynamic challenge paradigms:** Whole-body hyperthermia (Bergemann study, small N) reveals that elevated autophagy markers and reduced mitochondrial respiratory capacity in ME/CFS patients are dynamically modifiable, suggesting downstream rather than primary mitochondrial failure. scSLAM-seq proposed as a future method to capture nascent, stimulus-responsive transcriptional dynamics.

**Cross-cutting theme:** Long COVID research is framed as providing "statistical power, temporal anchoring, and institutional legitimacy" for pre-existing ME/CFS mechanistic hypotheses — not conceptual novelty. The review explicitly argues that Long COVID has confirmed, not originated, the mechanistic domains relevant to ME/CFS.

## Relevance

### Epistemic verdict: assembled narrative synthesis, not head-to-head data

This is the critical assessment the project requires. Watton and Prusty present a unified mechanistic model, but **its evidential basis is almost entirely assembled from separate single-disease studies — not from head-to-head designs comparing ME/CFS and other PAIS within a single study with the same platform and case definitions.**

Specific analysis:

1. **Primary scope is ME/CFS and Long COVID only.** The review does not substantively cover post-Lyme/PTLDS, post-dengue, post-Q-fever, post-Ebola, or post-SARS. The claim to a "unified model of chronic post-infectious diseases" rests on two conditions (ME/CFS, PASC) that share a substantial prior literature. Watton2026 cannot be cited as cross-trigger evidence beyond ME/CFS and Long COVID.

2. **Convergence is argued by pattern-matching.** The twelve mechanistic sections cite ME/CFS findings, then note that analogous findings have been reported in Long COVID, and conclude convergence. No section presents a study that enrolled patients with multiple different post-infectious triggers on the same platform. The fibronectin section (D) cites "increased circulating Fibronectin was also observed in ME/CFS patients in an independent cohort" but this is two ME/CFS cohorts, not ME/CFS vs another PAIS trigger.

3. **Most specific mechanistic claims are backed by ME/CFS-specific primary evidence:**
   - CDR/Naviaux: ME/CFS metabolomics (Naviaux et al.)
   - TRPM3: ME/CFS NK cells (Cabanas et al., Eaton-Fitch et al.)
   - ATG13/autophagy: ME/CFS (Gottschalk et al.)
   - Haptoglobin proteoforms: ME/CFS (Moreau group)
   - SMPDL3B: ME/CFS-specific work
   - Fibronectin-IgG complexes: one study in ME/CFS and PASC (reference [91])

4. **The authors acknowledge the gaps.** The "Concluding remarks" section explicitly names "key uncertainties," including "the upstream biological events that potentially stabilise persistent immune-metabolic dysregulation," "the contribution of viral persistence and immune-complex biology," and "why only a subset of individuals exposed to an acute infectious trigger develop persistent illness." These are not minor caveats — they are the fundamental questions hypothesis:0001 is trying to answer.

**Verdict:** Watton2026 should NOT be counted as independent supporting evidence for hypothesis:0001's claim that a shared dysregulated attractor spans post-infectious triggers broadly. It is an assembled narrative synthesis of primarily ME/CFS data, with Long COVID adduced as convergent evidence. This is the same epistemic move that `search:0002` already correctly categorises as "CLAIMS of convergence (review/synthesis level, not head-to-head data)." The paper is valuable as a mechanistic reference catalog and as a historical narrative, but it cannot close the head-to-head gap.

### D-002 check: PEM, pacing, and GET

The paper takes an **unambiguous and strong position against GET and in favor of pacing/energy-management,** fully aligned with project constraint D-002. Specifically:

- GET and CBT are described as having been developed in a "context of limited biological resolution" and the 2021 NICE guideline reversal that removed GET is presented as a vindication of ongoing biological research over operational convenience.
- PEM is reframed as "state-dependent physiological failure rather than deconditioning or effort-related variability" — the exact framing D-002 mandates.
- The "Translation to clinical practice" section calls for "individualised approaches that take into account functional limitation and post-exertional symptom exacerbation," not fixed-increment exercise programs.
- The 2-day CPET is endorsed as a diagnostic and investigative tool (in line with D-002's provoked-exertion endorsement under PEM-crash-risk consent) while no recommendation for graded exercise as treatment is made.

An influential JTM review that explicitly endorses GET would have been a tension with D-002. No such tension exists here.

### Comparison with Komaroff2023/2025 and Trautmann2025

Watton2026 overlaps substantially with its peers:
- **Overlapping with all three:** shared symptom domains (PEM, fatigue, brain fog, dysautonomia), broad mechanism families (immune dysregulation, mitochondrial dysfunction, endothelial dysfunction, gut dysbiosis), cross-trigger reasoning by analogy, vicious-cycle/attractor framing.
- **What Watton2026 adds distinctly:**
  - Historical narrative of psychosocial model rise and fall is the most detailed of the four reviews, with specific attention to PACE trial critique and NICE guideline reversal.
  - Mechanistic depth at specific molecular nodes not covered by Komaroff or Trautmann: TRPM3 ion channel (NK cells), fibronectin-IgG immune complex biology, SMPDL3B/lipid raft regulation, ATG13/autophagy, haptoglobin proteoforms, EVs as mechanistic messengers.
  - Prusty's abortive replication model (dUTPase-mediated immune activation without viraemia) is his group's specific contribution, not present in the other three reviews.
  - Explicit "state-dependent pathology" framing as the organizing principle — a sharper clinical inference point than Trautmann's interoceptive-loop framing.
- **What it lacks vs peers:** Trautmann2025 covers post-Lyme/PTLDS more substantively and proposes a composite subtyping strategy. Komaroff2023 provides detailed parallel tables across seven biological domains. Watton2026 is the most ME/CFS-centric of the group.

## Limitations

1. **Narrative synthesis, no systematic search protocol.** No PRISMA flow diagram, no pre-specified inclusion/exclusion criteria, no formal quality weighting of cited evidence. Selection reflects expert judgment, with the usual risks of confirmation bias.

2. **Primary scope is ME/CFS and Long COVID — not multi-trigger PAIS.** Despite the title's reference to "chronic post-infectious diseases," the evidence base covers two conditions. Generalization to PTLDS, post-dengue, QFS, and other PAIS relies on analogy and brief assertion, not evidence.

3. **Convergence between ME/CFS and Long COVID is argued by analogy, not head-to-head measurement.** The review does not present any single study that compared ME/CFS and Long COVID patients on the same platform with the same case definitions. Cross-condition claims derive from comparing separate study results.

4. **Several key mechanistic claims rest on small or preliminary studies.** The fibronectin-IgG immune complex finding (Section D) is attributed to a single study (reference [91]). The ATG13 finding (Section F) is a Gottschalk et al. study that has not been replicated at scale. The whole-body hyperthermia challenge (Section L) is described as "small but mechanistically informative." Effect sizes and reproducibility are generally not reported.

5. **Authorship and AI assistance.** The paper is by two authors — one with a clear research background in herpesviruses/mitochondria (Prusty), one not listed with academic affiliation (Watton). The paper declares use of ChatGPT for brainstorming and language refinement. This does not invalidate the synthesis but warrants awareness for critical reading.

6. **No quantitative synthesis.** The review does not attempt pooled effect estimates, forest plots, or formal heterogeneity assessment. Evidence quality varies widely across the cited studies; this is not systematically reported.

7. **Case definitions largely unstated.** The review discusses ME/CFS and Long COVID without systematically flagging which case definitions apply to cited studies (Fukuda, CCC, ICC, NAM 2015, or WHO for Long COVID). Apparent converging findings across studies may partly reflect definitional heterogeneity.

8. **Supplementary therapeutic table (Table S1) not evaluated.** The paper includes a supplementary table of candidate interventions for which clinical or mechanistic evidence has been reported; this reviewer did not access the supplementary materials.

## Follow-up

- The fibronectin-IgG immune complex finding (Section D, reference [91] — likely Solis-Moré or a 2025/2026 CIC study) merits a dedicated paper summary if it has not already been read. It is the most novel primary finding cited and provides a specific mechanistic bridge between immune clearance failure and endothelial/vascular dysfunction.
- Prusty's dUTPase/abortive-replication model (referenced as [85–90]) is his group's primary mechanistic contribution; if the key primary papers have not been summarized, they are higher priority than this review.
- The Bergemann whole-body hyperthermia study (Section L, reference [144]) is a rare ME/CFS challenge-paradigm intervention study; track for experimental design replication potential.
- The "state-dependent pathology" framing is the strongest conceptual contribution here and should be incorporated into topic:pais-case-definition-heterogeneity or a new `topic:state-dependent-pathology` if that concept is not yet captured.
- This review does not change the standing in `discussion:0002` (convergent domains, unproven shared molecules) — the decisive harmonized ≥3-trigger molecular test remains absent, and Watton2026 does not provide it.
