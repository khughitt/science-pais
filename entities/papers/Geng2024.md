# Nirmatrelvir-Ritonavir and Symptoms in Adults With Postacute Sequelae of SARS-CoV-2 Infection: The STOP-PASC Randomized Clinical Trial

- **Authors:** Linda N. Geng, Hector Bonilla, Haley Hedlin, Karen B. Jacobson, Lu Tian, Prasanna Jagannathan, Phillip C. Yang, Aruna K. Subramanian, … Upinder Singh (33 authors total; Stanford University, Pfizer Inc)
- **Year:** 2024
- **Journal:** JAMA Internal Medicine, vol. 184, no. 9, pp. 1024–1034
- **DOI:** 10.1001/jamainternmed.2024.2007
- **PMID:** 38848477
- **PMC:** PMC11161857
- **BibTeX key:** Geng2024
- **Tier:** 1 (phase-2 RCT, flagship journal, directly tests antigen-persistence treatment hypothesis)
- **Source:** Europe PMC full-text XML (open access; CC-BY-NC-ND)

## Key Contribution

STOP-PASC is the first published placebo-controlled RCT of nirmatrelvir-ritonavir (NMV/r, Paxlovid) specifically for established PASC (long COVID). Testing the hypothesis that residual SARS-CoV-2 antigen/viral material drives ongoing symptoms, the trial delivered a 15-day course of NMV/r to participants with moderate-to-severe PASC of ≥3 months' duration. The primary composite outcome — pooled Likert-scale severity of six core symptom clusters at 10 weeks — showed **no statistically significant benefit** of NMV/r over placebo-ritonavir. The trial was stopped early for futility (conditional power <10% at pre-specified interim). This null result anchors the therapeutic-antiviral arm of PASC research and foregrounds critical design challenges: heterogeneous PASC phenotypes, lack of validated biomarkers for patient selection, reliance on subjective PROs as primary endpoints, and the difficulty of detecting drug signal against a background of spontaneous symptom fluctuation.

## Key Findings

### Primary outcome — null

There was **no statistically significant difference** in pooled 6-symptom severity at 10 weeks between the NMV/r group (n=102) and the PBO/r group (n=53). Both groups showed improvement in symptom severity over time, confirming spontaneous partial recovery occurs over this time horizon. The trial was halted for futility before reaching the planned N=200.

### PROMIS secondary outcomes — null across all domains

| Domain | NMV/r change (SD) | PBO/r change (SD) | β (95% CI) | P |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Physical function | +2.73 (6.62) | +1.32 (5.75) | 0.57 (−1.96 to 3.10) | .66 |
| Fatigue | −3.92 (7.88) | −4.05 (5.90) | 0.38 (−2.40 to 3.15) | .79 |
| Dyspnea | −1.96 (7.90) | −2.38 (6.13) | 0.60 (−2.55 to 3.75) | .70 |
| Cognitive function | +4.84 (8.18) | +5.05 (7.56) | 0.03 (−3.21 to 3.28) | .98 |

(Note: for fatigue and dyspnea, negative change = improvement; for physical and cognitive function, positive change = improvement.)

### Global impressions — null

- PGIC at 10 weeks: NMV/r 3.38 vs PBO/r 3.13; β 0.10 (95% CI −0.48 to 0.67); P=.74
- PGIS at 10 weeks: NMV/r 4.00 vs PBO/r 3.79; β 0.19 (95% CI −0.25 to 0.62); P=.40
- Summative symptom score at 10 weeks: NMV/r 7.62 vs PBO/r 7.69; β −0.24 (95% CI −1.46 to 0.97); P=.69

### Proportion of weeks with mild/no symptoms — signal for fatigue and brain fog favoring placebo

Notably, the NMV/r group had a **lower** (worse) proportion of weeks with mild-or-no symptoms for fatigue (OR 0.55, 95% CI 0.33–0.92; P=.02) and brain fog (OR 0.50, 95% CI 0.31–0.82; P=.01) over the full 15-week period compared to PBO/r. The authors note these secondary/exploratory results were not adjusted for multiple comparisons and should be interpreted cautiously; a possible explanation is unintended unmasking via dysgeusia (see Limitations).

### Symptom relief and alleviation at 10 weeks

- Experiencing relief: NMV/r 32.4% vs PBO/r 41.5%; OR 0.55 (95% CI 0.27–1.09); P=.09
- Experiencing alleviation: NMV/r 6.9% vs PBO/r 9.4%; OR 0.72 (95% CI 0.21–2.44); P=.60

### Antigen persistence biomarker

No participant baseline stool specimens had detectable SARS-CoV-2 RNA by RT-PCR. Other tissue compartments were not assessed. This finding weakens the inference that active viral replication was present in the treated population, though it does not rule out RNA/protein persistence in tissue reservoirs.

### Safety

15-day NMV/r was generally well-tolerated:
- Dysgeusia: 61.8% NMV/r vs 7.5% PBO/r (likely responsible for partial unmasking risk)
- Diarrhea: 43.1% NMV/r vs 35.8% PBO/r
- Discontinuation due to AE/intolerability: 2.9% NMV/r vs 5.7% PBO/r
- Grade 3–4 AEs: 4.9% NMV/r vs 5.7% PBO/r
- Serious AEs: 3 NMV/r vs 1 PBO/r; no fatalities
- COVID-19 reinfection during study: 11.8% NMV/r vs 9.4% PBO/r

## Relevance

This paper is directly relevant to the **therapeutics track (t004)** of this project and to three intersecting PAIS mechanistic themes:

1. **Antigen/pathogen persistence hypothesis**: STOP-PASC is the most rigorous test to date of the idea that residual SARS-CoV-2 drives PASC and can be cleared by antivirals. The null result does not falsify antigen persistence as a mechanism — viral RNA/protein may persist without active replication, and nirmatrelvir targets protease-dependent replication — but it strongly constrains the therapeutic implication of that hypothesis for protracted vaccinated PASC (median 17.5 months post-infection).

2. **Objective-endpoint problem** (topic:biomarkers-and-objective-endpoints): The trial relied entirely on subjective PROs (Likert, PROMIS, PGIS, PGIC) as its primary and key secondary outcomes. The authors explicitly acknowledge that "there are no validated clinical end points or biomarkers of PASC established for clinical trials." This creates interpretive ambiguity: a null PRO result could reflect true lack of efficacy, symptom fluctuation masking signal, wrong patient selection (no viral reservoir confirmed), or the wrong treatment duration/timing. Future trials are argued to need objective biomarkers for patient enrichment and endpoint validation.

3. **Shared-dysregulated-attractor hypothesis** (hypothesis:0001): Spontaneous improvement in both arms over 15 weeks is consistent with a system that is slowly recovering toward a new quasi-stable attractor state; NMV/r did not meaningfully accelerate that trajectory. This is also consistent with a scenario where antigen clearance is not the rate-limiting step once chronic downstream immune dysregulation is established.

## Limitations

1. **Single academic center (Stanford)**: limits generalizability; recruitment skewed educated, mostly White, vaccinated outpatient population — differs substantially from unvaccinated or hospitalized cohorts.
2. **Protracted PASC, mostly vaccinated**: mean 17.5 months since infection; antivirals may need to be administered earlier in illness course, before downstream effects become entrenched or "irreversible."
3. **No biomarker for active viral persistence**: all stool RT-PCR negative; no tissue reservoir data. The treated population may not have had the antigen burden that the treatment hypothesis requires.
4. **Unintended unmasking via dysgeusia**: ritonavir was included in the placebo arm precisely to minimize dysgeusia-based unmasking, yet 61.8% of NMV/r vs 7.5% of PBO/r developed dysgeusia — potentially biasing self-reported outcomes.
5. **Heterogeneous PASC phenotype**: pooling 6 symptom clusters as a composite may mask subgroup responses; the trial was not powered to detect differential effects in PASC subtypes.
6. **Underpowered relative to planned N**: stopped at 155/200 for futility; may have missed modest effects or specific subgroup signals.
7. **Drug-drug interaction exclusions**: high exclusion rate limits generalizability and potentially excludes subgroups who may be responders.
8. **Spontaneous recovery in placebo arm**: substantial within-group improvement over time in both arms; trial requires treatment to accelerate this trajectory, which is a high bar.
9. **Secondary outcome multiplicity**: secondary/exploratory P-values not corrected for multiple comparisons; paradoxical signals (worse fatigue/brain fog weeks in NMV/r arm) require cautious interpretation.
10. **Industry funding and co-design**: Pfizer funded the trial, collaborated on design and execution, and reviewed the manuscript (disclosures are transparent, but should be noted).
11. **Post-hoc variant-era subgroup**: pre-Omicron vs Omicron-era subgroup analysis was post-hoc, not pre-specified.

## Model / Tool Availability

None. Ancillary analyses (molecular biomarkers, digital wearable data) from STOP-PASC biospecimens were forthcoming at time of publication but are not described in this paper.

## Follow-up

- Ancillary STOP-PASC biomarker/wearable analyses: watch for companion publications that may reveal immune or molecular correlates of response despite PRO null result.
- Larger/longer RCTs of NMV/r for PASC with earlier enrollment (e.g., <6 months post-infection), better patient enrichment via viral-reservoir assays, and objective endpoints (e.g., exercise physiology, autonomic function, inflammatory biomarkers).
- Concurrent RECOVER-VITAL trial (NIH; NCT05668091) and other nirmatrelvir-for-long-COVID trials will provide additional evidence; results should be synthesized against STOP-PASC.
- Patient selection via validated biomarkers of antigen persistence (plasma SARS-CoV-2 antigens, RNA from tissue biopsies) could identify subpopulations most likely to respond to antiviral approaches.
- The signal of lower proportion of mild/no-symptom weeks for fatigue and brain fog in the NMV/r arm (OR ~0.50–0.55) warrants scrutiny: consider whether unmasking bias, dysgeusia-related distress, or ritonavir-related drug interactions could explain this unexpected direction.
