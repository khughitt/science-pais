# Post-COVID pulmonary sequelae: Mechanisms and potential targets to reduce persistent fibrosis

- **Authors:** Emma C. A. Vreeman, Janesh Pillay, Janette K. Burgess
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal:** Pharmacology & Therapeutics, vol. 272, article 108891
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.pharmthera.2025.108891
- **BibTeX key:** Vreeman2025
- **Source:** PDF

## Key Contribution

This comprehensive narrative review synthesizes current understanding of post-COVID-19 pulmonary fibrosis (PCPF) — a fibrotic sequela affecting an estimated 9–45% of hospitalised COVID-19 survivors — covering its prevalence and reversibility, the multifactorial pathophysiology (aberrant ECM repair, ACE2/angiotensin axis disruption, integrin-mediated TGF-β activation, epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition), candidate biomarkers, and pharmacological treatment strategies. By drawing on data from the prior SARS-CoV epidemic (2003), the authors argue that PCPF is likely to be persistent but non-progressive in the majority of affected patients, and they map the lung as a primary organ-level locus of failed homeostatic recovery in long COVID/PASC. The review uniquely positions pharmacological reversal of fibrosis — not merely slowing progression — as the central unmet need, and identifies mesenchymal stem cell therapy and HDAC inhibitors as the most promising candidates for achieving this goal beyond the currently approved pirfenidone and nintedanib.

## Methods

This is a narrative review. The authors searched the literature on pulmonary fibrosis following COVID-19, with comparators drawn from idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) and prior coronavirus-associated lung disease (SARS-CoV 2003, MERS-CoV). Cohort studies were compiled into a summary table (Table 1) covering seven prospective and retrospective cohorts (n ranging from 15 to 8,858 patients) at follow-up intervals from 3 months to 2 years (plus a 15-year SARS follow-up study). No formal meta-analytic pooling or risk-of-bias assessment was performed; inclusion criteria for reviewed studies are not formally stated. Coverage spans epidemiology, pathophysiology (mechanistic cell-biology and animal studies), biomarker discovery, and pharmacological interventions (in vitro, preclinical, and clinical trial data).

## Key Findings

**Prevalence and trajectory of post-COVID-19 pulmonary fibrosis**
- At 3–6 months post-hospital discharge, HRCT abnormalities (ground-glass opacities, parenchymal bands, traction bronchiectasis, honeycombing) are present in 44.9–66% of moderate-to-severe COVID-19 patients (Table 1); impaired DLCO (< 80%) affects 47–87% of these cohorts.
- At 1–2 years, most GGO and consolidation decrease but fibrotic lesions (honeycombing, bands) persist or increase in severity in the subset with initially severe disease (Babar et al. 2024 meta-analysis, n = 8,858).
- The long-term SARS-CoV benchmark (Li et al. 2021, n = 58, 15-year follow-up): ~1/3 of recovered patients still had decreased lung function; 12.7% had persistent pulmonary fibrosis on HRCT; 36.5% showed impaired alveolar diffusion (DLCO < 80%) and 30.4% had moderate-to-severe dyspnoea at 15 years. Serial CT scans showed abnormalities remained largely unchanged after year 1, consistent with persistent but non-progressive fibrosis.
- Population-level extrapolation: assuming the SARS-CoV 15-year persistence rate (~12.7%) represents the long-term PCPF floor, and given the SARS-CoV-2 infection scale (777 million cases as of January 2025, WHO), even a low prevalence of PCPF represents a substantial global burden. The authors estimate PCPF long-term prevalence among PASC patients at approximately 9.2% using this methodology.
- Vaccination: one vaccination dose before SARS-CoV-2 was associated with lower PASC incidence and milder symptoms; vaccination post-infection showed no difference in PASC symptoms over 3-month follow-up (Wynberg et al. 2022). One study reported reduced incidence of newly diagnosed ILD post-COVID in vaccinated vs. unvaccinated controls (Kim et al. 2024).

**Pathophysiology (four main mechanisms)**

1. *Aberrant tissue repair and excess ECM deposition:* Plasma proteomics in ARDS survivors identified 35 proteins declining with DLCO impairment, involved in cell proliferation, ECM remodeling, angiogenesis, and immune responses (MET, VEGF, WNTS, TNFR2, NF-κB pathways; García-Hidalgo et al. 2022). SARS-CoV-2 infection induces hyaluronan production (linked to COVID-19 morbidity and lung impairment; Hellman et al. 2024). Collagen turnover is higher in critically ill COVID-19 patients and is associated with increased mortality (Pillay et al. 2025). Excessive ECM drives a feed-forward loop of persistent inflammation and fibrosis (Fig. 1).

2. *ACE2 downregulation and angiotensin II accumulation:* SARS-CoV-2 binds and internalises/sheds ACE2 on type II alveolar epithelial cells, reducing its availability. This abolishes ACE2-mediated conversion of angiotensin II to the anti-fibrotic Ang-(1–7). Accumulated angiotensin II signals through AT1R to produce oxidative stress, pro-inflammatory cytokines, and apoptosis, and — by losing Ang-(1–7) suppression — unleashes TGF-β, promoting fibroblast differentiation and activation. Auto-antibodies against ACE2 found in recovered COVID-19 patients further suppress ACE2 activity (Arthur et al. 2021), amplifying the profibrotic environment (Fig. 2).

3. *αvβ6 integrin-mediated TGF-β activation:* αvβ6 integrins (restricted to alveolar epithelial cells) bind the latency-associated peptide (LAP) of TGF-β, inducing conformational change and releasing active TGF-β. αvβ6 overexpression drives pulmonary fibrosis in IPF. Elevated αvβ6 integrin levels were found on PET imaging in an IPF and a PASC patient vs. healthy controls (Kimura et al. 2023). Critically, the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein functions as an integrin agonist at αvβ6 via an RGD motif (Calver et al. 2021; Norris et al. 2023), suggesting a direct virus→integrin→TGF-β profibrotic pathway.

4. *Epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT):* SARS-CoV-2 upregulates EMT-associated signaling (TGF-β, growth factors) in alveolar epithelial cells (A549 cells; Lai et al. 2021). Proteins of the ECM (fibronectin) induce αvβ6-dependent latent TGF-β activation, linking EMT to aberrant ECM deposition. EMT genes are upregulated post-SARS-CoV-2, generating a profibrotic lung microenvironment (L. Zhang et al. 2022). Endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EndoMT) is additionally indicated (Falleni et al. 2021).

**Biomarkers**
- PRO-C3 (type III collagen formation) and PC3X (type III collagen crosslinking) were elevated in PCPF patients vs. COVID-19 patients who did not develop ILD at 3 months post-discharge (Breisnes et al. 2024); PRO-C3 elevation is a known IPF progression and mortality marker.
- MMP7, MMP1, and periostin were elevated in post-COVID patients with early fibrotic HRCT changes (Safont et al. 2022); MMP7 and MMP1 are established plasma biomarkers in IPF.
- Soluble ITGaM and ITGb2 associated with pulmonary complications in post-COVID patients (Siekacz et al. 2023). D-dimer elevated in COVID-19 with CT abnormalities; high MMP7 and periostin in patients with reduced DLCO (Balbi et al. 2021; Safont et al. 2022).

**Treatment strategies**
- *Pirfenidone:* Anti-inflammatory, anti-fibrotic, anti-oxidant; downregulates furin (SARS-CoV-2 entry protease) and TGF-β activation (Al-Kuraishy et al. 2022). A deuterated form (deupirfenidone / LYT-100) was evaluated in a phase II trial (NCT04652518) for post-acute COVID-19 respiratory disease; the trial was terminated. A single case report showed improvement after 2 months and substantial progress after 2 years of pirfenidone treatment in PCPF (Zhou et al. 2022).
- *Nintedanib:* Tyrosine kinase inhibitor targeting FGF, PDGF, and VEGF receptors; inhibits fibroblast proliferation, differentiation, and migration. Cannot reverse fibrosis but slows progression (approved for IPF and systemic sclerosis–ILD).
- *HDAC inhibitors:* Epigenetically suppress components of the TGF-β / fibroblast-to-myofibroblast differentiation pathway. Several FDA-approved for specific cancers. Early HDAC-inhibitor therapy after HRCT detection of fibrotic lesions could potentially reduce PCPF development (Murthy et al. 2021). Not yet tested specifically in COVID-19 survivors.
- *Angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs):* AT1R antagonists; counter angiotensin II-driven profibrotic signaling resulting from ACE2 downregulation. Animal studies show ACE2 increase in multiple tissues; clinical use widespread and safety-established. Not yet formally tested in PCPF patients.
- *Mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) and lung spheroid cells (LSCs):* Immunosuppressive, anti-inflammatory, and regenerative secretomes can rescue damaged alveolar epithelial cells and reduce fibroblast activation. LSC secretome outperforms MSC secretome in reverting alveolar damage to healthy levels in PF mouse models (Dinh et al. 2020). Multiple IPF trials ongoing. High cost, labor intensity, and safety concerns are barriers; the secretome (not the cells themselves) is identified as the active component. Potentially the only treatment class capable of *reversing* (not just halting) fibrotic damage.

## Relevance

This paper is directly relevant to the project research question (**research-question:post-acute-infection-syndromes**) and the PAIS frame of **failed homeostatic recovery after acute infection**, specifically for the organ-level (pulmonary) failure mode.

**PCPF as an instance of failed homeostatic recovery:** The lung's normal post-injury response — inflammation resolution, ECM remodeling back to baseline, epithelial regeneration — fails in PCPF. The review provides a mechanistic account of *why* this recovery fails: dysregulation of the TGF-β/ECM/ACE2 axis creates a self-reinforcing profibrotic loop that the normal repair program cannot exit. This directly instantiates the project's "locked-in dysregulated state" framing.

**Long-term persistence quantified:** The 15-year SARS-CoV follow-up data (Li et al. 2021) provide the best available estimate of what PCPF trajectory looks like beyond the 1–2 year window: persistent (12.7% fibrosis by HRCT) but non-progressive — an important distinction from IPF, where progression is relentless. This implies PCPF may represent a stable aberrant attractor rather than a deteriorating one, with implications for intervention timing.

**Mechanism taxonomy for the project:** The four-mechanism framework (aberrant ECM repair, ACE2 disruption, αvβ6-integrin/TGF-β activation, EMT) maps onto the project's mechanistic hypothesis space for post-infectious tissue damage. The ACE2-axis mechanism is SARS-CoV-2-specific; the TGF-β/ECM/EMT mechanisms are shared with other fibrotic lung diseases, connecting to a broader non-pathogen-specific PAIS biology.

**Therapeutic target evidence:** The review provides the most detailed current pharmacological target map for post-COVID lung failure. The identification of MSCs/LSCs as potentially reversing (not merely halting) fibrosis is significant for the project's interest in recovery mechanisms: it implies homeostatic re-entry may be achievable pharmacologically, not just slowed decline.

**Connection to shared PAIS mechanisms:** ACE2 downregulation and the resulting angiotensin II accumulation may also drive non-pulmonary PAIS pathology (cardiovascular, neurological), making this mechanism more broadly relevant to the project than the lung-specific framing implies.

## Limitations

1. **Narrative review, no formal meta-analysis:** Studies in Table 1 are heterogeneous in case definition, severity, follow-up interval, and imaging/functional outcome measurement. The authors do not apply a formal risk-of-bias or quality assessment, and quantitative prevalence estimates should be treated as indicative ranges rather than definitive point estimates.

2. **PCPF case definition absent:** As the authors explicitly note in section 7, a consensus clinical definition of PCPF is lacking; individual studies use different HRCT criteria, PFT thresholds, and follow-up windows, making cross-study comparison unreliable.

3. **Vaccination status inconsistently recorded:** Many of the reviewed cohort studies (especially pre-2021) did not systematically record vaccination status or included exclusively unvaccinated patients, limiting conclusions about vaccination as a PCPF modifier.

4. **Mechanistic evidence is largely preclinical or correlational:** The four pathophysiological mechanisms are supported primarily by in vitro data, animal models, and cross-sectional biomarker correlations. Direct causal evidence in human PCPF cohorts (e.g., intervention studies targeting ACE2 or αvβ6 integrins) is absent.

5. **Selection toward severity:** Most reviewed cohort studies enrolled hospitalised or post-ICU patients; the prevalence of PCPF in mild-to-moderate (non-hospitalised) COVID-19 — the majority of infections — is poorly characterized.

6. **Therapeutics section lacks controlled trial data:** No RCT data for PCPF specifically exist; pirfenidone phase II trial (LYT-100) was terminated; nintedanib data are from IPF and systemic sclerosis–ILD. All treatment recommendations are extrapolated from IPF biology or preclinical models.

7. **Maximum follow-up of 2 years for SARS-CoV-2 cohorts:** Long-term natural history beyond 2 years is unavailable for COVID-19 specifically; 15-year SARS-CoV data are used as a proxy with important caveats (different virus, smaller cohort, n = 58).

8. **Competing interests:** Janesh Pillay is supported by the Netherlands Organization for Health Research and Development and has received funding from Siemens and UMCG. Janette Burgess reports a relationship with Boehringer Ingelheim (nintedanib manufacturer) as Associate Editor.

## Model / Tool Availability

No computational model, dataset, or software tool is released. The review is a synthesis of published literature. Clinical instruments discussed (EndoPAT, HRCT, PFT/DLCO, PET with αvβ6-targeting tracer) and biological reagents (MSC / LSC secretomes in preclinical models) are not directly released by this paper.

## Follow-up

- **Pillay et al. 2025 (Respiratory Research):** Collagen turnover in critically ill COVID-19 patients associated with increased mortality — primary data paper from co-author Pillay directly supporting the ECM remodeling mechanism. Priority read.
- **García-Hidalgo et al. 2022 (Clin Transl Med):** Plasma proteomics in SARS-CoV-2–induced ARDS survivors linking 35 proteins to DLCO impairment — key mechanistic dataset for the aberrant ECM/immune response mechanism.
- **Breisnes et al. 2024 (BMC Pulm Med):** PRO-C3/PC3X as early biomarkers of PCPF — validate in independent cohorts; would enable earlier identification of PAIS patients at risk for persistent lung damage.
- **Li et al. 2021 (Lancet):** 15-year follow-up of SARS-CoV survivors including pulmonary function and HRCT — the foundational long-term natural history dataset for the persistent-but-non-progressive PCPF conclusion.
- **Kimura et al. 2023 (Rare Med):** PET imaging with αvβ6 integrin tracer in IPF and PASC patients — extends the integrin mechanism to living human imaging; relevant to biomarker and mechanistic hypothesis.
- **ACE2 auto-antibody studies (Arthur et al. 2021):** Characterize the autoimmune contribution to PCPF; bridge between mechanism hypothesis 2 (autoimmunity) and lung-specific fibrotic failure.
- **Open question:** Does the ACE2-downregulation/angiotensin-II mechanism contribute to non-pulmonary PAIS sequelae (cardiac, neurological)? If so, ARBs may have broader utility in PAIS than the lung-centric framing implies.
