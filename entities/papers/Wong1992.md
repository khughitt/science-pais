# Skeletal muscle metabolism in the chronic fatigue syndrome

- **Authors:** R. Wong, G. Lopaschuk, G. Zhu, D. Walker, D. Catellier, D. Burton, K. Teo, R. Collins-Nakai, T. Montague
- **Year:** 1992
- **Journal:** Chest, 102(6), 1716-1722
- **DOI:** 10.1378/chest.102.6.1716
- **PMID:** 1446478
- **BibTeX key:** Wong1992
- **Source:** PubMed record

## Key Contribution

An early in-vivo 31P-NMR study of gastrocnemius muscle metabolism during graded dynamic exercise to exhaustion in CFS. It provides a historical primary anchor for the claim that skeletal-muscle energy metabolism during exertion is abnormal in at least some CFS cohorts, while also showing that patient-control patterns can look qualitatively similar when the endpoint is measured at exhaustion.

## Methods

- **Cohort:** 22 CFS patients and 21 normal controls.
- **Protocol:** graded dynamic gastrocnemius exercise to clinical exhaustion, with recovery monitoring.
- **Readouts:** minute-to-minute phosphocreatine, inorganic phosphate, pH, and ATP by 31P nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy.

## Key Findings

- CFS patients exercised for a shorter duration than controls (about 8.1 vs 11.3 minutes).
- Phosphocreatine, inorganic phosphate, and pH changed in qualitatively similar patterns in both groups and reached similar metabolic endpoints.
- Those metabolic endpoints were reached faster in the CFS group.
- ATP was significantly lower at exhaustion in CFS patients.
- The authors interpreted the pattern as suggesting impaired oxidative metabolism with accelerated glycolysis in working skeletal muscle.

## Relevance

This supports `proposition:0030` as a second, independent in-vivo exercise-muscle signal. It is weaker than Jones2012 for t056 because it does not test repeat-exercise recovery or modern PEM criteria, but it is valuable because it shows the ME/CFS muscle-endpoint literature did not begin with whole-body CPET alone.

## Limitations

- Early CFS-era case definition and no modern PEM enrichment.
- The key abnormalities are acceleration to fatigue and ATP at exhaustion, not a specific post-exertional recovery lesion.
- Not directly comparable to Appelman2024's long-COVID biopsy OXPHOS/SDH/fiber-type phenotype.
