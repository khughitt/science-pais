# Characterizing Long COVID in Children and Adolescents

## Key Contribution

RECOVER-Pediatrics cohort study deriving age-specific PASC research indices for school-age children
(6-11 years) and adolescents (12-17 years). It is the main phenotype anchor showing that pediatric long
COVID is multisystem but developmentally staged.

## Methods

- **Design:** multicenter longitudinal observational cohort.
- **Setting:** more than 60 US health-care and community settings.
- **Participants:** 5367 total; 898 school-age children and 4469 adolescents, with infected and
  uninfected groups.
- **Exposure:** SARS-CoV-2 infection history.
- **Outcome:** 89 prolonged symptoms across 9 domains; LASSO-derived age-specific PASC research indices
  and symptom clusters.

## Key Findings

- Fourteen symptoms were more common after SARS-CoV-2 infection in both age groups, with additional
  age-specific symptoms.
- The school-age index emphasizes neurocognitive, pain, GI, sleep, skin/rash, lightheadedness, and
  school-refusal features.
- The adolescent index emphasizes smell/taste change, pain, daytime tiredness/low energy, exertional
  tiredness, cognition, headache, and lightheadedness.
- The derived indices correlate with poorer overall health and quality of life.
- Clustering identified four school-age and three adolescent PASC symptom phenotypes.

## Relevance

This paper blocks a naive adult-to-pediatric transfer of long-COVID definitions. It supports treating
pediatric PASC as part of PAIS while requiring age-specific measurement.

## Limitations

- Symptom-index study, not a mechanistic biomarker study.
- Infection history and symptom reporting have residual misclassification.
- Recruitment includes multiple channels, including self-referral and clinical sources.
- The indices are for research characterization, not clinical diagnosis.
