# Advances in Understanding Inflammation and Tissue Damage: Markers of Persistent Sequelae in COVID-19 Patients

- **Authors:** Raul Patrascu, Cristina Stefania Dumitru
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal:** Journal of Clinical Medicine, vol. 14, no. 5, p. 1475
- **DOI:** 10.3390/jcm14051475
- **BibTeX key:** Patrascu2025
- **Source:** PDF

## Key Contribution

This narrative review synthesizes the landscape of established and emerging biomarkers of inflammation and tissue damage in post-acute sequelae of SARS-CoV-2 (PASC / long COVID), organizing them by biological system (immune/inflammatory markers, neurological, pulmonary, fatigue-related, and gastrointestinal) and by clinical utility (diagnosis, prognosis, therapeutic targeting). The review's distinctive contribution is its integration of novel markers — soluble urokinase plasminogen activator receptor (suPAR), neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs/citrullinated histone H3), neurofilament light chain (NfL), glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP), Krebs von den Lungen-6 (KL-6), surfactant protein D (SP-D), intestinal fatty acid-binding protein (I-FABP), and zonulin — alongside established markers (CRP, IL-6, D-dimer, ferritin, ESR) into a single clinical framework. The review also includes a dedicated section on Multisystem Inflammatory Syndrome in Children (MIS-C) as a discrete post-infectious sequela driven by hyperinflammation. It argues that biomarker-guided, personalized therapeutic approaches will be essential for long COVID management, and calls for longitudinal studies tracking biomarker trajectories and their interaction with therapeutic outcomes.

## Methods

This is a narrative review published in the Journal of Clinical Medicine (received 3 December 2024; accepted 20 February 2025; published 22 February 2025). Authors are from the Department of Functional Sciences and Department of Microscopic Morphology/Histology at Victor Babes University of Medicine and Pharmacy, Timisoara, Romania. The review synthesizes published observational studies, meta-analyses, and clinical guidance documents through approximately late 2024 across 96 references. No systematic search strategy or PRISMA framework is described. The paper is organized thematically: immune response basics, established and novel inflammatory markers in PASC, organ-system-specific tissue damage biomarkers, MIS-C, clinical implications (diagnostic/prognostic utility, therapeutic personalization, future potential), and future directions. A summary table (Table 2) lists 20 specific biomarkers with their relevance and clinical utility.

## Key Findings

**Established inflammatory markers in PASC (Section 3.1)**
- CRP remains persistently elevated in many long COVID patients, continuing to serve as an indicator of ongoing systemic inflammation.
- Elevated ferritin and ESR have been correlated with prolonged recovery and increased severity of post-acute symptoms.
- IL-6 and TNF-alpha are noted for their roles in chronic inflammation and tissue damage; IL-6 elevated levels predict persistence and have been therapeutically targeted (tocilizumab, sarilumab).
- D-dimer serves as a strong predictor of thrombotic complications and mortality in COVID-19, and guides anticoagulation decisions.

**Novel inflammatory biomarkers (Section 3.2–3.3)**
- **suPAR** (soluble urokinase plasminogen activator receptor): gains attention as a predictor of disease progression and severity. Elevated suPAR associated with sustained immune activation and endothelial dysfunction, particularly in long COVID fatigue, suggesting ongoing low-grade inflammation contributing to mitochondrial dysfunction and impaired energy metabolism.
- **Chemokines CCL2 (MCP-1) and CXCL10 (IP-10)**: key mediators of immune cell migration and activation; elevated in severe acute disease and believed to contribute to chronic inflammatory state in PASC.
- **IL-10 and TGF-beta**: paradoxically linked to worse outcomes despite anti-inflammatory roles — complex involvement in fibrosis and organ healing, particularly lung and cardiovascular system.
- **NETs (neutrophil extracellular traps)** and the associated marker **citrullinated histone H3 (Cit-H3)**: web-like DNA structures contributing to inflammation and thrombosis; could serve as novel therapeutic targets to reduce thrombosis risk and improve outcomes in post-acute COVID-19.

**Organ-system tissue damage markers (Section 4)**
- *Neurological:* **NfL** (neurofilament light chain, marker of axonal injury) elevated in patients with persistent neurological symptoms — brain fog, memory impairment — indicating ongoing neurodegeneration or blood-brain barrier dysfunction. **GFAP** (marker of astrocytic injury) associated with white matter changes in long COVID, reinforcing the role of neuroinflammation.
- *Pulmonary:* **KL-6** (alveolar epithelial damage marker) and **SP-D** (secreted by alveolar epithelial cells) elevated in patients with prolonged respiratory impairment, reflecting ongoing fibrotic processes. Anti-SARS-CoV-2 spike (anti-S) antibody levels identified as an independent predictor of persistent respiratory symptoms and reduced lung function in a large prospective study; higher titers correlated with prolonged radiological lung abnormalities.
- *Fatigue/ME-CFS overlap:* suPAR linked to long COVID fatigue syndrome with similarities to ME/CFS; elevated levels suggest impaired energy metabolism as a pathophysiological mechanism.
- *Gastrointestinal:* **I-FABP** (intestinal fatty acid-binding protein) elevated in COVID-19 patients with prolonged GI symptoms, reflecting enterocyte damage. **Zonulin** linked to increased gut permeability and systemic inflammation, possibly contributing to ongoing immune activation.

**MIS-C (Section 5)**
- MIS-C typically presents 2–4 weeks post-acute COVID-19 in children and adolescents; pathophysiology rooted in a hyperinflammatory/cytokine storm response.
- Key inflammatory markers consistently elevated: CRP, ESR, procalcitonin, ferritin, IL-6.
- Cardiac involvement evidenced by elevated troponin and BNP/NT-proBNP; ranges from mild myocarditis to cardiogenic shock.
- Standard treatment: IV immunoglobulins (IVIGs) and corticosteroids, which mitigate severity and improve outcomes significantly.
- Exact global incidence of MIS-C under investigation; geographic/genetic variation in prevalence noted.

**Clinical and therapeutic implications (Section 6–7)**
- CRP, IL-6, and D-dimer have facilitated early detection of severe manifestations and prediction of ICU admission and need for mechanical ventilation (Section 6.1).
- IL-6 inhibitors (tocilizumab, sarilumab) demonstrated efficacy in acute COVID-19 and may have a role in persistent post-COVID-19 inflammatory syndromes.
- Elevated D-dimer used to stratify patients for extended anticoagulation therapy.
- suPAR and KL-6/SP-D guide emerging organ-specific therapeutic decisions (vascular inflammation therapies, antifibrotic therapy, lung rehabilitation).
- Microbiome-derived markers (short-chain fatty acids, gut permeability indicators, microbial metabolomics) flagged as a promising emerging biomarker frontier.
- Multi-omics (genomics, proteomics, metabolomics, transcriptomics) approaches and AI-driven pattern recognition identified as the key future research directions.

**Summary table of 20 biomarkers (Table 2)** covers: CRP, IL-6, D-dimer, cardiac troponins, ferritin, BNP, NLR, procalcitonin, ESR, IL-10, IFN-gamma, IL-1beta, suPAR, NETs, KL-6, NfL, GFAP, I-FABP, zonulin, anti-S antibodies.

## Relevance

This review directly addresses the PAIS research question — specifically, what biological markers identify and quantify failed homeostatic recovery after acute SARS-CoV-2 infection, and how those markers map onto specific organ-system failure modes. Its relevance to the project's PAIS frame is multi-dimensional:

1. **Biomarker panel for failed homeostatic recovery:** The 20-marker inventory in Table 2 provides an operationalizable starting point for identifying individuals who have not returned to baseline after acute infection. The persistence of CRP, ESR, IL-6, D-dimer, and suPAR weeks to months post-infection is direct empirical evidence that homeostatic immune and coagulation regulation has not been restored — the core PAIS phenotype.

2. **Organ-system specificity:** The organ-stratified structure (neurological NfL/GFAP, pulmonary KL-6/SP-D, GI I-FABP/zonulin, cardiovascular troponin/BNP) aligns directly with the project's framing of PAIS as multi-system failed recovery. Each organ-specific biomarker axis represents a distinct homeostatic failure mode that can be monitored longitudinally.

3. **ME/CFS connection:** The observation that suPAR-linked fatigue in long COVID shares features with ME/CFS, and that chronic fatigue is one of the most prevalent long COVID symptoms, situates this review within the broader PAIS comparative framework — one of the project's key analytical dimensions.

4. **MIS-C as a discrete post-infectious syndrome:** MIS-C's inclusion frames it as a pediatric post-infectious homeostatic failure mode driven by delayed hyperinflammation, extending the PAIS concept beyond adults and providing a model system where the lag between acute infection and immune dysregulation is highly reproducible (2–4 weeks).

5. **Therapeutic targets:** By linking biomarkers to treatment strategies (IL-6 inhibitors, anticoagulation, antifibrotic therapy), the review identifies intervention points that are directly relevant to the project's interest in therapeutic implications of PAIS mechanisms.

The review's explicit call for longitudinal biomarker trajectory studies aligns with the project's computational track ambitions: as longitudinal datasets become available, the biomarker panel described here provides an annotated measurement framework.

## Limitations

- Narrative review with no stated systematic search strategy or PRISMA framework; findings reflect selective synthesis and may overrepresent positive/published biomarker studies.
- Most cited studies lack matched healthy controls, use heterogeneous PASC case definitions, and have high selection bias toward more symptomatic participants — the review does not consistently flag this.
- Quantitative claims about biomarker prevalence (e.g., proportion of PASC patients with elevated CRP) are not consistently cited with specific effect sizes or cohort sizes in the main text, making cross-study comparison difficult. Table 2 presents clinical utility in qualitative terms only.
- Many novel biomarkers discussed (suPAR, NfL, GFAP, KL-6, I-FABP, zonulin in PASC) are based on small or early-phase studies; clinical validation at scale is lacking.
- The MIS-C section relies heavily on clinical case series and expert guidance rather than mechanistic studies; incidence estimates are explicitly described as uncertain.
- The review does not address biomarker performance in specific demographic subgroups (age, sex, comorbidities, vaccination status, viral variant) that are known to influence PASC incidence and severity.
- Anti-S antibody titer data for predicting lung dysfunction is attributed to "a large prospective study" but specific cohort size and effect size are not quoted directly in the reviewed text.
- No formal risk-of-bias assessment or meta-analytic synthesis is performed.

- Read the suPAR primary studies (Patterson et al. 2021, Front. Immunol.; Phetsouphanh et al. 2022, Nat. Immunol. ref 48) for quantitative suPAR trajectories in long COVID fatigue.
- Obtain the anti-S antibody / lung function prospective study (Freund et al. 2023, Clin Exp Med, ref 46) for specific cohort size and effect sizes.

- Follow up on microbiome-derived biomarkers (Zuo et al. 2020, Gastroenterology; Yeoh et al. 2021, Gut; refs 75–76) as a potential computational analysis target connecting gut dysbiosis to persistent immune activation.
- Assess whether the organ-stratified biomarker framework (NfL/GFAP, KL-6/SP-D, I-FABP/zonulin, troponin/BNP) could be used as a multi-domain PAIS phenotyping instrument in existing long COVID cohort datasets.
- Evaluate the MIS-C literature for its potential as a clean experimental model of post-infectious immune homeostasis failure (defined 2–4 week lag, reproducible biomarker profile, treatment-responsive).
