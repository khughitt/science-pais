# Muscle abnormalities worsen after post-exertional malaise in long COVID

- **Authors:** Brent Appelman, Braeden T. Charlton, Richie P. Goulding, Tom J. Kerkhoff, Ellen A. Breedveld, Wendy Noort, Carla Offringa, Frank W. Bloemers, Michel van Weeghel, Bauke V. Schomakers, Pedro Coelho, Jelle J. Posthuma, Eleonora Aronica, W. Joost Wiersinga, Michèle van Vugt, Rob C. I. Wüst
- **Year:** 2024
- **Journal:** Nature Communications, vol. 15, p. 17
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s41467-023-44432-3
- **PMCID:** PMC10766651
- **BibTeX key:** Appelman2024
- **Source:** Europe PMC XML (full text)

## Key Contribution

The most direct published evidence that post-exertional malaise (PEM) in long COVID has a tissue-level muscle pathology basis, not merely a functional or deconditioning origin. Using a before/after exercise-biopsy design that deliberately provoked PEM in a well-characterised long COVID cohort, this study showed that multiple pre-existing muscle abnormalities — reduced mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation capacity, glycolytic fiber-type shift, amyloid-containing deposits, and signs of ongoing myopathy — are significantly worsened in the day following the maximal exercise test. The design is uniquely suited to distinguish PEM pathophysiology from the baseline impairment of exercise capacity, and the findings collectively rule out deconditioning as a sufficient explanation while leaving open whether the primary lesion is mitochondrial, vascular, or immune-mediated.

## Methods

**Study design.** Longitudinal case-control (registered NCT05225688). Vastus lateralis muscle biopsies and venous blood were obtained at rest (baseline) and one day after a maximal cardiopulmonary exercise test (CPET) on a cycle ergometer designed to systematically induce PEM.

**Cohort.** 25 long COVID patients and 21 age- and sex-matched healthy controls who had fully recovered from mild SARS-CoV-2 infection. Both groups: PCR-confirmed prior SARS-CoV-2, no hospitalisation. Long COVID defined by WHO criteria; PEM was a required inclusion criterion. Mean age ~41 years; ~48% male in both groups. Median time since initial infection: 545 days (long COVID) vs. 142 days (controls).

**Case definition.** WHO long COVID criteria with mandatory PEM as an inclusion criterion. This is an important strength: the cohort is enriched for a biologically coherent PEM-positive phenotype rather than a heterogeneous long COVID sample.

**Exercise test.** Maximal CPET on cycle ergometer; biopsies and blood drawn before and ~1 day after (PEM state). All long COVID patients experienced PEM following the test (muscle pain, increased fatigue severity, cognitive symptoms persisting up to 7 days).

**Muscle readouts.**
- Mitochondrial respiration: ex-vivo high-resolution respirometry of fresh muscle biopsy in hyperoxic conditions (removing in-vivo oxygen-diffusion limitation as a confounder); oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) capacity reported.
- Mitochondrial content: succinate dehydrogenase (SDH) histochemical activity on muscle sections.
- Fiber-type composition: immunofluorescence for type I (slow-oxidative), IIa (fast-oxidative), IIx (fast-glycolytic) fibers in vastus lateralis.
- Capillary density and capillary-to-fiber ratio: histology.
- Amyloid-containing deposits: Thioflavin T staining with confocal microscopy; co-localised with capillary (CD31) and lymphatic vessel (LYVE-1) markers and cell nuclei (DAPI).
- Pathological features: semi-quantitative scoring for atrophic fibers, focal necrosis, centralized nuclei (regeneration), and regenerative basophilic fibers.
- Inflammatory infiltration: CD3+ T-cells, CD68+ macrophages, CD20+ B-cells (immunohistochemistry).

**Metabolomics.** 116 metabolites in skeletal muscle and 83 in venous blood at both time points; pathway annotation covering TCA cycle, glycolysis, purine synthesis, and amino acids.

**Exercise capacity (CPET).** VO2max, peak power, gas exchange threshold, peripheral O2 extraction via near-infrared spectroscopy (NIRS), maximal ventilation, PETCO2.

**Controls for effort.** Confirmed maximal effort in both groups: no group difference in proportion attaining VO2 plateau; similar gas exchange threshold as % of VO2max; secondary lactate, RER, and heart rate criteria met.

**Statistics.** Generalized linear mixed models for longitudinal comparisons; two-sided t-tests and Wilcoxon tests for group comparisons; Pearson correlations; emmeans with BH correction for post-hoc tests.

## Key Findings

### 1. Reduced exercise capacity — peripheral, not cardiovascular
Long COVID patients had significantly lower VO2max (p < 0.0001), peak power (p = 0.001), and gas exchange threshold (p = 0.014). Cardiovascular parameters (heart rate, stroke-volume proxy) were not impaired, ruling out cardiac limitation. Lower maximal O2 pulse and lower peripheral O2 extraction by NIRS implicated skeletal muscle as the primary limiting site.

### 2. Glycolytic fiber-type shift — before exercise challenge
At baseline, patients had a significantly higher proportion of type IIx (fast-glycolytic, highly fatigable) fibers in vastus lateralis (p = 0.036). Female long COVID patients additionally had smaller type I (slow-oxidative) fiber cross-sectional area. Fiber cross-sectional area correlated with peak power in both groups, but with a lower intercept in patients — meaning patients achieved less power per unit of muscle mass, implicating intrinsic contractile or metabolic defects beyond fiber size alone.

### 3. Reduced OXPHOS capacity — worsened after PEM induction
Mitochondrial OXPHOS capacity was significantly lower in patients at baseline (p = 0.003). One day after PEM induction, OXPHOS capacity decreased in both groups (significant time effect), but remained lower in patients (group effect persisted). This test was conducted in hyperoxic conditions, deliberately removing any in-vivo oxygen-delivery limitation — isolating an intrinsic mitochondrial functional defect rather than a delivery problem.

### 4. SDH activity — selectively reduced after exercise in patients
At baseline, SDH activity (a histochemical proxy for mitochondrial enzyme content/density) was not significantly different between groups (p = 0.06, borderline). However, after exercise/PEM induction, SDH activity decreased only in long COVID patients, not in controls (significant interaction). The combination of reduced OXPHOS capacity (quality/function defect) and exercise-induced reduction in SDH activity (content/density defect) together constitute a more severe post-PEM mitochondrial impairment in patients. Notably, the SDH–VO2max correlation present in healthy controls was absent in patients, meaning mitochondrial content no longer predicts exercise capacity in long COVID — consistent with a qualitative functional defect.

### 5. Metabolic shift away from oxidative metabolism
In skeletal muscle at rest, TCA cycle intermediates (glutamate, FAD+, alpha-ketoglutarate, citric acid) were lower in patients, and the citric acid:lactate ratio was lower — indicative of a shift from oxidative to glycolytic metabolism. Creatine was also lower in patients, consistent with reduced phosphate buffering capacity for OXPHOS. Lower S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) suggests reduced methylation capacity. After PEM, dihydroxyacetone phosphate (a glycolytic/lipid-biosynthesis intermediate) decreased in patients. In blood, glycolytic metabolites were higher at baseline in patients but TCA cycle metabolites (pyruvate and others) were lower, and blood glycolytic metabolites declined one week post-PEM.

### 6. Amyloid-containing deposits — elevated at baseline, not inside capillaries
Amyloid-containing deposits (Thioflavin T+) were significantly more abundant in skeletal muscle of long COVID patients at baseline (group effect p < 0.001). Exercise increased amyloid deposits in both groups similarly (time effect p = 0.008). Critical topographic finding: deposits were located in the extracellular matrix between fibers and adjacent to (not inside) capillaries or lymphatic vessels, and did not co-localise with cell nuclei. This directly tested and refuted the hypothesis that amyloid microclots obstruct capillary perfusion causing local ischemia: no capillary occlusion was seen, and capillary-to-fiber ratios and intracellular lactate did not differ between groups (no hypoxia signal). The origin and functional consequence of extramuscular amyloid deposits remain unresolved.

### 7. Exercise-induced myopathy — exacerbated in long COVID
Semi-quantitative histological scoring showed a significantly greater proportion of long COVID patients had small atrophic fibers both at baseline and after exercise (group p < 0.001). Focal necrosis was observed in 36% of patients after exhaustive exercise vs. far fewer controls (borderline group p = 0.09). Centralized nuclei (a marker of fiber repair/regeneration from prior damage) were significantly more abundant in patients before exercise (p = 0.002). Both groups showed increased regenerative fibers post-exercise, but patients had more signs of pre-existing ongoing myopathy. This pattern — atrophy, necrosis, and active regeneration — constitutes a chronic myopathic process that exercise acutely exacerbates.

### 8. Immune infiltration in muscle
CD3+ T-cells (p = 0.046) and CD68+ macrophages (p = 0.03) were significantly more abundant in skeletal muscle of long COVID patients. CD20+ B-cells were sparse in both groups. This immune infiltration pattern is consistent with an ongoing inflammatory myopathy or a persistent post-infectious immune response in muscle tissue.

## Relevance to PAIS

This paper is the most direct exercise-provoked, tissue-level evidence linking PEM in long COVID to objective muscle pathology, and is the key empirical anchor for `question:0011-mitochondrial-basis-of-pem`.

**On the mitochondrial question (primary vs. secondary defect).** The study demonstrates a genuine intrinsic mitochondrial functional impairment (reduced OXPHOS in hyperoxic ex-vivo conditions, decoupled from delivery) that is worse after PEM and present as reduced SDH activity post-exercise. However, the data cannot cleanly separate primary mitochondrial disease from secondary mitochondrial failure. The concurrent findings of glycolytic fiber-type shift, TCA metabolite depletion, immune infiltration, amyloid deposits, and focal necrosis are consistent with multiple interacting mechanisms. The absence of a hypoxia signal despite amyloid deposits argues against ischemia-reperfusion as the sole driver, but the immune infiltration (CD3+ T-cells, macrophages) raises the possibility that an inflammatory process is damaging mitochondria secondarily. The SDH–VO2max correlation loss in patients — qualitative dysfunction without proportionate content reduction at baseline — points toward a functional lesion that could be intrinsic to the mitochondrion or driven by a local inflammatory or metabolic environment.

**Against the deconditioning explanation.** Deconditioning would predict: (a) proportional reductions in fiber size, capillary density, and SDH activity together; (b) normal intrinsic muscle contractile and metabolic function per unit of fiber; (c) recovery from exercise comparable to healthy controls. The data are inconsistent with this on multiple counts: the capillary-to-fiber ratio was not reduced; for a given fiber cross-sectional area, patients produced less power; OXPHOS was impaired even in hyperoxic conditions (i.e., independent of delivery); and SDH activity fell specifically in patients after exercise (the opposite of the normal recovery trajectory). The authors explicitly conclude that deconditioning cannot explain the exercise capacity deficit.

**Provoked-state design as methodological advance.** The before/after exercise-biopsy design is a key strength: it captures both the resting abnormality and the additional PEM-specific changes, allowing the two to be distinguished. Most prior long COVID muscle studies are cross-sectional resting biopsies and cannot determine what changes specifically in the provoked PEM state.

**Connection to `hypothesis:0001-shared-dysregulated-attractor`.** The pattern of mutually reinforcing abnormalities (mitochondrial dysfunction → energy deficiency → increased glycolysis → metabolic shift + immune infiltration + amyloid accumulation + myopathic changes) is consistent with a self-reinforcing local failure mode in skeletal muscle that may be the peripheral effector of the systemic attractor described in hypothesis:0001. The finding that exercise worsens these abnormalities rather than producing normal adaptive remodeling explains why exertion perpetuates rather than resolves the condition.

## Limitations

- **Small N.** 25 long COVID patients and 21 controls; adequately powered for the primary mitochondrial readouts but underpowered for subgroup analyses (e.g., by PEM severity, time since infection, sex-specific effects on fiber composition).
- **Single exercise timepoint for biopsy.** Post-exercise biopsy taken ~1 day after the CPET. The kinetics of the muscle pathology during PEM are unknown; the biopsy may not capture the peak or resolution of all abnormalities.
- **Causal ambiguity: primary vs. secondary mitochondrial defect.** The study design cannot determine whether the mitochondrial functional impairment is a primary lesion or secondary to immune infiltration, amyloid accumulation, or metabolic shifts. The immune infiltration (T-cells, macrophages) is a credible driver of secondary mitochondrial damage via cytotoxic or inflammatory mechanisms.
- **Amyloid deposit composition and significance unresolved.** Thioflavin T staining detects amyloid-like structures but cannot distinguish fibrin amyloid microclots (as proposed by Pretorius et al.) from other amyloid-containing proteins. The extravascular, extramuscular location rules out the microclot-occlusion hypothesis, but the nature and origin of these deposits are not established.
- **Controls not SARS-CoV-2 naive.** Controls recovered from mild SARS-CoV-2, which is appropriate for ruling out confounding by infection history but means it is not possible to determine which abnormalities are SARS-CoV-2-specific vs. general post-viral.
- **Time since infection differed.** Long COVID patients were median 504 days post-most-recent-infection vs. 135 days for controls. This difference is inherent to the cohort structure but means some baseline differences could partly reflect duration of illness rather than its pathological nature.
- **Metabolomics is resting state only.** Muscle biopsies for metabolomics were obtained before exercise; metabolic changes during the exercise itself or at later PEM time points are not captured.
- **No functional mitochondrial genetics.** The study does not test for mutations, mitochondrial copy number changes, or transcriptional profiles that would help determine whether the OXPHOS defect is intrinsic or environmentally driven.
- **Heterogeneity in exercise capacity.** Despite the mean differences, there was marked inter-patient heterogeneity in VO2max and other exercise parameters in the long COVID group. Subgroup analyses by severity or exercise capacity are not reported.
- **Single-center, European academic cohort.** Amsterdam-based, largely non-hospitalized, vaccinated cohort; generalizability to other long COVID populations (e.g., post-severe illness, unvaccinated, other variants) is not established.

## Model / Tool Availability

None. Data deposited as source data files associated with the published paper. No software or computational tools released. Muscle respirometry used standard Oroboros O2k high-resolution respirometry protocols (Gnaiger et al. 2020/2009).

## Follow-up

- Compare the OXPHOS / SDH / fiber-type findings directly against Che2025's ME/CFS exercise-challenge dataset to assess whether the peripheral muscle phenotype is shared across triggers or long-COVID-specific.
- Seek studies that characterise the amyloid-containing deposits in skeletal muscle of long COVID more precisely (e.g., fibrin vs. spike protein vs. other amyloid constituents) — the extravascular location rules out a simple microclot model but the deposit composition matters for mechanism.
- The immune infiltration (CD3+ T-cells, macrophages) in muscle warrants follow-up: are these antigen-specific (e.g., SARS-CoV-2 peptide-reactive)? Are they producing cytokines that impair mitochondrial function? Studies pairing T-cell receptor sequencing with muscle infiltrate histology would address this.
- Investigate whether the SDH–VO2max correlation loss in patients is reproducible and whether it tracks with PEM severity or recovery over time — it is potentially a diagnostic or monitoring marker of the qualitative mitochondrial dysfunction.
- The centralized-nuclei and focal necrosis findings suggest ongoing myopathic injury even at rest. Serial biopsies in patients who recover vs. remain ill over time would test whether these resolve and whether mitochondrial function normalizes in parallel.
- This paper is directly relevant to `question:0011` and provides the strongest existing evidence that PEM has an objective tissue substrate in long COVID, though the question of whether the mitochondrial defect is primary or secondary remains open.
