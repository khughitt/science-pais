## Key Contribution

This population-based cohort study from UK Biobank quantifies short-term VTE risk exclusively in **ambulatory (non-hospitalized) COVID-19 patients** — the lowest acute-severity stratum — and identifies independent clinical and genetic risk factors within that outpatient-only cohort. The central finding is that ambulatory COVID-19 carries a 21-fold elevated 30-day VTE hazard relative to propensity-score-matched uninfected controls. Crucially, **male sex is an independent VTE risk factor within ambulatory patients** (aHR 1.69, 95% CI 1.30–2.19), demonstrating that the male-biased VTE excess survives acute-severity adjustment and is not merely a carryover from hospitalization or severe acute illness.

## Methods

**Design:** Retrospective population-based cohort study using UK Biobank (UKBB) linked records (primary care EHRs, Hospital Episode Statistics, Public Health England PCR test data, national death registry, and individual genome-wide SNP genotyping arrays).

**Cohort construction:** UKBB participants in England alive on 2020-03-01 with confirmed positive PCR SARS-CoV-2 between 2020-03-01 and 2021-09-30 were enrolled. Participants hospitalized at the time of testing were excluded, leaving a strictly ambulatory (outpatient) infected cohort. Individuals with prior VTE history or antithrombotic/antiplatelet drug use in the preceding year were also excluded.

**Sample sizes:** 18,818 ambulatory patients with COVID-19 (10,580 women [56.2%]; mean age 64.3 years [SD 8.0]) propensity-score matched 1:5 to 93,179 uninfected participants (52,177 women [56.0%]; mean age 64.3 years [SD 7.9]).

**Propensity score matching:** Multivariable logistic regression estimated PS as the probability of infection based on all pre-specified covariates; 1:5 matching with caliper width 0.2 SD of logit-PS and exact matching on calendar index dates. Post-matching balance confirmed by standardized mean differences (all SMD < 0.10 after matching).

**Outcome:** Composite incident VTE (deep vein thrombosis or pulmonary embolism) occurring within 30 days after index date, ascertained from ICD-10-coded hospital records. Death was treated as a competing risk in cause-specific Cox models.

**Primary VTE risk analysis (infection vs controls):** Cause-specific Cox survival models estimating HR for VTE by infection status, overall and by vaccination subgroup.

**Clinical risk factor analysis (within the COVID-19 cohort only):** Multivariable Cox models for 30-day VTE in ambulatory COVID-19 patients. Covariate list: age, sex, race/ethnicity, socioeconomic status (Index of Multiple Deprivation), obesity (BMI ≥ 30 vs < 30), vaccination status, cancer, fall, fracture, and number of comorbidities (Charlson Comorbidity Index).

**Genetic risk factor analysis:** Among 21,055 infected participants with complete genetic data, inherited thrombophilia was defined as carrying risk variants in factor V Leiden (rs6025) or prothrombin G20210A (rs1799963). A 297-SNP polygenic risk score (PRS) for VTE served as a positive genetic control. Cox models adjusted for age, sex, and first 3 genetic ancestry principal components.

**Sensitivity analyses:** (1) Repeat clinical risk factor models in the uninfected cohort as a negative control (vaccination should not predict COVID-unrelated VTE); (2) PRS as a positive exposure control; (3) arterial thromboembolism as a negative outcome control for the inherited thrombophilia analysis.

## Key Findings

**30-day VTE incidence:**
- Ambulatory COVID-19 patients: 73 VTE events; incidence rate 50.99 per 1,000 person-years.
- Matched uninfected controls: 17 VTE events; incidence rate 2.37 per 1,000 person-years.
- Overall HR: 21.42 (95% CI 12.63–36.31).

**Vaccination interaction:**
- Unvaccinated/partially vaccinated COVID-19 patients: HR 27.94 (95% CI 15.11–51.65).
- Fully vaccinated breakthrough infection: HR 5.95 (95% CI 1.82–19.51); interaction P = 0.02.
- VTE risk is substantially but not fully attenuated by complete vaccination.

**Independent clinical VTE risk factors within ambulatory COVID-19 patients (adjusted HRs):**
- Age: aHR 1.87 (95% CI 1.50–2.33) per 10-year increment.
- **Male sex: aHR 1.69 (95% CI 1.30–2.19)** — men have ~70% higher VTE hazard than women, adjusted for all other covariates.
- Obesity (BMI ≥ 30): aHR 1.83 (95% CI 1.28–2.61).
- No/partial vaccination: aHR 5.50 (95% CI 3.00–10.08).
- Race/ethnicity, socioeconomic status, cancer, fall, fracture, and number of comorbidities: also included in the model; point estimates shown in Figure 2 but not individually highlighted as significant in the text beyond sex, age, obesity, and vaccination.

**Sensitivity control check:** Vaccination was not associated with COVID-unrelated VTE in the uninfected cohort (HR 1.07, 95% CI 0.80–1.42), validating the clinical risk factor models against residual confounding.

**Inherited thrombophilia in ambulatory COVID-19 patients:**
- Prevalence of inherited thrombophilia: 6.11% of infected participants with genetic data (factor V Leiden 4.32%, prothrombin G20210A 1.86%).
- Inherited thrombophilia: adjusted HR 2.05 (95% CI 1.15–3.66) for post-COVID-19 VTE (P = 0.01).
- Factor V Leiden alone: aHR 2.17 (95% CI 1.13–4.15).
- Prothrombin G20210A alone: aHR 1.52 (95% CI 0.48–4.79; underpowered — minimum detectable IRR 3.58 at current sample size).
- No association between inherited thrombophilia and arterial thromboembolism (negative control: aHR 0.98, 95% CI 0.53–1.80), confirming specificity.
- Polygenic risk score (continuous per-SD): aHR 1.34 (95% CI 1.11–1.60; P < 0.001), confirming the positive genetic control behaves as expected.

## Relevance

This paper provides the decisive severity-discriminating evidence for the **male-biased post-COVID VTE excess** in the PAIS project's thromboinflammation mechanism frame. The key relevance is:

1. **Cohort restriction to ambulatory patients eliminates acute-severity confounding.** By excluding all hospitalized cases at time of testing, the study holds acute severity at the lowest plausible stratum. The male VTE excess (aHR 1.69) that emerges within this stratum cannot be attributed to male patients having experienced more severe acute illness — they did not; all are outpatients. This establishes that male sex is an *independent* risk modifier for COVID-associated VTE even at low acute-illness severity.

2. **Connection to hypothesis:0004 (acute-severity threshold).** Hypothesis 0004 frames acute-severity as a gating variable for post-infectious pathology. This paper provides an empirical counter-point: male-biased thromboinflammation risk is already detectable *below* the hospitalization severity threshold — i.e. within the group hypothesis 0004 designates as "self-resolving." This suggests that thromboinflammation risk factors (sex, thrombophilia) operate independently of severity-gating and may populate even the sub-threshold stratum.

3. **Genetic risk layering.** Factor V Leiden thrombophilia doubles VTE risk in ambulatory COVID-19 patients. Combined with male sex (aHR 1.69) and age (aHR 1.87/decade), these factors could identify high-risk ambulatory subgroups warranting prophylaxis — with potential implications for post-acute thromboinflammation as a PAIS mechanism.

4. **Vaccination protective signal.** Full vaccination reduces VTE hazard from 27.94-fold to 5.95-fold vs uninfected controls, a ~79% attenuation. Relevant to PAIS mechanism: if the acute-phase VTE storm is substantially vaccine-modifiable, so too might be downstream post-acute thromboinflammatory sequalae.

## Limitations

- **UK Biobank cohort bias:** UKBB participants are older (mean age 64) and predominantly White (~88–89%); generalizability to younger and more ethnically diverse populations, and to more recent SARS-CoV-2 variants, is uncertain.
- **VTE ascertainment from hospital records only:** Outpatient or incidentally detected DVT/PE would be missed; event counts (73 vs 17) are therefore likely underestimates of true incidence. All cases are clinically severe enough to trigger hospitalization for VTE diagnosis.
- **Small absolute event count:** 73 VTE events in 18,818 COVID-19 patients; this limits power for subgroup analyses (prothrombin G20210A is explicitly underpowered).
- **Cross-sectional genetic data + retrospective design:** Genetic thrombophilia was measured at UKBB baseline (2006–2010); however, germline variants are stable, so this is not a temporal confounding concern.
- **Acute-phase window only (30 days):** The study does not examine post-acute or long-COVID thrombotic sequelae; it characterizes the acute VTE surge. Extrapolation to chronic PAIS thromboinflammation requires additional evidence.
- **Single infection time epoch (2020–2021):** Pre-Omicron, pre-Delta-dominant variant landscape; VTE risk may differ across variants.
- **"Ambulatory" defined by testing setting, not clinical severity score:** Some outpatients may have had significant acute illness managed without hospitalization; no formal clinical severity score was applied.
- **Correction published:** A published correction exists (JAMA Intern Med. 2022 Nov 7;182(11):1234; PMC9641534); the nature of the correction is not specified in the retrieved full text.

## Follow-up

- **Correction (PMC9641534):** Check JAMA Intern Med 2022;182(11):1234 for the nature of the published correction and confirm whether it affects the key sex-VTE estimates.
- **Complement with post-acute evidence:** This study captures 30-day acute VTE only. Pair with studies examining VTE or endothelial dysfunction in post-acute/long-COVID phases to trace whether the acute thromboinflammatory insult persists.
- **Male-biased PAIS mechanisms:** Connect this sex effect to the broader literature on male-biased endothelial dysfunction, platelet hyperactivation, and coagulation differences in COVID-19 — does the aHR 1.69 for VTE magnitude correspond to the male-biased signal in PASC cardiovascular phenotypes?
- **Thrombophilia × long COVID:** Are factor V Leiden carriers at elevated risk of long-COVID thromboinflammatory phenotypes (microclots, small-fiber neuropathy, POTS) beyond the 30-day acute window?
- **Papers to read next:** CerviaHasler2024 (microclots in PASC), any sex-stratified long-COVID cardiovascular outcome studies, and the Appelman2024 muscle biopsy paper for downstream ischemic/metabolic sequelae of acute VTE.
