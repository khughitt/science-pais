# A central role for amyloid fibrin microclots in long COVID/PASC: origins and therapeutic implications

- **Authors:** Douglas B. Kell, Gert Jacobus Laubscher, Etheresia Pretorius
- **Year:** 2022
- **Journal:** Biochemical Journal, vol. 479, no. 4, pp. 537–559
- **DOI:** 10.1042/bcj20220016
- **PMID:** 35195253
- **PMCID:** PMC8883497
- **BibTeX key:** Kell2022
- **Source:** Europe PMC full text (XML, OA)

## Key Contribution

This review argues that long COVID/PASC is causally underpinned by persistent amyloid-form fibrin microclots ("fibrinaloids") that resist normal fibrinolysis, occlude capillaries, and thereby produce tissue hypoxia sufficient to explain the syndrome's multisystemic symptom burden. The central mechanistic claim is that SARS-CoV-2 spike protein (S1) directly induces fibrinogen misfolding from its native conformation into a β-sheet-rich amyloid state — without any change in primary sequence — analogous to prion-like conformational conversion. The resulting microclots (1–200 µm), detectable in platelet-poor plasma via thioflavin T fluorescence, entrap inflammatory molecules and α2-antiplasmin, are resistant to plasmin-mediated fibrinolysis, and may generate novel autoantigens driving autoantibody production. The authors propose this forms the primary lesion of long COVID and advance a "triple anticoagulation" regime as a mechanistically justified treatment approach.

**Important caveat:** This paper presents a mechanistic hypothesis with supporting observational and in-vitro evidence, not a completed causal demonstration. The microclot hypothesis is scientifically contested (see Limitations). It should be read as a hypothesis-generating review with a strong advocacy posture, not as established PAIS mechanism.

## Methods

This is a narrative/perspective review, not a primary data paper. Evidence cited includes:

- **In vitro and ex vivo microclot detection:** platelet-poor plasma (PPP) from long COVID patients, controls, and acute COVID-19 subjects, stained with thioflavin T (5 µM) and visualized by fluorescence microscopy (Zeiss Axio Observer 7, 63×/1.4 oil objective). Microclot presence is compared between patient and control PPP; sizing is by fluorescence imaging (1–200 µm range).
- **Spike-protein induction experiment:** recombinant SARS-CoV-2 S1 spike protein added to coagulation-competent normal plasma; clot morphology compared by electron microscopy and thioflavin T staining.
- **Bradford-Hill framework:** authors apply all six criteria (strength, consistency, specificity, experiment, modification, biologic plausibility) qualitatively to argue microclots → long COVID symptoms.
- **Case series (preliminary):** uncontrolled observations of "triple anticoagulation" patients (Laubscher et al., cited as reference 231) reporting symptom improvement alongside microclot clearance — no randomization, no controls, described explicitly by the authors as proof-of-concept only.
- **Comparative literature synthesis:** ME/CFS coagulopathy literature, Berg et al. (coagulopathies in ME/CFS), and prior Pretorius/Kell lab work on LPS-induced amyloid fibrin clotting (fibrinogen + bacterial LPS → dense matted deposits → amyloid staining).

The microclot detection method (thioflavin T fluorescence microscopy of PPP) is semi-quantitative and had not been independently standardized or validated at the time of publication.

## Key Findings

**Fibrinogen amyloid misfolding (mechanistic foundation):**
- Fibrinogen, normally present at 1.5–3.5 g/L in plasma, can adopt a thermodynamically more stable β-sheet amyloid conformation triggered by highly substoichiometric amounts of LPS, spike protein, or other inflammatory stimuli — analogous to prion-like conformational conversion (PrP^C → PrP^Sc model).
- The amyloid form is cross-linked by Factor XIII and heavily incorporates α2-antiplasmin, creating fibrinolysis-resistant microclots.
- Addition of purified recombinant S1 spike protein to normal plasma is sufficient to induce anomalous amyloid-form fibrin clots that resist fibrinolysis.

**Microclot characteristics in long COVID:**
- Fibrinaloid microclots (1–200 µm) are readily detectable in PPP of long COVID patients by thioflavin T fluorescence; they are largely absent or minimal in matched normal control PPP.
- Microclots are present without exogenous thrombin addition — they are already circulating in vivo.
- They entrap multiple proteins including α2-antiplasmin and (reportedly) the virus itself; their composition makes them far more resistant to plasmin-mediated clot dissolution than normal fibrin.
- S1 spike protein can persist in CD16+ monocytes in PASC for up to 15 months post-infection (cited external finding), providing a continuous trigger for ongoing microclot production from substoichiometric stimulus.

**Proposed symptom mechanism:**
- Microclots of 1–200 µm can occlude microcapillaries, restricting red blood cell passage and O₂ exchange in tissues. The authors argue this single mechanism — capillary-level hypoxia — is sufficient to explain breathlessness, fatigue, myalgia, brain fog, dysautonomia (PoTS), kidney dysfunction, neurological symptoms, and lactic acidosis.
- Ground-glass opacities on CT and SPECT perfusion defects in long COVID are cited as imaging correlates.
- Amyloid structures are directly cytotoxic via membrane disruption, adding a second injury mechanism.

**Additional co-pathologies:**
- Platelet hyperactivation forms a co-pathology; platelets interact with fibrinogen/VWF, viral particles, and damaged endothelium, driving granule secretion and hyperactivation pathways.
- Virus-induced endotheliitis (endotheliopathy) activates microthrombotic pathways and provides additional pro-coagulant stimulus.
- Autoantibodies to (conformationally altered) fibrin are noted in long COVID; the microclots may present novel epitopes not recognized as self.

**What makes long COVID long:**
- Two proposed perpetuation mechanisms: (1) persistent SARS-CoV-2 reservoirs in intracellular compartments that occasionally replicate; (2) S1 spike protein persisting in monocytes, acting as a substoichiometric trigger sufficient to amplify ongoing microclot formation.

**Bradford-Hill causation argument:**
- Authors apply all six Bradford-Hill criteria qualitatively and conclude evidence is "very strongly consistent" with microclots as the cause of long COVID symptoms. This argumentation is done narratively without formal quantitative effect-size reporting and is disputed by critics (see Limitations).

**Therapeutic implications:**
- "Triple anticoagulation" protocol: dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT: clopidogrel 75 mg + aspirin 75 mg once daily) + direct oral anticoagulant (apixaban 5 mg twice daily) + proton pump inhibitor (pantoprazole 40 mg/day) for one month, with mandatory coagulation monitoring (TEG/ROTEM) to guard against hypocoagulation.
- The rationale for combining antiplatelet + anticoagulant: addressing only platelet hyperactivation or only coagulation in isolation is predicted to fail, consistent with negative single-agent COVID-19 trials (Lawler et al. heparin trial; Lopes et al.; Viecca et al. antiplatelet trial in acute severe COVID-19).
- Preliminary (uncontrolled) observations report both microclot clearance and symptom resolution under this regime; the authors explicitly call for randomized controlled trials.
- High-efficiency extracorporeal LDL-apheresis (HELP apheresis) is mentioned as an alternative/complementary approach to physically remove microclots from circulation.
- Not recommended: fibrinolytic "clot-busters" (e.g. tPA), given the amyloid nature of microclots and that their continuous re-formation is the primary problem.

**Similarities to ME/CFS:**
- Berg et al. document coagulopathies in ME/CFS; the authors speculate fibrinaloid microclot analysis would yield positive results in ME/CFS, Gulf War syndrome, and other related post-infectious conditions, but primary ME/CFS microclot data are not presented.

## Relevance

This paper is the **canonical anchor** for the fibrinaloid/amyloid-fibrin microclot hypothesis in PAIS and directly grounds:

1. **`question:0010-vascular-microclot-subphenotype`:** Kell2022 presents the strongest advocacy for the microclot/thromboinflammatory component of the proposed vascular subphenotype. It provides the mechanistic model (spike-induced fibrinogen amyloid misfolding → fibrinolysis-resistant microclots → capillary occlusion → hypoxia) and the proposed vascular/anticoagulant treatment response prediction. The paper's contested status and its call for standardized assays and RCTs directly frame the unresolved parts of question:0010.

2. **`question:0038-netosis-released-histones-and-extracellular-dna-as-the-structural`:** While Kell2022 does not focus on NETosis or eDNA as scaffolding, it establishes the downstream amyloid-fibrin microclot target that question:0038 asks about as a nucleation mechanism. The question's proposed NETosis → histone/eDNA scaffold → fibrinaloid formation pathway extends the Kell2022 model upstream.

3. **`hypothesis:0014-nk-failure-clear-senescent-endothelium-sasp-propagation`:** Kell2022's endotheliopathy and platelet hyperactivation arm provides the sustained thromboinflammatory vascular state that h0014 proposes is maintained by NK-failure-driven SASP. The SASP effectors PAI-1 (plasminogen activator inhibitor-1) and pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, IL-8) would exacerbate fibrinolysis resistance and microclot persistence, creating a potential feedback loop between the senescent-endothelium mechanism and the coagulation arm.

4. **`hypothesis:0016-fibrinaloid-microclots-nonspecific-inflammatory-marker`:** Kell2022 is the primary paper being challenged by h0016. Kell2022 asserts microclots are causally central and long-COVID-specific; h0016 counterposes that they are a non-specific inflammatory marker. The paper's own acknowledged limitations (semi-quantitative unvalidated detection, no rigorous inflammatory controls) are the evidentiary gap that h0016 exploits.

5. **Companion paper `paper:Nicolai2023`:** Nicolai2023 is the broader thromboinflammation review that places the microclot findings in the context of endothelial dysfunction, VWF/ADAMTS-13 imbalance, platelet-neutrophil aggregates, and D-dimer trajectories. Kell2022 and Nicolai2023 together constitute the project's primary evidence anchor for the thromboinflammatory axis.

## Limitations

**Methodological / evidentiary (per the paper itself and external critique):**

- **Detection method not standardized:** Thioflavin T fluorescence microscopy of PPP is semi-quantitative, operator-dependent, and was not independently validated or inter-laboratory reproducible at the time of publication. Controls can show signal; the threshold distinguishing "normal" from "abnormal" is not objectively defined.
- **Imaging artifact concerns:** critics have raised the possibility that some fluorescent structures represent preparation artifacts (protein aggregates induced by centrifugation or staining) rather than in-vivo microclots. This has not been definitively resolved.
- **No standardized controls:** the case–control comparison does not include matched inflammatory controls (e.g., other chronic inflammatory diseases such as rheumatoid arthritis, sepsis, DIC). Without this, the claimed long-COVID specificity cannot be assessed — a key objection developed in `hypothesis:0016`.
- **Uncontrolled treatment observations:** the "triple anticoagulation" case series (Laubscher et al., their ref 231) lacks randomization, blinding, or controls. Symptom improvement cannot be attributed to microclot clearance versus time course, placebo effect, or other aspects of clinical management. The authors acknowledge this explicitly and call for RCTs.
- **Bradford-Hill framework applied qualitatively:** the paper does not report standardized effect sizes, confidence intervals, or statistical tests to support the Bradford-Hill criteria; the argumentation is narrative and strongly advocacy-driven.
- **Causation vs. correlation for microclots themselves:** even granting that microclots are elevated in long COVID, whether they are a cause of symptoms or a consequence of ongoing systemic inflammation is not resolved by the evidence presented.
- **Review scope is partisan:** all authors are from the same group that developed the fibrinaloid hypothesis and the detection assay. Independent replication of the core observations and the treatment effect is sparse at the time of publication (2022).

**Scientific context (external):**
- **Hunt2024** (see `hypothesis:0016`) conducted a critical appraisal of the microclot/thrombosis evidence base in long COVID and concluded supporting inferences are insufficient for clinical practice claims. Key concerns: non-standardized assays, absent rigorous controls, and no completed RCT demonstrating reversal of symptoms by microclot clearance.
- Microclots have been documented in sepsis, DIC, rheumatoid arthritis, and other inflammatory conditions — raising the non-specificity concern directly.
- As of mid-2026, no randomized controlled trial has confirmed that pharmacological microclot clearance reverses long-COVID symptoms in a blinded controlled setting.

**Scope limitations:**
- Paper focuses exclusively on SARS-CoV-2 / long COVID despite briefly invoking ME/CFS; no primary ME/CFS microclot data are presented.
- Pediatric long COVID, severity stratification, and the effect of vaccination on microclot burden are not addressed.
- The proposed role of autoantibodies in amplifying microclot pathology is noted but underdeveloped; the identity and epitope specificity of anti-fibrin autoantibodies are described as unknown.

## Model / Tool Availability

No computational model, dataset, or software tool is released. The fluorescence microscopy protocol (thioflavin T PPP staining, Zeiss Axio Observer 7) is described in methods sufficient for replication in principle, but the assay requires independent standardization before it can be used as a validated clinical or research tool. HELP apheresis is a clinical device-based intervention available in specialized centers, not a research tool.

## Follow-up

- **`paper:Nicolai2023`** — the broader thromboinflammation review; provides the endothelial-dysfunction and VWF/ADAMTS-13 evidence base that contextualizes the microclot findings with independent biomarker support.
- **Hunt2024** (critical appraisal of microclot/thrombosis evidence in long COVID) — essential counterpoint; directly assesses whether the Kell/Pretorius evidence base meets the bar for clinical inference. Already cited in `hypothesis:0016`.
- Laubscher et al. (the triple anticoagulation uncontrolled case series, cited as ref 231 in this paper) — the primary treatment-evidence paper; needs a separate summary to assess the clinical claims.
- STIMULATE-ICP trial results (rivaroxaban arm for long COVID, n=4,500; noted in `paper:Nicolai2023`) — the most powered test of the anticoagulant arm of the microclot hypothesis; results are directly relevant to both `question:0010` and `hypothesis:0016`.
- Pretorius et al. 2021 and 2022 (the primary laboratory microclot characterization papers from the same group) — needed to fully evaluate the detection method and the entrapped-protein findings before accepting or rejecting the analytic claims.
- Raise as research question: does the PAI-1 component of the SASP (produced by senescent endothelium in `hypothesis:0014`) directly inhibit fibrinolysis of fibrinaloid microclots, creating a mechanistic link between the NK-senescence axis and the coagulation arm? This would be a novel cross-hypothesis prediction worth formalizing.
