# Altered Lipid Metabolism in Recovered SARS Patients Twelve Years after Infection

- **Authors:** Qi Wu, Lina Zhou, Xin Sun, Zhongfang Yan, Chunxiu Hu, Junping Wu, Long Xu, Xue Li, Huiling Liu, Peiyuan Yin, Kuan Li, Jieyu Zhao, Yanli Li, Xiaolin Wang, Yu Li, Qiuyang Zhang, Guowang Xu (corr.), Huaiyong Chen (corr.)
- **Year:** 2017
- **Journal:** Scientific Reports, vol. 7, article 9110
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s41598-017-09536-z
- **PMID:** 28831119
- **PMCID:** PMC5567209
- **BibTeX key:** Wu2017
- **Source:** Full-text via science paper-fetch (DOI 10.1038/s41598-017-09536-z), read 2026-06-20

## Key Contribution

This is the only published molecular-omics study of 2003-SARS-CoV-1 survivors identified in this project. Using dual-platform serum metabolomics and lipidomics (UPLC-MS and GC-MS) in 25 recovered SARS patients versus 25 age- and sex-matched healthy controls examined 12 years after acute infection, the study identifies a persistent and highly distinctive serum lipid signature dominated by near-comprehensive elevation of phosphatidylinositol (PI) and lysophosphatidylinositol (LPI) species. Secondary analyses in steroid-treated non-SARS pneumonia patients implicate high-dose methylprednisolone given during acute illness as a probable contributor to the PI/LPI phenotype.

**Critical framing for this project:** The primary endpoint is metabolic/lipid sequelae, not a fatigue-defined post-infectious syndrome. Fatigue, cognitive symptoms, or ME/CFS-like phenotypes are not the focus and are not formally assessed; the SF-36 data show broadly reduced quality of life but are not disaggregated by symptom domain. Relevance to the PAIS fatigue attractor is therefore indirect.

## Methods

**Study design:** Cross-sectional case–control; SARS survivors (n = 25, 5 M / 20 F, mean age 47 years, mean BMI 24 kg/m²) from the 2002–2003 Tianjin outbreak, recruited in 2014 (~12 years post-infection). Controls: 25 age- and sex-matched healthy medical-staff volunteers from the same hospital. All subjects fasted overnight; serum and urine samples were collected.

**Omics platforms:**
- *LC-MS metabolic profiling*: UPLC (Waters) coupled to TripleTOF 5600+ (AB Sciex); BEH C8 column; positive-ion mode; gradient elution.
- *GC-MS metabolic profiling*: Shimadzu GCMS-QP 2010 Plus; DB-5 MS capillary column; pseudotargeted SIM mode; standard oximation/silylation derivatization.
- *Lipidomics*: UPLC (Waters) + triple-quadrupole TOF (AB Sciex); C8 ACQUITY column; both positive and negative ion modes; m/z 400–1500.

**QC:** Pooled QC samples run every 10 injections; 80% rule for missing-value filtering; local linear regression normalization to adjacent QC intensities; peak areas normalized to class-matched internal standards (lipidomics).

**Statistics:** Mann-Whitney U tests; FDR correction (q-value, threshold q < 0.20); PLS-DA / OPLS-DA score plots (multivariate separation); hierarchical cluster analysis; Pearson correlation for inter-lipid associations.

**Steroid sub-study:** 23 non-SARS patients with severe pneumonia who received methylprednisolone (301 ± 235 mg over ~9 days) versus 23 non-SARS pneumonia patients without steroid treatment; serum PI profiling as secondary experiment to test steroid confound.

**Clinical questionnaire:** SF-36 health survey; symptom/hospitalization history since SARS recovery; Harris Hip Score; 6-min walk test; pulmonary ventilation function; routine biochemistry (lipid panel, glucose, thyroid function, CBC, urine).

**Case definition:** PCR/clinical-confirmed SARS-CoV-1 infection, 2002–2003 outbreak; "recovered" = clinically discharged. All SARS patients had received methylprednisolone (mean cumulative dose 5,454 ± 3,153 mg over 36 ± 7 days; max daily pulse 381 ± 306 mg).

## Key Findings

**Clinical profile at 12 years (SARS vs. controls):**
- Hyperlipidemia: 68% vs. 40%
- Cardiovascular abnormality: 44% vs. 0%
- Abnormal glucose metabolism: 60% vs. 16%
- Osteonecrosis: 60% vs. 0%
- Lung infection since recovery: 64% vs. 12%
- Re-hospitalization: 48% vs. 12%
- Elevated TG: 44% vs. 16%; elevated VLDL-C: 16% vs. 0%
- Urine red blood cells: 33% vs. 4%
- SF-36: broadly reduced across most domains

**Serum metabolome (PLS-DA/OPLS-DA):** Clear separation of SARS from controls in serum (no significant differences in urine).

**Upregulated in SARS:** myo-inositol phosphate, pyroglutamic acid, lactic acid, succinic acid, cysteine, alanine, aspartic acid, sarcosine, spermidine, proline, histidine, long-chain acylcarnitines (C18:0, C18-OH), 2,3,4-trihydroxybutyric acid, fructose.

**Downregulated in SARS:** free carnitine, arginine, citrulline, methyl esters (9,12-octadecadienoic acid, hexadecanoic acid).

**Increased acylcarnitine/free carnitine ratio [(C16+C18)/C0]** suggests enhanced mitochondrial fatty-acid beta-oxidation flux.

**Lipidome — the dominant signal:** Near-comprehensive elevation of PI and LPI species:
- All 3 detected LPI species elevated: LPI 18:0 (ratio 2.13, p < 0.001), LPI 18:2 (ratio 1.75, p < 0.001), LPI 20:4 (ratio 1.74, p < 0.001).
- 12 of 14 detected PI species elevated (PIs 34:1–40:6; ratios ~1.36–1.78, most p < 0.001).
- Elevated LPCs (18:2, 20:2, 21:3, 22:6) and LPEs (18:1, 22:6).
- Decreased sphingomyelins (SM 34:1;3, 36:0;2, 37:4;2, 38:0;2) and reduced SM/(SM+PC) ratio.
- PI levels positively correlated with VLDL and total TG, suggesting upstream link to de novo PI synthesis.
- LPI/PI ratios and FFA 20:4 pattern consistent with elevated phospholipase A2 (PLA2) activity on PI substrate, with released arachidonic acid (FFA 20:4) channeled toward eicosanoid synthesis.

**Steroid sub-study:** Methylprednisolone-treated non-SARS pneumonia patients showed significantly higher serum PI levels vs. untreated pneumonia controls (14 PI species elevated, ratios 1.34–3.16, p < 0.001 to 0.022). Rat methylprednisolone injection experiment did not replicate the PI elevation, suggesting the steroid–PI relationship in humans is more complex.

**Interpretation by authors:** PI/LPI elevation may partly reflect acute treatment effects rather than (or in addition to) direct SARS-CoV-1 pathology; LPI-GPR55 signaling proposed as contributor to glucose dysregulation.

## Relevance

**Cross-pathogen PAIS signature matrix:** This paper fills the post-SARS cell of the matrix (discussion:0002-cross-pathogen-pais-signature-convergence). It is currently the only molecular-omics study of SARS-CoV-1 survivors in the project. The PI/LPI elevation has not been reported as a dominant metabolomic signature in long-COVID or ME/CFS lipidomics to date, making this both a potential distinguishing feature and a cautionary data point: SARS-CoV-1 acute treatment (high-dose methylprednisolone) differs sharply from standard SARS-CoV-2 management, complicating direct comparison.

**Shared molecular signatures (question:0001-shared-molecular-signature-across-triggers):** The broad metabolic disruption — acylcarnitine imbalance, altered TCA-cycle intermediates, elevated glycolysis markers, arginine/urea-cycle perturbation — overlaps qualitatively with metabolic findings in ME/CFS and long COVID. However, the PI/LPI signal is pronounced and drug-confounded in a way not seen in other PAIS studies; it should be interpreted as a hypothesis-generating data point rather than confirmed cross-pathogen signature.

**Metabolic/mitochondrial dysfunction mechanism:** Acylcarnitine accumulation and altered free carnitine parallel findings in ME/CFS (e.g., impaired fatty-acid oxidation or mitochondrial entry) but the direction differs from some ME/CFS reports — here acylcarnitines are elevated alongside increased beta-oxidation rate, which may reflect a different physiological state.

**Fatigue relevance is indirect:** The paper does not phenotype fatigue, cognitive symptoms, or post-exertional malaise. SF-36 shows reduced QoL but domains are not separately reported in the paper body. This study cannot be used to directly support a fatigue-attractor argument for post-SARS syndrome.

## Limitations

1. **Glucocorticoid confound (critical):** All 25 SARS patients received very high-dose methylprednisolone (mean 5,454 mg cumulative over 36 days). No SARS-without-steroid control arm exists. The steroid sub-study in non-SARS pneumonia patients shows PI elevation with much lower doses (301 mg), strongly suggesting the PI/LPI signal is partly or wholly drug-related rather than a post-viral immune-metabolic signature. This confound cannot be resolved in the dataset.

2. **Small sample, cross-sectional design:** n = 25; no pre-infection baseline metabolomics; no longitudinal trajectory. Causation and timing of metabolic alterations cannot be established.

3. **No fatigue phenotyping:** The study was not designed to characterize ME/CFS-like PAIS. SF-36 shows reduced QoL but fatigue, PEM, cognitive symptoms, and sleep disturbance are not systematically assessed or reported by domain.

4. **Medication confounds beyond steroids:** The paper notes some patients with glucose metabolic disorders may have been on medication at time of sampling; this was not controlled.

5. **Case definition and cohort specificity:** All patients are from a single Tianjin hospital, predominantly female (80%), with confirmed SARS-CoV-1 infection; findings may not generalize to post-COVID or other PAIS populations, or even to SARS survivors treated with different protocols.

6. **Statistical power and FDR threshold:** q < 0.20 is a liberal FDR threshold; with n = 25 per group and multiple metabolite tests, false positives among the less-significant hits are probable.

7. **No immune profiling:** The study is purely metabolomic/lipidomic — no cytokines, T-cell phenotyping, autoantibodies, or viral-persistence markers. The immune mechanism driving metabolic changes (if viral-intrinsic) is completely uncharacterized.

8. **Rat steroid experiment was negative:** Intraperitoneal methylprednisolone in rats did not elevate PI, complicating the drug-effect interpretation. Species differences or route/dose differences leave the mechanism unresolved.

## Model / Tool Availability

No computational model or tool is released. Metabolomics and lipidomics raw data are not deposited in a public repository (no accession number reported in the paper). Platforms used: UPLC-TripleTOF 5600+ (AB Sciex / Waters); GCMS-QP 2010 Plus (Shimadzu); Lipid-View software (AB Sciex); Peak-View software; Multi Experiment Viewer (MeV); KEGG pathway annotation.

## Follow-up

- **Resolve the steroid confound:** Are there any SARS-CoV-1 survivor cohorts treated without high-dose corticosteroids? This would be the only clean test of virus-intrinsic vs. drug-induced metabolic sequelae.
- **Compare PI/LPI to long-COVID lipidomics:** Does the same PI elevation signature appear in SARS-CoV-2 survivors (who largely did not receive high-dose methylprednisolone)? Absence would support the drug-effect interpretation; presence would support virus-intrinsic pathophysiology.
- **Fatigue phenotyping in SARS survivors:** No study in the project database has prospectively characterized fatigue, PEM, or ME/CFS criteria in SARS-CoV-1 survivors. This remains a gap (see question:0001-shared-molecular-signature-across-triggers).
- **Arginine/NO pathway:** Reduced arginine + citrulline in SARS survivors may link to endothelial dysfunction and NO dysregulation — a mechanism proposed in long COVID (thromboinflammation, dysautonomia). Cross-pathogen comparison warranted.
- **Acylcarnitine pattern:** Elevated long-chain acylcarnitines with depleted free carnitine echo findings in ME/CFS (Norman et al.; Germain et al.). A cross-study quantitative comparison would strengthen the metabolic-mechanism column of the PAIS signature matrix.
