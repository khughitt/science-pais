# Post-Acute COVID-19 Joint Pain and New Onset of Rheumatic Musculoskeletal Diseases: A Systematic Review

- **Authors:** Jacopo Ciaffi, Elena Vanni, Luana Mancarella, Veronica Brusi, Lucia Lisi, Federica Pignatti, Susanna Naldi, Elisa Assirelli, Simona Neri, Massimo Reta, Cesare Faldini, Francesco Ursini
- **Year:** 2023
- **Journal:** Diagnostics, vol. 13, no. 11, p. 1850
- **DOI:** 10.3390/diagnostics13111850
- **BibTeX key:** Ciaffi2023
- **Source:** PDF

## Key Contribution

This PRISMA 2020-compliant systematic review synthesises 54 original studies (literature searched to October 1, 2022) on post-acute COVID-19 musculoskeletal manifestations of rheumatological interest, focusing on three outcomes: arthralgia prevalence, new-onset inflammatory rheumatic musculoskeletal diseases (RMDs), and post-COVID autoantibody profiles (rheumatoid factor [RF] and anti-citrullinated protein antibodies [ACPA]). The review establishes that joint pain is among the most common persistent complaints after SARS-CoV-2 infection (prevalence 2–65% across studies), that clinically heterogeneous new-onset inflammatory arthritis and polymyalgia rheumatica (PMR)-like presentations are documented, and that 31–40% of post-COVID patients meet classification criteria for fibromyalgia. It frames SARS-CoV-2 as a trigger for autoimmune loss of tolerance through mechanisms including molecular mimicry, bystander activation, and epitope spreading — the same mechanisms implicated by other viral arthritides — while noting that RF and ACPA evidence remains largely inconsistent across studies.

## Methods

- **Design:** Systematic review following PRISMA 2020 guidelines; no meta-analysis performed due to heterogeneity in post-COVID definitions, follow-up windows, and outcome ascertainment.
- **Databases:** MEDLINE (via PubMed) and Web of Science (WOS), searched up to October 1, 2022. 11,602 records identified (PubMed: 3,933; WOS: 7,669); after deduplication 8,835 unique records screened; 188 full-texts assessed; 54 studies included.
- **Case definition used:** Post-acute COVID-19 defined as persistent or delayed-onset joint pain or new onset of inflammatory rheumatic musculoskeletal conditions at least 4 weeks after SARS-CoV-2 infection. For joint pain studies, minimum cohort size of 100 patients required.
- **Study types included:** Non-randomized studies and case series (≥5 cases); English language; peer-reviewed full-text publications.
- **Quality appraisal:** Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS) for 47 non-randomized observational studies (18 good, 24 fair, 5 poor quality); Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) critical appraisal for 7 case series.
- **Outcomes extracted:** First author; year; country; study design; sample size; time between COVID-19 and RMD onset; prevalence of arthralgia, inflammatory arthritis, fibromyalgia, RF, or ACPA.
- **Geographic distribution:** 23 European, 19 Asian, 5 South/Central American, 4 African, 2 North American, 1 multi-country (UK/USA/other).

## Key Findings

**Joint pain (arthralgia) — 39 studies:**
- Prevalence ranged widely from 2% to 65%, with follow-up time frames of 4 weeks to 12 months post-infection.
- 7 studies (N = 460,716) reported <10% prevalence; the largest was a UK population-based study (N = 456,002) finding 2–3% at ≥4 weeks.
- 10 studies (N = 9,756) found 10–19%; 11 studies (N = 28,303) found 20–29%; 4 studies (N = 7,058) found 30–39%; 7 studies (N = 1,748) found ≥40%.
- In a U.S. Veterans Affairs electronic health record study (N = 73,435 non-hospitalized COVID-19 patients vs. ~5 million unexposed controls), arthralgia had an excess hazard ratio of 5.16 (95% CI 3.18–7.01), the highest reported adjusted estimate in the review.
- Arthralgia was one of the most common persistent post-COVID symptoms in the Wang et al. EHR cohort (21% of 26,117 patients).

**Inflammatory arthritis — 8 studies:**
- Multiple distinct clinical phenotypes reported: symmetrical polyarthritis with RA-like pattern (wrists/hands small joints), monoarthritis or oligoarthritis of large joints resembling reactive arthritis, and PMR-like presentations with predominantly axial involvement.
- Ursini et al. (N = 23; mean 33 days post-COVID): 15 patients with asymmetric mono/oligoarthritis, 3 PMR-like, 2 RA-like polyarthritis, 2 enthesitis, 1 predominantly axial involvement.
- Derksen et al. (Netherlands; N = 61 ACPA-tested + Dutch rheumatology clinics): 5 new RA-like polyarthritis cases; 4 of 5 fulfilled RA classification criteria at ~7 weeks post-COVID; no new ACPA positivity detected.
- Taha et al. (Egypt; N = 100, excluding pre-existing RMDs): arthritis (not further specified) prevalence 37% at 6 months — the highest reported — with knee (72%), ankle (67%), and wrist (50%) most commonly affected.
- Maestre-Muniz et al. (Spain; N = 543): 13 (2%) developed new inflammatory arthritis at 12 months.

**Fibromyalgia — 2 studies:**
- Italian web-based cross-sectional survey (N = 616): 31% fulfilled fibromyalgia classification criteria at mean 6 months post-COVID; strongest predictors were obesity and male sex.
- Swedish online survey (N = 100): 40% met 2016 fibromyalgia criteria after mean 47 weeks; 22% reported being healthy before COVID-19.

**Autoantibodies (RF and ACPA) — 7 studies:**
- Results were largely inconsistent across studies, precluding firm conclusions.
- Derksen et al.: 0 new ACPA positivity in 61 post-COVID patients (excluding 2 known ACPA+ RA).
- Ursini et al. (N = 23 inflammatory arthritis): none positive for RF or ACPA.
- Acosta-Ampudia et al. (Colombia; N = 33): RF/ACPA frequency did not differ from healthy controls at median 266 days post-COVID.
- Lingel et al. (Germany; N = 68): ACPA elevated in convalescents compared to unexposed donors, still elevated at 8 months; RF did not differ.
- Taha et al. (Egypt; N = 100): 19% RF-positive; 39% ACPA-positive — highest reported, but in a cohort without prior RMD exclusion consistently applied.
- Xu et al. (China; N = 129): RF detected in 20% of hospitalised patients; RF titres peaked later in disease and persisted.

**Other musculoskeletal findings:**
- 5 cases of septic arthritis with avascular necrosis of the femoral head (mean 42 days post-COVID); FDG-PET in 13 post-COVID patients showed radiotracer uptake in skeletal muscle in 8 (62%), compatible with myositis.

**Proposed autoimmune mechanisms (Discussion):**
- Molecular mimicry: SARS-CoV-2 spike protein epitopes cross-reacting with host self-antigens.
- Bystander activation: nonspecific over-reactive antiviral immune response releasing self-antigens and cytokines from damaged tissue, activating autoreactive T cells via APCs.
- Epitope spreading: perpetuation of tissue damage releasing new self-antigens activating additional autoreactive T cells.
- NETosis: formation of neutrophilic extracellular traps promoting humoral autoimmunity against intra-nuclear antigens.
- Immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome: paradoxical autoimmunity following late immunosuppressant withdrawal.

## Relevance

This review directly addresses the PAIS project research question (research-question:post-acute-infection-syndromes) by characterising the musculoskeletal dimension of failed homeostatic recovery after SARS-CoV-2 infection. Key connections to the PAIS frame:

1. **Shared post-infectious failure mode:** Arthralgia at 2–65% prevalence and fibromyalgia at 31–40% in the months following acute COVID-19 are precisely the kind of persistent, multi-domain symptom burden that defines the PAIS phenotype of incomplete recovery. These are not self-limiting post-viral aches but clinically categorisable rheumatic entities — RA, PMR, reactive arthritis — emerging de novo after infection.

2. **Immune dysregulation as the driver:** The mechanistic discussion explicitly situates post-COVID RMD in the framework of virus-induced loss of immune tolerance, invoking molecular mimicry, bystander activation, and epitope spreading — all established immune-homeostasis failure modes that the project tracks across pathogens. The inconsistency in ACPA/RF data suggests that classical antibody-mediated autoimmunity may not be the primary driver in all cases, pointing to alternative immune-activation pathways.

3. **Persistence of autoantibodies:** The Lingel et al. finding that ACPA remain elevated 8 months post-COVID in convalescents (compared to unexposed donors) is consistent with the antigen-persistence and chronic immune activation hypotheses central to PAIS.

4. **Non-autoimmune pain phenotypes:** Fibromyalgia at 31–40% suggests a nociplastic or central sensitisation component that parallels ME/CFS pathophysiology and extends the PAIS frame beyond purely autoimmune mechanisms to include dysregulation of pain processing — relevant to the project's dysautonomia and metabolic dysfunction sub-themes.

5. **Acute myalgia as risk factor:** The observation (Discussion) that myalgia during acute SARS-CoV-2 hospitalisation predicts persistent musculoskeletal sequelae aligns with the PAIS hypothesis that acute-phase inflammatory severity shapes long-term recovery trajectory.

## Limitations

1. **High heterogeneity, no meta-analysis possible:** Post-COVID definitions varied across studies (first symptom onset vs. first positive swab vs. hospital discharge), follow-up windows ranged from 4 weeks to 12 months, and outcome ascertainment differed substantially — precluding quantitative synthesis. Reported prevalences cannot be meaningfully pooled.
2. **Absence of unexposed control groups in most studies:** Without matched controls, the background prevalence of arthralgia in non-COVID populations cannot be reliably subtracted, inflating apparent post-COVID attribution. The Al-Aly VA study is a notable exception.
3. **Vaccination status not systematically reported:** COVID-19 vaccination has been proposed to both trigger RMD (case series of PMR and inflammatory arthritis) and reduce long-COVID risk — this confound is uncontrolled in most included studies.
4. **Selection bias in case ascertainment:** Many studies used self-reported symptoms (online surveys), rheumatology clinic referrals, or hospitalised cohorts, limiting generalisability to the broader post-COVID population.
5. **Short follow-up in many studies:** The longest follow-ups were 12 months; longer natural history data needed to distinguish truly new chronic RMDs from transient post-viral inflammation.
6. **RF/ACPA inconsistency:** Results on autoantibody seroprevalence are contradictory across studies, likely reflecting differences in timing of sampling, assay methodology, and whether patients with pre-existing RMDs were excluded. No conclusions about autoantibody-driven RMD risk are defensible at this stage.
7. **Comorbidities and medications as confounders:** Pre-existing musculoskeletal pain, immunosuppressant use, and corticosteroid treatment — all strongly associated with COVID-19 severity — are potential confounders incompletely addressed across included studies.
8. **Fibromyalgia data limited to 2 studies, both using online surveys:** Self-report bias inflates prevalence; clinical validation with structured rheumatological assessment is lacking.

## Follow-up

- **Mechanistic follow-up:** Studies investigating whether SARS-CoV-2 spike protein peptides cross-react with citrullinated self-antigens (molecular mimicry for RA) or synovial antigens (reactive arthritis pathway) — to move from observed associations to mechanism.
- **Longitudinal follow-up:** Studies with ≥2-year follow-up distinguishing transient post-viral arthritis from established autoimmune RMD (RA, PMR, SpA) fulfilling classification criteria at later timepoints.
- **Fibromyalgia mechanism:** Studies comparing post-COVID fibromyalgia to post-Lyme, post-EBV, and post-Chikungunya fibromyalgia/central sensitisation to assess whether this is a generic post-infection nociplastic phenotype vs. COVID-specific.
- **ACPA dynamics study:** The Lingel et al. finding of persistent ACPA elevation at 8 months warrants replication in larger cohorts with matched controls and serial sampling — critical for determining whether post-COVID ACPA represent stable autoimmune priming vs. transient cross-reactive responses.
- **Related papers in project:** Choutka et al. (paper:Choutka2022) provides the broader cross-pathogen PAIS framing; Bai et al. (paper:Bai2023) provides the PTLDS/ME-CFS convergence data — together these establish the immune failure-mode context within which post-COVID RMD sits.
