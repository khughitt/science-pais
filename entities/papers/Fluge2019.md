# B-Lymphocyte Depletion in Patients With Myalgic Encephalomyelitis/Chronic Fatigue Syndrome: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial

- **Authors:** Øystein Fluge, Ingrid G. Rekeland, Katarina Lien, Hanne Thürmer, Petter C. Borchgrevink, Christoph Schäfer, Kari Sørland, Jörg Aßmus, Irini Ktoridou-Valen, Ingrid Herder, Merethe E. ## Key Contribution

RituxME is the definitive Phase III randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (DBPCT) of rituximab (anti-CD20 B-cell depletion) in ME/CFS. It found **no benefit** of rituximab over placebo on either the primary fatigue-based response endpoint or 24-month fatigue trajectory, directly contradicting the promising Phase II open-label signals from the same Bergen group (Fluge & Mella 2011, 2015). As the largest and most rigorously controlled ME/CFS intervention trial to date at the time of publication, it is the canonical cautionary example of how uncontrolled trials can mislead: a high placebo response rate (35%) matched and exceeded the rituximab response rate (26%), explaining the earlier open-label apparent benefit. It does not disprove B-cell or autoimmunity involvement in ME/CFS, but it conclusively refutes rituximab as a monotherapy at the doses and schedule tested.

## Methods

**Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group, multicenter Phase 3 trial (5 Norwegian hospitals: 4 university hospitals + 1 general hospital). Registered as NCT02229942 / EudraCT 2014-000795-25 (RituxME). Recruitment September 2014 – September 2015; follow-up completed September 2017.

**Population:** 152 patients randomly assigned (ITT population: n = 77 rituximab, n = 74 placebo; 151 commenced treatment). Inclusion: ME/CFS per **Canadian Consensus Criteria (CCC)**, age 18–65, disease duration ≥2 years (≥5 years if severity was mild) and <15 years. Patients with very severe disease were excluded. ~76% had a reported infection before ME/CFS onset. Mean disease duration 8.4 years (rituximab arm) / 7.6 years (placebo arm).

**Intervention:** Induction — 2 infusions of rituximab 500 mg/m² BSA (max 1000 mg), 2 weeks apart. Maintenance — 4 infusions of rituximab at a **fixed dose of 500 mg** at months 3, 6, 9, and 12. Placebo: matched saline + human albumin (0.4 mg/mL) to ensure visual indistinguishability. All patients received premedication (acetaminophen 1 g, cetirizine 10 mg, dexamethasone 8 mg) 1 hour before each infusion. Total active infusion period: 12 months; follow-up: 24 months.

**Primary endpoints (co-primary; Bonferroni-adjusted significance threshold α = 0.025 for each):**
1. **Overall response rate** — proportion of patients achieving fatigue score ≥4.5 (on a 0–6 scale, where 3 = no change from baseline) for ≥8 consecutive weeks at any point over 24 months.
2. **Repeated fatigue score over 24 months** — between-group difference in mean fatigue score averaged across 4-month intervals through 24 months, assessed by general linear model for repeated measures.

**Secondary endpoints:** Repeated measurements of self-reported function level (% of healthy), SF-36-PF, SF-36-PCS, Fatigue Severity Scale over 24 months; change from baseline to 18 months in SF-36-PF, function level, steps/24 h; longest duration of lasting clinical response.

**Follow-up duration:** 24 months post-randomization.

## Key Findings

**Primary endpoint 1 — overall response rate:** Placebo 35.1% (26/74; 95% CI 25.2%–46.5%) vs. rituximab 26.0% (20/77; 95% CI 17.5%–36.7%). Difference: 9.2 pp favoring placebo (95% CI −5.5 to 23.3 pp). Mantel-Haenszel common OR 0.65 (95% CI 0.32–1.30); P = 0.22. Not significant. (Rituximab was numerically worse.)

**Primary endpoint 2 — repeated fatigue score over 24 months:** Between-group difference in average fatigue score 0.02 (95% CI −0.27 to 0.31); P = 0.80. No difference.

**Secondary endpoints:** No significant differences on any secondary outcome. Specific values (rituximab vs. placebo, 18-month change from baseline): SF-36-PF difference −0.41 (95% CI −7.73 to 6.92; P = 0.67); function level difference −0.68 (95% CI −5.90 to 4.54; P = 0.31); SF-36-PCS difference −0.21 (95% CI −3.18 to 2.77; P = 0.27); Fatigue Severity Scale difference −0.07 (95% CI −3.21 to 3.08; P = 0.68); mean steps/24 h difference −127 (95% CI −1004 to 749; P = 0.58).

**Timing of response:** Median time to first clinical response (fatigue score ≥4.5 for ≥8 consecutive weeks) was 41 weeks among the 46 patients who fulfilled the response criterion. First clinical response was recorded before 18 weeks in 25% of responders and after 12 months in 25%. Time lags were similar in both arms.

**Clinical course during follow-up:** Stable symptoms reported by 90 patients (51 rituximab, 39 placebo). Sustained worsening for ≥12 weeks in 15 patients (7.8% rituximab, 12.2% placebo). Regardless of treatment group, 30% of patients reported some degree of improvement.

**Blinding integrity:** Only 12.6% of included patients correctly guessed their intervention at 6 weeks, suggesting effective blinding.

**Safety:** Adverse events of CTCAE grade ≥2 occurred in 81.8% (rituximab) vs. 64.9% (placebo) — reflecting high symptom burden in ME/CFS patients rather than drug-specific toxicity. Serious adverse events (SAEs) in 26.0% (20 patients, 31 events) of the rituximab group vs. 18.9% (14 patients, 16 events) of the placebo group; the placebo count includes 1 unintended pregnancy. All SAEs except the pregnancy were hospital admissions. SAEs with possible/probable relation to intervention: 8 (10.4%) rituximab vs. 7 (0.5%) placebo. Two late-onset neutropenia events in the rituximab group required hospitalization for IV antibiotics. Infusion-related reactions: 10 (13.0%) rituximab vs. 0 placebo. Three suspected unexpected serious adverse reactions: 1 metrorrhagia with hysterectomy + 1 suspected but unconfirmed coronary artery disease in the rituximab group; 1 transient paresis of left extremities in the placebo group. **No deaths in either group.**

**Conclusion (authors'):** "B-cell depletion using several infusions of rituximab over 12 months was not associated with clinical improvement in patients with ME/CFS."

**The placebo response illuminates the prior open-label results.** The 35.1% placebo response rate across 24 months of close clinical attention substantially exceeds historical estimates (pooled CFS placebo response ~19.6% in a meta-analysis cited by the authors). It plausibly accounts for the 30–50% response rates seen in the earlier uncontrolled Phase II studies. The authors specifically note that only 12.6% of patients correctly guessed their intervention, so selective expectations cannot fully explain it; natural symptom variation over time and selection/attention effects are the most parsimonious explanations. This is the trial's most important methodological lesson for the field.

## Relevance

**For `hypothesis:0001` (shared dysregulated attractor):** The RituxME null directly supports the attractor hypothesis's core prediction — *single-target interventions fail*. If ME/CFS were driven by a straightforward B-cell-mediated autoimmune effector mechanism (a single, linearly addressable pathophysiological node), adequate B-cell depletion should have worked. Its failure, despite near-complete CD20+ B-cell depletion for months, argues against a simple autonomous B-cell loop and is consistent with a multi-loop self-sustaining state that does not depend on peripheral B-cells as a sole driver. This is direct empirical pressure on the narrower "ME/CFS as a classical autoimmune disease" reading.

**For `topic:biomarkers-and-objective-endpoints`:** RituxME is a master-class in endpoint design. The primary endpoint — a sustained fatigue threshold — was clinically meaningful but subjective and susceptible to placebo/attention effects. The 35% placebo rate demonstrates that for ME/CFS trials, subjective fatigue-based endpoints alone cannot serve as reliable primary outcomes. The field urgently needs objective, biological endpoints (e.g. post-exertional physiological markers, serum immune signatures, autonomic measures) to protect against inflated placebo signal. This null anchors every subsequent discussion of trial methodology in ME/CFS.

**For `topic:post-infectious-dysautonomia-and-autoimmunity`:** RituxME tested one specific mechanistic hypothesis — that pathogenic IgG autoantibodies (hypothesized to target beta-adrenergic or muscarinic receptors, or other autonomic/vascular targets) sustain ME/CFS and would be cleared by B-cell depletion. The null weakens this specific IgG-autoantibody-as-sufficient-cause hypothesis, though it does not rule out autoantibodies as one component of a multi-loop system, or the possibility that the maintenance schedule was not long enough to clear long-lived plasma cells.

**Therapeutic task context (t004):** RituxME is the definitive cautionary anchor for therapeutic evaluation in ME/CFS. It should be the first reference considered when evaluating any new uncontrolled ME/CFS trial result. The pattern it established — promising open-label → placebo-controlled null — has since recurred (e.g. some microclot/anticoagulation open-label claims). The lesson is not that the mechanism is wrong, but that uncontrolled evidence in ME/CFS is insufficient for mechanistic conclusions.

## Limitations

- **Endpoint subjectivity.** The primary endpoint is patient-reported fatigue; no objective physiological or biomarker endpoint was primary. The 35.1% placebo response — substantially above the ~19.6% pooled historical estimate — strongly suggests that attention, expectation, and care-protocol effects substantially contaminate the signal. The authors themselves list this as the trial's stated limitation ("self-reported primary outcome measures and possible recall bias").
- **Long-lived plasma cells.** Rituximab depletes CD20+ B-cells but not CD20− long-lived plasma cells, which may sustain autoantibody titers indefinitely. The trial did not measure autoantibody titers; no autoantibody sub-study results are reported in this paper. [UNVERIFIED: whether a separate autoantibody sub-study was conducted and published].
- **Lower rituximab dose than prior open-label maintenance study.** The paper explicitly notes that maintenance doses in RituxME were 50%–60% lower than in the earlier open-label maintenance study (Fluge 2015). The authors argue this cannot explain the lack of early-phase responses (which should be dose-independent), but it remains a residual methodological difference.
- **Population heterogeneity.** Despite CCC, ME/CFS is clinically heterogeneous; a subset with true B-cell-mediated autoimmunity might have responded but been diluted. No pre-specified subgroup analyses by autoantibody status, onset type, or severity tier were conducted — the authors call for future subgroup analyses.
- **Single-country, hospital-recruited sample.** Norwegian academic hospital recruitment may not represent ME/CFS across severity or healthcare-access strata.
- **No biomarker arm.** Unlike a platform trial, RituxME has no embedded biomarker sub-study to identify responders prospectively or to confirm B-cell depletion depth. The absence of B-cell depletion confirmation (e.g. flow cytometry serial measurements) is not addressed in the paper. This forecloses mechanistic interpretation of the null.
- **Statistical power assumption vs. realized rates.** The trial was powered assuming 50% response in rituximab and 25% in placebo (N=152 for 80% power). The actual placebo rate (35.1%) far exceeded assumptions; had the true placebo rate been known, a larger N would have been required to detect any residual drug-specific benefit.

## Follow-up

- **Fluge & Mella 2011, 2015** (earlier open-label Phase II rituximab ME/CFS studies from same group) — reading these alongside RituxME illustrates the open-label→RCT reversal pattern in detail.
- **Scheibenbogen et al.** — German work on beta-2-adrenergic and M3/M4-muscarinic autoantibodies in ME/CFS; addresses the specific autoantibody hypothesis RituxME was designed around without resolving whether those antibodies require B-cell depletion to clear [UNVERIFIED: current status of that work].
- **BC007** (anti-autoantibody aptamer, Germany) — ongoing trial targeting functional autoantibodies more directly without systemic B-cell depletion; represents the next-generation test of the same mechanistic hypothesis.
- **Hoel2026** — the largest ME/CFS serum proteome; does not find a clear antibody-effector signature, providing independent null pressure on the autoantibody-dominant model.
- **Open question:** Could a rituximab trial with (a) prospective autoantibody enrichment, (b) objective biomarker primary endpoints, and (c) longer follow-up produce a different result in a defined subgroup? This is the residual mechanistic question RituxME leaves open.
- **Cross-reference `topic:biomarkers-and-objective-endpoints`:** RituxME should be cited as the trial-design cautionary case study in any discussion of ME/CFS or PAIS endpoint selection.
