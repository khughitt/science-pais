# The Burden of Post-Acute Sequelae of Coronavirus Disease 2019 in Individuals with Rheumatic Diseases

- **Authors:** Shruthi Srivatsan, Naomi J. Patel
- **Year:** 2025 (published online October 2024)
- **Journal:** Rheumatic Disease Clinics of North America, vol. 51 no. 1, pp. 15–28
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.rdc.2024.08.003
- **PMID:** 39550103
- **BibTeX key:** Srivatsan2025
- **Source:** Europe PMC abstract + PMC full text of primary studies (triangulation); review is paywalled (Elsevier, no OA copy via Unpaywall)

## Key Contribution

Narrative review synthesizing the evidence on PASC burden in patients with systemic autoimmune rheumatic diseases (SARDs / ARDs / IRDs), written by members of the Mass General Brigham group that produced several of the key primary studies (Patel NJ is senior/co-author on at least three primary papers cited). The review frames the exposure question — pre-existing rheumatic disease as a risk modifier for PASC — and identifies the main factors (infection severity, female sex, comorbidities, immunosuppressive medications) while flagging that PASC diagnosis in this population is complicated by symptom overlap with the underlying rheumatic condition. It does not conduct pooled analysis; it is a pointer to the primary cohort literature.

## Methods

Narrative literature review (no formal systematic search reported, no meta-analytic pooling) [INACCESSIBLE — full methods section paywalled]. Synthesizes primary studies published through mid-2024, with particular reliance on four cohorts / data sources:

1. **COVAD Study** — Sen et al. 2023 (Lancet Rheumatol): international electronic survey (N = 1,677 with confirmed SARS-CoV-2; 755 ARD, 713 healthy controls), WHO PASC definition (symptoms ≥3 months).
2. **Netherlands IRD cohort** — Boekel et al. 2023 (Lancet Rheumatol): prospective cohort (N = 1,974 IRD + 733 age/sex-matched healthy controls), PCR-confirmed, PASC = persistent symptoms ≥8 weeks within 3 months of infection.
3. **GRA Vaccine Survey** — DiIorio et al. 2022 (RMD Open): cross-sectional survey (N = 441 SARDs with confirmed COVID-19), dual CDC/WHO definitions (≥28 days; ≥90 days), no external control.
4. **Mass General Brigham EHR cohort** — Patel NJ et al. 2024 (J Rheumatol): retrospective cohort (SARDs only), EHR-based ICD-10/lab/vital-sign PASC definition ≥30 days post-infection, no external control.

Additional primary inputs include Patel NJ et al. 2022 (Ann Rheum Dis) on vaccination and PASC, and Leon et al. 2022 (Rheumatol Adv Pract) on post-discharge symptoms.

## Key Findings

[Core content from abstract; numerical details triangulated from primary papers cited by the review. All numerical claims carry the primary-paper source in parentheses.]

- **Elevated PASC risk in ARD/SARD patients**: Compared to healthy controls, ARD patients were approximately twice as likely to develop PASC under WHO definition (OR ~2.1; COVAD/Sen 2023). The Netherlands IRD cohort found nominally higher rates (IRD 21% vs. controls 13%; unadjusted OR 1.73) but the association attenuated after covariate adjustment (aOR 1.53, p = 0.12, not significant; Boekel 2023). The large difference in crude PASC prevalence across cohorts (10.7% COVAD vs. 21% Boekel vs. 57% from the Mass General Brigham EHR cohort) reflects definitional and ascertainment heterogeneity [INACCESSIBLE — whether the review explicitly reconciles these discrepancies].
- **Acute infection severity is the dominant predictor**: Across primary studies, hospitalization/ICU admission is the strongest PASC predictor (aOR 6.49 for hospitalization, DiIorio 2022; aOR 3.8 for ICU admission, Sen/COVAD 2023; aOR 3.07 for WHO score ≥3, Boekel 2023). Adjusting for severity attenuates the IRD-PASC association substantially in Boekel 2023.
- **Female sex is consistently flagged as a risk factor**: The COVAD study found female sex aOR 2.9 (p = 0.037) for PASC in the full cohort; the review abstract explicitly identifies female sex as a key risk factor [INACCESSIBLE — whether the review discusses sex as a confounder of the ARD-PASC association vs. a within-ARD predictor].
- **Vaccination is protective**: Full vaccination was associated with markedly lower PASC odds in SARDs (aOR 0.49 at 28 days; aOR 0.10 at 90 days; Patel NJ 2022), though immunosuppressive medications may blunt vaccine efficacy.
- **Symptom overlap is a diagnostic challenge**: The review explicitly notes that PASC diagnosis is clinical and that symptom overlap with rheumatic disease manifestations complicates case ascertainment [INACCESSIBLE — whether a formal recommendation on differential ascertainment is provided].
- **Immunosuppressive medications**: Some immunosuppressive regimens may modify PASC risk; the COVAD study found advanced antiviral/monoclonal antibody treatment aOR 1.9 for PASC, suggesting acute-treatment intensity tracks with PASC risk but may also proxy severity [INACCESSIBLE — how the review handles this confound].

## T078 Focus — Six Targeted Questions

These answers draw on the abstract, the reference list, and the four primary papers confirmed to be synthesized by this review. Items marked [INACCESSIBLE] could not be verified from the paywalled full text.

**1. EXPOSURE**: Yes. The review explicitly treats pre-existing autoimmune rheumatic disease — diagnosed before the index SARS-CoV-2 infection — as the exposure. Disease strata covered (drawn from primary papers synthesized): rheumatoid arthritis (RA), systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), myositis, Sjögren's syndrome, spondyloarthritis (including ankylosing spondylitis, psoriatic arthritis), vasculitis, polymyalgia rheumatica, and connective tissue disease. The umbrella term used across primary papers is "systemic autoimmune rheumatic disease" (SARD) or "inflammatory rheumatic disease" (IRD). Disease-specific PASC rate estimates are sparse: DiIorio 2022 reports "no statistical association of SARD type with prolonged symptom duration"; the Mass General Brigham EHR cohort (Patel NJ et al., J Rheumatol 2024) identifies SLE and vasculitis as risk factors within the SARD cohort (no external reference group).

**2. CONTROL/MATCHING**: Not applicable as a pooled design (narrative review without meta-analysis). Among the synthesized primary studies: Boekel 2023 is the strongest design (prospective; N = 733 age/sex-matched healthy controls, concurrent enrollment). COVAD/Sen 2023 included self-recruited healthy controls (N = 713) without formal matching. DiIorio 2022 and Patel NJ 2024 have no external control arm (within-SARD risk-factor analyses only). No study in the reference list appears to use matched infected controls from the general population without rheumatic disease, which would be the appropriate counterfactual for an exposure estimate.

**3. SEX HANDLING**: Female sex is identified as a risk factor in the abstract and in primary papers (COVAD: female aOR 2.9 in the full cohort). However, the strong female predominance of rheumatic disease (72–83% female across primary cohorts) means the aggregate ARD-PASC association is heavily sex-confounded. Sex-interaction analyses (i.e., comparing ARD-vs-control PASC odds stratified by sex, or testing sex × ARD interaction) are not reported in any primary study and are [INACCESSIBLE — whether the review calls this out as a confound or gap]. The sex and disease-type confounds are not decomposed.

**4. ACUTE SEVERITY**: Consistently addressed across primary papers; acute severity is the strongest PASC predictor in every model that includes it (hospitalization aOR 3–6.5; ICU aOR 3.8). The review explicitly identifies infection severity as a key risk factor. However, the degree to which the ARD-PASC association survives severity adjustment varies by cohort: Boekel 2023 found the IRD-PASC association becomes non-significant after adjustment, while COVAD 2023 (Sen) maintained a significant ARD-vs-control difference (OR 2.1) even though severity was a covariate. **5. HEALTHCARE UTILIZATION**: Not directly addressed as an ascertainment/utilization confound in the abstract. The closest proxy is the observation that PASC is a clinical diagnosis with symptom overlap with rheumatic disease itself (noted in abstract), and the Mass General Brigham EHR-based estimate (57% PASC) is likely inflated by higher healthcare contact rates among SARD patients driving more diagnostic code capture. No primary paper in the reference list explicitly adjusts for healthcare-contact frequency or propensity for PASC code assignment. - CDC (symptoms ≥28 days): DiIorio 2022, Patel NJ 2022
- WHO-equivalent (symptoms ≥90 days): DiIorio 2022, Patel NJ 2022
- EHR-derived ICD-10/lab/vital-sign composite (≥30 days): Patel NJ 2024

## Relevance

Directly relevant to task:t078 (autoimmune diathesis as effect-modifier for PAIS). This review confirms:
1. There is a nominal elevated PASC risk signal in ARD/SARD patients, but it attenuates after severity and covariate adjustment in at least one strong study (Boekel 2023).
2. The sex confound — rheumatic disease is female-predominant and PASC is female-predominant — is unaddressed in sex-interaction analyses across all synthesized primary papers.
3. Healthcare utilization ascertainment bias is not adjusted for, and the EHR-based estimate is likely upwardly biased.
4. No existing study provides the ideal estimate needed for t078: sex-stratified ARD-vs.-matched-infected-controls PASC odds with severity and utilization adjustment.

Also relevant to hypothesis:0008-measurement-channel-and-ascertainment-bias (the broad PASC literature on rheumatic disease is a case study in the predicted inflation direction) and hypothesis:0005-reproductive-stage-immune-homeostatic-margin (female sex is a consistent predictor but sex × ARD interaction is uncharacterized).

## Limitations

- **Narrative review without pooled estimates**: No meta-analytic synthesis; quantitative claims depend entirely on heterogeneous primary studies with different designs, definitions, and populations.
- **No sex × ARD interaction analysis**: The core confound for t078 (both ARD and PASC are female-predominant) is not addressed by any primary study reviewed, nor, based on the abstract, explicitly flagged as a gap in the review [INACCESSIBLE].
- **Healthcare utilization confound not addressed**: EHR-based PASC estimates in high-healthcare-contact SARD patients are likely upwardly biased; the review does not appear to adjust for this or recommend utilization-matched designs.
- **Case definition heterogeneity**: PASC prevalence ranges ~5× across primary papers (10.7% to 57%) depending on definition; the review acknowledges clinical diagnosis challenges but [INACCESSIBLE — whether it gives formal guidance on which estimate to trust].
- **Autoimmune lead author affiliation**: Senior author Naomi J. Patel (Mass General Brigham) is co-author on multiple primary papers synthesized (Ann Rheum Dis 2022; J Rheumatol 2024), which may introduce narrative framing bias toward that group's cohort findings [SPECULATION].
