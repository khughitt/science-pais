# Efficacy of repeated immunoadsorption in patients with post-COVID myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome and elevated beta2-adrenergic receptor autoantibodies: a prospective cohort study

- **Authors:** Elisa Stein, Cornelia Heindrich, Kirsten Wittke, Claudia Kedor, Rebekka Rust, Helma Freitag, Franziska Sotzny, Anne Krüger, Markus Tölle, Patricia Grabowski, Carmen Scheibenbogen, Laura Kim
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal:** The Lancet Regional Health - Europe
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.lanepe.2024.101161
- **PMID:** 39759581
- **PMCID:** PMC11699797
- **BibTeX key:** Stein2025
- **Source:** Europe PMC (full XML, OA CC BY)

## Key Contribution

First published prospective cohort study to demonstrate that repeated immunoadsorption (IA) — extracorporeal IgG depletion — produces broad, multi-domain symptom improvement in a biomarker-selected (elevated β2-adrenergic receptor autoantibody; β2 AR-AB) subgroup of post-COVID ME/CFS patients. The central methodological move is using β2 AR-AB elevation as an enrolment criterion rather than a post-hoc correlate, which operationalizes the functional-autoantibody hypothesis as a stratifying biomarker and narrows the treatment to patients with putative autoantibody-driven pathology. Seventy percent of participants met the pre-specified responder threshold at four weeks, and gains were maintained at six months. The authors propose this as proof-of-concept for a downstream randomized controlled trial and for combining IA with B-cell depletion (inebilizumab, anti-CD19) to address autoantibody regeneration.

## Methods

- **Design:** Single-centre, uncontrolled prospective cohort (pre-post). No sham/placebo arm.
- **Setting:** Outpatient immunodeficiency department, Charité – Universitätsmedizin Berlin; October 2022 – October 2023.
- **N:** 20 patients completing 6-month follow-up (23 enrolled, 3 withdrew before treatment; 87% retention).
- **Case definition:** ME/CFS by modified Canadian Consensus Criteria (CCC) with PEM ≥ 14 h; confirmed SARS-CoV-2 onset (PCR, antigen, or nucleocapsid serology).
- **Biomarker eligibility criterion:** Elevated β2 AR-AB at screening (>14 U/l = > 90th percentile of healthy controls, ELISA via CellTrend GmbH). Among 402 ME/CFS patients screened, 189 (47%) were elevated.
- **Demographics:** 13 female / 7 male; median age 40 years (IQR 36–51); median disease duration 22 months (IQR 15–31); median Bell score 30 (range 20–40, i.e., typically housebound at 50% function). Note: severely housebound patients unable to attend outpatient sessions were excluded.
- **Intervention:** Five IA sessions (TheraSorb-Ig omni 5 adsorber, Miltenyi Biotech) over a 10-day outpatient period. Sessions removed IgG (all subclasses), IgA, IgM, IgE, and immune complexes. Seven patients who worsened after initial response received a second IA cycle (6–12 months later).
- **Primary endpoint:** Change in SF-36 Physical Functioning domain (SF-36 PF) from baseline to 4 weeks post-IA. Responder = ≥ 10-point improvement.
- **Secondary endpoints:** SF-36 energy/fatigue, SF-36 pain; Fatigue Severity Scale (FSS); Bell disability score; DePaul Symptom Questionnaire – PEM (DSQ-PEM); COMPASS31 (autonomic symptoms); numeric rating scales for fatigue, muscle pain, cognitive impairment, immunological symptoms; handgrip strength (HGS, 10-rep repeat protocol after 60 min rest); endothelial function via EndoPAT® reactive hyperemia index (RHI).
- **Autoantibodies measured:** β1 AR-AB, β2 AR-AB, M3 AChR-AB, M4 AChR-AB (CellTrend ELISA, pre/post in same run).
- **Statistics:** Linear mixed-effects models (lme4/lmerTest in R 4.3.0); Satterthwaite degrees of freedom; between-group comparisons via Mann–Whitney U; correlations via Spearman ρ; two-tailed α = 0.05.
- **COI / funding:** BMBF (German Federal Ministry of Education and Research) + Weidenhammer Zöbele Research Foundation. CS holds a CellTrend consulting agreement; Charité/CellTrend co-hold a β2 AR-AB diagnostic patent.

## Key Findings

### Immunoglobulin and autoantibody depletion

- IgG reduced by a mean of 79% (CI: 73–84%) at day 5 of the treatment course.
- β2 AR-AB reduced in parallel: mean reduction 26.57 U/l (CI: 20.11–33.02 U/l), 77% (CI: 58–95%).
- β1 AR-AB, M3 AChR-AB, M4 AChR-AB also reduced; all four autoantibodies showed positive Spearman correlations with immunological symptom score at baseline (β1: r = 0.53, p = 0.017; β2: r = 0.46, p = 0.040; M3: r = 0.60, p = 0.006; M4: r = 0.60, p = 0.006).
- Importantly, IgG and β2 AR-AB reductions occurred in all 20 patients, independent of clinical response — depletion efficacy did not distinguish responders from non-responders.

### Primary endpoint — physical functioning

- Mean SF-36 PF increase of 17.75 points (CI: 13.41–26.16, p < 0.001) at 4 weeks; Cohen's d = 1.19 (large effect).
- **Responders:** 14/20 (70%) achieved ≥ 10-point SF-36 PF improvement at 4 weeks. Two non-responders showed delayed improvement at months 2–3.
- Mean SF-36 PF improvement remained significant at 6 months: 12.81 points (CI: 4.99–20.61, p = 0.002).
- Peak improvement occurred at months 2–3, followed by gradual decline; improvement above baseline maintained through month 6.

### Secondary symptom outcomes (all 20 patients; selected highlights)

- **SF-36 energy/fatigue:** Peak mean increase +19 points (CI: 11.61–26.39, p < 0.001) at month 2; sustained through month 6.
- **SF-36 pain:** Peak mean increase +22.63 points (CI: 13.28–31.96, p < 0.001) at month 2; sustained through month 6.
- **PEM (DSQ-PEM):** Both frequency and severity significantly decreased; maintained through month 6.
- **Autonomic symptoms (COMPASS31):** Mean decrease 12.23 points (CI: 5.4–19.06, p = 0.001) at month 1; orthostatic, secretomotor, and gastrointestinal sub-domains most improved.
- **Fatigue (FSS):** Mean decrease 4.9 points (CI: 1.34–8.46, p = 0.009) at month 1.
- **Cognitive, muscle pain, immunological symptoms (NRS):** Significant improvements maintained through month 6, maximum at months 2–3.
- **Bell disability score:** Significant at months 2, 4, 5, 6; maximum +5.18 points (CI: 1.24–9.12, p = 0.013) at month 2.

### Objective measures

- **Handgrip strength (female patients, n = 13):** Repeat HGS in the second session (1 h after first) increased by mean 20% (CI: 2–39%, p = 0.042) at 6 months. Female responders showed HGS improvement as early as 4 weeks.
- Female responders had significantly higher baseline maximum HGS than female non-responders (Mdn 23.5 kg vs. 9.8 kg, z = −2.62, p = 0.006, r = 0.73), suggesting baseline muscular reserve predicts response.
- **Endothelial function (RHI):** No significant change at 6 months. 6/20 patients had impaired RHI at baseline.

### Second IA cycle (n = 7 relapsers)

- Patients who worsened after an initial response were re-treated 6–12 months later. Symptoms improved again at 4 weeks post-second cycle and were maintained at a level similar to post-first-cycle gains.

### Safety

- IA was generally well tolerated; treatment was frequently physically exhausting and triggered PEM in the short term (lorazepam offered as supportive therapy).
- One serious adverse event: internal jugular vein thrombosis from a Shaldon's catheter (in 5/20 patients who required catheter access); resolved with temporary anticoagulation.

## Relevance

This study is directly relevant to the project's therapeutics focus (task t004) and to the functional-autoantibody thread in the literature. It is the largest published uncontrolled cohort testing IA in post-COVID ME/CFS and the first to use β2 AR-AB elevation as a prospective enrolment criterion.

**Operationalizing the autoimmunity hypothesis with a stratifying biomarker.** Prior work by the Scheibenbogen group showed β2 AR-AB associate with symptom severity and distinguish post-COVID from recovered patients. This study takes the next step: using β2 AR-AB elevation as eligibility criteria selects a putative autoantibody-driven subgroup before treatment. The 70% response rate in this selected cohort contrasts with the overall ~47% β2 AR-AB prevalence in the broader ME/CFS population screened (189/402), illustrating how biomarker stratification concentrates therapeutic signal.

**Design echoes and warnings from RituxME.** The positive open-label signal here recalls early rituximab studies in ME/CFS, which also showed symptom improvement in small uncontrolled cohorts but failed to replicate in the larger RituxME randomized controlled trial (NCT01156909, Fluge et al., 2019). The authors explicitly invoke this precedent and acknowledge the need for sham-controlled trials. The discussion mentions that rituximab efficacy in ME/CFS was associated with β2 AR-AB depletion, further tying the autoantibody thread. This paper should be held at proof-of-concept level, not efficacy-confirmed.

**Mechanistic ambiguity is acknowledged.** β2 AR-AB and total IgG decreased equivalently in all patients regardless of response, which the authors explicitly note. This means the β2 AR-AB level reduction alone does not explain who responds — either other removed autoantibodies, non-autoantibody IgG effects, or other mechanisms contribute to response variation. This is a critical caveat for the functional-autoantibody causal claim.

**Downstream trial signal.** The authors are initiating a trial of inebilizumab (anti-CD19, targeting B cells and plasma blasts) in IA responders — a mechanistically logical combination. If IA removes circulating autoantibodies and B-cell depletion removes the source, the combination may sustain remission. Additional RCTs of IA with sham control are underway at multiple German sites.

## Project Framework Mapping

- **Hypothesis 0001 (shared dysregulated attractor):** The autoantibody/B-cell dysfunction loop is one of the candidate feedback mechanisms sustaining the dysregulated attractor. IA temporarily breaks the loop; rebound in non-sustained responders is consistent with self-regenerating autoantibody production re-seeding the attractor.
- **Question 0009 (functional GPCR autoantibodies drive dysautonomia):** This paper is the primary interventional evidence: β2 AR-AB depletion co-occurs with COMPASS31 autonomic improvement (orthostatic, secretomotor domains), directly bearing on whether these autoantibodies mechanistically drive dysautonomia. The lack of endothelial RHI change despite COMPASS31 improvement is a secondary finding worth tracking.
- **Autoimmunity mechanism thread:** Connects to literature on IgG transfer experiments (Chen et al.; Santos Guedes de Sa et al., cited in paper) showing PCS patient IgG induces distinct symptoms in mice. The IA result is complementary: removal of IgG (not just β2 AR-AB) improves symptoms, consistent with the transfer model.

## Limitations

1. **Uncontrolled design.** No sham apheresis arm. Placebo response in ME/CFS / PCS is substantial; the magnitude of the observed SF-36 PF improvement cannot be attributed to IA per se without a controlled comparator. This is the primary reason to hold findings at proof-of-concept level.
2. **Small N = 20.** Study was powered to detect large effect sizes (Cohen's d ≥ 1); smaller clinically meaningful effects may not be detectable.
3. **Selected, relatively ambulatory cohort.** Severe housebound patients unable to attend outpatient sessions were excluded; thus safety and feasibility data cannot be generalized to the most severe PAIS phenotype.
4. **Self-reported primary and most secondary endpoints.** Patient-reported outcomes dominate; the objective HGS finding is limited to female patients and the RHI did not change.
5. **DSQ-PEM not validated for longitudinal tracking** (designed for diagnostic use), limiting the PEM outcome's interpretability.
6. **β2 AR-AB change does not predict response.** IgG and β2 AR-AB were depleted equally in all patients regardless of clinical outcome, suggesting the active therapeutic mechanism is not the same as the enrollment biomarker — or that additional autoantibodies or immune factors mediate response. This weakens the causal chain from β2 AR-AB → symptom.
7. **Conflict of interest.** CS holds a CellTrend consulting agreement and Charité co-holds the β2 AR-AB diagnostic patent with CellTrend, which supplied autoantibody measurements. Warrants acknowledgment when citing quantitative autoantibody claims.
8. **No healthy control comparator for outcome measures** (controls used only for β2 AR-AB normal range derivation).
9. **Temporal dynamics unclear for full 12-month window.** Follow-up data presented only through 6 months for the main cohort; 12-month data were collected but not reported in this paper.
