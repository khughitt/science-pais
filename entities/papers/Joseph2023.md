# Exercise Pathophysiology in Myalgic Encephalomyelitis/Chronic Fatigue Syndrome and Postacute Sequelae of SARS-CoV-2

- **Authors:** Phillip Joseph, Inderjit Singh, Rudolf Oliveira, Christine A. Capone, Mary P. Mullen, Dane B. Cook, Mary Catherine Stovall, Johanna Squires, Kristine Madsen, Aaron B. Waxman, David M. Systrom (senior/corresponding)
- **Year:** 2023
- **Journal:** CHEST, Vol. 164, No. 3, pp. 717–726
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.chest.2023.03.049
- **PMCID:** PMC10088277
- **BibTeX key:** Joseph2023
- **Source:** XML full text via Europe PMC (OA)

## Key Contribution

This narrative review from the Systrom group at Brigham and Women's Hospital / Harvard Medical School is the definitive synthesis of invasive cardiopulmonary exercise testing (iCPET) findings in both ME/CFS and PASC. Its central argument, captured in the subtitle "More in Common Than Not?", is that hemodynamic and gas-exchange derangements observed during iCPET in PASC overlap substantially with those in ME/CFS — and that both are inconsistent with simple deconditioning. The two dominant physiological signatures are: (1) **preload failure** — low biventricular filling pressures limiting cardiac output and peak VO2 — and (2) **impaired peripheral O2 extraction** — elevated cardiac output/VO2 slope and elevated peak mixed-venous O2 saturation, indicating that oxygenated blood bypasses capillary beds unextracted. This review serves as the field's principal arbiter in the deconditioning-vs-pathology debate by showing that the iCPET hemodynamic profile in ME/CFS and PASC is mechanistically incompatible with a deconditioning explanation.

## Methods

This is a **narrative review** (classified by CHEST as an "Education and Clinical Practice: CHEST Reviews" article), not a primary prospective study with a single enrolled cohort. The review synthesizes published iCPET and noninvasive CPET (niCPET) data from multiple primary studies by the Systrom group and others.

**iCPET protocol (as described for the primary studies reviewed):**

- Right-heart catheterization (Swan-Ganz catheter) plus radial arterial line placed under ultrasound/fluoroscopic guidance.
- Intracardiac O2 saturations measured at rest to screen for shunting.
- Patients perform maximal incremental upright cycle ergometry while ventilation and pulmonary gas exchange are continuously measured.
- Continuous recording of right atrial pressure (RAP), mean pulmonary artery pressure (mPAP), mean arterial pressure; PAWP (pulmonary artery wedge pressure) and mixed venous blood gases measured every minute.
- Cardiac output (Qc) computed by the direct Fick principle.
- Variables key for differentiating mechanisms: peak VO2, peak Qc, Qc/VO2 slope, peak mixed-venous O2 saturation, RAP, PAWP, and arteriovenous O2 content difference.

**Primary studies reviewed include:**
- Systrom et al. iCPET study in ME/CFS (including a randomized placebo-controlled arm of pyridostigmine)
- Systrom et al. iCPET study enriched with PASC patients
- Multiple niCPET studies in ME/CFS and post-COVID (including 2-day serial CPET protocols)
- Skin biopsy studies assessing small-fiber neuropathy (SFN) prevalence in ME/CFS and PASC
- Pediatric cohort studies (MIS-C and long COVID in children)

**Populations reviewed** (across cited primary studies, not a single enrolled cohort):
- Adult ME/CFS patients meeting National Academy of Medicine criteria (substantial impairment >6 months, PEM, unrefreshing sleep, plus cognitive impairment or orthostatic intolerance)
- Adult PASC patients (post-SARS-CoV-2, symptoms including intractable fatigue, PEM, and/or orthostatic intolerance)
- Pediatric PASC patients and MIS-C survivors
- A heterogeneous referral population with unexplained exertional intolerance (where ~20% exhibit preload failure; enriched ME/CFS/PASC cohorts show it to be "ubiquitous")

## Key Findings

### 1. Preload Failure — dominant hemodynamic phenotype in both ME/CFS and PASC

In a heterogeneous population referred for iCPET investigation of unexplained exertional intolerance, low biventricular filling pressures ("preload failure") explains depressed aerobic capacity in approximately 20% of patients. In studies **enriched** with PASC or ME/CFS patients, preload failure is described as "ubiquitous." The hemodynamic signature — low peak Qc, low RAP, and low PAWP at peak exercise — is the opposite of the deconditioning signature (where detraining causes cardiac atrophy and *elevated* filling pressures). This mechanistic inversion is the iCPET argument against deconditioning.

A randomized placebo-controlled iCPET trial of pyridostigmine in ME/CFS (reviewed herein) showed that the cholinesterase inhibitor increased peak RAP, Qc, and VO2, and that these patients were not fasted (ruling out volume depletion) and were off diuretics/venodilators — pointing to **neurovascular dysregulation** (not hypovolemia) as the mechanism of preload failure.

**Proposed mechanism:** Small-fiber neuropathy (SFN) disrupts sympathetic venoconstriction. SFN prevalence is ~33% (definitive diagnosis by PGP 9.5-immunolabeled lower-leg epidermal biopsy) in ME/CFS and reportedly ~90% in one small PASC study. SFN impairs venoconstriction via loss of sympathetic and cholinergic perivascular innervation, reducing peripheral venous return and biventricular preload during exercise. A co-existing ganglionopathy (sometimes with autoantibodies to the acetylcholine receptor) may extend the dysautonomia.

### 2. Impaired Systemic O2 Extraction — peripheral signature shared across ME/CFS and PASC

An elevated Qc/VO2 slope and high peak mixed-venous O2 saturation during iCPET are observed in both ME/CFS and PASC. This "high-flow state" indicates oxygenated blood is returning to the venous circulation without releasing O2 to exercising muscle — i.e., oxygen extraction failure, not a cardiac output ceiling.

Two candidate mechanisms are discussed:

- **Microcirculatory left-to-right shunts:** Dysautonomia (SFN, ganglionopathy) dysregulates arteriovenous shunts, allowing oxygenated blood to bypass muscle capillary beds. Skin biopsy data in fibromyalgia/SFN show abnormal arteriovenous shunt innervation enabling exactly this bypass. Recent data also implicate red blood cell deformability defects and endothelial dysfunction compromising microcirculatory O2 delivery.
- **Mitochondrial myopathy:** Both ME/CFS and PASC have been separately linked to mitochondrial dysfunction. iCPET can distinguish: mitochondrial dysfunction produces depressed VO2 peak with **normal** peak Qc (the heart compensates normally; the muscle cannot use the delivered O2), whereas left-to-right shunting produces depressed VO2 peak with **supranormal** peak Qc.

Table 1 in the paper formalizes this differential:

| Condition | Peak VO2 | Peak Qc | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Athletic heart | High | High | Commensurate increases |
| Preload failure | Low | Low | Cardiac output limited by venous return |
| Left-to-right shunt | Low | High (supranormal) | O2 bypasses capillaries |
| Mitochondrial dysfunction | Low | Normal | Extraction failure, cardiac output intact |

### 3. Dyspnea and Hyperventilation — not dead-space driven

Elevated VE/VCO2 (ventilatory inefficiency) is present in both conditions. iCPET data show this is driven entirely by **hyperventilation** (low arterial PCO2), not by failure of the VD/VT ratio to fall normally during exercise. This distinguishes ME/CFS/PASC from heart failure and pulmonary arterial hypertension, where elevated VD/VT contributes. The proposed driver is an exaggerated skeletal muscle metaboreflex (group III/IV afferents), analogous to heart failure physiology. Secondary consequence: respiratory alkalemia shifts the O2-hemoglobin curve leftward, increasing hemoglobin-O2 affinity and further inhibiting capillary O2 offloading — a synergistic worsening of peripheral extraction.

### 4. Argument against deconditioning

The paper devotes a dedicated section to why iCPET evidence is **incompatible with a deconditioning explanation**:
- Deconditioning produces high intracardiac filling pressures (cardiac atrophy → stiffer, less compliant ventricles) — opposite the low-RAP/low-PAWP preload failure phenotype.
- Peripheral O2 extraction is **little affected** by deconditioning — a finding diametrically opposed to the impaired extraction seen in these patients.
- Pyridostigmine RCT improvements in iCPET parameters in ME/CFS confirm reversibility via neurovascular, not reconditioning, mechanisms.

### 5. Pediatric PASC / MIS-C

Meta-analysis: long COVID symptoms in 25% of children post-SARS-CoV-2. Exercise intolerance, fatigue, PEM, and orthostatic intolerance are prominent. In MIS-C survivors at 6 months, 45% had 6-min walk test below the 3rd percentile for age/sex; iCPET in a small post-MIS-C sample showed lower peak VO2, impaired oxidative metabolism, and ventilatory inefficiency in all patients. Some impairment occurred in children with normal inflammatory markers and normal LV systolic function on echocardiogram — pointing again to a pathophysiology beyond residual cardiac disease.

### 6. Future directions flagged

- Combining niCPET with plasma multi-omics (metabolomics reveal perturbations in lipid/energy pathways and glutamate metabolism post-exercise in ME/CFS)
- PASC metabolic profiles show elevated ferritin, D-dimer, ESR, and CRP — chronic inflammatory state
- NIH RECOVER program and MUSIC study (pediatric long COVID) for prospective multicenter data

## Relevance to PAIS

This paper is the strongest objective evidence available in the project for **shared exercise pathophysiology across ME/CFS and PASC**, and the principal counterargument to the claim that PASC exertional intolerance reflects deconditioning from acute illness.

Its relevance to PAIS in this project operates on three levels:

1. **Arbitrates the deconditioning debate:** The iCPET hemodynamic signatures in ME/CFS and PASC are mechanistically incompatible with simple deconditioning. This matters because the deconditioning hypothesis carries treatment implications (graded exercise therapy) that can harm ME/CFS patients. The invasive hemodynamic data provide the most direct evidence that the exercise ceiling is pathological, not behavioral or deconditioning-driven.

2. **Grounds question:0011 (mitochondrial/bioenergetic basis of PEM):** The iCPET framework maps directly onto the PEM bioenergetic question. Preload failure limits O2 *delivery*, while impaired peripheral extraction limits O2 *utilization* (with mitochondrial dysfunction as one candidate). The hyperventilation-induced respiratory alkalemia creates a third compounding mechanism (impaired O2 offloading). These are not merely descriptive — they are objectively measurable hemodynamic correlates of the energy-delivery and utilization failures that underlie PEM. The paper does not resolve whether the primary lesion is mitochondrial or microvascular/shunting, but frames the iCPET as the tool to distinguish them.

3. **Supports hypothesis:0001 (shared dysregulated attractor across triggers):** The cross-trigger convergence on the same iCPET phenotype (preload failure + impaired extraction + hyperventilation) in ME/CFS and PASC, in the absence of intrinsic cardiopulmonary disease, is strong evidence for a shared pathophysiological attractor rather than coincidental symptom overlap. The Systrom group's data are the best direct physiological evidence for this convergence claim in the project.

## Project Framework Mapping

- **Mechanism cluster:** Dysautonomia / small-fiber neuropathy → impaired venous return → preload failure; microvascular dysfunction → peripheral O2 extraction failure; possible mitochondrial myopathy as co-occurring or overlapping mechanism.
- **Shared failure mode:** iCPET phenotype is consistent across ME/CFS and PASC (and possibly other PAIS). The cross-trigger convergence is evidence for hypothesis:0001 (shared dysregulated attractor).
- **Biomarker angle:** iCPET parameters (peak VO2, peak Qc, Qc/VO2 slope, mixed-venous O2 saturation, RAP, PAWP) constitute objective, reproducible, provoked endpoints for the bioenergetic failure underlying PEM — directly relevant to question:0011.
- **Deconditioning controversy:** This paper provides the hemodynamic argument that settles the debate in favor of pathology: preload failure and extraction failure are not signatures of deconditioning.

## Limitations

1. **Review design, not a primary prospective study:** The paper does not report a single enrolled cohort with defined inclusion criteria and pre-specified outcomes. It synthesizes findings from multiple published iCPET and niCPET studies with different designs, cohort sizes, and case definitions. Effect sizes and prevalence figures come from the cited primary literature, not from this paper's own analysis.

2. **Referral population selection bias:** iCPET studies are conducted at tertiary referral centers in patients with unexplained exertional intolerance who were referred for invasive testing. These cohorts are likely enriched for severe, treatment-refractory, and phenotypically extreme presentations and may not be representative of the broader ME/CFS or PASC population.

3. **Absence of healthy controls in most iCPET studies:** The paper notes the use of age-predicted normal values rather than simultaneously recruited controls in most of the cited iCPET work.

4. **Case-definition heterogeneity for PASC:** The review does not apply a single diagnostic standard for PASC; the cited studies predate consensus WHO/CDC long-COVID definitions and use variable inclusion criteria. Apparent overlap between ME/CFS and PASC may partly reflect definitional overlap rather than shared biology.

5. **Causal direction unresolved:** The review acknowledges the SFN-preload-failure mechanism is proposed rather than established. It is not demonstrated that SFN causes preload failure via venoconstriction loss (vs. SFN being a parallel manifestation of a common upstream process). Similarly, the distinction between mitochondrial dysfunction and microvascular shunting as the dominant peripheral extraction mechanism is acknowledged as unresolved.

6. **Pediatric data are sparse and mostly noninvasive:** iCPET data in children with PASC/MIS-C are very limited at the time of review; pediatric conclusions rest on 6-min walk tests and niCPET in small samples.

7. **Pyridostigmine RCT:** The randomized trial of pyridostigmine in ME/CFS (providing causal support for the neurovascular dysregulation mechanism) is reviewed but not fully described here; the review represents it as supporting, but the full RCT evidence should be read in the primary report.

- The CPET differential diagnosis table (Table 1) is a useful framework to connect to question:0011 — specifically, the distinction between left-to-right shunting (supranormal Qc) vs. mitochondrial dysfunction (normal Qc) as mechanisms of O2 extraction failure.
- Track metabolomics follow-up: the paper flags post-exercise glutamate metabolism perturbations as a future direction — relevant to bioenergetic profiling of PEM (question:0011).
- The SFN prevalence data (~33% ME/CFS, ~90% in one small PASC study) connects directly to question:0004 (convergent SFN substrate) and topic:dysautonomia.
- Consider whether iCPET-derived hemodynamic parameters (preload failure, extraction failure) should be added as objective endpoints in the project's biomarkers topic.
