# Long Covid and menopause - the important role of hormones in Long Covid must be considered

- **Authors:** Louise Newson, Rebecca Lewis, Margaret O'Hara
- **Year:** 2021
- **Journal:** Maturitas
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.maturitas.2021.08.026
- **BibTeX key:** Newson2021
- **Source:** PDF: `~/d/health/processes/post-acute-infection/papers/pdfs/2021_Newson_Long-Covid-and-menopause-the-important-role-of-hormones-in-Long-Covid-must-be-considered.pdf`

## Key Contribution

Newson et al. report a conference abstract survey of women with long COVID focused on menstrual changes, perceived menopause overlap, and whether clinicians asked about hormonal status.
It is low-level evidence but captures patient-reported diagnostic ambiguity that later studies examine more formally.

## Methods

An online survey link was shared through social media groups.
Questions asked about long-COVID symptom duration, period changes, menstrual-cycle-linked symptom changes, and whether healthcare professionals had asked about hormones.
The abstract reports responses from 460 women.

## Key Findings

Forty-eight percent of respondents had symptoms for more than 6 months.
Half reported that periods had stopped or changed since COVID-19 infection, and 80% said periods had not returned to pre-infection patterns.
Sixty-two percent reported long-COVID symptoms worsened in the days before periods, when sex-hormone levels are usually lower.
Seventy percent thought some long-COVID symptoms could reflect perimenopause or menopause, while 84% had not been asked by a healthcare professional whether they could be perimenopausal or menopausal.

## Relevance

This abstract is useful for patient-centered hypothesis generation.
It supports screening for menstrual and menopausal status in long-COVID clinics, but it does not establish whether hormone disturbance causes long COVID.
Because recruitment was via social media and analysis is descriptive, it should be weighted below clinic cohorts and prospective studies.

## Limitations

This is a conference abstract, not a full peer-reviewed article with detailed methods.
The sample is self-selected from social media, has no comparison group, and relies on retrospective self-report.
The authors' interpretation favors hormone deficiency and HRT, which may overstate causal evidence.

## Model / Tool Availability

No model, tool, or dataset was released.

## Follow-up

Use as a qualitative/patient-signal source only.
The strongest follow-up is a prospective symptom diary study linking menstrual cycle phase, hormone levels, inflammatory markers, and long-COVID symptom flares.
