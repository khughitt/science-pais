# Post-Acute COVID-19 Syndrome: Prevalence of Peripheral Microvascular Endothelial Dysfunction and Associations With NT-ProBNP Dynamics

- **Authors:** Marcus Ståhlberg, Katarina Fischer, Maged Tahhan, Allan Zhao, Artur Fedorowski, Michael Runold, Malin Nygren-Bonnier, Mikael Björnson, Lars H. Lund, Judith Bruchfeld, Liyew Desta, Frieder Braunschweig, Ali Mahdi
- **Year:** 2025 (published online October 2024)
- **Journal:** The American Journal of Medicine, Vol 138(6):1019–1028
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.amjmed.2024.10.012
- **BibTeX key:** Stahlberg2025
- **Source:** PDF

## Key Contribution

This prospective cross-sectional study provides the first systematic quantification of peripheral microvascular endothelial dysfunction prevalence in a well-characterised PACS cohort, using the EndoPAT reactive-hyperemia index (RHI) assessed at a median of 31 months post-infection — demonstrating that 41% of patients meet the dysfunction threshold (RHI <1.67) and 20% have impaired function (RHI 1.67–2.0), all following non-hospitalised mild acute illness. Crucially, the paper links the spatial (vascular) failure to temporal progression of cardiac stress by showing that NT-proBNP rises over a ~16-month follow-up window specifically in the groups with impaired or dysfunctional RHI but not in those with normal endothelial function, providing the first longitudinal biomarker evidence linking peripheral microvascular dysfunction to subclinical cardiac remodelling in PACS.

## Methods

**Design:** Prospective, single-centre cross-sectional cohort; UppCov registry; Karolinska University Hospital tertiary post-COVID clinic.

**Case definition:** WHO criteria (Soriano et al., *Lancet Infect Dis* 2022): confirmed/probable prior SARS-CoV-2, ongoing symptoms ≥3 months, no alternative explanation. All patients contracted COVID-19 in wave 1 or 2 (March 2020–January 2021); none required hospitalisation for acute illness. Referral threshold: ≥6 months of disabling symptoms + ≥50% sick-leave.

**Cohort:** n = 92; 82% female; median age 48 years (IQR 41–54); median 31 months from acute infection to RHI evaluation (IQR 812–987 days).

**Microvascular function:** EndoPAT device (Itamar-Medical), pulse amplitude tonometry (PAT). Reactive hyperemia induced by 5-min supra-arterial occlusion of the index finger; RHI = PAT signal at 60–120 s post-release / pre-cuff baseline. Contralateral hand served as internal control. Cutoffs: RHI <1.67 = microvascular endothelial dysfunction; RHI 1.67–2.0 = impaired; RHI >2.0 = normal. Subjects fasted ≥6 h, abstained from caffeine/nicotine 12 h, withheld morning medications on examination day.

**NT-proBNP:** Measured at two clinical time points: Visit 1 (mean 9 months post-infection; median 286 days IQR 233–382) and Visit 2 (mean 25 months post-infection; median 757 days IQR 529–1125), yielding a median inter-visit span of ~471 days.

**Statistics:** Kruskal-Wallis with Dunn post-hoc (3 groups), Mann-Whitney / Wilcoxon (2 groups), Spearman correlation, multivariable linear regression (RHI as dependent; adjusting for age, BMI, sex, SBP, DBP, POTS diagnosis, days between infection and each NT-proBNP draw). Two-sided α = 0.05. GraphPad Prism v7 / R v4.3.0.

## Key Findings

**Prevalence of microvascular endothelial dysfunction:**
- RHI <1.67 (dysfunction): 41% (n = 38)
- RHI 1.67–2.0 (impaired): 20% (n = 18)
- RHI >2.0 (normal): 39% (n = 36)
- Only 39% of patients had normal peripheral endothelial function >2 years after mild COVID-19; the authors note that in comparable-age healthy women, microvascular dysfunction is rare in prior literature.

**NT-proBNP dynamics by RHI group:**
- Overall cohort: 21% median increase in NT-proBNP between visits (Visit 1 median 42 ng/L → Visit 2 median 51 ng/L).
- Normal RHI group: Visit 1 median 49.5 → Visit 2 50.5 ng/L; change not significant (p = 0.716).
- Impaired RHI group: Visit 1 median 36.5 → Visit 2 50.5 ng/L; significant increase (p = 0.007).
- Dysfunction RHI group: Visit 1 median 35.5 → Visit 2 51.0 ng/L; significant increase (p = 0.006).
- Importantly, baseline NT-proBNP levels did not differ across the three RHI groups; it is the *dynamics* (rate of rise), not the absolute level, that is associated with endothelial dysfunction.

**Relative change and delta NT-proBNP vs RHI:**
- Subjects with >50% increase in NT-proBNP (n = 33) had significantly lower RHI than those with no increase or decrease (n = 37; p = 0.013).
- Subjects with above-median delta NT-proBNP (median = 7 ng/L) had significantly lower RHI (p = 0.006).
- Spearman correlation between % change in NT-proBNP and RHI: r = −0.232 (p = 0.026).

**Multivariable regression (RHI as outcome; Table 4):**
- % Change NT-proBNP: β = −0.217 (95% CI −0.325 to −0.005), p = 0.049 (adjusted).
- Delta NT-proBNP: β = −0.254 (95% CI −0.377 to −0.012), p = 0.043 (adjusted).
- NT-proBNP at Visit 1 or Visit 2 alone: not significant (p = 0.368, 0.349).

**Symptom burden:**
- No significant differences in most symptoms across RHI groups. Fatigue prevalent in all (84–100%). POTS diagnosed in 27% overall (22% normal, 44% impaired, 24% dysfunction).
- Minor group differences: shortness of breath higher in dysfunction vs impaired (85% vs 53%; p < 0.05); walking difficulties exclusive to dysfunction group (12% vs 0%); lower depression/anxiety in dysfunction group (13% vs 42% normal; p < 0.001).

**Comorbidities and medications:**
- Cardiovascular comorbidities low overall (hypertension 17%; no heart failure or CKD in any group). Beta-blockers prescribed in 49% overall (58% in dysfunction group vs 39% normal, p < 0.05) — likely for palpitations/POTS rather than heart failure.
- All NT-proBNP values remained below the upper normal limit for non-acute heart failure (125 ng/L) in the majority of patients (Visit 1: 6 above; Visit 2: 14 above).

## Relevance

This paper is directly relevant to the project's core research question (**research-question:post-acute-infection-syndromes**) and the PAIS frame of **failed homeostatic recovery after acute infection**.

**PAIS failure-mode evidence:** The finding that 61% of a non-hospitalised, low-cardiovascular-comorbidity PACS cohort exhibit at least impaired peripheral microvascular endothelial function at ~31 months post-infection is strong quantitative evidence that vascular homeostasis has not been restored. This is not a transient post-acute perturbation but a durable failure state — exactly the "failed homeostatic recovery" frame the project investigates.

**Progressive subclinical deterioration, not static residual:** The NT-proBNP dynamics finding is particularly important: it suggests the cardiovascular system is not in a new stable post-PACS equilibrium but is *continuing to deteriorate* in the groups with worst endothelial function. Rising NT-proBNP in the subdiagnostic range, while below the heart-failure threshold, tracks known epidemiological predictors of incident heart failure (>25% rise predicts events per MESA/JAMA Cardiol 2023). This aligns with the project hypothesis that PAIS represents a failed recovery trajectory, not just a delayed one.

**Mechanism convergence:** The paper situates microvascular endothelial dysfunction as a unifying mechanism integrating multiple candidate PAIS pathways: pro-inflammatory cytokine dysregulation, coagulation factor alterations, reduced nitric oxide bioavailability, free-radical production, and dysautonomia. These converge on the vascular bed as the shared effector. The study adds NT-proBNP as a downstream readout that may bridge microvascular and cardiac homeostatic failure.

**Mild acute illness, severe post-acute sequelae:** All participants had non-hospitalised COVID-19. This dissociation between acute severity and chronic post-infectious impairment is a hallmark of PAIS across pathogens (cf. Choutka 2022) and reinforces the project's position that PAIS biology is driven by post-acute mechanisms rather than acute-phase damage magnitude.

**Biomarker utility for the project:** RHI (EndoPAT) and NT-proBNP dynamics offer non-invasive, quantifiable readouts of the vascular/cardiac homeostatic failure mode that could serve as outcome measures in future PAIS mechanistic and intervention studies.

## Limitations

1. **Single centre, tertiary referral bias:** All patients from the Karolinska post-COVID clinic; referral requires ≥6 months of disabling symptoms and ≥50% sick-leave. Findings likely represent a severe PACS subpopulation and may not generalise to milder or self-resolved PACS.

2. **Small sample size (n = 92):** Limited statistical power for subgroup analyses and detection of modest effect sizes; some group comparisons (e.g., delta NT-proBNP vs MVD, p = 0.063) approach but do not reach significance.

3. **Cross-sectional endothelial measurement:** No pre-infection or early post-infection RHI baseline. Cannot determine whether microvascular dysfunction arose from PACS or was a pre-existing vulnerability that predisposed to PACS development. This is a fundamental causal ambiguity acknowledged by the authors.

4. **NT-proBNP dynamics interpretation:** The NT-proBNP increase is within the subdiagnostic range and could reflect biological variability, medication non-adherence (notably beta-blockers, which would mask a larger NT-proBNP rise), or other confounders rather than cardiac remodelling.

5. **Wave 1–2 cohort only:** All infections occurred March 2020–January 2021 (pre-Omicron, pre-vaccination). Findings may not apply to other SARS-CoV-2 variants, post-vaccination PACS, or re-infection contexts.

6. **No healthy control group:** RHI values cannot be compared to a matched control population in this study directly; the authors invoke external literature for reference, introducing selection and measurement heterogeneity.

7. **Symptom data source:** Symptoms retrieved from electronic medical charts rather than standardised prospective questionnaires; likely underestimates symptom burden and limits symptom–RHI correlation analyses.

8. **Predominantly female cohort (82%):** Limits generalisability to male PACS patients and precludes sex-stratified analyses at current sample size.

## Model / Tool Availability

No computational model or software tool is released. The EndoPAT device is a commercially available FDA-cleared instrument (Itamar-Medical, Caesarea, Israel). RHI is computed automatically by the device software. The UppCov cohort is an ongoing registry at Karolinska; data-sharing arrangements are not specified in this paper.

## Follow-up

- **Mahdi et al. 2023 (Sci Rep):** Multi-omic profiling of PACS with/without POTS — cited as Ref 6 here; measures dysregulation in hemostasis, metabolism, immune response, and angiogenesis. Directly extends the mechanistic picture proposed here.
- **Mahdi et al. 2023 (Circ Arrhythm Electrophysiol):** Coronary and peripheral microvascular dysfunction in PACS-POTS with reduced cardiac stress reactivity — cited as Ref 5; companion paper with coronary imaging.
- **Wu et al. 2023 (Cardiol Res Pract):** Enhanced external counterpulsation pilot for PACS microvascular angina — cited as Ref 28; first intervention targeting this mechanism.
- **Kuchler et al. 2023 (Angiogenesis):** Persistent endothelial dysfunction in post-COVID-19 syndrome and associations with symptom severity and chronic inflammation — referenced as comparator; different RHI cutoffs and follow-up timing.
- **Longitudinal studies needed:** Prospective cohort tracking RHI + NT-proBNP + cardiac imaging (strain, CMRI) + inflammatory/proteomic markers in PACS over ≥3 years with pre-infection reference; multi-wave / multi-variant design to test generalisability.
