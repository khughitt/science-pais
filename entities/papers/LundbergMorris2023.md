# Covid-19 vaccine effectiveness against post-covid-19 condition among 589 722 individuals in Sweden

- **Authors:** Lisa Lundberg-Morris, Susannah Leach, Yiyi Xu, Jari Martikainen, Ailiana Santosa, Magnus Gisslen, Huiqi Li, Fredrik Nyberg, Maria Bygdell
- **Year:** 2023
- **Journal:** BMJ
- **DOI:** 10.1136/bmj-2023-076990
- **PMID:** 37993131
- **PMCID:** PMC10666099
- **BibTeX key:** LundbergMorris2023

## Key Contribution

This Swedish population-register study is a large, clinician-coded post-COVID-condition analysis with a
dose-response pattern by number of vaccine doses before first registered infection.

## Methods

The cohort included 589,722 adults with first registered COVID-19 in Sweden's two largest regions.
Participants were followed until post-COVID condition diagnosis, vaccination after infection,
reinfection, death, emigration, or end of follow-up. The outcome was a clinical ICD-coded
post-COVID-condition diagnosis.

## Key Findings

- Post-COVID condition was diagnosed in 0.4% of vaccinated infected individuals versus 1.4% of
  unvaccinated infected individuals.
- Any pre-infection vaccination was associated with reduced PCC diagnosis risk (adjusted HR 0.42,
  95% CI 0.38-0.46), corresponding to estimated vaccine effectiveness of 58%.
- Estimated effectiveness increased by dose: 21% after one dose, 59% after two doses, and 73% after
  three or more doses.

## Relevance

The dose-response pattern is important for `question:0012`: it supports a prevention/modification
signal rather than a pure null. It is also consistent with `hypothesis:0004` because vaccination plausibly
changes the acute insult relative to host reserve.

## Limitations

The outcome is clinician-coded PCC, producing much lower incidence than symptom-survey studies and
making healthcare access/coding central. Vaccination status is confounded by calendar period, variant,
health behavior, comorbidity, and healthcare engagement despite adjustment.
