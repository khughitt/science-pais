# Single-cell transcriptomics of the immune system in ME/CFS at baseline and following symptom provocation

- **Authors:** Luyen Tien Vu, Faraz Ahmed, Hongya Zhu, David Shing Huk Iu, Elizabeth A. Fogarty, Yeonui Kwak, Weizhong Chen, Carl J. Franconi, Paul R. Munn, Ann E. Tate, Susan M. Levine, Jared Stevens, Xiangling Mao, Dikoma C. Shungu, Geoffrey E. Moore, Betsy A. Keller, Maureen R. Hanson, Jennifer K. Grenier, Andrew Grimson (Cornell ME/CFS Collaborative Research Center / Weill Cornell / Ithaca College)
- **Year:** 2024
- **Journal:** Cell Reports Medicine, Vol. 5, No. 1, article 101373
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.xcrm.2023.101373
- **PMC:** PMC10829790
- **BibTeX key:** Vu2024
- **Source:** Europe PMC XML (full text)

## Key Contribution

The first single-cell RNA-seq (scRNA-seq) study of the peripheral-blood immune system in ME/CFS profiled both at baseline (BL) and 24 hours after a standardized cardiopulmonary exercise test (CPET) used to provoke post-exertional malaise (PEM). Two distinct immune-arm findings emerge:

1. **Baseline:** Classical monocytes show the strongest and most reproducible transcriptomic dysregulation, with upregulation of chemokine/cytokine signaling genes consistent with aberrant priming for tissue migration. A subset of monocytes within each patient is transcriptomically "diseased" (predicted-diseased, pD), and the fraction of pD monocytes per individual correlates with disease severity (MFI-20 fatigue score, SF-36, PEM severity).

2. **Post-CPET (PEM provocation):** Platelets are the only cell type showing marked transcriptomic change after exercise in ME/CFS patients versus controls. At BL, ME/CFS platelets display a gene expression pattern consistent with older platelets (reduced platelet-function transcripts, elevated ribosomal/translational machinery genes). After CPET, the platelet transcriptome in ME/CFS patients shifts toward the normal profile — suggesting either selective removal of dysregulated platelets (e.g., through microclot formation) or an influx of fresher platelets. No other cell type changes significantly after exercise challenge in ME/CFS.

The study constitutes a scRNA-seq atlas of the ME/CFS circulating immune system and provides the first cell-type-resolved provoked-immune signature paired with an objective PEM induction protocol.

## Methods

**Study design:** Observational, cross-sectional (BL) plus within-person paired (BL vs. 24 h post-CPET) scRNA-seq study. PBMCs collected before the COVID-19 pandemic, ruling out long-COVID confounding.

**Cohort:** 30 ME/CFS patients and 28 sedentary healthy controls matched for sex and BMI. Sub-cohorts separated by sex for certain analyses (female cohort: 20 cases, 20 controls). Samples were pre-COVID pandemic.

**ME/CFS case definition:** Cornell ME/CFS Collaborative Research Center clinical protocol (Weill Cornell Medical College and Ithaca College IRBs). Cases required objective evidence of physiological dysfunction: a significant decrease in peak VO₂ across two CPETs. Self-reported PEM confirmed by modified CFS severity score. Broader symptom burden assessed via MFI-20 (fatigue) and SF-36v2 (general health/quality of life). Specific ME/CFS consensus criteria version not named in the text, but standard research criteria applied via the Cornell center protocol.

**Exercise provocation:** Single CPET; PBMCs collected 24 h post-CPET (PC). This design is pre-specified as "symptom provocation" to capture gene-expression changes coincident with PEM onset.

**scRNA-seq platform:** 10X Chromium Single Cell 3′ v3; ~5,000 PBMCs per sample; average sequencing depth 136 million reads per sample; integration with Seurat v4 + Harmony; 29 clusters identified; 28 clusters annotated to known immune cell types.

**Additional assays:**
- Bulk RNA-seq of purified classical monocytes (CD14+CD16−) from an independent validation cohort (4 female ME/CFS, 4 female controls) at PC
- Mass-spec proteomics of classical monocytes (3 female cases, 3 female controls)
- Bulk RNA-seq of platelet-derived plasma particles (large particles enriched by low-speed centrifugation; 3 cases, 3 controls; BL and PC; validated CD41+/CD45−/CD235a− by flow cytometry)
- CellChat intercellular communication modeling (female BL cohort; clusters downsampled to 500 cells)
- Positive unlabeled (PU) machine learning to classify individual monocytes as predicted-diseased (pD) or predicted-normal (pN) within patient samples

**Analytical methods:** Seurat FindMarkers for DEG; GSEA for pathway enrichment; pseudobulk DEG analysis; paired ΔRNA metric per individual for exercise-response analysis; CellChat for ligand-receptor communication modeling; Calinski-Harabasz index for cluster quality; Spearman correlation of pD monocyte fraction with clinical severity scores.

**GEO accession:** GSE214284 (single-cell and bulk RNA-seq; de-identified and publicly available as of publication).

## Key Findings

### Baseline immune dysregulation

**Broad landscape:** Most cell types show modest or no dysregulation. The strongest signals concentrate in:
- Classical monocytes (clusters 2, 9, 10) — the largest signal by gene count and pathway enrichment
- CD4+ T cells (naive and effector/memory, clusters 0/1) — second-strongest
- Cytotoxic NK cells (cluster 3)

**Cross-cell-type finding — translational repression:** Downregulation of ribosomal protein genes (cytoplasmic translation gene set, GSEA NES <0) is observed across multiple cell types (monocytes, NK cells, T cells) at both BL and PC. This broad translational quiescence is consistent with a less proliferative or more energy-conserving immune state.

**Classical monocyte dysregulation:**
- Upregulation of chemokine/cytokine signaling pathways: cell migration by cytokines/chemokines, IL-10 signaling, CCL pathway genes (CCL3, CCL4, CCL5, CCL20), CSF2 (GM-CSF; also elevated in ME/CFS plasma/extracellular vesicles), CSF3 (G-CSF), RETN (Resistin)
- Downregulation of CXCR4 (tissue retention receptor), TMEM176B (anti-inflammatory DC-maturation brake)
- Upregulation of CCL4 is the single strongest intra-individual signal in paired pD vs. pN analysis; elevated CCL4 is more prominent in females than males
- The overall pattern is consistent with monocytes aberrantly primed for tissue migration (to joints, adipose tissue, and inflamed tissue) rather than normal circulating homeostasis

**Monocyte heterogeneity and disease-severity correlation:**
- PU machine learning applied to scRNA-seq reveals that ME/CFS patients harbor a *heterogeneous* monocyte pool: only a fraction of classical monocytes per patient are transcriptomically "diseased" (pD); the remainder resemble controls (pN)
- The % pD monocytes per patient is stable between BL and PC (Spearman correlation between time points is significant)
- The % pD monocytes correlates with MFI-20 fatigue score, SF-36 physical component summary, and PEM severity — establishing a quantitative immune → disease-severity link
- pD cells specifically upregulate cytosolic DNA sensing, cytokine-receptor interaction, and CCL chemokine signaling genes; they downregulate IFN-γ pathway genes (suggesting reduced IFN-γ–mediated inflammatory resolution in the most dysregulated cells)

**CellChat intercellular communication (female BL cohort):**
- In controls, a diverse network of intercellular interactions is predicted
- In ME/CFS patients, monocytes dominate the overall information exchange; monocyte-to-T-cell and monocyte-to-NK-cell signaling is elevated; monocyte self-interactions increase
- CCL pathway upregulation in monocytes drives increased monocyte-to-platelet communication (via CCL3/CCL5 → platelet receptors) — a pro-inflammatory monocyte–platelet crosstalk signal
- 782 upregulated and 1,046 downregulated ligand-receptor pairs in patients vs. controls

### Post-CPET changes (PEM provocation)

**Platelets are the unique PEM-responsive cell type:**
- Paired analysis of ΔRNA (PC/BL ratio) comparing ME/CFS vs. controls finds significant GSEA enrichment only in platelets; all other cell types show negligible post-exercise change
- At BL, ME/CFS platelets are enriched for ribosomal protein / translational machinery transcripts and depleted for platelet-function gene sets (platelet activation, regulation of platelet activation) — a pattern consistent with older circulating platelets whose functional mRNA has degraded while more stable ribosomal transcripts remain
- After CPET, the platelet transcriptome in ME/CFS patients shifts toward the control profile (platelet-function gene sets normalized; ribosomal enrichment resolved)
- This shift is absent in controls (who already have normal platelet transcriptomes) and represents an ME/CFS-specific post-exercise platelet response
- Validated in bulk RNA-seq of platelet-derived plasma particles (CD41+/CD45−/CD235a−) from an independent 3+3 cohort at both BL and PC

**Two mechanistic models proposed:**
1. Exercise induces microclot formation that removes the pool of dysregulated (older, platelet-function-depleted) platelets, leaving a normal platelet population post-CPET
2. Exercise triggers an influx of newly released, transcriptomically normal platelets from megakaryocytes (plus possible concurrent clearance of older platelets)

Both models are consistent with the known 6–24 h half-life of platelet transcriptomes (mRNA inherited from megakaryocytes and degrading without replenishment). Authors note that fibrin amyloid microclots have been reported in ME/CFS and long COVID (though with smaller microclot loads in ME/CFS), which fits model 1.

**Adaptive immune cells (T cells, NK cells, B cells) show no significant post-exercise transcriptome change** in ME/CFS patients vs. controls, in contrast to the monocyte (stable BL dysregulation) and platelet (BL dysregulation, post-CPET normalization) findings.

## Relevance

This paper is directly relevant to question:0011 (mitochondrial/bioenergetic basis of PEM) and constitutes a key data point for hypothesis:0001 (shared dysregulated attractor) and hypothesis:0003 (immune-exhaustion feedback loop). Its contributions map onto the project's PAIS framework as follows:

**1. Immune arm of the PEM bioenergetic model (question:0011, Che2025 link):**
Che2025's bioenergetic model of PEM proposes that an exertion-triggered innate immune response feeds back onto metabolic/mitochondrial failure (the immune↔metabolic loop). Vu et al. provide the *cell-type resolution* for the immune arm of that loop: the PEM-associated immune response is not a diffuse change across all PBMC cell types, but is specifically platelet-localized. The BL monocyte dysregulation represents a chronic immune activation baseline (consistent with Che2025's elevated innate activation), while the post-CPET platelet shift represents the PEM-specific immune event. Crucially, the fact that most immune cell types (including T cells, NK cells, B cells) do not change after CPET suggests that the immune trigger of PEM is not primarily adaptive immune cell activation but involves the innate arm (platelets, monocyte-platelet crosstalk).

**2. Quantitative immune → severity link (hypothesis:0001 — dysregulated attractor):**
The % pD classical monocytes per patient correlates with objective severity metrics (MFI-20, SF-36 PCS, PEM severity). This is one of the few scRNA-seq demonstrations of a continuous immune parameter that tracks within-syndrome severity — not just case vs. control — consistent with the attractor model's prediction that patients with more co-active dysfunction nodes are more severe.

**3. Monocyte priming and tissue recruitment (hypothesis:0003 — immune-exhaustion feedback):**
The upregulation of CCL3/4/5, CSF2, CSF3, RETN in classical monocytes — interpreted as aberrant migration priming — suggests that ME/CFS monocytes are not merely activated in blood but are being driven toward tissue compartments. This would be consistent with monocyte-derived macrophage accumulation in tissues, contributing to the chronic inflammatory environment that sustains the exhaustion-feedback loop (hypothesis:0003). The pD monocyte population downregulates ATF3 (an IFN-response and CCL4-suppression regulator) and TMEM176B (DC maturation brake), suggesting loss of anti-inflammatory brakes within the most dysregulated cells.

**4. Platelet-microclot connection and thromboinflammation:**
The BL platelet transcriptome (old-platelet phenotype, reduced activation capacity) followed by post-exercise normalization is mechanistically consistent with microclot formation during PEM — connecting this immune finding to the thromboinflammation concept (topic:thromboinflammation). If exercise-induced microclotting is the mechanism of platelet clearance, it positions platelet-monocyte crosstalk (via CCL3/CCL5) as a potential initiating signal for vascular events during PEM.

**5. ME/CFS vs. long COVID (topic:mecfs-long-covid-convergence):**
The cohort predates COVID-19, providing clean ME/CFS-only data. The authors explicitly frame their scRNA-seq atlas as an ideal comparison dataset for future long-COVID studies to test whether classical monocyte dysregulation is shared across post-infectious triggers.

**6. Case definition and design rigor:**
Objective CPET-based VO₂ decline criterion adds diagnostic confidence beyond self-report. The pre-COVID sampling eliminates long-COVID confounding. However, the specific ME/CFS consensus criteria (Fukuda, CCC, ICC) are not stated in the text; the Cornell center protocol is referenced but not named.

## Limitations

1. **Single 24 h post-CPET time point:** PEM can persist for days to weeks. This study captures only the 24 h window; longer time courses after exercise provocation may reveal different or additional immune shifts.

2. **PBMC-only (no tissue):** The study profiles circulating PBMCs; if monocytes are aberrantly migrating to tissues (as the CCL gene signature suggests), the most dysregulated macrophages may have already left the blood compartment, leading to underestimation of monocyte dysfunction. Tissue biopsies or bronchoalveolar lavage were not performed.

3. **Modest cohort size and sex matching:** 30 patients and 28 controls; sub-cohorts for monocyte RNA-seq (4+4), proteomics (3+3), and platelet RNA-seq (3+3) are very small. CellChat and some sub-analyses focused on the female cohort (20+20), limiting male-specific conclusions.

4. **ME/CFS case definition not explicitly stated:** The specific consensus criteria applied (Fukuda, CCC, or ICC) are not named in the methods; the Cornell center protocol reference must be followed for full reproducibility.

5. **Platelet microclot model is speculative:** The BL → PC platelet transcriptome shift is consistent with microclot-based platelet removal, but no direct microclot measurement (e.g., fluorescent fibrin staining) was performed in this study. The mechanism remains a hypothesis generated from the transcriptome data.

6. **CellChat communication modeling is inferential:** Ligand-receptor interactions predicted from co-expression do not confirm actual signaling; CellChat predictions require functional validation.

7. **Cross-cell-type translational repression not mechanistically resolved:** The broad ribosomal gene downregulation across cell types is noted but not mechanistically explained; p53, mTOR, and other regulatory pathways are proposed but not tested.

8. **No long COVID comparator in this dataset:** The pre-COVID cohort design eliminates confounding but means no direct ME/CFS vs. long COVID comparison is possible from this single dataset.

**Questions this raises for the project:**

1. Does the % pD monocyte metric replicate in long COVID cohorts, and does it correlate with long-COVID severity in the same quantitative way as in ME/CFS? (Tests cross-PAIS sharing of the immune severity quantifier.)
2. Can the BL → PC platelet transcriptome shift (the PEM immune signature) be replicated in long COVID patients using the same CPET provocation protocol? (Central to question:0011 — immune arm of PEM shared vs. trigger-specific.)
3. What is the identity of the megakaryocyte population producing the dysregulated platelets at BL — is megakaryopoiesis itself abnormal in ME/CFS, or is platelet transcriptome aging accelerated by elevated monocyte-platelet crosstalk (CCL3/CCL5 signaling)?
4. Does direct microclot quantification (fibrin staining, flow cytometry) before and after CPET confirm the platelet-clearance-by-microclotting model proposed here?
