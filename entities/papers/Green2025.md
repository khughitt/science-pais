# A systematic review and meta-analysis of the impact of vaccination on prevention of long COVID

- **Authors:** Rhiannon Green, Zoe Marjenberg, Gregory Y. H. Lip, Amitava Banerjee, Juan Wisnivesky, Brendan C. Delaney, Michael J. Peluso, Elke Wynberg, Sultan Abduljawad
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal:** Nature Communications
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s41467-025-65302-0
- **Article:** 16:10326
- **BibTeX key:** Green2025

## Key Contribution

Green2025 is the most up-to-date systematic-review anchor found for `task:t010`. It focuses on
COVID-19 vaccination as prevention of long COVID, including Omicron-era and booster-dose evidence.

## Methods

The review identified 31 observational studies. Eleven were suitable for pairwise meta-analysis. The
authors separated any vaccination versus none, primary-course vaccination versus none, booster
vaccination versus none, and booster versus primary-course vaccination.

## Key Findings

- Any pre-infection vaccination was associated with lower odds of long COVID versus no vaccination
  (pooled OR 0.77, 95% CI 0.70-0.85).
- Primary-course vaccination was associated with lower odds versus no vaccination (OR 0.81,
  95% CI 0.79-0.83).
- Booster vaccination was associated with lower odds versus no vaccination (OR 0.74,
  95% CI 0.63-0.86).
- Booster vaccination was associated with additional protection versus primary-course vaccination
  (OR 0.77, 95% CI 0.65-0.92).

## Relevance

This supports the `question:0012` prevention answer at the population/observational level: vaccination
before infection is associated with reduced subsequent long-COVID risk, including in Omicron-era data.
The finding is consistent with `hypothesis:0004` because vaccination can reduce acute infection,
severity, inflammatory burden, and viral burden before the chronic state is established.

## Limitations

The certainty is low: all contributing studies are observational, long-COVID definitions vary, severity
stratification is generally absent, and heterogeneity is high in several pooled estimates. Therefore this
is not a clean mechanism-specific test of antigen burden, and it should not be used to promote
`hypothesis:0004` without a more direct acute-burden mediator analysis.
