# A 24-month National Cohort Study examining long-term effects of COVID-19 in children and young people

## Key Contribution

CLoCk 24-month follow-up showing why pediatric long-COVID definitions must separate broad symptom
reporting from persistent, impairment-aware post-COVID condition.

## Methods

- **Design:** national cohort of children and young people aged 11-17 at SARS-CoV-2 PCR testing.
- **Setting:** England, recruited through the UKHSA testing database.
- **Participants:** 12,632 respondents at 24 months from 31,012 eligible invitees.
- **Groups:** initial test-negative/no subsequent positive, initial test-negative/subsequent positive,
  initial test-positive/no reinfection, and initial test-positive/reinfected.
- **Outcome:** operationalized Delphi pediatric PCC research definition, symptom burden, validated health
  scales, and longitudinal consistency across 3, 6, 12, and 24 months.

## Key Findings

- Broad symptoms were common across all infection-status groups at 24 months.
- Reinfected children and young people had the highest symptom burden; never-positive/no-reported
  infection had the lowest.
- In the longitudinal subset, 7.2% of index-positive participants consistently met the PCC research
  definition at 3, 6, 12, and 24 months.
- Persistent PCC was associated with a stable multi-symptom burden and worse health/function measures.

## Relevance

This paper is the main pediatric warning against single-timepoint, broad symptom prevalence. It supports
longitudinal, impairment-aware case definitions for pediatric PAIS.

## Limitations

- Response rate at 24 months was about 41%, with demographic differences between responders and
  nonresponders.
- By 24 months, infection-status separation is complicated by widespread infection, vaccination, and
  self-reported reinfection.
- Symptom data are not paired to deep mechanistic biomarkers.
