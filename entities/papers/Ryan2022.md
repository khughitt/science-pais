# Long-term perturbation of the peripheral immune system months after SARS-CoV-2 infection

- **Authors:** Feargal J. Ryan, Christopher M. Hope, Makutiro G. Masavuli, Miriam A. Lynn, Zelalem A. Mekonnen, Arthur Eng Lip Yeow, Pablo Garcia-Valtanen, Zahraa Al-Delfi, Jason Gummow, Catherine Ferguson, Stephanie O'Connor, Benjamin A. J. Reddi, Pravin Hissaria, David Shaw, Chuan Kok-Lim, Jonathan M. Gleadle, Michael R. Beard, Simon C. Barry, Branka Grubor-Bauk, David J. Lynn
- **Year:** 2022
- **Journal:** BMC Medicine, vol. 20, no. 1, p. 26
- **DOI:** 10.1186/s12916-021-02228-6
- **BibTeX key:** Ryan2022
- **Source:** PDF

## Key Contribution

This study provides the most longitudinally comprehensive multi-modal characterization of the peripheral immune system in COVID-19 convalescents at 12, 16, and 24 weeks post-infection (wpi), integrating anti-Spike/anti-RBD serology, deep immunophenotyping of ~130 immune cell subpopulations, and whole-blood RNA sequencing on 69 convalescent individuals plus 14 healthy controls. The central finding is that SARS-CoV-2 infection causes persistent, broad perturbations to both innate and adaptive immune cell populations and the blood transcriptome for at least 6 months post-infection, even in individuals who had mild disease — and that a specific subset of these perturbations is associated with referral to a long COVID clinic. The study demonstrates that failed immunological return to baseline at the transcriptional level corresponds to clinical long COVID symptom persistence, directly instantiating the PAIS frame of incomplete homeostatic recovery.

## Methods

**Study design:** Prospective longitudinal cohort. Blood collected at 12, 16, and 24 weeks (±14 days) post first positive PCR test from 69 COVID-19 convalescent individuals (35 male, 36 female) recruited via the Central Adelaide Health Network (CALHN), South Australia. Age-matched healthy controls (HCs; n=14) had no history of SARS-CoV-2 infection, no significant systemic disease, and negative anti-Spike/anti-RBD serology.

**Disease severity classification:** NIH scoring — mild (n=50), moderate (n=6), severe (n=7), critical (n=6). 58% were hospitalized during acute illness.

**Cohort uniqueness:** All samples collected in South Australia where strict border control eliminated community virus transmission during the collection period (March–April 2020), eliminating re-infection confounding. No participant received a COVID-19 vaccine at the time of sample collection.

**Long COVID ascertainment:** 21/69 (30.4%) were referred to a dedicated long COVID clinic (South Australian State health service). Symptom questionnaire administered at ~18 months post-infection was completed by 83% (n=57).

**Serology:** Anti-Spike and anti-RBD IgG, IgG1, IgG3, IgM, IgA ELISA (area-under-curve) at all three timepoints using recombinant prefusion Spike ectodomain (HexaPro mutations) and RBD (residues 319–541).

**Immunophenotyping:** Multi-parameter flow cytometry on PBMCs with 3 master-mix panels (lineage 15-color; memory 8-color; T helper/Treg 14-color) using a BD FACS Symphony. ~130 distinct immune subpopulations enumerated including deep CD4 and CD8 compartments, NK cells, B cells, monocyte subsets, granulocytes, and LD neutrophils. Statistical significance: Wilcoxon rank sum tests with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction (FDR < 0.05).

**RNA-Seq:** Total RNA sequencing of 138 blood samples (PAXgene Blood RNA kit; 2×150 bp paired-end reads; Illumina NovaSeq 6000; mean 68.2 million read pairs/sample). Alignment to GRCh38 with HISAT2; DEG calling with EdgeR glmLRT (adjusting for sex and batch). Pathway analysis via hypergeometric test; Gene Ontology and REACTOME enrichment. Blood Transcriptional Module (BTM) activity scored per-sample using GSVA (256 modules). Correlation network analysis between BTMs, immune cell populations, and serology visualized in Cytoscape.

## Key Findings

**Symptom burden (long COVID case series)**
- 57.9% of convalescents reported at least one ongoing symptom at ~18 months.
- Most common: fatigue 40.4%, dyspnoea 35.1%, worsened memory/concentration 35.1%, decreased muscle strength 33.3%.
- 30.4% (21/69) referred to long COVID clinic. Referral was not significantly associated with age (P=0.1), sex (P=0.9), or acute COVID-19 severity (P=0.09).

**Serology (anti-Spike and anti-RBD antibodies)**
- Anti-Spike and anti-RBD IgG responses were largely stable up to 24 wpi and correlated positively with disease severity.
- Severe/critical convalescents had significantly higher anti-Spike IgG and IgG3 at 12, 16, and 24 wpi vs. mild/moderate. At 24 wpi specifically, severe/critical had significantly higher anti-RBD IgG, IgG3, and IgM.
- Anti-Spike and anti-RBD IgA and IgM declined over time, consistent with expected kinetics. Anti-Spike/RBD total IgG (not subclasses) were significantly correlated at all timepoints.

**Deep immunophenotyping — innate immune changes**
- Significant lymphopenia in convalescents at 12 and 16 wpi (Fig. 2E).
- CD56++ NK cells significantly elevated at 12 and 16 wpi (Fig. 2J).
- Total granulocytes significantly increased at all three timepoints (12, 16, 24 wpi).
- CXCR3+ LD (low-density) neutrophils — actively recruited to sites of tissue damage — elevated at 12 wpi but returned to baseline by 16 wpi.
- CD14+CD16+ neutrophils significantly decreased at 12 and 16 wpi.
- HLA-DR+ (activated, antigen-presenting) inflammatory monocytes and HLA-DR- regulatory monocytes significantly increased at 12 wpi.

**Deep immunophenotyping — adaptive immune changes**
- CD3+ T cells significantly increased at 12 wpi (% of lymphocytes); CD4+ effector memory (EM) pool significantly reduced at 12 and 16 wpi.
- Migratory central memory (CM) CD4+ T cells (CCR7+CD62L-) reduced at all timepoints.
- CD19+ B cells significantly increased at 12 and 16 wpi; CD38+CD27+ memory B cells elevated at 16 wpi.
- Th lineage perturbations: significant decrease in Th9 cells at all timepoints; increased proportions of Th17 and Th22 central memory cells at all timepoints; increased Th2/22 cells at 16 wpi and Th2/22 memory at 12 wpi.
- Tfh (follicular helper) compartment: Tfh1 significantly elevated at 12 and 16 wpi; total TfhR (follicular regulatory T cells) significantly reduced at 12 and 16 wpi, recovering to baseline at 24 wpi. TfhR2, TfhR22, and TfhR2/22 all reduced at 12 and 16 wpi — consistent with early TfhR suppression that licenses B cell help and germinal centre formation.
- Treg compartment: naïve Tregs significantly increased at all timepoints; CM and EM Tregs significantly decreased at 12 and 16 wpi; TEMRA Tregs (effector memory re-expressing CD45RA) significantly increased at 12 and 16 wpi. ThR2 Tregs decreased at 12 and 16 wpi; ThR22 and ThR2/22 Tregs decreased at all timepoints — suggesting persistent block in tissue-homing regulatory lineage commitment.

**Blood transcriptome perturbations**
- >950 DEGs (FDR<0.05, fold change >1.25) at 12 wpi compared to HCs; 738 upregulated, 230 downregulated.
- DEGs persisted with >250 DEGs still identified at 24 wpi.
- Strongly enriched upregulated pathways at all three timepoints: transcription/translation/ribosome biosynthesis, viral mRNA translation, rRNA processing, innate immune system, neutrophil degranulation, cytokine signaling.
- Strongly enriched downregulated pathways: oxidative phosphorylation (platelet-localized), platelet activation/signaling/aggregation, hemostasis, respiratory electron transport.
- Differential expression of nuclear vs. mitochondrially encoded oxidative phosphorylation genes (nuclear OXPHOS upregulated; mitochondrial OXPHOS downregulated) — a pattern previously associated with cognitive impairment.
- Elevated markers of inflammation/innate immunity: S100A8 (calcium-binding protein, innate biomarker), HMGB1, AZI2, GZMA (granzyme A), MALAT1 (non-coding RNA regulating immunity/cell cycle) — significantly elevated at 12 wpi compared to HCs.

**Long COVID-specific transcriptional signatures (24 wpi)**
- 446 genes significantly differentially expressed at 24 wpi in individuals referred to the long COVID clinic vs. those not referred (FDR<0.05, fold change >1.25). These differences were not evident at 12 or 16 wpi (when all convalescents showed substantial DEGs vs. HCs), suggesting divergent recovery trajectories emerged by 6 months.
- Platelet-related pathways were most strongly enriched among downregulated genes in long COVID referrals: platelet factor 4 (PF4), platelet glycoprotein IX (GP9), thrombopoietin receptor (MPL), coagulation factor XIII A chain (F13A1) all significantly reduced.
- IFN-I-inducible genes MX1, OAS3, OASL were significantly decreased in long COVID referrals — suggesting blunted type I interferon response.
- S100B (neurological damage biomarker) was significantly increased in long COVID referrals.
- Cell type enrichment analysis showed significant downregulation of platelet/megakaryocyte gene sets and significant enrichment of CD4+ T cell gene sets among upregulated genes in long COVID referrals.

**BTM network analysis**
- 80 of 256 BTMs differentially active in convalescents. At 12 wpi, >30 different immune cell populations correlated with BTM activity scores.
- Monocyte-, DC-, neutrophil-, and inflammasome-related BTMs were positively correlated with each other and with metabolism-related BTMs.
- Platelet-related BTMs (M32.0, M32.1) significantly negatively correlated with anti-RBD IgM at 16 wpi.
- At 24 wpi, 48 BTMs differentially active by long COVID clinic referral status, including platelet, cell cycle, and immune-related BTMs.
- Inverse correlations between dysregulated BTMs and anti-Spike/anti-RBD antibody responses — suggesting prolonged transcriptional dysregulation may be associated with reduced antibody durability.

## Relevance

This paper is directly central to the project research question (research-question:post-acute-infection-syndromes) and the PAIS frame along multiple axes:

**1. Empirical demonstration of failed homeostatic recovery.** The core finding — persistent, broad immune perturbations for at least 6 months even in mild COVID-19 — is a quantitative, multi-modal demonstration of what the PAIS frame defines as failed recovery of immune homeostasis after acute infection. The longitudinal design (12/16/24 wpi vs. age-matched HCs) makes the "failure to return to baseline" inference direct, not inferred from cross-sectional snapshots. This paper is the strongest single source in the project for quantifying the timeline and breadth of post-COVID immune non-resolution.

**2. Divergent recovery trajectories as the mechanistic driver of long COVID.** The observation that long COVID-referral-associated transcriptional differences only emerged at 24 wpi — not at 12 or 16 wpi when both groups showed similar perturbations — provides a strong mechanistic framing: PAIS is not a failure to begin recovery, but a failure to complete it. Most convalescents normalize their transcriptome by ~6 months; those who develop long COVID do not. This "incomplete recovery" frame is the central working model for this project.

**3. Platelet/megakaryocyte dysregulation as a long COVID-specific signature.** The strong downregulation of platelet gene sets, platelet activation pathways, and multiple coagulation factors specifically in long COVID referrals (not in non-referred convalescents at the same timepoint) provides a concrete molecular signature to investigate. The link between this signature and fatigue — given reports of thrombocytopenia as a COVID complication and pre-existing literature linking peripheral blood gene expression in PBMCs to fatigue following infectious mononucleosis — connects to the shared post-infectious failure mode hypothesis (see Komaroff2023, Peppercorn2023).

**4. Mitochondrial OXPHOS dysregulation and cognitive symptoms.** The differential nuclear vs. mitochondrial encoding of dysregulated OXPHOS genes, and the known association of mitochondrial PBMC dysfunction with cognitive impairment in other contexts, provides a mechanistic link between the transcriptional signature and the brain fog/memory/concentration symptoms that are among the most disabling features of long COVID and ME/CFS.

**5. Multi-modal systems integration strategy.** The BTM-serology-immunophenotyping network analysis demonstrates that the immune perturbations are not reducible to changes in a single cell population, but reflect a systems-level dysregulation. This supports the project's computational analysis goal of multi-modal integration across blood data types.

## Limitations

- **PBMC-enriched immunophenotyping:** Flow cytometry was performed on PBMCs (not whole blood) to enrich for rare lymphocyte subsets, which means mature granulocytes are removed during Ficoll isolation. This biases cell proportion estimates and the authors note it explicitly; tissue-resident populations are invisible.
- **Relatively small sample of severe/critical patients:** n=7 severe, n=6 critical at baseline. Multiple comparison correction with small subgroups reduces power to detect severity-stratified differences; authors acknowledge this.
- **Long COVID case definition by clinic referral, not standardized criteria:** Referral to the long COVID clinic was not based on a published symptom score or clinical case definition. Referral was associated with self-reported symptom burden (Fig. 5A) but may miss symptomatic individuals who were not referred. The overlap between "referred" and "symptomatic" is imperfect.
- **Retrospective symptom questionnaire at ~18 months:** Symptom data were collected retrospectively at a single time-point (~18 months post-infection) with recall bias risk. The questionnaire was completed by 83% (n=57) of convalescents, not 100%.
- **No T cell functional assays or antigen-specific measurements:** The flow cytometry panel measured phenotypes (maturation, homing receptor expression) but did not assess T cell function (cytokine production, cytotoxicity, exhaustion markers like PD-1/LAG-3 in detail), nor did it measure antigen-specific T cell frequencies to SARS-CoV-2 peptide pools.
- **No dendritic cell measurements:** The authors note their flow cytometry panel did not include DC markers, a recognized limitation given reports of DC dysregulation in COVID-19.
- **Transcriptomics at protein level unverified:** Transcriptional changes were not validated against proteomic data; it is possible some dysregulated transcripts do not translate to proportional protein-level differences.
- **Australian pre-vaccination, pre-variant cohort:** All infections in March–April 2020 (original Wuhan strain); no vaccination prior to sampling. Findings may not generalize to Omicron-era infections or vaccinated individuals who subsequently contracted COVID-19.
- **No longitudinal follow-up beyond 24 wpi:** The study ends at 6 months. Whether long COVID immune perturbations resolve further, plateau, or worsen beyond 24 wpi is not addressed.
- **Absolute vs. relative cell counts:** All flow cytometry data expressed as proportions (% of parent gate), not absolute counts — a recognized limitation when interpreting whether reduced proportions reflect migration out of blood vs. true depletion.

## Model / Tool Availability

- **RNA-Seq data:** Deposited in Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) accession GSE169087. Count tables, metadata, and R code deposited.
- **R code:** Deposited to the Lynn Laboratory BitBucket repository (https://bitbucket.org/lynnlab/covid-sa).
- **No pre-trained models or stand-alone tools** released. The BTM analysis framework uses publicly available GSVA and limma R packages with pre-defined BTM gene sets from Li et al. (MSigDB-compatible).

## Follow-up

- **Antigen persistence studies:** The blunted IFN-I signature (MX1, OAS3, OASL) in long COVID referrals raises the question of whether residual viral antigen drives chronic innate signaling that eventually exhausts the response. See Peluso2024 (SARS-CoV-2 plasma antigen persistence in post-acute COVID-19) for direct evidence.
- **ME/CFS molecular convergence:** The mitochondrial OXPHOS dissociation, platelet dysregulation, and fatigue link warrants cross-comparison with ME/CFS transcriptomic datasets. Peppercorn2023 (PBMC proteome in long COVID vs. ME/CFS) and Komaroff2023 directly address this overlap.
- **Thrombocytopenia mechanism:** PF4, GP9, MPL, F13A1 downregulation needs validation against platelet count measurements (not performed in this study) and against the growing thrombocytopenia literature in COVID-19 (Ryan et al. cite refs. 57–59).
- **Longitudinal extension:** What happens beyond 24 wpi? Whether the divergent trajectories identified here stabilize, worsen, or eventually resolve requires studies with longer follow-up windows.
- **Causal inference on IFN-I suppression in long COVID:** Decreased MX1/OAS3/OASL is correlative; whether this reflects (a) ongoing antigen/viral persistence suppressing innate sensing, (b) a pre-existing deficiency in IFN-I signaling that predisposed to long COVID, or (c) exhaustion of innate signaling after chronic low-level stimulation remains undetermined. Studies with IFN-I stimulation assays on convalescent PBMCs would help.
- **Cross-PAIS comparison:** The South Australian cohort was uniquely controlled for re-infection confounding. Comparative studies using these methods in post-dengue, post-EBV, or post-Lyme disease cohorts would test whether the same recovery divergence pattern (early shared perturbation → bifurcating trajectory at 5–6 months) is a generalizable PAIS feature.
