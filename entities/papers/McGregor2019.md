## Key Contribution

This is the only published study (as of mid-2026) that performs a true **within-cohort, between-group PEM-stratified metabolomic contrast** in ME/CFS. By splitting 46 CCC-defined ME/CFS cases into those with recent PEM (PEM+, n=35, 7-day severity score ≥ threshold) versus those without (NoPEM, n=11) and comparing both against 25 healthy controls, it provides the closest existing template for designs that ask whether PEM status creates a separable molecular subgroup. The study's load-bearing finding is that serum hypoxanthine — a purine salvage metabolite and indirect index of ATP availability — tracks continuous PEM severity (r ≈ −0.35 whole-group, −0.43 for 12-month frequency), sitting alongside a hypermetabolic urinary excretion signature and evidence of systemic hypoacetylation during active PEM episodes.

## Methods

**Design:** Observational, cross-sectional. Single time-point blood and urine sampling in a clinical cohort.

**Case definition:** Canadian Consensus Criteria (CCC) for ME/CFS, with exclusionary criteria applied. All cases were currently symptomatic. Depression, anxiety, and stress comorbidities were screened with the DASS questionnaire.

**Cohort:** 46 ME/CFS cases (≈80–100% female; Australian Anglo-Celtic; CFS Discovery clinic, Donvale VIC) and 25 fatigue-free, age/sex-matched healthy controls (±5 years). Disease duration: NoPEM 8.7 ± 5.4 years, PEM 12.1 ± 9.7 years.

**PEM grouping:** Subjects completed a large symptom questionnaire adapted from chronic-pain research. PEM was scored on two axes:
- *7-day severity*: 0–4 scalar (acute/recent PEM burden)
- *12-month frequency*: 0–4 scalar (chronic PEM burden)

Thirty-five of 46 ME/CFS cases reported PEM in the last 7 days and were allocated to the **PEM group**; the remaining 11 were allocated to **NoPEM**. The NoPEM group had a 12-month frequency score of 2.8 ± 1.2 (versus 3.5 ± 0.6 in PEM), indicating they experience PEM chronically but were not in an active episode at sampling. Controls had near-zero scores on both axes.

**Crucially**, no adjustment was made for overall illness or fatigue severity. Fatigue 7-day (3.2 vs 3.7) and 12-month scores (3.8 vs 3.8) were nearly identical between NoPEM and PEM, meaning the two ME/CFS subgroups were matched on overall fatigue but differed only on PEM timing.

**Biosampling and metabolomics platform:** First-morning fasted blood (serum) and urine, stored at −80 °C. NMR spectroscopy on an 800 MHz Bruker Avance II; metabolite identification and quantification via Chenomx v6.1. **Platform coverage: 29 serum metabolites and 30 urine metabolites** (targeted NMR panel — not untargeted). A fecal metabolome was also measured. Standard serum biochemistry (commercial NATA-accredited laboratory) was run in parallel. Ethics: University of Melbourne HREC# 0723086 (2010); first specimens collected September 2010.

**Statistics:** ANOVA with post-hoc Tukey HSD, Spearman rank correlations, Kruskal-Wallis, Fisher's exact test; Bonferroni multiplicity correction applied. Significance threshold: p < 0.01 after correction.

## Key Findings

### PEM-group differences (PEM vs NoPEM vs Control)

**Serum:**
- Hypoxanthine: Controls 15.7 ± 12.2 μM >> PEM 6.6 ± 8.2 μM > NoPEM 3.6 ± 1.4 μM (both vs controls p < 0.001). Paradoxically NoPEM had *lower* hypoxanthine than PEM, suggesting NoPEM may represent a chronically depleted state without the partial hypermetabolic release seen in acute PEM.
- Lactate: Controls 637 >> NoPEM 339 > PEM 399 μM (reduced in both ME/CFS subgroups)
- Glucose: Elevated in both ME/CFS groups vs controls (1266 and 1189 vs 971 μM); elevated glucose:lactate ratio consistent with glycolytic block at pyruvate dehydrogenase.
- Phenylalanine: Reduced in both ME/CFS subgroups vs controls.

**Urine (the PEM-specific signal):**
- Acetate: NoPEM most depleted (37 μM vs controls 92 μM); PEM intermediate (63 μM). NoPEM vs PEM difference p < 0.01 — acetate loss is greatest in the chronic-quiet state.
- Methylhistidine: Elevated in PEM (358 μM) vs NoPEM (230 μM), indicating active skeletal muscle protein catabolism during PEM episodes.
- Mannitol: Elevated in PEM (258 μM) vs the severely depleted NoPEM (96 μM), suggesting intestinal barrier disruption is a feature of acute PEM.
- Serum acetate:urine acetate ratio correlated negatively with 7-day PEM severity (r = −0.44, p < 0.002), indicating greater urinary metabolite loss with higher PEM.

**Fecal:**
- Butyrate % elevated in NoPEM vs both PEM and controls — gut microbiome butyrate production apparently tracks quiescent periods.

### Continuous PEM severity correlations (whole group, all subjects, Spearman)

The principal correlates of **7-day PEM severity** in serum were:
- Hypoxanthine: r = −0.35 (p < 0.01)
- Phenylalanine: r = −0.40 (p < 0.01)
- Lactate: r = −0.33 (p < 0.01)
- Glucose: r = +0.31 (p < 0.01)

For **12-month PEM frequency**:
- Hypoxanthine: r = −0.43 (p < 0.001) — strongest correlate overall
- Phenylalanine: r = −0.42 (p < 0.001)

Within-ME/CFS group correlations (n=46) were largely non-significant for serum, consistent with restricted range within a diseased group. Within-ME/CFS urine correlates of 7-day PEM severity included total metabolite excretion (r = +0.38), mannitol (r = +0.43), serine (r = +0.42), acetate (r = +0.41), methylhistidine (r = +0.40), glucose (r = +0.37).

### Purine metabolism analysis

- Serum hypoxanthine:urate ratio depressed 3.5-fold (PEM) and 5.4-fold (NoPEM) versus controls, despite normal urate levels. This indicates reduced *synthesis/salvage* of hypoxanthine rather than accelerated degradation.
- Hypoxanthine:urate ratio strongly correlated with serum lactate (r = 0.77, p < 0.001) and acetate (r = 0.49, p < 0.001), linking purine availability to the glycolytic/acetylation block.
- The correlation between hypoxanthine and its purine ring precursor amino acids was preserved in both ME/CFS and controls (r ≈ 0.63–0.66), arguing the upstream synthesis pathway is intact; the bottleneck is downstream salvage or ATP utilization.

### Proposed mechanistic sequence

McGregor et al. interpret the data as follows: glycolytic block at pyruvate dehydrogenase → reduced acetate production → systemic hypoacetylation → HDAC upregulation (HDAC2 ×4, HDAC3 ×2 confirmed in prior work) → broad epigenetic and enzymatic dysregulation (≥1750 acetylation-dependent proteins) → failure to maintain purine salvage (hypoxanthine) → impaired ATP regeneration during exertion → muscle catabolism + renal metabolite loss → PEM. The hypermetabolic urinary excretion pattern during active PEM is interpreted as an emergency metabolite mobilization analogous to exercise-induced hypermetabolism in healthy subjects, but occurring at lower exertion thresholds.

## Relevance

**To question:0015 (PEM requirement and cross-study comparability):** This paper provides the most direct existing evidence that PEM status creates a partially separable metabolomic subgroup within ME/CFS. The PEM vs NoPEM contrast shows qualitative differences in at least urinary methylhistidine and mannitol (PEM elevated) and urine acetate (NoPEM lower), even though the groups had near-identical overall fatigue severity scores. This supports the premise that PEM-stratified designs can reveal biology not captured by fatigue-only groupings. However, the study does not demonstrate that PEM-stratification *improves reproducibility across studies* — it shows internal PEM-correlated variance within one study. As a template for question:0015's comparison strategy, it is directly relevant but does not answer the question.

**To question:0011 (mitochondrial/bioenergetic basis of PEM):** Hypoxanthine depletion (reduced ATP salvage capacity), the glucose:lactate ratio anomaly (glycolytic block), and the hypermetabolic excretion pattern are all consistent with a bioenergetic lesion during PEM. The data support the "resting hypermetabolic/compensatory" pole of the bioenergetic direction-of-effect debate (q0011 sub-question table), but these are resting fasted samples — not provoked post-exertion. The NoPEM group's *more severe* hypoxanthine depletion than the active-PEM group suggests the chronic quiescent state may actually represent a deeper resting suppression, potentially reconcilable with the Naviaux2016 hypometabolic framing.

**To hypothesis:0006 (skeletal muscle ischemic mitochondrial myopathy):** Elevated urinary methylhistidine in the PEM group directly implicates skeletal muscle protein catabolism during PEM episodes, consistent with the hypothesis that muscle is a primary site of PEM pathology.

**To task t025 (PEM+ vs PEM− PASC molecular comparison):** This study is the design template. Its three-group structure (HC / NoPEM-ME/CFS / PEM-ME/CFS), PEM severity correlation approach, and multi-matrix metabolomics are the closest prior art. T025 would replicate and extend this in PASC with (a) fatigue severity adjustment, (b) standardized DSQ-PEM questionnaire, (c) larger n, (d) untargeted metabolomics/proteomics.

## Limitations

**Critical — no severity adjustment:** The study does not adjust for overall illness/fatigue severity. The PEM vs NoPEM split was made solely on 7-day PEM presence/absence, but the two groups had nearly identical 7-day fatigue severity (3.2 vs 3.7) and 12-month fatigue frequency (3.8 vs 3.8). Any metabolomic difference between PEM and NoPEM could therefore reflect *severity confounding* (the NoPEM group, despite similar fatigue, may have had a more severe chronic baseline metabolic depletion, as suggested by their lower hypoxanthine). **This is the single most important limitation for interpreting the PEM-specific signal: the study cannot separate a PEM-specific molecular signature from a general illness-severity marker.**

**Small N:** 46 ME/CFS cases (35 PEM, 11 NoPEM). The NoPEM subgroup (n=11) is severely underpowered for metabolomic comparisons; many between-group tests were non-significant within ME/CFS, which likely reflects power rather than absence of effect.

**Cross-sectional / single time-point:** Samples were collected at a single visit without exercise provocation. The "PEM" group reflects self-reported recent PEM, not a standardized provoked post-exertional state (e.g., CPET-based challenge). This means the molecular snapshot captures whatever state subjects happened to be in at clinic attendance, not a controlled exertional protocol.

**Non-standardized PEM instrument:** The PEM scores come from an in-house chronic-pain symptom questionnaire, not the DePaul Symptom Questionnaire PEM subscale (DSQ-PEM) or Canadian / ICC-defined operationalization. Pre-DSQ-PEM standardization (2019); cross-study replication using different instruments will be difficult.

**Narrow metabolomics panel:** Targeted NMR covering only 29 serum and 30 urine metabolites. No proteomics, transcriptomics, immune profiling, or lipidomics. Cannot determine whether the purine/acetylation signal is embedded in a broader pathway perturbation or is primary.

**Single ethnic cohort:** Australian Anglo-Celtic; generalizability to other populations uncertain.

**Sex imbalance:** ≈80–100% female across subgroups; too few males to assess whether the X-chromosome purine salvage mechanism the authors speculate about (HPRT1) is relevant.

**No longitudinal or repeated-measures design:** Cannot distinguish within-subject state changes from between-subject trait differences. The NoPEM group may simply be ME/CFS cases who were in a remission phase, not a biologically distinct subtype.

**Disease duration confound:** PEM group had longer disease duration (12.1 vs 8.7 years), which could independently predict metabolic chronicity.

**No correction for multiple comparisons across tables:** While Bonferroni correction was applied within each statistical test, the number of comparisons across tables 2–4 raises the risk of some false positives in the correlation analyses.

## Model / Tool Availability

No computational model, software tool, or public dataset was released. The metabolomic data were not deposited in a public repository (pre-FAIR practices for this era).

## Follow-up

**Papers to read next:**
- Armstrong et al. (2015, PLOS ONE) — the prior serum metabolomics paper in ME/CFS females by the same group that originally identified hypoxanthine depletion; provides the baseline for the PEM-stratified analysis here.
- Naviaux et al. (2016, PNAS) — the resting hypometabolic "dauer" model; comparison with McGregor2019's partial hypermetabolic PEM pattern is needed to resolve the direction-of-effect sub-question in q0011.
- Germain et al. (2022) — provoked (post-exercise) metabolomics in ME/CFS; the gold-standard comparison for whether a PEM-provoked design changes the findings.
- Che2025 — the most mechanistically complete provoked-challenge multi-omics study; provides direct comparison for the PEM signature.

**Questions this raises for the project:**
- Can the hypoxanthine depletion in McGregor2019 be replicated in PASC using the same NMR-targeted platform, and does it also separate PEM+ from PEM− PASC subjects?
- Does fatigue-severity adjustment (ANCOVA) within the McGregor2019 dataset eliminate or attenuate the PEM-group metabolomic differences? (Requires access to individual-level data.)
- The NoPEM group's *lower* hypoxanthine than the active-PEM group is counterintuitive — does this reflect chronic depletion in remission-phase patients, or a methodological artifact of small NoPEM n?
- Does the DSQ-PEM subscale correlate with the in-house PEM score used here, and does re-grouping by DSQ threshold change which subjects fall in the PEM vs NoPEM arms?
