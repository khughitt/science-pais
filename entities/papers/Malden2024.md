# Post-COVID conditions following COVID-19 vaccination

- **Authors:** Debbie E. Malden, In-Lu Amy Liu, Lei Qian, Lina S. Sy, Bruno J. Lewin, Dawn T. Asamura, Denison S. Ryan, Cassandra Bezi, Joshua T. B. Williams, Robyn Kaiser, Matthew F. Daley, Jennifer C. Nelson, David L. McClure, Ousseny Zerbo, Michelle L. Henninger, Candace C. Fuller, Eric S. Weintraub, Sharon Saydah, Sara Y. Tartof, et al.
- **Year:** 2024
- **Journal:** Nature Communications
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s41467-024-48022-9
- **Article:** 15:4101
- **BibTeX key:** Malden2024

## Key Contribution

Malden2024 is a large US Vaccine Safety Datalink matched cohort of vaccinated and unvaccinated
SARS-CoV-2-positive patients. It is useful because it conditions on infection and matches on test date,
site, age, sex, and acute-infection severity.

## Methods

The study matched 161,531 vaccinated SARS-CoV-2-positive cases to 161,531 unvaccinated cases across
eight integrated healthcare systems. Post-COVID conditions were new diagnoses in 13 categories from
30 days to 6 months after a positive test.

## Key Findings

Vaccinated cases had similar or lower risk in most PCC categories. Risk reductions of at least 10% were
reported for sensory, circulatory, blood/hematologic, skin/subcutaneous, and non-specific COVID-related
disorder categories. Mental-health diagnoses were slightly higher among vaccinated cases, plausibly
reflecting residual healthcare-seeking confounding.

## Relevance

This strengthens the `question:0012` vaccine-prevention signal because it uses an infection-conditioned,
severity-matched comparator design and broad post-COVID outcome categories.

## Limitations

The outcome is EHR-diagnosis based, not a validated symptom or biomarker index. Vaccinated and
unvaccinated people differ in healthcare utilization, influenza vaccination, prior infection, Medicaid
status, and other health-behavior proxies; matching and adjustment reduce but cannot remove those
differences. The finding is prevention/modification evidence, not a mechanism-specific antigen test.
