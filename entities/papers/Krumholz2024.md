# The PAX LC Trial: A Decentralized, Phase 2, Randomized, Double-Blind Study of Nirmatrelvir/Ritonavir Compared with Placebo/Ritonavir for Long COVID

## Key Contribution

This paper is the **trial protocol and design report** for the PAX LC trial (NCT05668091) — a fully decentralized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase-2 RCT of 15-day nirmatrelvir/ritonavir (Paxlovid) for established long COVID (n=100). It is not a results paper. Its primary contributions are (1) detailed description of a novel all-digital, decentralized trial infrastructure for long COVID that recruits nationwide without a physical clinic site, and (2) the trial's mechanistic rationale: targeting **viral persistence** as a potential cause of persistent symptoms. The companion results paper (Sawano/Bhattacharjee/Krumholz et al., Lancet Infectious Diseases, 2025) reported the primary endpoint negative: adjusted Day-28 PROMIS-29 PHSS change did not differ significantly between nirmatrelvir-ritonavir and placebo-ritonavir.

Together with STOP-PASC (Geng2024, JAMA Internal Medicine 2024 — the other published nirmatrelvir-for-long-COVID RCT), PAX-LC anchors the antiviral-treatment evidence base for PASC: two independent phase-2 RCTs of 15-day NMV/r both returned null primary endpoints in established, predominantly vaccinated long COVID.

## Methods

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Design | Phase 2, 1:1 randomized, double-blind, superiority, placebo-controlled RCT |
| Trial registration | NCT05668091 |
| Setting | Fully decentralized (48 contiguous US states); no physical trial site required |
| Funding | Pfizer Inc |
| N enrolled | 100 (49 NMV/r, 51 placebo-ritonavir) |
| Population | Community-dwelling adults with long COVID; highly symptomatic; confirmed COVID-19 |
| Case definition | Adults with documented prior SARS-CoV-2 infection and long-COVID symptoms beginning within 4 weeks of infection and persisting for at least 12 weeks |
| Intervention | Nirmatrelvir/ritonavir 300 mg/100 mg orally twice daily × 15 days vs placebo + ritonavir 100 mg twice daily × 15 days |
| Primary endpoint | Change in PROMIS-29 Physical Health Summary Score (PHSS) from baseline to Day 28, evaluated by mixed-model repeated measures (MMRM) |
| Key secondary endpoints | PROMIS-29 Mental Health Summary Score; all PROMIS-29 item domains; Modified GSQ-30 supplemental symptom questionnaire; COVID Core Outcome Measures for Recovery; EQ-5D-5L (utility score + items); Patient Global Impression of Severity (PGIS 1 & 2); Patient Global Impression of Change (PGIC 1 & 2); healthcare utilization |
| Recruitment | Patient advocacy groups, cultural ambassadors, social media platforms |
| EHR access | Participant-mediated platform enabling nationwide electronic health record retrieval |
| Drug delivery | Study drug shipped directly to participants' homes |
| Blood draws | Conducted at or near participants' homes |
| Daily monitoring | Web-based digital diary during treatment phase |
| Biomarker arm | Immunophenotyping to identify long COVID biomarkers and treatment responders |
| Sex breakdown | 66 female (66%), 34 male (34%) |

### Decentralized design rationale

Traditional site-based trials create barriers for long COVID patients, who often cannot travel, tolerate in-clinic schedules, or reside near academic medical centers. PAX LC addressed this through fully digital enrollment, home-based blood draws, mail-order study drug, and participant-mediated EHR acquisition — enabling nationwide recruitment across diverse geographies and demographics.

## Key Findings

This paper reports **design and methods only**, not efficacy results.

Results from the completed trial were published separately in:

> Krumholz HM, Sawano M, et al. "Nirmatrelvir-ritonavir versus placebo-ritonavir in individuals with long COVID in the USA (PAX LC): a double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled, phase 2, decentralised trial." *Lancet Infectious Diseases* (April 2025). DOI: 10.1016/S1473-3099(25)00073-8

Key results from that paper (PubMed/Lancet abstract; full-table extraction still deferred):

- **Primary endpoint: null.** Nirmatrelvir-ritonavir did not significantly improve the PROMIS-29 Physical Health Summary Score versus placebo-ritonavir at Day 28.
- **Safety:** Drug-related treatment-emergent adverse events were more common in the NMV/r arm (76% of 46 evaluable) than placebo (55% of 49), driven predominantly by dysgeusia; the regimen was otherwise tolerated.
- **Immunophenotyping:** The Lancet results abstract reports clinical efficacy and safety endpoints; it does not report a viral-reservoir or immune-signature enrichment analysis. A separate exploratory immunologic-features preprint was posted later and is not ingested in this protocol summary.
- **Individual variability:** Despite group-level null result, individual participant responses varied; researchers indicated ongoing analysis of immune signatures to identify potential responder subgroups.

## Relevance

### Therapeutics track (t004) — antiviral treatment for established long COVID

PAX-LC directly tests the therapeutic hypothesis that viral persistence drives long COVID and can be reversed by antiviral intervention. Its decentralized architecture is also a methodological contribution to PAIS trial design: it demonstrates that community-dwelling, highly symptomatic long COVID patients can be enrolled nationwide without clinic visits, addressing a critical barrier for this population.

### Pairing with STOP-PASC (Geng2024)

Two independent placebo-controlled RCTs of 15-day NMV/r for long COVID now exist:

| Feature | STOP-PASC (Geng2024) | PAX-LC (Krumholz2024 / Lancet 2025) |
|---|---|---|
| Journal (results) | JAMA Internal Medicine 2024 | Lancet Infectious Diseases 2025 |
| N | 155 (2:1 randomized) | 100 (1:1 randomized) |
| Setting | Single-site (Stanford) | Fully decentralized (48 states) |
| Primary endpoint | Pooled 6-symptom Likert severity at 10 weeks | PROMIS-29 PHSS change at Day 28 |
| Primary result | Null | Null |
| Stool viral RNA | All negative at baseline | Not reported as an enrichment or target-engagement marker in the results abstract |
| Dysgeusia | 61.8% NMV/r vs 7.5% placebo | 76% NMV/r vs 55% placebo (including ritonavir effect) |
| Funding | Pfizer Inc | Pfizer Inc |

Both trials enrolled predominantly vaccinated, community-dwelling patients with protracted long COVID (months to over a year post-infection). Both found no primary endpoint benefit.

### Implications for antigen/viral-persistence hypothesis

The dual null result does not definitively falsify antigen persistence as a pathogenic mechanism — it constrains the **therapeutic implication** of that hypothesis in a specific clinical scenario:

- Nirmatrelvir targets the SARS-CoV-2 main protease (Mpro); it inhibits active viral replication but may not clear RNA or protein fragments already deposited in tissue reservoirs.
- Both trials enrolled patients late in the disease course (STOP-PASC median ~17.5 months; PAX-LC enrollment similarly remote from acute infection), when downstream immune remodeling may be independent of ongoing viral activity.
- Neither trial confirmed active viral replication in enrolled participants (STOP-PASC: all stool RT-PCR negative; PAX-LC: no viral-replication enrichment or clearance marker reported in the results abstract).
- Short treatment duration (15 days) may be insufficient to clear a chronic reservoir, if one exists.

The evidence currently argues against routine NMV/r for established, vaccinated, protracted long COVID but leaves open questions about earlier intervention, longer courses, patient enrichment by viral-reservoir biomarker, or targeting of alternative PASC mechanisms.

## Limitations

1. **Protocol paper only:** This AmJMed paper reports design and methods; efficacy conclusions require the Lancet 2025 results paper.
2. **Small N (100):** Phase 2; not powered for definitive efficacy claims or subgroup analyses.
3. **No biomarker-based patient enrichment:** Participants were not selected for evidence of active viral persistence (e.g., plasma SARS-CoV-2 antigen, tissue RNA), so the treated population may not have had the viral reservoir the drug targets.
4. **15-day treatment duration may be too short** for a chronic reservoir, if one exists; STOP-PASC had the same design limitation.
5. **Late enrollment:** Long COVID patients enrolled months to years post-infection; antiviral timing may matter critically.
6. **Predominantly female (66%), vaccinated, US-based:** Limits generalizability to unvaccinated, non-US, or male-predominant cohorts.
7. **Dysgeusia as unmasking risk:** Higher adverse-event rate in NMV/r arm (driven by dysgeusia) creates potential for unblinding and subjective PRO bias — same concern documented in STOP-PASC where dysgeusia was 61.8% vs 7.5%.
8. **Pfizer funding:** Sponsor funded both PAX-LC and STOP-PASC; transparent, but should be noted.
9. **Immunophenotyping results pending:** The main biomarker contribution of PAX-LC (immune signatures of responders) was not reported in this protocol paper and may not yet be fully published.

## Model / Tool Availability

None. The decentralized trial platform (participant-mediated EHR, web-based diary, home blood draw logistics) was developed for this trial but is not a public tool. Immunophenotyping data from enrolled participants is expected to be published separately.

## Follow-up

- Read the PAX-LC results paper (Lancet Infectious Diseases, 2025; DOI 10.1016/S1473-3099(25)00073-8) for full tables, subgroup analyses, and any immunophenotyping follow-up beyond the abstract-level clinical results summarized here.
- Watch for the immunophenotyping companion publication from Iwasaki lab (Yale) — if immune subgroups predict NMV/r response, this could rescue the antigen-persistence therapeutic hypothesis for a biomarker-enriched population.
- Synthesize PAX-LC + STOP-PASC into the topic:antigen-pathogen-persistence entity: two null RCTs now provide the strongest current evidence against antiviral monotherapy as a general treatment for protracted vaccinated long COVID.
- Consider whether earlier treatment window (e.g., <6 months post-infection), longer NMV/r duration, or combination approaches (antivirals + immune modulators) warrant investigation; STOP-PASC and PAX-LC do not address these questions.
- Decentralized design elements (home blood draws, participant-mediated EHR, mail-order study drug) are a model for future PAIS trials where clinic-attendance is a hardship; note this in topic:biomarkers-and-objective-endpoints as a methodological advance.
- The Proal et al. 2025 Lancet Infectious Diseases commentary "Targeting the SARS-CoV-2 reservoir in long COVID" (citing PAX-LC) may provide interpretive context for why both trials returned null results despite plausible mechanistic rationale.
