# Post-acute COVID-19 syndrome and gut dysbiosis linger beyond 1 year after SARS-CoV-2 clearance

## Key Contribution

One-year follow-up letter from the CUHK microbiome group extending the Liu2022 long-COVID dysbiosis
finding beyond the 6-month window. It is useful as a durability check: the microbiome abnormality was not
limited to the immediate post-acute period.

## Methods

- **Design:** follow-up microbiome analysis of post-COVID participants approximately 1 year after
  SARS-CoV-2 clearance.
- **Assay:** fecal microbiome profiling, interpreted against the prior CUHK post-COVID microbiome cohort.

## Key Findings

- PACS and dysbiosis persisted beyond 1 year in a subset of participants.
- The signal remained concentrated in reduced beneficial/SCFA-producing taxa and enrichment of taxa
  associated with inflammatory or symptomatic states.

## Relevance

This paper strengthens the temporal claim in `proposition:0031`: gut dysbiosis can persist into the
chronic phase of long COVID, so the axis is not simply an acute-infection artifact.

## Limitations

- Short report/letter rather than a large independent cohort.
- Not independent of the CUHK long-COVID microbiome program represented by Liu2022.
- Still observational; it does not prove that dysbiosis maintains symptoms.
