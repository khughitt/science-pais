# Six-Month Outcomes in the Long-Term Outcomes After the Multisystem Inflammatory Syndrome in Children Study

## Key Contribution

Large prospective MUSIC cohort showing that MIS-C can be severe acutely but usually has reassuring
cardiovascular and global health outcomes by 6 months.

## Methods

- **Design:** multicenter cohort with follow-up through 6 months after hospitalization.
- **Setting:** 32 North American pediatric hospitals.
- **Participants:** 1204 persons younger than 21 meeting the 2020 CDC MIS-C case definition.
- **Outcomes:** echocardiography core-lab assessment, cardiac dysfunction, coronary z scores,
  noncardiac sequelae, return to pre-MIS-C baseline health, and PROMIS global health instruments.

## Key Findings

- MIS-C was often severe acutely: 45.9% required vasoactive support, 1.4% required ECMO, and 0.3% died
  during hospitalization.
- Among core-lab-reviewed echocardiograms, 42.3% had LVEF below 55% during hospitalization; all but one
  normalized by 6 months among those with follow-up.
- Of coronary z-score abnormalities during hospitalization, 92.3% normalized by 6 months.
- More than 95% reported being more than 90% back to baseline health across energy, sleep, appetite,
  cognition, and mood by 6 months.
- Fatigue fell from 15.9% at 2 weeks to 3.4% at 6 months.

## Relevance

This paper makes MIS-C a recovery-contrast syndrome for PAIS: a post-infectious inflammatory illness that
often resolves rather than persisting as chronic long COVID.

## Limitations

- Hospitalized MIS-C cohort; not comparable to community long-COVID sampling.
- Follow-up is midterm, not lifelong.
- It does not explain why the minority with residual symptoms persist.
