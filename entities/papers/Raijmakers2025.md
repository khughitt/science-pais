# Immunological associations in post-infective fatigue syndromes including Long COVID: a systematic review and meta-analysis

## Key Contribution

This is the broadest existing cross-trigger immunological systematic review and meta-analysis of post-infective fatigue syndromes (PIFS), spanning five infectious aetiologies: Epstein–Barr virus (EBV/infectious mononucleosis), SARS-CoV-2 (Long COVID), Borrelia burgdorferi (Lyme disease / PTLDS), Coxiella burnetii (Q-fever fatigue syndrome), and Ross River virus. Applying rigorous eligibility criteria that required both documented acute infection and a formal PIFS case definition, only 30 studies (from 14,985 screened) met inclusion standards. The meta-analysis found a narrow but statistically significant signal for persistent immune activation in PIFS: elevated RANTES (CCL5) and TNFα at 6–12 months post-infection, and elevated white blood cell (WBC) counts at 3–6 months. The paper provides a methodological benchmark and argues that a large fraction of the published literature is uninformative due to inadequate case characterisation.

## Methods

- **Study design:** PRISMA/SWiM-compliant systematic review and meta-analysis. PROSPERO registration: CRD42021281161.
- **Databases searched:** PubMed (Medline), EMBASE, Web of Science (through 10 September 2024).
- **PECO framework:**
  - Population: adults and adolescents with a documented acute infection
  - Exposure: development of PIFS (6–12 months post-infection)
  - Comparator: recovered controls from the same infective exposure
  - Outcomes: circulating immunological biomarkers measured up to 12 months post-infection
- **Inclusion criteria (key):** (i) laboratory-confirmed or probable acute infection; (ii) formal PIFS caseness applied (ME/CFS criteria: Fukuda/CDC94, Canadian Consensus, or IOM; IDSA criteria for PTLDS; Dutch Guideline for QFS); (iii) circulating immunological markers measured; (iv) validated multi-item fatigue questionnaire; (v) control group; (vi) minimum n = 5. Languages: English, Norwegian, Swedish, Spanish, German, Dutch.
- **Confidence tiering:** Each study assigned a dual confidence level — Level 1 (confirmed) vs. Level 2 (probable) for both the acute infection diagnosis and the PIFS diagnosis. Long COVID studies were included if they met these criteria.
- **Quality appraisal:** Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) tool for study design; BIOCROSS tool (original 0–20 and a modified 0–28 version) for laboratory methodology. Threshold of ≥70% for high quality. Studies not excluded purely on quality score, but scores informed interpretation.
- **Biomarker categories:** Cytokines/chemokines, leucocyte gene expression, clinical inflammatory markers (CRP, ESR, WBC), immune cell subsets/activation status, immunoglobulins/autoantibodies, pathogen-specific antibody and T-cell responses.
- **Statistical analysis:** Random-effects meta-analyses. Effect sizes reported as Cohen's d. Publication bias assessed via Egger's regression test. Time windows: 0–1 month (acute), 1–3 months (early post-infective), 3–6 months (PIFS caseness), 6–12 months (chronic).
- **Included studies:** 30 articles; 5,102 total participants; 833 PIFS or PIFS-like cases; 4,269 recovered controls. Studies derived from 18 prospective cohorts. Meta-analyses performed on 11 studies (413 PIFS cases) with adequate quantitative data.
- **Infectious aetiologies represented:** EBV (n = multiple studies), SARS-CoV-2, B. burgdorferi, C. burnetii, Ross River virus. Post-dengue and post-Q-fever (combined) included under the cross-trigger umbrella.

## Key Findings

### Shared vs. trigger-specific signals — the central extraction for question:0001

**SHARED across triggers (meta-analysis significant):**

The meta-analysis identified three biomarkers elevated in PIFS vs. recovered controls that span more than one trigger:

1. **RANTES (CCL5) at 6–12 months** — Cohen's d = 0.45 (95% CI 0.16–0.73, P < 0.05, I² = 0.31). Based on two studies: one post-EBV (n = 58 PIFS cases, n = 324 controls) and one post-SARS-CoV-2. Egger's test P = 0.44 (low publication bias). This chemokine, which drives T-cell recruitment, appears elevated across at least two distinct viral triggers at the chronic timepoint.

2. **TNFα at 6–12 months** — Cohen's d = 0.30 (95% CI 0.04–0.57, P = 0.02, I² = 0.58). Based on two post-SARS-CoV-2 studies (n = 83 PIFS, n = 339 controls). Although both studies share the same trigger here, this pro-inflammatory cytokine was identified in the meta-analysis as a cross-study consistent signal. A separate Long COVID systematic review (not in this meta-analysis) also found TNFα elevated vs. recovered controls, lending modest multi-trigger corroboration.

3. **WBC count at 3–6 months** — Cohen's d = 0.42 (95% CI 0.09–0.73, P = 0.01, I² = 0.36). Based on three post-EBV studies (n = 48 PIFS cases, n = 237 controls). Elevated neutrophil counts were observed in individual studies at later timepoints for both EBV and SARS-CoV-2.

**Important caveat on cross-trigger pooling:** The RANTES result spans EBV and SARS-CoV-2, and the WBC elevation spans three EBV studies. The TNFα result is drawn exclusively from two SARS-CoV-2 studies. No single biomarker was meta-analysed across all five infectious triggers simultaneously; the cross-trigger span is partial. The paper argues that the symptom complex is stereotyped across triggers (analogous to the acute sickness response), but the biomarker evidence for a shared molecular signature remains limited by the small number of studies per analyte per timepoint.

**TRIGGER-SPECIFIC or inconsistent findings:**

- **CCL-19:** Elevated in post-Lyme PIFS (PTLDS) at multiple timepoints in one study (Aucott 2016), with predictive AUC of 0.85 for PTLDS development. Not formally meta-analysed; not replicated across other triggers. This represents a potentially trigger-specific or at least trigger-enriched signal.
- **Leucocyte gene expression (PBMCs):** Five studies using RNAseq or microarrays; no consistent significant findings across studies or triggers.
- **Immune cell subsets:** Eight flow-cytometry studies; no significant group differences for CD4/CD8 subsets, NK cells, or activation markers between PIFS and recovered controls. No consistent findings.
- **Immunoglobulins and autoantibodies:** Nine studies; no consistent findings. No formal meta-analysis possible.
- **Pathogen-specific serology and T-cell responses:** Ten studies; no significant differences in pathogen-specific antibody responses at 3–6 months meta-analysis; no consistent differences in individual studies. SARS-CoV-2-specific CD4/CD8 T-cell increases in Long COVID were noted in some (but not all) studies that were ineligible for this review due to lack of formal PIFS case definition.
- **Other markers (single-study signals):** ET-1 increased and sVEGFR1 decreased in some Long COVID studies; IL-18 decreased (one study); Ang-2 and kynurenine pathway changes mentioned in Discussion as emerging signals but not part of the formal meta-analysis.

### Methodological yield

- Only 30/14,985 (0.2%) screened studies met inclusion criteria. The dominant exclusion reason (94% of full-text exclusions) was inadequate case characterisation: either the acute infection was not formally documented, or PIFS criteria were not formally applied. This represents a major indictment of the existing literature quality.
- Of the 30 included studies: 22 had Level 1 (confirmed) acute infection diagnosis; 21 had Level 1 PIFS diagnosis. Nine studies were PIFS-like only (Level 2).
- The studies covered a broad timespan (2001–2024) and multiple countries, with heterogeneous assay platforms limiting pooling.

## Relevance

This paper bears most directly on **question:0001** (is there a molecular signature shared across PAIS triggers?). The short answer from the best-quality evidence currently available is: **tentative yes for RANTES and WBC elevation, very limited cross-trigger evidence for TNFα, but the shared signal is narrow and fragile**:

- RANTES (CCL5) is the strongest candidate for a cross-trigger shared chemokine signal, appearing elevated in both post-EBV and post-SARS-CoV-2 PIFS at 6–12 months.
- WBC/neutrophil elevation spanning EBV and SARS-CoV-2 (independently) suggests a non-trivial shared innate immune component.
- TNFα and RANTES are both recognised neuroinflammatory mediators; their co-elevation is consistent with the neuroinflammation hypothesis and with metabolic dysfunction via IDO/kynurenine pathway, which the Discussion flags as a convergent mechanism.
- The null findings for lymphocyte subsets, pathogen-specific responses, and autoantibodies argue against adaptive immune expansion or autoimmunity as a robust shared signal at this timepoint window (though detection power is limited).

The paper strengthens **hypothesis:0001** (shared dysregulated attractor) at the immune-activation level but does not rule out the possibility that the shared immune signal is downstream of trigger-specific mechanisms. It also supports the interpretation in **discussion:0002** (cross-pathogen PAIS signature convergence) that a shared innate/inflammatory phenotype exists, while cautioning that the signal is modest and may reflect heterogeneous aetiopathology within the PIFS category.

The paper explicitly tests the hypothesis that PIFS is "a largely stereotyped condition independent of the specific initiating infection," framing it as analogous to the stereotyped acute sickness response — this directly maps onto the project's working frame of PAIS as a shared failure mode of homeostasis recovery.

## Limitations

1. **Extreme literature attrition (94%):** The stringent quality gate excluded nearly all of the published literature. While this strengthens internal validity, it leaves the meta-analysis based on just 11 studies and yields small group sizes (10–53 participants per analyte per timepoint). The cross-trigger analysis is severely underpowered.

2. **Partial cross-trigger coverage in meta-analysis:** RANTES spans EBV and SARS-CoV-2 (not Lyme, QFS, or RRV). TNFα meta-analysis is SARS-CoV-2-only. WBC elevation is EBV-only in the formal meta-analysis. True cross-trigger pooling was not possible for most analytes.

3. **Modest effect sizes:** All significant biomarker effects are small to medium (Cohen's d 0.30–0.45). Effect sizes may not survive meta-regression for confounders (sex, age, severity) given limited power.

4. **Heterogeneous assay platforms:** Studies span 2001–2024 with varying immunoassay technologies, making pooling of concentration data approximate. Modified BIOCROSS ≥70% achieved in only 5 of the 11 meta-analysed studies.

5. **Predominantly female cohorts:** Most studies enrolled predominantly female participants; sex-stratified analyses were only possible for a subset of analytes.

6. **No post-exertional malaise (PEM) requirement in all studies:** Some included studies did not require PEM as a core symptom, introducing phenotypic heterogeneity within the PIFS category.

7. **Absence of later timepoints:** Meta-analysis was restricted to 12 months post-infection; biomarker trajectories beyond 12 months (especially relevant for ME/CFS which can be chronic) are unknown in this dataset.

8. **No tissue-level data:** All markers are from circulating blood. Skeletal muscle, CNS, and gut-barrier compartment signals, which may be more informative, are not captured.

9. **Author-group over-representation:** Several included studies were from research groups of co-authors, acknowledged by the authors as a limitation.

10. **Case definition heterogeneity:** Multiple ME/CFS criteria sets were accepted (Fukuda, CCC, IOM); differences in diagnostic stringency across PIFS subtypes may mask or dilute cross-trigger signals.

## Model / Tool Availability

No computational model, tool, or analysis pipeline is released with this paper. It is a systematic review and meta-analysis.

Data sharing: Raw data available upon email request to the corresponding author (Ruud Raijmakers, ruud.raijmakers@radboudumc.nl). Supplementary materials (PROSPERO protocol, search strategies, quality appraisal scores, full biomarker table, and forest plots) are published with the paper at the DOI above.

The COFFI collaborative (www.coffi-collaborative.com) is an ongoing international consortium developing standardised PIFS research case definitions; this is a relevant infrastructure resource for future replication studies.

## Follow-up

- **RANTES/CCL5 deep-dive:** This is the highest-priority candidate for a cross-trigger shared signal. Search for CCL5/RANTES in QFS, PTLDS, post-RRV, and post-EBV cohorts independently; the two studies in this meta-analysis are post-EBV and post-SARS-CoV-2 only.
- **TNFα replication across triggers:** TNFα was significant but limited to two SARS-CoV-2 studies here. Prioritise pulling TNFα data from QFS and PTLDS cohorts.
- **CCL-19 in PTLDS:** The Aucott 2016 study showed a strong predictive signal for CCL-19 in PTLDS (AUC 0.85 at 1 month). This paper was included but CCL-19 was not meta-analysed cross-trigger. Worth reviewing Aucott separately and checking whether CCL-19 is measured in SARS-CoV-2 / EBV cohorts.
- **Ineligible Long COVID literature:** The authors note that a large body of Long COVID immunology studies was ineligible because they used WHO Long COVID definition without formal PIFS case criteria. Some of these did find SARS-CoV-2-specific T-cell elevations. These studies (cited as refs 70–73 in the paper) could be worth reviewing separately as a lower-quality tier.
- **Kynurenine / IDO2 pathway:** Mentioned in Discussion as an emerging convergent mechanism in Long COVID neuropathogenesis via tryptophan catabolism. Not covered in this meta-analysis but flags a metabolic-immune intersection worth tracking (refs 78–79 in paper).
- **Complement dysregulation:** Mentioned as independently reported by multiple groups in Long COVID (refs 80–81). Not captured in this meta-analysis; relates to thromboinflammation topic.
- **COFFI collaborative output:** Monitor for the upcoming standardised PIFS case definition from the COFFI group (www.coffi-collaborative.com), which this paper's authors are developing. This will be the methodological foundation for next-generation cross-trigger studies.
- **Tissue compartment studies:** The Discussion explicitly flags skeletal muscle and CNS as under-studied compartments. Any papers on CNS or muscle biopsy immunology in PIFS would complement these circulating-marker findings.
