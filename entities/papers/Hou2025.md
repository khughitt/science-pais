# Global Prevalence of Long COVID, Its Subtypes, and Risk Factors: An Updated Systematic Review and Meta-analysis

- **Authors:** Yiren Hou, Tian Gu, Zhouchi Ni, Xu Shi, Megan L. Ranney, Bhramar Mukherjee
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal:** Open Forum Infectious Diseases
- **DOI:** 10.1093/ofid/ofaf533
- **PMCID:** PMC12461872
- **BibTeX key:** Hou2025
- **Source:** PDF

## Key Contribution

This "mega-systematic review" is the largest meta-analysis of long COVID prevalence to date,
synthesizing 429 studies (>2 million confirmed COVID-19 cases, 6 continents) into unified
quantitative estimates of global and regional prevalence, 8 organ-system subtypes, 41 specific
symptoms, and 11 risk factors, using a consistent random-effects framework
(DerSimonian-Laird) across the search window July 2021–July 2024. It updates and
substantially expands the same group's 2022 meta-analysis (Chen et al. 2022; 33 studies).
The global pooled long COVID prevalence is 36% (95% CI 33%–40%), with vaccination status,
variant era, and female sex as the three strongest risk factors.

## Methods

- **Design:** PRISMA-compliant systematic review and meta-analysis. PubMed, Embase, and Web
  of Science searched May 29, 2024; supplementary update July 23, 2024.
- **Inclusion:** Human studies with confirmed COVID-19 (PCR, antibody, or clinical);
  outcome = long COVID prevalence, risk factors, subtypes, or symptoms; follow-up ≥2 months
  after index date.
- **Definition used:** ≥1 new or persistent symptom at ≥2 months post-infection. Deliberately
  broad to accommodate heterogeneous study definitions. Captures "ever" prevalence, not
  current point prevalence.
- **Pooling:** Random-effects model, inverse variance-weighted; DerSimonian-Laird
  between-study variance estimator. Minimum sample size threshold applied per subtype/symptom
  to ensure margin of error ≤5%.
- **Scope:** 429 studies meta-analyzed; 13 additional included in systematic review but
  excluded from meta-analysis due to overlap. Total N > 2 million with confirmed COVID-19.
- **Risk of bias:** Joanna Briggs Institute checklist for prevalence studies (score 0–9;
  lower = higher bias). Sensitivity analysis removing 79 studies with score ≤5/9 confirmed
  robustness (pooled prevalence unchanged at 35%).
- **Software:** R packages `meta` and `metafor`.

## Key Findings

### Global and stratified prevalence

Global pooled long COVID prevalence: **36% (95% CI 33%–40%)** from 144 studies. I² = 100%,
reflecting extreme between-study heterogeneity.

| Stratum | Pooled prevalence (95% CI) | Studies |
|---|---|---|
| Overall global | 36% (33%–40%) | 144 |
| Hospitalized only | 44% (38%–51%) | 27 |
| Mixed hosp./non-hosp. | 35% (31%–40%) | 104 |
| Non-hospitalized only | 29% (14%–50%) | 13 |
| South America | 51% (35%–66%) | 10 |
| Europe | 39% (31%–48%) | 59 |
| Asia | 35% (25%–46%) | 45 |
| North America | 30% (24%–38%) | 24 |
| United States only | 29% (21%–37%) | 19 |
| Africa | 53% (38%–67%) | 3 [unreliable] |
| Follow-up <1 year | 35% (31%–39%) | 122 |
| Follow-up 1–2 years | 46% (37%–57%) | 18 |
| Follow-up >2 years | 43% (24%–64%) | 4 |
| Female (sex-stratified) | 45% (33%–58%) | 7 |
| Male (sex-stratified) | 37% (27%–48%) | 7 |
| Adults >18 | 35% (31%–40%) | 87 |
| Non-adults | 23% (14%–34%) | 8 |

Note: The US pooled "ever-prevalence" (29%) aligns closely with the CDC Household Pulse
Survey phase 4.2 estimate of 29.8% — an external validation. Current point prevalence
(Household Pulse) is 8.7%, approximately 3-fold lower than "ever" prevalence.

### Subtype prevalence (among confirmed COVID-19 cases)

| Subtype | Pooled (95% CI) | Studies | <1 yr | 1–2+ yr |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| General fatigue | 20% (18%–23%) | 119 | 19% | 26% |
| Respiratory | 20% (14%–28%) | 31 | 20% | 19% |
| Psychological | 18% (11%–28%) | 10 | 10% | 29% |
| Neurological | 16% (8%–30%) | 23 | 13% | 27% |
| Dermatological | 12% (8%–17%) | 10 | 11% | 12% |
| Cardiovascular | 10% (4%–25%) | 16 | 8% | 20% |
| Musculoskeletal | 9% (5%–16%) | 13 | 8% | 30% [1 study] |
| Gastrointestinal | 5% (5%–7%) | 34 | 6% | 4% |

The neurological and cardiovascular subtypes show marked increases between <1 year and 1–2
year follow-up (13% → 27% neurological; 8% → 20% cardiovascular). This temporal pattern
could reflect delayed manifestation, diagnosis lag, or enrichment of severe/hospitalized
cases in longer-follow-up cohorts.

| Muscle weakness | 11% (5%–23%) | 5 |
| Breathlessness | 8% (3%–22%) | 4 |
| Dyspnea | 7% (6%–10%) | 31 |
| Joint pain | 7% (4%–11%) | 14 |

The general fatigue estimate drops to 7% when restricted to "constant/severe/unusual fatigue"

### Risk factors (pooled odds ratios vs. reference)

| Risk factor | Pooled OR (95% CI) | Studies |
|---|---|---|
| Unvaccinated for COVID-19 | 2.09 (1.55–2.81) | 7 |
| Pre-Omicron variant (vs. Omicron) | 1.74 (1.40–2.17) | 6 |
| Diabetes | 1.77 (0.78–4.01) | 12 [wide CI] |
| Female sex | 1.56 (1.32–1.84) | 33 |
| ≥1 comorbidity | 1.52 (1.27–1.82) | 17 |
| Preexisting CVD | 1.50 (1.24–1.81) | 5 |
| ICU admission | 1.43 (1.02–2.02) | 8 |
| History of COPD | 1.37 (1.03–1.82) | 6 |
| Hypertension | 1.37 (1.08–1.74) | 9 |
| Obesity | 1.28 (0.99–1.66) | 6 [borderline] |
| Age (per 5-year increase) | 1.06 (0.99–1.14) | 14 [non-significant] |

Vaccination and variant era are the two strongest risk factors but are confounded: calendar
year is a proxy for both vaccine rollout and variant dominance. The authors note this and
recommend stratified analyses.

## Relevance

**hypothesis:0004-acute-severity-threshold.** The hospitalization-stratified prevalence
gradient (non-hosp. 29% → mixed 35% → hosp. 44%) and ICU admission OR of 1.43 provide
population-level epidemiological support for severity-gated PAIS risk. Important caveat:
longer follow-up cohorts are disproportionately hospital-based, so the temporal persistence
signal partly reflects severity-based sampling, not necessarily disease chronification.

**hypothesis:0005-reproductive-stage-immune-homeostatic-margin.** Female sex is the strongest
demographic risk factor, with OR 1.56 from 33 studies — the largest and most-replicated
estimate in this meta-analysis. Sex-stratified prevalence (45% vs. 37%) echoes this. The
meta-analysis cannot decompose biological from behavioral (help-seeking, reporting) factors.

**hypothesis:0008-measurement-channel-and-ascertainment-bias-predictably-shapes-apparent.**
Figure 4 explicitly models observed I² as a product of true etiological heterogeneity (age
structure, genetics, healthcare access, comorbidities, vaccine uptake) versus
design-induced heterogeneity (outcome definition, clinical vs. survey diagnosis,
hospitalization labeling). The paper's own team's Haupert2022 case-crossover PheWAS showed
fatigue and anxiety are similarly elevated post-flu, flagging COVID-19-specificity problems.
The threefold difference between "ever" (29% US) and "current" (8.7% US) prevalence
directly illustrates how ascertainment frame inflates apparent burden.

**hypothesis:0020-host-immune-baseline-reserve-gate.** Vaccination (OR 2.09 unvaccinated),
comorbidities (ORs 1.27–1.82), CVD (OR 1.50), hypertension (OR 1.37), and COPD (OR 1.37)
collectively show that host immune-metabolic baseline — not acute severity alone — shapes
PAIS risk. The comorbidity cluster appearing together at similar effect sizes is consistent
with a shared immune-reserve-depletion frame. Diabetes shows a wide CI (0.78–4.01),
suggesting heterogeneous effect across populations.

**hypothesis:0001-shared-dysregulated-attractor.** The persistence of long COVID across all
follow-up strata and the increase in neurological/cardiovascular subtype prevalence at 1–2
years is consistent with a self-sustaining chronic state rather than simple exponential
symptom decay. However, the temporal trend is confounded by severity-based sampling (longer
follow-up cohorts are hospital-enriched), limiting causal inference about chronification.

## Limitations

1. **"Ever" prevalence, not current:** Estimates capture whether an individual *ever* had
   ≥1 symptom, not active long COVID. The US current prevalence (8.7%) is ~3x lower than the
   "ever" estimate (29%). Most downstream burden calculations should use current prevalence.
2. **I² = 100%:** Extreme between-study heterogeneity renders the pooled global estimate an
   imprecision-weighted average of incomparable populations and definitions. Individual study
   estimates range from 1%–92%.
3. **Definition heterogeneity:** No diagnostic biomarker; any symptom-based definition
   produces inconsistent case ascertainment. The paper's operationalization (≥1 symptom ≥2
   months) is deliberately broad and captures non-COVID-specific symptoms.
4. **Hospitalization confound in temporal trends:** Longer follow-up studies are
   disproportionately hospital-based (13% hospitalized at <1 yr vs. 75% at >2 yr), partly
   explaining the apparent prevalence increase over time.
5. **Fatigue specificity:** General fatigue and psychological symptoms are highly prevalent
   post-flu and in the general population; the 20% fatigue estimate likely includes
   non-COVID-specific contributions. Restricting to severe fatigue yields 7%.
6. **Variant–vaccination confounding:** Pre-Omicron vs. Omicron period proxied by calendar
   time, which co-tracks vaccine rollout; the two risk factors cannot be cleanly separated.
7. **Absent functional outcomes:** Quality of life, functional limitation, work incapacity,
   and mortality were not meta-analyzed. The 24.3% activity-limitation rate among *current*
   long-COVID patients (Household Pulse) suggests clinical burden is considerably lower than
   crude prevalence implies.
8. **African/Oceanian under-representation:** Africa: 3–5 studies, estimate unreliable.
   Oceania: 3 studies. Global pooling obscures profound data inequity.
9. **No pathophysiology arm:** Purely epidemiological prevalence/risk-factor synthesis;
   mechanisms, biomarkers, and treatment are out of scope.

## Model / Tool Availability

No software or model released. R code uses `meta` and `metafor` packages. Data derived
from previously published studies, publicly available through PubMed, Embase, and Web of
Science.
