# Immunoglobulin G complexes from post-infectious ME/CFS, including post-COVID ME/CFS, disrupt cellular energetics and alter inflammatory marker secretion

## Key Contribution

This study is the first to demonstrate directly that IgG complexes purified from post-infectious ME/CFS patients — including post-COVID ME/CFS (termed PCS-CFS) — selectively enter human endothelial cells and disrupt mitochondrial architecture and cellular energetics in a cell-type-specific manner. The Fab fragment (antigen-binding domain) is sufficient to reproduce the energetics effect, implicating specific antigen-antibody interactions rather than non-specific Fc-receptor-mediated uptake. The paper also provides parallel immune-complex proteomics revealing disease-specific signatures: ME/CFS complexes are enriched for proteins linked to extracellular matrix (ECM) dysregulation, while PCS-CFS complexes point toward altered hemostasis and blood-clot pathways. The authors frame their findings as evidence for antibody-mediated metabolic dysregulation as a candidate mechanism in both forms of the disease and propose it as a therapeutic target.

## Methods

**Design:** In-vitro mechanistic study.

**Cohort:** IgG isolated from 106 individuals recruited at Charité Universitätsmedizin Berlin (2020–2023):
- ME/CFS: n = 39; diagnosis by 2003 Canadian Consensus Criteria (CCC) with exclusion of other fatigue-causing diseases
- PCS-CFS (post-COVID ME/CFS): n = 15; same CCC diagnostic criteria applied after confirmed SARS-CoV-2 infection
- Multiple sclerosis (MS): n = 20; relapsing-remitting, fatigue-relevant, relapse-free and steroid-free for ≥6 months (disease control)
- Healthy donors (HD): n = 41; gender- and age-matched

Additional systemic sclerosis serum samples (n = 21, group of Prof. Riemekasten) were used only for biomarker validation.

**IgG purification:** Protein G Sepharose bead-based purification from serum; quality assessed by SDS-PAGE (Coomassie staining).

**Cell models:**
- Primary human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVEC-TERT2): permissive to IgG entry; used for all functional assays
- Primary human foreskin fibroblasts (HFF-TERT): non-permissive to IgG entry; used as cell-type specificity control

**Mitochondrial morphology:** Confocal microscopy of HUVECs stably expressing mitochondria-targeted GFP; average mitochondrial surface area quantified per subject (≥10 cells per sample, 3 replicates). Gender-stratified analysis also performed.

**Cellular energetics (Seahorse XFe96):** Mitochondrial stress test (OCR and ECAR) after 36 h IgG exposure; assays performed in both glucose-containing and galactose-containing media. Fab and Fc fragments tested separately after papain digestion.

**Cytokine secretion:** Multiplex bead-based assay (10 cytokines) on healthy-donor PBMCs after 48 h IgG exposure. ELISA validation for selected cytokines.

**Autoantigen profiling:** Pilot IgG/IgM levels against 120 autoantigens (commercial protein microarray) in ME/CFS (n = 12) vs. healthy controls (n = 3); blinded analysis.

**Immune complex proteomics:** Mass spectrometry of purified IgG fractions: ME/CFS (n = 40), PCS-CFS (n = 16), HD (n = 39), MS (n = 11). PCA and GSEA Reactome pathway analysis.

## Key Findings

### 1. Cell-type-specific IgG entry

IgG entered HUVEC endothelial cells efficiently (detectable up to 16 h; Fc-specific fragments persisted to 48 h) but did not enter HFFs. Fc-receptor blockade only partially reduced entry, suggesting an additional Fc-independent uptake route. No difference in entry efficiency was observed between disease groups.

### 2. IgG from ME/CFS and PCS-CFS induces mitochondrial fragmentation in HUVECs

- At 1 µg/ml, IgG from ME/CFS patients significantly reduced mitochondrial surface area vs. HD controls (P = 0.0004 per subject; P < 0.0001 per image).
- PCS-CFS IgG also caused significant fragmentation (P = 0.0164 per subject; P = 0.0067 per image).
- MS IgG did not cause significant fragmentation (P = 0.87), demonstrating disease specificity rather than a general autoimmune effect.
- Effect was more pronounced in female patients: statistically significant in females (ME/CFS P = 0.0018; PCS-CFS P = 0.0173); not significant in males.
- Separated Fab and Fc fragments each did not recapitulate the fragmentation phenotype of intact IgG, suggesting a structural requirement for intact immunoglobulin.

### 3. Fragmentation is not Drp1-mediated

IgG from ME/CFS and PCS-CFS reduced protein levels of Drp1, PLD6, Miga1, and mitofilin, indicating general mitochondrial protein destabilization/degradation rather than classical Drp1-driven fission. MS IgG did not produce these changes.

### 4. Altered cellular energetics — spared respiration capacity and glycolytic compensation

- ME/CFS IgG (subgroup with mitochondrial fragmentation phenotype) significantly increased spared respiratory capacity in HUVECs in glucose medium.
- No change in ATP generation (glycolytic, mitochondrial, or total), consistent with a stress-adaptation response rather than overt bioenergetic failure.
- In galactose medium (forcing OXPHOS reliance), the ME/CFS IgG energetics effect was abolished, pointing to glycolytic compensation as the primary mode of cellular stress response.
- PCS-CFS IgG did not alter cellular energetics to the same degree as ME/CFS IgG.
- Fab fragments reproduced the ME/CFS IgG energetics effect; Fc fragments from HDs induced a non-specific overall metabolic increase.
- Interpretation: mitochondrial structural changes and energetics changes represent two partially independent phenotypes both driven by the antigen-binding region.

### 5. Disease-distinct cytokine secretion profiles in PBMCs

- ME/CFS IgG induced IL-1β (P = 0.0460, the only statistically significant cytokine), with trends for IFN-γ, TNF-α, and IL-6 (heterogeneous across patients).
- PCS-CFS and MS IgG induced distinct patterns: IL-5, IL-10, TNF-β elevated; TGF-α reduced. These profiles were largely absent from ME/CFS IgG-treated PBMCs.
- No IFN-α2 change across all groups. Results suggest IgG-mediated inflammatory signaling follows disease-specific (not shared) patterns.

### 6. Autoantigen IgM profiling (pilot)

IgM (not IgG) levels against known autoantigens (PCNA, collagen V/VI, complement C3, CRP) distinguished ME/CFS patients from HDs and stratified by disease severity in a 12-patient pilot. IgG levels against the same antigens did not separate cases from controls.

### 7. Immune complex proteomics: disease-specific signatures

- PCA on mass-spec data separated HDs from all three disease groups; ME/CFS clustered slightly apart from PCS-CFS and MS.
- ME/CFS-enriched immune complex proteins: IGHV1-58, IGHV4-8, IGHV3-16, IGHV3-66, IGHV1-69-2 (variable region gene products); SPEG (muscle-maintenance kinase, potential autoantibody target); CPN1 (metalloprotease regulating inflammatory peptides). VWF was decreased within ME/CFS immune complexes (confirmed by concurrent serum VWF increase).
- PCS-CFS-enriched: IGHV1-58, KLKB1 (kallikrein B, involved in contact activation and bradykinin pathway).
- GSEA Reactome analysis: ECM organization was the only significant pathway enriched in ME/CFS immune complexes; PCS-CFS immune complexes showed enrichment for hemostasis, platelet activation/signaling, and fibrin clot formation pathways (not seen in ME/CFS).
- No EBV or HHV-6A protein signatures detected in immune complex proteome.

### 8. Cross-trigger convergence and divergence

The two post-infectious subgroups (classical ME/CFS vs. post-COVID PCS-CFS) share the IgG-driven mitochondrial fragmentation phenotype but diverge in energetics changes and in cytokine secretion profiles. The authors hypothesize that PCS-CFS represents an earlier, more inflammation-dominant stage and classical ME/CFS a later, hypometabolic-dominant stage.

## Relevance

This paper is directly relevant to the PAIS cross-pathogen track (t001) and provides functional convergence evidence linking two mechanistic axes central to this project:

**Autoimmunity axis:** IgG from both classical post-infectious ME/CFS and post-COVID ME/CFS (PCS-CFS) directly disrupts endothelial cell mitochondrial architecture. This is in-vitro experimental evidence (not passive-transfer mouse model inference) for antibody-mediated cellular pathology — a key plank of hypothesis:0001.

**Bioenergetic / mitochondrial axis:** The same IgG isolates shift cellular energetics toward elevated glycolytic compensation and increased spared respiratory capacity without reducing ATP output, consistent with a stress-adaptation (rather than terminal failure) model. This mechanistically grounds question:0011 (mitochondrial basis of PEM): if exertion removes the residual energetic reserve that IgG forces endothelial cells to maintain, that would produce the vascular-metabolic collapse of PEM.

**Cross-trigger convergence (question:0001):** Both classical and post-COVID ME/CFS IgGs cause mitochondrial fragmentation in HUVECs, establishing a shared functional endpoint at the IgG level across infectious triggers. This is a functional convergence data point for the shared-signature hypothesis — different upstream triggers converging on the same antibody-mediated endothelial disruption mechanism.

**Divergence is also informative:** PCS-CFS and classical ME/CFS differ in energetics impact and cytokine profiles, and differ in immune complex proteomics (hemostasis/clot pathways enriched in PCS-CFS; ECM organization in classical ME/CFS). This differential aligns with the project framing that apparent heterogeneity across triggers may reflect stage-of-disease rather than fundamentally distinct mechanisms.

**ECM and thromboinflammation links:** The ME/CFS immune complex ECM signature and the PCS-CFS hemostasis/platelet/fibrin signature connect to the project's thromboinflammation topic.

## Limitations

1. **Case definition:** ME/CFS by 2003 CCC; this is a well-validated but post-hoc-applied definition — no longitudinal clinical follow-up within this study is described.
2. **Cell model:** HUVECs (TERT2 immortalized line) are endothelial but represent large-vessel biology; microvascular endothelium may behave differently. In-vivo endothelial exposure to IgG at 1 µg/ml is not established.
3. **IgG purification method:** Protein G Sepharose enriches IgG but co-purifies IgG-bound immune complexes; the "immune complex" proteome results are for this mixed fraction, not pure monomeric IgG or pure circulating immune complexes.
4. **N for key assays is small for some subgroups:** Fab/Fc fragment experiments used n = 5 ME/CFS and n = 5 HDs; autoantigen microarray pilot was n = 12 ME/CFS vs. n = 3 HDs — too small for definitive conclusions.
5. **Female-skewed fragmentation effect:** Male ME/CFS IgG did not produce significant mitochondrial fragmentation. The energetics results are drawn from the mitochondrial-fragmentation-positive subgroup, so sex composition and selection criteria of the Seahorse cohort are not fully described.
6. **Cytokine findings are largely non-significant:** Only IL-1β reached P < 0.05 for ME/CFS vs. HD in PBMCs; other trends (IFN-γ, TNF-α, IL-6) are reported but should be treated as preliminary.
7. **No causal autoantigen identified:** The Fab-driven energetics effect implies antigen-specific antibodies, but the specific autoantigen(s) responsible for mitochondrial fragmentation and energetics shifts are not identified.
8. **Cross-sectional design:** No pre-infection baseline; cannot exclude that the IgG phenotype predates infection or that it reflects prior immune history.
9. **PCS-CFS staging hypothesis is speculative:** The authors' hypothesis that PCS-CFS = early ME/CFS and classical ME/CFS = chronic hypometabolic stage is interpretive and not tested longitudinally.
10. **No EBV/HHV-6A signature in immune complexes:** Absence of viral protein in the IgG-bound fraction does not exclude active viral reactivation contributing elsewhere.

## Model / Tool Availability

None (in-vitro reagents; cell lines are commercially available; mass spectrometry data not described as deposited in a public repository in the main text).

## Follow-up

- Identify the specific autoantigen(s) recognized by the ME/CFS Fab fragments that drive the energetics phenotype — the antigen-binding specificity is established here but the target is unknown.
- Replicate the Seahorse energetics results in a larger, sex-balanced cohort of ME/CFS and PCS-CFS patients, and in primary (non-TERT2) microvascular endothelial cells.
- Test whether IgG from other post-infectious ME/CFS triggers (Lyme, EBV, Q-fever) produces the same mitochondrial fragmentation and energetics phenotype — this would directly test cross-trigger functional convergence for question:0001.
- Determine whether the PCS-CFS → ME/CFS staging hypothesis can be tested longitudinally (i.e., does the inflammation-dominant PCS-CFS IgG phenotype evolve toward the hypometabolic ME/CFS phenotype over months-to-years?).
- Investigate whether the ECM-linked immune complex proteins in ME/CFS (and hemostasis-linked ones in PCS-CFS) represent true autoantibody targets vs. bystander proteins co-purified with Protein G Sepharose fractions.
- Evaluate whether VWF serum increase (paired with decreased immune-complex-bound VWF) reflects reduced phagocytic clearance, as proposed, and whether it correlates with microvascular or thromboinflammation markers in PAIS cohorts.
