# Serotonin reduction in post-acute sequelae of viral infection

## Key Contribution

Mechanistic long-COVID study linking viral persistence and type-I-interferon inflammation to reduced
peripheral serotonin through impaired intestinal tryptophan absorption, platelet effects, and increased
MAO-mediated turnover. The paper offers a gut-brain/vagus pathway for neurocognitive symptoms, but it is
not itself a microbiome-composition study.

## Methods

- Integrated human PASC cohorts, metabolomics, viral-persistence measurements, organoid and animal-model
  experiments.
- Tested whether viral RNA persistence and antiviral inflammation can reduce tryptophan absorption and
  peripheral serotonin, and whether serotonin/vagal impairment affects memory-related behavior.

## Key Findings

- PASC was associated with reduced peripheral serotonin.
- Viral infection and type-I-interferon inflammation reduced serotonin through diminished intestinal
  absorption of the serotonin precursor tryptophan, platelet hyperactivation/thrombocytopenia, and
  enhanced MAO-mediated serotonin turnover.
- Peripheral serotonin reduction impaired vagal activity and hippocampal responses in the proposed model.
- The authors frame the pathway as potentially relevant to other post-viral syndromes.

## Relevance

For `task:t007`, this is the key gut-brain mechanistic bridge. It connects intestinal absorption,
antiviral inflammation, vagal signaling, and cognition. It should not be used as direct evidence that
microbiome dysbiosis causes PAIS, but it makes the gut axis biologically plausible as part of the
immune-metabolic-neural loop in `hypothesis:0001`.

## Limitations

- The load-bearing pathway is viral-persistence/IFN -> tryptophan/serotonin/vagus, not stool microbiome
  composition.
- Translational chain spans human associations and model systems; not all steps are shown within the same
  human participants.
- Treatment implications remain hypothetical.
