# Gut microbiota dynamics in a prospective cohort of patients with post-acute COVID-19 syndrome

## Key Contribution

Prospective Hong Kong cohort linking serial fecal shotgun-metagenomic profiles to post-acute COVID-19
syndrome (PACS) status at 6 months. The key finding for this project is that the microbiome of patients
without PACS returned toward the non-COVID control profile, whereas PACS cases retained dysbiosis marked
by enrichment of opportunistic/pathobiont taxa and depletion of butyrate-producing organisms.

## Methods

- **Design:** prospective cohort, hospitalized acute COVID-19 patients followed from admission to 6 months.
- **Participants:** 106 COVID-19 patients across acute-severity strata plus 68 non-COVID controls.
- **Samples:** 258 serial fecal samples profiled by shotgun metagenomic sequencing.
- **Outcome:** PACS at 6 months, defined as at least one persistent symptom 4 weeks after SARS-CoV-2
  clearance.

## Key Findings

- At 6 months, 76% of patients had PACS; common symptoms included fatigue, poor memory, and hair loss.
- Admission microbiome composition was associated with later PACS occurrence.
- Patients without PACS showed microbiome recovery by 6 months, approximating non-COVID controls.
- PACS cases showed higher *Ruminococcus gnavus* and *Bacteroides vulgatus* and lower
  *Faecalibacterium prausnitzii*.
- Butyrate-producing bacteria, especially *Bifidobacterium pseudocatenulatum* and
  *Faecalibacterium prausnitzii*, had the largest inverse correlations with PACS at 6 months.
- Respiratory symptoms correlated with opportunistic gut pathogens; neuropsychiatric symptoms and fatigue
  correlated with nosocomial/pathobiont taxa including *Clostridium innocuum* and
  *Actinomyces naeslundii*.

## Relevance

This is the main primary long-COVID microbiome anchor for `task:t007`. It supports a recurring PAIS
axis: persistent post-infectious dysbiosis, especially depleted butyrate/SCFA-producing capacity, tracks
with persistent symptoms rather than merely reflecting acute infection.

## Limitations

- Observational; cannot separate driver, consequence, antibiotic effect, diet, hospitalization, or acute
  severity residual confounding.
- Hong Kong hospitalized cohort; generalizability to community Omicron-era long COVID is uncertain.
- PACS definition was broad (any persistent symptom), so the microbiome signal is not phenotype-specific.
- No microbiome-targeted intervention was tested in this cohort.
