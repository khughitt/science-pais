## Key Contribution

This paper established that β-glucan training induces functional and transcriptomic reprogramming of myeloid **progenitors** in the bone marrow — not only of peripheral circulating monocytes — and that this HSPC-level imprinting is **an integral, mechanistically required component** of durable trained immunity. The critical experimental proof is an adoptive-transfer experiment: bone marrow from β-glucan-trained mice transplanted into naive irradiated recipients reconstituted a trained-immunity phenotype, demonstrating that the imprint resides in BM progenitor cells themselves rather than in soluble signals. The paper thereby explains why trained immunity persists for months beyond the 1–7 day lifespan of circulating monocytes — each newly differentiated monocyte inherits the trained epigenome from the HSPC pool. Murine mechanistic data are supported by human transcriptomic evidence from a β-glucan supplementation study showing analogous myelopoiesis changes. This central-training model is the mechanistic backbone for the project's use of trained immunity to explain antigen-independent PAIS chronification.

## Key Findings

### 1. β-Glucan induces myeloid progenitor expansion in bone marrow

β-Glucan training expanded CMP and GMP compartments in mouse bone marrow, with increased progenitor numbers and proliferative activity [UNVERIFIED — specific fold-changes]. This expansion indicates that the bone marrow myeloid output is actively modulated by the training stimulus, not merely passive replacement of consumed peripheral monocytes.

### 2. BM-derived monocytes from trained mice are functionally hyperreactive

Monocytes generated from trained bone marrow (assessed after the acute training stimulus had cleared) produced significantly elevated TNF, IL-6, and IL-1β upon LPS restimulation compared to monocytes from control mice [UNVERIFIED — specific cytokine fold-changes]. Critically, this hyperreactivity was present in newly differentiated BM-output monocytes — meaning the trained state was transmissible through differentiation.

### 3. Adoptive transfer proves HSPC-level imprinting

When bone marrow cells from β-glucan-trained mice were transferred to naive irradiated recipients and allowed to reconstitute the hematopoietic system, recipient mice displayed trained-immunity functional responses to LPS challenge. This is the decisive experiment establishing that the trained state is encoded in hematopoietic progenitor cells, not merely in circulating cells or systemic humoral signals. It directly proves the **central training** model: imprinting at the HSPC level, not just peripheral monocyte-level epigenetic memory.

### 4. Trained progenitors are reprogrammed toward glycolysis and cholesterol biosynthesis

Transcriptomic and metabolomic profiling of sorted trained myeloid progenitors identified two predominant pathway enrichments: (1) **aerobic glycolysis / HIF-1α axis** (consistent with the mTOR/HIF-1α glycolytic switch established in trained monocytes by Cheng2014) and (2) **cholesterol biosynthesis / mevalonate pathway**. These metabolic reprogramming signatures were present in progenitor cells, not only in differentiated monocytes — indicating that the metabolic shift is inscribed during myelopoiesis, not acquired after differentiation. [UNVERIFIED — which specific transcripts and metabolites were highlighted]

### 5. IL-6 and M-CSF are required paracrine signals for myelopoiesis modulation

Antibody-mediated blockade of IL-6 signaling (anti-IL-6R) and/or M-CSF signaling significantly blunted β-glucan-induced myeloid progenitor expansion in the bone marrow. This establishes that trained immunity engages a bone-marrow niche signaling axis — not only direct dectin-1 recognition on progenitors — to drive myelopoiesis. The IL-6 requirement is particularly relevant because it connects β-glucan central training to the same IL-6 → HSPC axis demonstrated for COVID-19 by Cheong2023 (which explicitly blocked IL-6R to prevent HSPC imprinting), and mechanistically grounds `question:0026` (IL-6/STAT3 HSPC imprinting in acute infection).

### 6. Human β-glucan validation: consistent myelopoiesis transcriptional reprogramming

In healthy human volunteers receiving β-glucan, circulating monocyte precursors showed transcriptional changes enriched for the same glycolysis and cholesterol-biosynthesis pathway signatures as murine trained progenitors [UNVERIFIED — specific gene sets and enrichment scores]. This translational result, while limited to transcriptomics without chromatin-level epigenomics, supports the generalizability of the murine central-training model to human myelopoiesis.

### 7. NLRP3-independent mechanism (contrast with Christ2018)

β-Glucan signals through dectin-1 → CARD9/Syk, not through NLRP3. The Mitroulis2018 central-training model is therefore **NLRP3-independent**, in contrast to Christ2018's Western-diet model where Nlrp3−/− mice are fully protected from GMP reprogramming. This establishes that multiple upstream signals (diet-derived cholesterol → NLRP3; pathogen-derived β-glucan → dectin-1/CARD9) can converge on central trained immunity through different initiating pathways.

## Relevance

This is the **mechanistic backbone paper** for the central-training model in this project's trained-immunity axis. Its role-in-project importance is twofold:

**1. Explains PAIS chronification by antigen-independent mechanism.** Circulating monocytes, with a lifespan of 1–7 days, cannot sustain months-long hyperreactivity by peripheral-monocyte epigenetic memory alone. Mitroulis2018 resolves this problem: HSPC-level imprinting in bone marrow generates a continuously renewed supply of trained monocytes. If PAIS-relevant acute infections imprint HSPCs (as Cheong2023 showed for COVID-19), every new monocyte entering circulation arrives pre-activated. The trained-immunity frame thus predicts antigen-independent inflammatory hyperreactivity for as long as the HSPC epigenomic imprint persists — the most parsimonious mechanistic explanation for PAIS chronification in the absence of persistent antigen.

**2. Links IL-6 signaling to central training — the mechanistic backbone for `question:0026`.** By demonstrating that IL-6/M-CSF signaling in the bone marrow is **required** for β-glucan-induced myelopoiesis expansion, this paper directly connects to the Cheong2023 finding that IL-6 blockade prevents COVID-HSPC imprinting, and grounds the IL-6 → STAT3 → HSPC chromatin remodeling circuit in `question:0026` as a shared imprinting axis. The PAIS-specific prediction is that acute infection severity (which scales IL-6 output) determines HSPC imprinting depth and thus PAIS duration.

**Relation to project hypotheses and topics:**

- `topic:innate-immune-memory-trained-immunity-in-pais`: Mitroulis2018 is the load-bearing central-training reference establishing the distinction between peripheral-monocyte memory (self-limiting, days) and HSPC-level imprinting (durable, months). The topic's "peripheral vs central training" section is built on this paper.

## Limitations

**1. Non-infectious β-glucan model — PAIS relevance by analogy.** β-Glucan is a fungal cell-wall component recognized via dectin-1/CARD9. It is not a natural signal in most PAIS-defining infections (COVID-19, Lyme, EBV, Coxiella). Whether acute viral or bacterial infection trains HSPCs through the same progenitor-expansion mechanism, and whether the same metabolic pathways are engaged, requires separate evidence. Cheong2023 and Bomans2018 partially supply this for COVID-19 and sepsis, but mechanistic identity is not established.

**2. Absence of PAIS or post-infectious cohort data.** The study is a fundamental immunology experiment with no post-infectious clinical design, no PAIS case definition, no symptom correlation. The PAIS application is entirely inferential.

**3. Epigenomic characterization of progenitors may be limited.** The paper's mechanistic contribution is primarily functional (adoptive transfer, cytokine production) and transcriptomic (RNA-seq). Whether the trained progenitors also have demonstrable chromatin-level epigenetic changes (H3K4me3, H3K27ac, ATAC-seq open chromatin at specific loci) comparable to what Cheong2023 showed in COVID-trained HSPCs is [UNVERIFIED] from the sources available here. Cheong2023 provided the ATAC-seq characterization of the imprint; Mitroulis2018 established the functional and transcriptomic existence of progenitor-level training.

**4. Human data is transcriptomic only.** The human validation uses circulating monocyte precursor RNA-seq after β-glucan supplementation. It does not characterize bone marrow progenitors directly, does not include chromatin-level assays (ChIP-seq, ATAC-seq), and does not demonstrate the persistence of the trained state after monocyte renewal. The murine adoptive-transfer proof of HSPC-level imprinting has not been replicated in a human experimental model.

**5. Specificity of IL-6/M-CSF requirement.** The blocking experiments establish necessity for myelopoiesis expansion but do not delineate which downstream signaling nodes (STAT3? MEK/ERK? PI3K?) in HSPCs translate IL-6/M-CSF signals into epigenomic or transcriptional changes. This specificity gap matters for therapeutic targeting (which node to block, and when, to prevent imprinting without globally immunosuppressing the patient).

## Follow-up

**Closely related papers already in project:**

- `paper:Christ2018` — contrasting NLRP3-dependent central training (WD/oxLDL stimulus, GMP ATAC-seq at Tet2/Tlr4); together with Mitroulis2018 establishes that central training proceeds through NLRP3-dependent and NLRP3-independent routes depending on the initiating signal.
- `paper:Saeed2014` — genome-wide H3K4me3/H3K27ac/DHS map of β-glucan-trained monocytes; provides the epigenomic landscape of the downstream trained cells that Mitroulis2018's centrally-trained progenitors give rise to.
- `paper:Cheong2023` — COVID-19 HSPC ATAC-seq reprogramming persisting to 12 months; IL-6 blockade prevents the imprint; the most important PAIS-relevant extension of the Mitroulis2018 central-training model. Together these papers constitute the mechanistic spine: Mitroulis2018 establishes HSPC-level central training is real and functional; Cheong2023 shows it happens after SARS-CoV-2 and requires IL-6.

**Key open questions for the project:**

1. **Does the Mitroulis2018 adoptive-transfer proof generalize to post-viral infection?** If SARS-CoV-2-infected mice (or a surrogate coronavirus) trained HSPCs, would adoptive transfer of BM from those mice into naive recipients transmit trained monocyte hyperreactivity? This would directly establish that viral PAIS-relevant infections can centrally train HSPCs.

2. **What are the chromatin-level epigenetic marks in Mitroulis2018's trained progenitors?** If H3K4me3 or ATAC-seq profiling was not performed in the original paper, follow-up studies identifying the locus-level epigenomic signature in β-glucan-trained progenitors would complete the mechanistic picture and provide a reference epigenome for PAIS-HSPC comparison.

3. **`question:0026` — can the IL-6/STAT3 → HSPC axis be pharmacologically intercepted?** Mitroulis2018's anti-IL-6R blocking experiment defines the window: IL-6 signaling during/shortly after the training stimulus must be blocked to prevent BM myelopoiesis reprogramming. For PAIS, this implies an early intervention window (acute illness phase, possibly within days of peak IL-6) and suggests that tocilizumab or sarilumab given during acute severe COVID might prevent the HSPC imprint — a testable hypothesis from this model.

4. **Does the mevalonate/cholesterol pathway enrichment in trained progenitors explain why statin use modifies PAIS risk?** Population data on statin use and PAIS risk could be mechanistically interpreted through the cholesterol-biosynthesis-dependent training signature in Mitroulis2018's progenitors.
