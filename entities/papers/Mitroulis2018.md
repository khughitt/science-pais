## Key Contribution

This **mouse-only** study established that β-glucan training induces functional, transcriptomic and
metabolic reprogramming of myeloid **progenitors** in the bone marrow — not only of peripheral
circulating monocytes — and that this progenitor-level modulation is **an integral, mechanistically
required component** of durable trained immunity. The decisive experiment is an adoptive transfer:
**sorted long-term hematopoietic stem cells (LT-HSCs)** from β-glucan-trained donor mice, transplanted
into lethally irradiated naive recipients, transmitted a trained-immunity phenotype — demonstrating
that the imprint resides in the HSPC compartment itself rather than in soluble/systemic signals. The
paper thereby explains why trained immunity persists for months beyond the 1–7 day lifespan of
circulating monocytes: the trained state is anchored upstream in the self-renewing progenitor pool, so
newly differentiated monocytes continue to arrive pre-conditioned. This central-training model is the
mechanistic backbone for the project's use of trained immunity to explain antigen-independent PAIS
chronification. It is a fundamental-immunology paper with **no infection, no human arm, and no PAIS
cohort** — its PAIS application is entirely by analogy.

## Methods

**Murine β-glucan training model.** C57BL/6 mice received β-glucan (single intraperitoneal injection)
and were analyzed at time points extending well beyond monocyte turnover (progenitor and adoptive-
transfer analyses at ~28 days after training). Bone marrow was harvested for progenitor analysis,
functional restimulation assays, transcriptomics, and metabolomics.

**Progenitor phenotyping and functional assays.** Bone-marrow hematopoietic progenitor compartments
(LT-HSCs, ST-HSCs / MPPs, common myeloid progenitors (CMPs), granulocyte-monocyte progenitors (GMPs),
and megakaryocyte-erythrocyte progenitors (MEPs)) were quantified by flow cytometry using
Lin−Sca1−c-Kit+ (LSK) and downstream gating strategies. Myeloid cells were restimulated ex vivo (e.g.
LPS) to measure cytokine production as a functional trained-immunity readout.

**Adoptive transfer (key mechanistic experiment).** **Sorted LT-HSCs** (CD45.2+) from β-glucan-trained
or PBS-control mice were transplanted into lethally irradiated recipient mice (CD45.1+). After
hematopoietic reconstitution, donor-derived myeloid progeny were assessed for trained functional
responses. This experiment provides the central evidence that the training imprint is carried by the
LT-HSC compartment, not by peripheral monocytes or systemic humoral signals.

**Transcriptomics (RNA-seq) of progenitors.** Sorted myeloid progenitor populations from β-glucan-
trained vs control mice were profiled by RNA-seq; pathway enrichment identified metabolic programs
differentially activated in trained progenitors.

**Metabolomics.** LC-MS-based metabolomics of trained progenitors characterized the intracellular
metabolic state (glycolytic and lipid/cholesterol-biosynthesis programs).

**Cytokine / signalling perturbation.** The study identified **IL-1β** and **GM-CSF (via the CD131
common β-chain)** as the paracrine signals driving β-glucan-induced myelopoiesis modulation: IL-1β
signaling promoted glycolysis and HSPC proliferation, and the GM-CSF/CD131 axis was required for the
progenitor response. Systemic **IL-6 was not altered** in this model — an important negative that
distinguishes this β-glucan central-training circuit from the IL-6-dependent COVID-HSPC circuit
reported later by Cheong2023.

## Key Findings

### 1. β-Glucan induces myeloid progenitor expansion in bone marrow

β-Glucan training expanded the CMP and GMP compartments and shifted HSPC output toward myelopoiesis,
indicating the marrow myeloid program is actively modulated by the training stimulus rather than merely
passively replacing consumed peripheral monocytes.

### 2. Progeny myeloid cells from trained marrow are functionally hyperreactive

Myeloid cells derived from trained bone marrow (assessed after the acute stimulus had cleared) produced
elevated inflammatory cytokines upon restimulation compared to controls, meaning the trained state was
transmissible through differentiation from the progenitor pool.

### 3. Adoptive transfer of sorted LT-HSCs proves HSPC-level imprinting

When **sorted LT-HSCs** from β-glucan-trained mice were transferred into naive irradiated recipients
and allowed to reconstitute the hematopoietic system, donor-derived progeny displayed trained-immunity
functional responses. This is the decisive experiment establishing that the trained state is encoded in
the long-term stem-cell compartment, not merely in circulating cells or systemic humoral signals — the
core evidence for the **central training** model.

### 4. Trained progenitors are reprogrammed toward glycolysis and cholesterol/lipid biosynthesis

Transcriptomic and metabolomic profiling of sorted trained progenitors identified enrichment of (1)
**glycolysis** (consistent with the mTOR/HIF-1α glycolytic switch established in trained monocytes by
Cheng2014) and (2) **cholesterol / lipid biosynthesis (mevalonate pathway)**. These signatures were
present in progenitor cells, indicating the metabolic shift is inscribed during myelopoiesis rather than
acquired only after differentiation.

### 5. IL-1β and GM-CSF are the required paracrine signals for myelopoiesis modulation

The study identified **IL-1β** (promoting glycolysis and HSPC proliferation) and **GM-CSF signaling via
CD131** as required for β-glucan-induced myeloid progenitor reprogramming and expansion. This
establishes that trained immunity engages a bone-marrow niche cytokine axis — not only direct dectin-1
recognition on progenitors — to drive myelopoiesis. **Note the contrast with the project's COVID
evidence:** the imprinting cytokine here is IL-1β/GM-CSF, and IL-6 was *not* altered; the IL-6→HSPC
axis relevant to `question:0026` is grounded separately by Cheong2023 (which blocked IL-6R to prevent
COVID-HSPC imprinting), not by this paper. Mitroulis2018 supplies the general principle — *a paracrine
cytokine signal gates central myelopoiesis reprogramming* — that Cheong2023 later instantiated for a
different cytokine.

### 6. NLRP3-independent β-glucan route (contrast with Christ2018)

β-Glucan signals through dectin-1 → CARD9/Syk, not through NLRP3. The Mitroulis2018 central-training
route is therefore **NLRP3-independent**, in contrast to Christ2018's Western-diet model where
*Nlrp3−/−* mice are protected from GMP reprogramming. Multiple upstream signals (diet-derived
cholesterol → NLRP3; pathogen-derived β-glucan → dectin-1/CARD9) can thus converge on central trained
immunity through different initiating pathways.

## Relevance

This is the **mechanistic backbone paper** for the central-training model in this project's
trained-immunity axis. Its role-in-project importance is twofold:

**1. Explains PAIS chronification by antigen-independent mechanism.** Circulating monocytes, with a
lifespan of 1–7 days, cannot sustain months-long hyperreactivity by peripheral-monocyte memory alone.
Mitroulis2018 resolves this: LT-HSC-level imprinting in bone marrow generates a continuously renewed
supply of trained monocytes. If PAIS-relevant acute infections imprint HSPCs (as Cheong2023 showed for
COVID-19), every new monocyte entering circulation arrives pre-conditioned. The trained-immunity frame
thus predicts antigen-independent inflammatory hyperreactivity for as long as the HSPC imprint persists
— the most parsimonious mechanistic explanation for PAIS chronification in the absence of persistent
antigen.

**2. Establishes cytokine-gated central training as a real axis — the mechanistic template for
`question:0026`.** By demonstrating that a paracrine cytokine signal (here IL-1β and GM-CSF) is
**required** for β-glucan-induced myelopoiesis reprogramming, this paper establishes the general
principle that acute cytokine output can imprint the marrow progenitor pool. `question:0026`'s specific
IL-6/STAT3→HSPC hypothesis is a *different cytokine instance* of this template, and its direct evidence
is Cheong2023 (IL-6 blockade prevents COVID-HSPC imprinting), **not** Mitroulis2018 — indeed Mitroulis
found IL-6 unaltered in the β-glucan model. Mitroulis2018 is the proof-of-concept that cytokine-gated
central training exists; Cheong2023 supplies the IL-6-specific, PAIS-relevant instance.

**Relation to project hypotheses and topics:**

- `topic:innate-immune-memory-trained-immunity-in-pais`: Mitroulis2018 is the load-bearing central-
  training reference establishing the distinction between peripheral-monocyte memory (self-limiting,
  days) and HSPC-level imprinting (durable, months). The topic's "peripheral vs central training"
  section is built on this paper.
- `hypothesis:0001-shared-dysregulated-attractor`: Central HSPC training provides a stability mechanism
  that could lock the system into the attractor state after pathogen clearance — the trained progenitor
  pool continuously replenishes inflammatory myeloid output, sustaining the attractor without antigen.
- `hypothesis:0003-immune-exhaustion-feedback`: Trained innate output (from HSPC-derived monocytes) and
  adaptive exhaustion (from antigen-driven T-cell exhaustion) are complementary: even as adaptive cells
  exhaust, the innate trained output continues, a candidate explanation for the paradoxical coexistence
  of inflammation and exhaustion observed in long COVID.
- `hypothesis:0004-acute-severity-threshold`: If a threshold level of paracrine cytokine signaling
  (IL-1β/GM-CSF here; IL-6 in the COVID instance) is required for BM myelopoiesis reprogramming,
  acute-illness severity (which governs cytokine peaks) becomes a gating variable for whether central
  imprinting is induced — mechanistically coherent with the severity-threshold for PAIS risk.

**What this paper does NOT provide:**
- Direct PAIS evidence: no infection, no post-infectious cohort, no PAIS phenotypes studied.
- Any human data: this is a mouse-only study; there is no human β-glucan supplementation arm or human
  transcriptomic validation.
- The IL-6→HSPC link: the imprinting cytokines here are IL-1β and GM-CSF; IL-6 was unaltered.
- Chromatin-level epigenomics of progenitors: the study characterized functional, transcriptomic and
  metabolomic changes; the authors explicitly note that epigenetic-level analyses of the trained
  progenitors were left to future work (Cheong2023 later supplied the ATAC-seq characterization for the
  COVID-HSPC case).

## Limitations

**1. Non-infectious β-glucan model — PAIS relevance by analogy.** β-Glucan is a fungal cell-wall
component recognized via dectin-1/CARD9. It is not a natural signal in most PAIS-defining infections
(COVID-19, Lyme, EBV, Coxiella). Whether acute viral or bacterial infection trains HSPCs through the
same progenitor-expansion mechanism, and whether the same cytokines and metabolic pathways are engaged,
requires separate evidence. Cheong2023 (COVID) partially supplies this — but via an IL-6-dependent
route distinct from the IL-1β/GM-CSF route here — and Bomans2018 (mouse sepsis) supplies a marrow-
monocyte parallel; mechanistic identity across triggers is not established.

**2. Absence of PAIS or post-infectious cohort data.** The study is a fundamental immunology experiment
with no post-infectious clinical design, no PAIS case definition, no symptom correlation. The PAIS
application is entirely inferential.

**3. Mouse-only; no human data.** All experiments are in mice. There is no human β-glucan supplementation
arm and no human transcriptomic validation. The LT-HSC adoptive-transfer proof of HSPC-level imprinting
has not been replicated in a human experimental model.

**4. No chromatin-level epigenomics of progenitors.** The paper's mechanistic contribution is functional
(adoptive transfer, cytokine production), transcriptomic (RNA-seq) and metabolomic. It did not perform
ChIP-seq / ATAC-seq on sorted progenitors; the authors flag locus-level epigenetic characterization as
future work. Cheong2023 later provided the ATAC-seq characterization of the COVID-HSPC imprint;
Mitroulis2018 established the functional/transcriptomic/metabolic existence of progenitor-level training.

**5. Specificity of the IL-1β/GM-CSF requirement.** The perturbation experiments establish necessity for
myelopoiesis modulation but do not fully delineate which downstream nodes translate these signals into
transcriptional/metabolic changes in HSPCs. This gap matters for therapeutic targeting.

## Follow-up

**Closely related papers already in project:**

- `paper:Christ2018` — contrasting NLRP3-dependent central training (WD/oxLDL stimulus, GMP ATAC-seq at
  Tet2/Tlr4); together with Mitroulis2018 establishes that central training proceeds through
  NLRP3-dependent and NLRP3-independent routes depending on the initiating signal.
- `paper:Saeed2014` — genome-wide H3K4me3/H3K27ac/DHS map of β-glucan-trained monocytes; provides the
  epigenomic landscape of the downstream trained cells that Mitroulis2018's centrally-trained
  progenitors give rise to.
- `paper:Cheong2023` — COVID-19 HSPC ATAC-seq reprogramming persisting to 12 months; IL-6 blockade
  prevents the imprint. The most important PAIS-relevant extension of the central-training model, and —
  crucially — the source of the IL-6→HSPC link that Mitroulis2018 does *not* provide. Together these
  papers constitute the mechanistic spine: Mitroulis2018 establishes LT-HSC-level central training is
  real and functional (via IL-1β/GM-CSF); Cheong2023 shows an analogous imprint occurs after
  SARS-CoV-2 and requires IL-6.

**Key open questions for the project:**

1. **Does the Mitroulis2018 LT-HSC adoptive-transfer proof generalize to post-viral infection?** If
   SARS-CoV-2-infected mice (or a surrogate coronavirus) trained HSPCs, would transfer of sorted
   LT-HSCs into naive recipients transmit trained monocyte hyperreactivity? This would directly
   establish that viral PAIS-relevant infections can centrally train HSPCs.

2. **What are the chromatin-level marks in trained progenitors?** ATAC-seq / H3K4me3 profiling of
   β-glucan-trained progenitors (not performed here) would complete the mechanistic picture and provide
   a reference epigenome for PAIS-HSPC comparison.

3. **`question:0026` — can the acute cytokine→HSPC axis be pharmacologically intercepted?** The
   Mitroulis2018 perturbations define an early window during/shortly after the training stimulus. For
   PAIS, Cheong2023's IL-6-blockade result (not the IL-1β/GM-CSF axis here) is the directly actionable
   lead — implying tocilizumab/sarilumab during acute severe COVID might prevent the HSPC imprint, a
   testable hypothesis.

4. **Does the mevalonate/cholesterol enrichment in trained progenitors explain statin effects on PAIS
   risk?** Population data on statin use and PAIS risk could be mechanistically interpreted through the
   cholesterol-biosynthesis-dependent training signature in Mitroulis2018's progenitors.
