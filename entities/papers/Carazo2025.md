# Effectiveness of COVID-19 Vaccination and Prior Infections to Reduce Long COVID Risk

- **Authors:** Sara Carazo, Manale Ouakki, Nektaria Nicolakakis, Emilia Falcone, Danuta M. Skowronski, Marie-Jose Durand, Marie-France Coutu, Simon Decary, et al.
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal:** Clinical Infectious Diseases
- **DOI:** 10.1093/cid/ciaf549
- **BibTeX key:** Carazo2025

## Key Contribution

Carazo2025 directly addresses the modern immunity state: vaccination plus prior infection in an
Omicron-era healthcare-worker cohort.

## Methods

The study combined survey data from Quebec healthcare workers with immunization-registry and laboratory
administrative data. Long COVID was self-reported symptoms persisting at least 12 weeks. Vaccine
effectiveness was estimated against symptomatic COVID-19 and long COVID across pre-Omicron and Omicron
periods, stratified by infection history.

## Key Findings

- Pre-Omicron, one- and two-dose vaccination was highly protective against COVID-19 and long COVID.
- During Omicron, booster-dose effectiveness was 41% against COVID-19 and 57% against long COVID, with
  waning by 6 months.
- Hybrid immunity estimates were high (roughly 81-92%) regardless of number of doses, prior infecting
  variant, or time since last immunological event up to 9 months.

## Relevance

This supports the t010 update that prior immunity is a risk modifier, but it also complicates simple
"more boosters always add large long-COVID protection" language: in a population with widespread hybrid
immunity, incremental booster benefit may be smaller than the primary contrast against immunological
naivete.

## Limitations

Healthcare-worker cohorts are exposure- and ascertainment-selected, and long COVID is self-reported.
The evidence is useful for modern risk modification but should not be used as a mechanistic mediator
test of antigen burden or threshold dynamics.
