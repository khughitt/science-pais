# Effect of covid-19 vaccination on long covid

- **Authors:** Oyungerel Byambasuren, Paulina Stehlik, Justin Clark, Kylie Alcorn, Paul Glasziou
- **Year:** 2023
- **Journal:** BMJ Medicine
- **DOI:** 10.1136/bmjmed-2022-000385
- **PMID:** 36936268
- **PMCID:** PMC9978692
- **BibTeX key:** Byambasuren2023

## Key Contribution

This systematic review is the main anchor for the weaker "vaccination after infection or after long-COVID
diagnosis modifies symptom trajectory" part of `task:t010`.

## Methods

The review searched PubMed, Embase, Cochrane COVID-19 trials, and Europe PMC through 2022-08-03 for
studies of vaccination before infection, after infection, or after long-COVID diagnosis. It found no
randomized trials and relied on observational studies.

## Key Findings

The review concluded that current studies suggested possible protective and therapeutic effects of
vaccination on long COVID, but certainty was low because the evidence base was observational and at risk
of confounding.

## Relevance

For this project, the paper separates two questions that should not be collapsed:

1. pre-infection vaccination reducing incidence, where later evidence is stronger; and
2. vaccination after long-COVID onset improving symptoms, where evidence remains weak and trial-needed.

## Limitations

No randomized trials were available in the search window. Studies often failed to adjust adequately for
health behavior, missing data, prior infection, severity, vaccination timing, and regression-to-the-mean
in symptom trajectories. This paper should not be treated as strong evidence that vaccination reverses
established PAIS.
