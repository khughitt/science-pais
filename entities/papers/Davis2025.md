# Dysregulation of lipid metabolism, energy production, and oxidative stress in myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome, Gulf War Syndrome and fibromyalgia

- **Authors:** Leah Davis, Maisy Higgs, Ailsa Snaith, Tiffany A. Lodge, James Strong, Jose A. Espejo-Oltra, Stawomir Kujawski, Pawel Zalewski, Etheresia Pretorius, Michael Hoerger, Karl J. Morten
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal:** Frontiers in Neuroscience, vol. 19, p. 1498981
- **DOI:** 10.3389/fnins.2025.1498981
- **BibTeX key:** Davis2025
- **Source:** Full-text PDF (papers/pdfs/2025_Davis_lipid-energy-oxidative-mecfs-gws-fibromyalgia.pdf), read 2026-06-20.

## Key Contribution

A narrative review arguing that ME/CFS, Gulf War Syndrome (GWS), and fibromyalgia (FM) share a common pattern of metabolic dysfunction — specifically convergent dysregulation of lipid metabolism, energy production (purine/TCA cycle, mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation), and oxidative stress — which may underlie overlapping symptoms including post-exertional malaise (PEM), fatigue, cognitive impairment, and pain. The paper synthesises plasma/serum metabolomics, proteomics, and neuroimaging (MRI/MRSI) evidence to argue these shared pathway disturbances warrant cross-condition biomarker discovery and could inform common therapeutic targets. No new primary data are generated; the contribution is synthesis and cross-condition framing.

## Methods

**Type.** Narrative review (not systematic, no PRISMA). Frontiers in Neuroscience, open access, received September 2024, published March 2025.

**Scope.** Plasma and serum metabolomics, proteomics of peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs), whole-body imaging (MRI/MRSI), and selected proteomics studies in cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) across ME/CFS, GWS, and FM cohorts. Comparisons to Long COVID, Alzheimer's disease, and bipolar disorder are used as contextual anchors.

**Case definitions used in reviewed studies.** ME/CFS: Fukuda CDC-1994 criteria, Canadian Consensus Criteria (CCC), and 2015 Institute of Medicine (IOM) criteria — heterogeneous across studies. FM: ICD-11 "chronic widespread pain" (MG30.01). GWS: CDC Chronic Multisymptomatic Illness (CMI) criteria (symptoms from at least two of three clusters: general fatigue, mood/cognitive abnormalities, musculoskeletal pain).

**Key primary study highlighted.** Naviaux et al. (2016) — targeted metabolomics in 20 HC, 20 GWS males, and 22 ME/CFS males using Fukuda/CCC/CDC-1994 criteria; identified purine metabolism downregulation in both conditions.

**Analytic platforms reviewed.** NMR spectroscopy, LC-MS, GC-MS (untargeted and targeted metabolomics); SWATH-MS (proteomics); ventricular lactate by MRSI; BOLD fMRI for brain metabolic response.

## Key Findings

**Purine metabolism.** Consistently downregulated in both ME/CFS and GWS. Decreased hypoxanthine serum levels observed during PEM (McGregor et al. 2019). Reduced uric acid in GWS serum. Purine dysregulation impairs intracellular energy homeostasis (NADP/NADPH) and extracellular purinergic signalling.

**Lipid metabolism.** Ceramides, sphingomyelins, phospholipids, diacylglycerols, and triglycerides are dysregulated across ME/CFS and GWS (and FM). Ceramide elevation activates pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF-α) in macrophages and serum. Sphingomyelin pathway products are increased in GWS and decreased in ME/CFS, pointing to condition-specific directions within a shared dysregulated pathway. Elevated odd-chain acylcarnitines in GWS plasma suggest peripheral fatty acid oxidation impairment (likely not CNS-primary).

**Mitochondrial/oxidative phosphorylation dysfunction.** Muscle biopsies from GWS patients show significantly reduced complex I and II oxidative phosphorylation activity, more pronounced when glucose (not fatty acids) is the fuel source; impairment correlates with peripheral inflammation markers. Mouse model of GWS (pyridostigmine bromide + permethrin exposure) reveals decreased respiratory chain complexes I, II, IV, and V in brain tissue, with mitochondria disturbances and cardiolipin reduction — a strong marker of ETC activity loss.

**Oxidative stress.** Consistently elevated across all three conditions: elevated malondialdehyde (MDA), isoprostanes, 8-OH-deoxyguanosine, 2,3-diphosphoglyceric acid, thiobutyric acid, and protein carbonyls in ME/CFS. Reduced cortical glutathione (GSH) in ME/CFS by MRSI. Elevated TALDO (transaldolase, NADPH-linked) in FM saliva and serum. Oxidative stress is proposed as both downstream consequence and upstream driver (mitochondrial ROS → DNA damage → further ETC impairment cycle).

**Tryptophan / kynurenine / NAD+ axis.** Tryptophan consistently reduced in FM, elevated in ME/CFS plasma; kynurenine consistently lower in both. IDO-1 upregulation (by IFN-γ, TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-12) diverts tryptophan to kynurenine, depleting NAD+. Reduced NAD+ impairs sirtuin activity (epigenetic/mitochondrial regulation), contributes to fatigue, and is implicated in impaired circadian coordination. Low kynurenine correlates with cognitive symptoms (brain fog, memory issues) in ME/CFS and FM.

**Taurine.** Elevated in FM, reduced in ME/CFS plasma. Taurine supports mitochondrial function, GSH stores, and bile acid synthesis; its dysregulation may contribute to gut microbiome dysbiosis (already elevated in ME/CFS).

**Proteomics (PBMCs).** Sweetman et al. (2020) identified 60 differentially expressed proteins in ME/CFS PBMCs; notable upregulation of BDH1 (ketone body metabolism) and ACAT2 (fatty acid/ketone processing), and SLC25A11 (mitochondrial oxoglutarate carrier). SLC25A11 elevation may increase malate import to complex I, augmenting mitochondrial ATP — or augment 2-oxoglutarate-dependent hydroxylation enzymes involved in stress/hypoxia sensing. Machine learning using 7 plasma proteins distinguished ME/CFS from HC with 79.1% accuracy (AUROC 0.891) in Giloteaux et al. (2023).

**MRI/MRSI.** ME/CFS patients show global BOLD signal increase post-exercise (consistent with systemic metabolic dysregulation); GWS shows localised BOLD decreases (dorsal midbrain, insula, Rolandic operculum), suggesting neurotoxic/regional effects rather than systemic metabolic failure. Ventricular CSF lactate elevated in ME/CFS and FM (not clearly in GWS); resting blood lactate elevated in 44.7% of ME/CFS patients and correlated with disease severity. These MRSI lactate findings support mitochondrial impairment with glycolytic compensation.

**Post-exertional malaise (PEM) metabolic signature.** Germain et al. (2022) two-CPET design in ME/CFS shows metabolic shift from hypometabolic at rest to hypermetabolic post-exercise, with significant changes in urea cycle, glutamate, and pyruvate metabolism. McGregor et al. (2019) confirmed hypoxanthine depletion in serum post-PEM, implicating insufficient ATP replenishment from adenosine precursors.

**Therapeutic implications reviewed.** TCA metabolite oxaloacetate improved fatigue scores in an ME/CFS trial (Cash and Kaufman 2022), attributed to ROS scavenging and mitochondrial integrity preservation. AXA1125 (leucine, isoleucine, valine, arginine, glutamine, N-acetylcysteine) showed fatigue improvement in a placebo-controlled Long COVID trial; mechanism attributed to combined mitochondrial bioenergetics enhancement and GSH replenishment.

## Relevance

**question:0011-mitochondrial-basis-of-pem.** The review directly addresses the question by synthesising evidence that PEM reflects failure of energy production pathways (purine depletion, mitochondrial OXPHOS impairment, TCA cycle dysfunction, lactate accumulation) rather than a behavioural or deconditioning phenomenon. The two-CPET metabolomics data and GWS muscle biopsy findings provide the strongest mechanistic anchors.

**question:0001-shared-molecular-signature-across-triggers.** Cross-condition comparison of ME/CFS, GWS, and FM metabolic profiles is the paper's core argument. Common pathway disruptions (oxidative stress, purine/lipid/tryptophan metabolism) appear across conditions with different aetiologies, lending support to the idea that shared downstream molecular signatures can emerge from distinct triggers.

**hypothesis:0001-shared-dysregulated-attractor.** The convergence of metabolic dysfunction patterns across ME/CFS, GWS, and FM — despite divergent triggers (viral infection vs. toxic chemical exposure vs. idiopathic/EBV-associated) — is consistent with the hypothesis that these conditions represent a common dysregulated attractor state in metabolic/immune homeostasis.

**paper:Naviaux2016.** Naviaux et al. (2016) is the primary quantitative metabolomics anchor for the ME/CFS and GWS purine/lipid findings in this review. Davis 2025 builds directly on that targeted metabolomics dataset.

**topic:mecfs-long-covid-convergence.** The review explicitly notes that 20-31% of Long COVID patients meet FM criteria at 6 months, and that EBV reactivation is shared across ME/CFS, FM, and Long COVID. The RECOVER study finding of many Long COVID patients meeting ME/CFS criteria is cited as a driver of increasing ME/CFS prevalence.

## Limitations

**Review type and selection bias.** Narrative, non-systematic review. No PRISMA, no explicit inclusion/exclusion criteria reported, no quality assessment of primary studies. Heterogeneous primary cohorts (varying case definitions, sample matrices, analytic platforms) are compared without formal meta-analytic correction.

**Scope mismatch for PAIS project.** GWS and FM are NOT primarily post-infectious conditions. GWS is linked to organophosphate/toxic chemical exposure; FM has multifactorial origins (though EBV reactivation is implicated). The overlap with PAIS is meaningful (shared symptomatology, some shared aetiology hypotheses, EBV link) but should not be treated as equivalent to post-infectious conditions. This limits how directly the cross-condition findings generalise to the post-infectious context.

**Causality undetermined.** The review explicitly notes that whether metabolic dysregulation drives symptoms or is a downstream consequence of chronic illness cannot be established from the reviewed cross-sectional and case-control metabolomics data. This is a critical interpretive caveat.

**Cohort heterogeneity.** ME/CFS cohorts reviewed use Fukuda, CCC, and IOM criteria — which select different patient subgroups. Many reviewed studies are small (n = 10-57 patients). Sex imbalance is common (many cohorts predominantly female for ME/CFS; Naviaux 2016 used males only for GWS and ME/CFS).

**Directionality of metabolite changes.** Some metabolites (e.g., sphingomyelins: increased in GWS, decreased in ME/CFS; tryptophan: elevated in ME/CFS plasma, reduced in FM) differ in direction across conditions, complicating the unified-pathway narrative. The review acknowledges but does not fully resolve these discrepancies.

**Therapeutic evidence thin.** Oxaloacetate and AXA1125 findings are preliminary (small trials, partly in Long COVID not ME/CFS); conclusions about therapeutic relevance are speculative.

**No primary data.** The paper generates no new metabolomics, proteomics, or imaging data. All quantitative claims are attributed to cited primary studies; the synthesis itself is the contribution.

## Follow-up

- **Naviaux et al. 2016** (paper:Naviaux2016) — the foundational targeted metabolomics dataset underlying the ME/CFS / GWS purine and lipid claims; should be read alongside this review.
- **Germain et al. 2022** — two-CPET metabolomics study in ME/CFS establishing the hypo-to-hypermetabolic PEM shift; key primary evidence for question:0011.
- **McGregor et al. 2019** — serum and urine metabolomics in ME/CFS (n=46, CCC criteria); hypoxanthine depletion during PEM; purine axis primary evidence.
- **Sweetman et al. 2020** — SWATH-MS proteomics of PBMCs in ME/CFS; BDH1, ACAT2, SLC25A11 findings.
- **Giloteaux et al. 2023** — machine learning on plasma proteomics (7 proteins, 79.1% accuracy); potential biomarker panel for ME/CFS.
- **Cash and Kaufman 2022** — oxaloacetate RCT in ME/CFS; TCA metabolite therapeutic angle.
- Consider whether the tryptophan/IDO-1/NAD+ depletion axis should be linked to the immune-activation mechanism in health-immunity, as IDO-1 is upregulated by IFN-γ (a canonical chronic immune activation signal).
- The micro-clot / fibrinolysis-resistant amyloid connection (Pretorius et al. 2021, cited in Discussion) bridges to the thromboinflammation topic in this project.
