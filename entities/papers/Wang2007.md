# Complex dynamic behavior in a viral model with delayed immune response

## Key Contribution

Early within-host viral dynamics model showing that delayed immune response can destabilize an otherwise
stable infection state and generate complex dynamics, including Hopf bifurcation and sustained
oscillations.

## Methods

- **Model type:** delay differential viral model.
- **Core variables:** viral infection dynamics and delayed immune response.
- **Analysis:** equilibrium stability and bifurcation analysis, using immune-response delay as a control
  parameter.
- **Output of interest:** conditions under which delay changes the qualitative behavior of the system.

## Key Findings

- Immune-response delay is sufficient to change stability properties of a within-host viral model.
- Delay can produce Hopf bifurcation and periodic dynamics.
- The paper is a methods precedent for treating immune timing as a dynamical control parameter rather
  than a background covariate.

## Relevance

Useful for `question:0008` because it supplies a concrete mathematical mechanism by which delayed immune
feedback creates non-monotonic trajectories. It supports the *formalization strategy* for h0001, not the
biological claim itself.

## Limitations

- Not PAIS-specific and not fitted to chronic post-acute syndrome data.
- Focuses on virus-immune dynamics rather than multi-loop homeostatic non-recovery.
- A periodic infection model is an analogy; it does not by itself establish a stable PAIS attractor basin.
