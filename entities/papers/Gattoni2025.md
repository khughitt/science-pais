# Two-day cardiopulmonary exercise testing in long COVID post-exertional malaise diagnosis

- **Authors:** Chiara Gattoni, Asghar Abbasi, Carrie Ferguson, Charles W. Lanks, Thomas W. Decato, Harry B. Rossiter, Richard Casaburi, William W. Stringer
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal:** Respiratory Physiology and Neurobiology, vol. 331, p. 104362
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.resp.2024.104362
- **PMID:** 39490617
- **BibTeX key:** Gattoni2025
- **Source:** Europe PMC abstract (full text inaccessible; abstract confirmed via paper-fetch PMID 39490617)

## Key Contribution

The first study to apply the two-day cardiopulmonary exercise test (2-day CPET) protocol — the objective functional standard for demonstrating post-exertional malaise (PEM) in ME/CFS — specifically in a long COVID cohort with documented PEM. The headline null result: long COVID patients self-reporting PEM showed **no significant day-1 versus day-2 decrement** in any CPET endpoint (gas exchange threshold [GET], peak oxygen uptake [VO2peak], or peak work rate [WRpeak]; all p > 0.05). This stands in direct contrast to the well-replicated 2-day CPET result in ME/CFS, where day-2 impairment of GET and VO2 is a defining physiological signature. The finding implies that the bioenergetic recovery impairment that operationalizes PEM in ME/CFS either does not transfer to long COVID or is below the detection threshold of this study.

## Methods

**Study design.** Single-cohort observational. Two ramp-incremental CPETs separated by 24 hours; pulmonary function test on day 1 prior to CPET.

**Cohort.** N = 15 long COVID patients (7 female; mean age 53 ± 11 yr; mean BMI 32.2 ± 8.5 kg/m²). Recruitment site and long COVID case definition are [INACCESSIBLE] from the abstract alone, but the cohort is clinically defined as long COVID (article title and framing confirm this is a post-SARS-CoV-2 persistent-symptom sample). No healthy or recovered-COVID control arm reported in the abstract.

**PEM ascertainment.** Modified DePaul Symptom Questionnaire (mDSQ), assessing PEM over the past six months. Prevalence: 80% of patients (n = 12/15) met PEM criteria.

**CPET endpoints.** Gas exchange threshold (GET; equivalent to ventilatory/anaerobic threshold), peak oxygen uptake (VO2peak), and peak work rate (WRpeak). Ratings of perceived dyspnoea and leg effort recorded at peak exercise using modified Borg 0–10 scale.

**Statistical approach.** One-sample t-tests on mean day-1 minus day-2 differences for each endpoint. Significance threshold p < 0.05. No correction for multiple comparisons noted in the abstract.

**Limitations by design.** No active ME/CFS comparison arm; PEM-negative long COVID arm absent; exact effect sizes, confidence intervals, and power calculation are [INACCESSIBLE] from the abstract.

## Key Findings

1. **Null 2-day CPET result.** No CPET variable showed a statistically significant day-2 change from day-1: GET, VO2peak, and WRpeak all p > 0.05 by one-sample t-test on the mean difference. Neither direction of change (day-2 worse or better) was significant.

2. **High PEM self-report prevalence.** 80% of the cohort reported PEM on the mDSQ — confirming this is a PEM-enriched long COVID sample, not a general long COVID convenience sample. The disconnect between subjective PEM (prevalent) and objective CPET decrement (absent) is the study's core finding.

3. **Aerobic deconditioning common.** 40% of patients had VO2peak < 80% predicted in the absence of cardiovascular or pulmonary limitations on day-1 CPET, meeting the study's criterion for aerobic deconditioning. Lung function was normal in the cohort as a whole.

4. **Authors' interpretation.** PEM symptoms in long COVID, in the absence of day-2 CPET decrement, suggests PEM is not due to impaired recovery of exercise capacity between days in this population. The authors implicitly contrast this with ME/CFS without making that comparison within-study (no ME/CFS arm; contrast with ME/CFS literature is by reference).

## Relevance

This paper is a **cross-trigger counterweight** for the project's working framework. The project treats ME/CFS and long COVID as candidate instances of a shared post-infectious failure mode (hypothesis:0001-shared-dysregulated-attractor). The 2-day CPET has been the best-validated *objective* functional marker of PEM in ME/CFS: a reproducible day-2 GET/VO2 decrement has been replicated across multiple independent ME/CFS cohorts and is cited as evidence that PEM in ME/CFS involves a bioenergetic recovery failure that is distinct from deconditioning and distinct from subjective symptoms. If this result replicates in long COVID, it would support the shared-failure-mode hypothesis at the bioenergetic level.

This study instead reports the opposite: long COVID patients with predominant PEM self-report show **no reproducible CPET decrement**. This has several implications:

- **Against naive convergence:** PEM as a symptom label may not index a single, reproducible bioenergetic failure mode that transfers across infectious triggers. The ME/CFS 2-day CPET result is not simply reproduced in long COVID.
- **Heterogeneity of PEM mechanism:** Long COVID PEM and ME/CFS PEM may share phenomenological overlap but diverge in their objective bioenergetic correlates. Possible explanations include: (a) different underlying mechanisms (autonomic, vascular, or neuroinflammatory rather than mitochondrial-bioenergetic); (b) deconditioning being a major driver in this specific long COVID sample (40% deconditioned by VO2peak); (c) insufficient power/sample size to detect a small day-2 decrement; or (d) the 24-hour inter-test interval being suboptimal for long COVID PEM kinetics (which may recover more slowly or differently than in ME/CFS).
- **For question:0015:** Directly relevant to whether PEM requirement in case definitions improves cross-study biological coherence. This paper shows that PEM-enriched long COVID patients are *not* equivalent to PEM-enriched ME/CFS patients on the most validated PEM-specific functional assay — weakening (though not refuting) the claim that PEM enrichment produces biologically coherent, exchangeable cohorts across triggers.
- **For question:0001:** Provides functional evidence against a simple shared failure mode at the exercise-physiology level. The contrast with ME/CFS is, effectively, a within-PEM-label cross-trigger comparison showing divergence rather than convergence on an objective endpoint.

## Limitations

1. **Small N (n = 15); underpowered null.** A null result from 15 participants cannot confidently exclude a clinically meaningful day-2 CPET decrement. The study is almost certainly underpowered to detect a small effect size (e.g., ≤0.4 SD decrease in GET). Absence of a reported power calculation or confidence intervals for the day-1 vs day-2 difference in the abstract is a notable gap.

2. **No ME/CFS comparison arm.** The contrast with ME/CFS is made by reference to external literature, not within-study. Differences in CPET protocol, patient selection, deconditioning prevalence, and statistical approach across studies make direct comparison imprecise.

3. **No PEM-negative long COVID arm.** Without a PEM-negative long COVID control group, it is not possible to confirm whether the null CPET decrement is specific to the long COVID-PEM phenotype or simply characteristic of long COVID generally.

4. **Deconditioning not addressed as a confounder.** 40% of the cohort was aerobically deconditioned by VO2peak criteria. Deconditioning may confound the PEM attribution and may itself explain some of the exercise intolerance, blurring mechanistic interpretation. Lack of a prehabilitation control or fitness-matched design is a limitation the authors note in the abstract's framing.

5. **Case definition detail inaccessible.** The exact long COVID case definition (e.g. WHO 4-week; NICE/CDC; RECOVER index) and whether symptomatic PEM was a required inclusion criterion or merely measured post-hoc is [INACCESSIBLE]. This matters because PEM ascertained by mDSQ (past six months) is retrospective and symptom-recall based, less stringent than requiring PEM on a standardized provocation test.

6. **24-hour inter-test interval may be suboptimal for long COVID PEM kinetics.** ME/CFS PEM characteristically peaks 12–48 hours post-exertion; whether long COVID PEM kinetics are identical is unknown. A 24-hour interval may miss a later nadir or allow partial recovery in some long COVID patients.

7. **Cross-sectional cohort design; no longitudinal follow-up.** Whether the null result is stable across different phases of long COVID illness duration is unknown.

8. **Missing Borg perceptual ratings in abstract.** Whether perceived exertion or dyspnoea differed between days — as a secondary indicator of worsened PEM state on day 2 — is [INACCESSIBLE]; only VO2/GET/WR endpoints are reported in the abstract.

## Follow-up

- The paper should be read alongside Appelman2024, which found day-2 muscle biopsy abnormalities in long COVID post-CPET (mitochondrial OXPHOS reduced, SDH activity decreased after PEM induction) using a different design (biopsy vs gas-exchange): if muscle pathology worsens but VO2 does not decline, this may indicate a dissociation between peripheral muscle changes and whole-body oxygen uptake kinetics.
- Future work: long COVID 2-day CPET with larger N, PEM-negative control arm, and longer inter-test intervals (48 h, 72 h) to test whether the null is stable or interval-dependent.
- The null CPET result alongside positive mDSQ-PEM provides a data point for question:0015 regarding whether PEM-defined long COVID cohorts are biologically equivalent to PEM-defined ME/CFS cohorts.
