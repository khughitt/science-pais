## Key Contribution

This is the first published randomized placebo-controlled trial of a SARS-CoV-2-specific monoclonal antibody (AER002, a long-acting fully-human IgG1 anti-RBD mAb) in Long COVID, conducted by the UCSF LIINC group (outSMART-LC; NCT05877508). In 36 participants meeting WHO case-definition Long COVID, a single 1,200 mg intravenous infusion of AER002 did not produce significant benefit on the primary efficacy endpoint (PROMIS-29 Physical Health Summary Score at Day 90) nor on any pre-specified secondary patient-reported, objective, or blood-based endpoints. However, exploratory analyses revealed that participants with lower baseline anti-spike antibody levels who also achieved higher drug exposure (AER002 AUC) were significantly more likely to self-report clinical improvement, and PET-CT imaging suggested lower T-cell activation in several tissues in the AER002 arm at Day 90, indicating a possible biological effect of AER002 on immune activation that did not translate to group-level symptomatic benefit.

## Methods

**Trial design:** Phase 2a double-blind, 2:1 randomized, placebo-controlled, single-infusion mechanistic trial; 360-day follow-up (blinded to Day 180, open-label for safety thereafter). NCT05877508. Enrolment July 2023 to April 2024 at UCSF.

**Case definition:** WHO consensus Long COVID definition; at least two moderate-or-severe symptoms new or worsened since SARS-CoV-2 infection, present ≥60 days, with test-confirmed infection occurring before August 15, 2022 (when AER002 was expected to retain neutralizing activity against circulating variants). Pre-existing ME/CFS and dysautonomia preceding SARS-CoV-2 were excluded.

**Participants:** N=36 randomized (AER002 n=24; placebo n=12). Median age 42 years; 56% female sex at birth; 75% White, 14% Asian. Median time since Long COVID index infection: 740 days (IQR 576-1113); 33% had experienced a reinfection after August 15, 2022. All had received primary SARS-CoV-2 vaccination series. At baseline, median of 14 Long COVID symptoms in prior 30 days; 86% had post-exertional malaise (PEM); 58% met 2015 IOM ME/CFS criteria; median quality of life 49/100. A highly symptomatic, long-duration cohort.

**Intervention:** Single IV infusion of AER002 1,200 mg (or matching placebo buffer) over 30 minutes. AER002 is a fully human IgG1 mAb targeting the receptor-binding domain (RBD) of SARS-CoV-2 spike, with Fc modifications to extend half-life; estimated median terminal half-life ~107 days in male participants and ~76 days in female participants. AER002 retains activity against Omicron BA.2.12.1, BA.4, BA.5, and partial activity against BQ.1.

**Primary endpoint:** Change in PROMIS-29 Physical Health Summary Score (PHSS) from baseline to Day 90 (pre-specified primary efficacy endpoint).

**Secondary endpoints:** PROMIS-29 Mental Health Summary Score, EQ-5D-5L Index, 100-point VAS quality of life, WHODAS 2.0, Duke Activity Status Index (DASI), 6-minute walk test (6MWT), Everyday Cognition Scale (ECog-39), CNS-Vital Signs neurocognitive index, COMPASS-31 autonomic score, active standing test, inflammatory biomarkers (CRP, ESR, fibrinogen, D-dimer), cytokines (IL-6, TNF-alpha, IL-1beta, IFN-gamma), Patient Global Impression of Change (PGIC).

**Exploratory/optional procedures (opt-in subsets):**
- Flexible sigmoidoscopy with colorectal biopsy and RNAscope for SARS-CoV-2 RNA (n=17: 12 AER002, 5 placebo); nCounter gene expression panel
- [18F]F-AraG PET-CT whole-body T-cell activation imaging (AER002 n=6, placebo n=4)
- Cardiopulmonary exercise testing (CPET; n=9 baseline, n=5 follow-up)
- Plasma proteomics via Olink Reveal (1,034 proteins)
- AER002 pharmacokinetics via validated LC-MS/MS (CDR1-region signature peptide)
- Anti-SARS-CoV-2 antibody levels (Simoa: anti-S, anti-S1, anti-RBD, anti-N)

**Statistical analysis:** Linear mixed-effects models for primary and secondary outcomes, adjusted for baseline; log-transformation for laboratory outcomes. Per-protocol analysis excluded participants with on-study reinfections before primary endpoint. Exploratory responder analyses used Wilcoxon rank-sum tests and logistic regression.

## Key Findings

**Primary endpoint — null result:**
- PROMIS-29 PHSS at Day 90: AER002 estimated marginal mean 40.9 vs placebo 45.0; mean difference −4.1 (95% CI −8.6, 0.47), p=0.08. No significant group difference.
- Both groups improved modestly from baseline across most measures, consistent with a robust placebo response.

**Secondary clinical outcomes — all null:**
- PROMIS-29 MHSS: mean difference −4.7 (95% CI −9.6, 0.2), p=0.06 at Day 90; not significant.
- EQ-5D-5L Index Score: mean difference −0.15 (95% CI −0.3, 0.01), p=0.07.
- 100-point VAS: mean difference −9.4 (95% CI −19.6, 0.8), p=0.07.
- 6-minute walk test: mean difference −31.3 m (95% CI −76.0, 13.4), p=0.17.
- DASI, ECog-39, CNS-VS NCI, COMPASS-31, WHODAS 2.0, active standing test: all non-significant at Day 90 and secondary timepoints.
- Inflammatory biomarkers and cytokines (CRP, ESR, D-dimer, fibrinogen, IL-6, TNF-alpha, IL-1beta, IFN-gamma): no significant differences at any timepoint.
- PEM: no difference in PEM improvement between arms (p=0.45 at Day 90, 5/19 AER002 vs 4/10 placebo improved).
- Per-protocol analysis (excluding on-study reinfections) yielded identical null findings across all measures.

**Antigen/viral persistence readouts:**

Gut biopsy (n=17 AER002/placebo; optional, RNAscope):
- Only 1 of 17 participants had detectable SARS-CoV-2 RNA in baseline gut biopsy (single-stranded spike RNA); that participant received AER002 and reported improvement, with RNA undetectable post-infusion.
- 3 of 5 participants with known on-study reinfections (all AER002) had viral RNA detected in post-infusion biopsies (attributable to reinfection, not pre-existing persistence).
- No significant differential gene expression between treatment arms in viral immune response transcripts.
- Net interpretation: baseline tissue viral persistence was rare (1/17) in this cohort, precluding detection of a clearance effect. No validated blood-based antigen-persistence readout was measured during the trial.

No direct plasma antigen (spike/nucleocapsid) measurement was reported in this trial — a noted limitation. The authors explicitly state that the absence of a validated persistence biomarker at enrollment was a key design limitation.

**PET-CT T-cell activation imaging ([18F]F-AraG; n=10 total, exploratory):**
- Post-treatment, AER002 arm showed significantly lower mean SUV in temporal brain lobes, thoracic spinal cord, cauda equina, and submandibular glands vs placebo (all p<0.05).
- Within-arm, a trend toward decreased submandibular gland tracer uptake was observed in AER002 vs increase in placebo arm.
- Interpretation by authors: possible AER002-mediated blunting of immune activation in oral glandular tissue and CNS-adjacent tissues, potentially reflecting action on SARS-CoV-2 reservoirs; highly exploratory given n=10 and no multiple-comparison correction.

**Plasma proteomics (Olink, 1,034 proteins):**
- Day 14: modest differences in a small number of proteins (BTLA, SPINK6, MYL3) in AER002 vs placebo.
- Day 90: broader pattern of lower plasma levels in AER002 arm for multiple proteins involved in interferon response, innate immune signaling, apoptosis regulation, and NF-kB/MAP kinase pathways (GBP2, IGBP1, CASP7, BAX, CSDMD, DFFA, MAP2K1, NFKBIE, IRAK4, AKT1S1). Only PTPN14 (cell signaling regulator) was elevated with AER002. Gene set analysis did not detect significant pathway-level enrichment (underpowered sample size).
- Interpretation: proteomic signal is consistent with attenuation of immune/inflammatory activation pathways by AER002, though not statistically robust after multiple-testing correction.

**Exploratory responder analysis:**
- "Responders" defined as PGIC "Moderately Better" or more at both Day 90 and Day 180: 6/24 (25%) AER002, 6/12 (50%) placebo — not significantly different and numerically favoring placebo.
- Responders in both arms had significantly higher baseline anti-S (median 127 vs 60.5, p=0.033), anti-S1 (207 vs 106, p=0.036), and anti-RBD (151 vs 65.1, p=0.033) antibodies than non-responders; anti-N was not different.
- In the low-baseline-antibody subgroup only: each 100 mg·day/L increase in AER002 AUC was associated with 1.41-fold higher odds of response for anti-S (OR 1.41, 95% CI 1.07-2.50, p=0.011), 1.36-fold for anti-S1 (p=0.019), and 1.41-fold for anti-RBD (p=0.010). In the high-antibody subgroup, AUC was not associated with response.
- Implication: antibody therapy may preferentially benefit those with weak immune responses to the virus; higher drug exposure (or repeat dosing) may be needed to detect a therapeutic signal.

**Safety:** AER002 was safe and well-tolerated. 112 AEs in 32 participants, most common: confirmed COVID-19 reinfection (9%), COVID-19-negative URI (8%), PEM exacerbation (6.3%), diarrhea (3.6%). One grade 3 AE (planned hospital admission for intractable migraine, unrelated to study drug). No clinically significant laboratory abnormalities. No anaphylaxis.

## Relevance

This trial is the most direct evidence available bearing on hypothesis:0002 (tissue-reservoir/antigen-fragment persistence drives PAIS) and question:0002 (does antigen clearance rescue symptoms?). Key implications:

1. **Null primary result — important boundary condition.** Targeting viral persistence with a single-dose monoclonal antibody did not improve Long COVID in a broadly-defined, heterogeneous cohort. This constrains rather than refutes the antigen-persistence hypothesis: a single dose may have been insufficient, most participants may not have had active persistence at enrollment (~2 years post-infection), the drug may not reach tissue reservoirs, or antigen persistence may not be causally dominant in this late-phase population.

2. **Absence of persistence biomarker at enrollment is the crucial confound.** The trial did not stratify participants by baseline viral persistence. Only 1/17 gut biopsies showed detectable SARS-CoV-2 RNA at baseline. Blood-based antigen-persistence assays (cf. paper:Peluso2024 using Simoa) were not used for eligibility screening. This means the trial enrolled a predominantly persistence-negative cohort, making a persistence-targeted therapy unlikely to work. This is a design lesson, not a refutation.

3. **Exploratory signals (PET imaging, proteomics, low-Ab responders) suggest biological activity.** The convergent evidence from PET-CT, proteomics, and the pharmacokinetic-responder interaction is consistent with AER002 having a pharmacodynamic effect on immune activation, which in a properly enriched cohort might translate to clinical benefit. These findings are hypothesis-generating, not confirmatory.

4. **Dose and exposure may matter.** The responder analysis implicating AER002 AUC in the low-antibody subgroup suggests that inadequate drug exposure — or a single-dose regimen — may have been insufficient. Repeat dosing strategies are flagged as priority future directions.

5. **The placebo response is large and must be designed around.** Both arms improved substantially. Baseline antibody levels predicted response in both arms, possibly reflecting spontaneous improvement in those with intact anti-viral immunity. Future trials need enrichment, crossover designs, or persistence-based stratification to detect true drug effects above placebo noise.

6. **Connection to topic:therapeutics-and-clinical-trials:** This joins the STOP-PASC (nirmatrelvir, Geng et al. 2024) and PAX LC (nirmatrelvir, Sawano et al. 2025) null trials as evidence that broadly-defined, unenriched Long COVID populations do not respond to antiviral-mechanism drugs. Enrichment for persistence-positive participants is the field-level lesson.

## Limitations

1. **Small sample size (n=36, placebo arm n=12).** The 2:1 randomization resulted in a particularly underpowered control arm. Many effect estimates have wide confidence intervals; the trial was explicitly a proof-of-concept study with a mechanistic, not definitive-efficacy, objective.

2. **No baseline persistence biomarker for enrollment enrichment.** The most critical limitation acknowledged by the authors. Without confirming active SARS-CoV-2 persistence at enrollment, the trial tested the intervention in a population most of whom likely lacked the target biology. Future trials must enrich using validated plasma antigen, tissue biopsy, or imaging-based persistence measures.

3. **Long disease duration at enrollment (~2 years median since index infection).** Antigen/viral persistence has been most consistently linked to Long COVID within the first 3-14 months post-infection. By ~2 years, persistence may have resolved in most participants, even those still symptomatic, meaning disease is likely sustained by downstream mechanisms (chronic immune dysregulation, autoimmunity, metabolic dysfunction) rather than ongoing antigen drive.

4. **Reinfections during the study period (33% of cohort had reinfections after the AER002 activity deadline; 12 confirmed/suspected on-study reinfections).** Reinfections confound both persistence assessments and clinical trajectory.

5. **Drug neutralizing capacity waning against modern variants.** AER002 was expected to retain activity only against variants circulating before August 2022; one-third of participants had reinfections with later strains, potentially creating mixed/unresponsive reservoir biology.

6. **Optional procedures in very small subsets.** PET imaging (n=10), gut biopsy (n=17), and CPET (n=9) are underpowered and exploratory. PET findings are particularly preliminary — no multiple-comparison adjustment was applied.

7. **Single infusion; possibly insufficient drug exposure.** The exploratory AUC-response finding suggests dose may matter; a single 1,200 mg infusion may not sustain adequate tissue concentrations to clear reservoirs.

8. **Exclusion of most severely ill.** Homebound participants unable to attend in-person visits were excluded, limiting generalizability to the severe end of Long COVID.

9. **Unvalidated outcome measures.** Several PRO instruments have not been specifically validated for Long COVID contexts; the study acknowledges that commonly used measures may not adequately capture symptomatic change in this population.

10. **Unblinding residual bias risk.** 42% of AER002 participants correctly guessed their treatment assignment (vs 50% in placebo), suggesting partial unblinding; confidence was generally low.

11. **Preprint status.** This is a medRxiv preprint; has not been peer-reviewed at time of reading.

## Model / Tool Availability

No computational model or software tool is released. The [18F]F-AraG PET-CT protocol and LC-MS/MS AER002 pharmacokinetic assay are described in supplemental methods. The statistical analysis plan and protocol are available on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05877508). Data available from corresponding author upon reasonable request.

## Follow-up

- **Persistence-enriched trial design:** The direct next step called for by this trial is an analogous RCT that screens for baseline SARS-CoV-2 antigen or RNA persistence (blood, gut biopsy, or imaging) and enrolls only persistence-positive participants. This would sharply test hypothesis:0002.
- **Plasma antigen assays as eligibility criteria:** paper:Peluso2024 (Simoa-based spike/N antigen detection in plasma) provides a potential blood-based enrichment tool. Validation and clinical accessibility of such assays is a priority gap (see question:0002).
- **Earlier disease stage:** Future trials should consider enrolling participants within 3-14 months post-infection, when persistence is better documented and may still be causally upstream of symptoms.
- **Repeat-dosing / higher-exposure regimens:** The AUC-response finding motivates pharmacokinetic modeling to identify dosing regimens that sustain tissue-level concentrations.
- **Broad-variant coverage:** Next-generation monoclonal antibodies with activity across both ancestral and Omicron-era variants would address the mixed-reservoir problem.
- **PET-CT as mechanistic readout:** The submandibular gland and CNS-adjacent [18F]F-AraG signal is a candidate pharmacodynamic endpoint for future antiviral trials.
- **Peluso and Deeks 2024** (Cell 187(20):5500-5529, Mechanisms of long COVID and the path toward therapeutics): conceptual companion paper from the same group.
- **RECOVER-VITAL (nirmatrelvir/ritonavir RCT):** The other major antiviral trial underway in the same population; cross-trial comparison of null results will be informative.
