# A core outcome set for post-COVID-19 condition in adults for use in clinical practice and research

- **Authors:** Daniel Munblit, Timothy Nicholson, Athena Akrami, Christian Apfelbacher, Jessica Chen, Wouter De Groote, Janet Diaz, Sarah L. Gorst, Nicola Harman, Alisa Kokorina, Piero L. Olliaro, Callum Parr, Jacobus Preller, Nicoline Schiess, Jochen Schmitt, Nina Seylanova, Frances Simpson, Allison Tong, Dale M. Needham, Paula R. Williamson, and the PC-COS project steering committee
- **Year:** 2022
- **Journal:** The Lancet Respiratory Medicine
- **Volume/Pages:** 10(8):715-724
- **DOI:** 10.1016/S2213-2600(22)00169-2
- **BibTeX key:** Munblit2022PCCOS

## Key Contribution

This international Delphi consensus study defines the adult Post-COVID Core Outcome Set
(PC-COS): the minimum outcome domains that should be measured and reported in adult
post-COVID condition research and clinical practice. It is an outcome-harmonization tool,
not a diagnostic case definition and not a mechanistic endpoint battery.

## Methods

**Design:** Multi-step modified Delphi consensus process: literature review, two online
Delphi rounds, and an online consensus meeting.

**Participants:** 1,535 participants from 71 countries, including people with lived
experience, caregivers/family members, health-care professionals, and researchers.

**Scope:** Adults with post-COVID condition / long COVID, for use in both clinical
research and practice.

## Key Findings

The adult COS includes 12 outcome domains:

| Domain class | Included outcomes |
|---|---|
| Cross-cutting symptoms/impacts | fatigue or exhaustion; pain; post-exertion symptoms; work/occupational and study changes |
| Global outcomes | survival; recovery |
| Functioning/symptoms/conditions | cardiovascular; respiratory; nervous system; cognitive; mental; physical |

The study explicitly frames the COS as a **minimum** set. Absence of an outcome from the
COS does not mean the outcome is unimportant, and study-specific outcomes can and should
be added when required by a mechanistic or clinical question.

## Relevance

For `task:t026`, this paper is the primary source for deciding whether this project
should adopt PC-COS. The correct adoption is domain-level harmonization: report PC-COS
domains where computable, while keeping binary PAIS case definitions and mechanism-specific
endpoints separate.

## Limitations

1. It is adult long-COVID-specific; pediatric long COVID and non-COVID PAIS triggers
   need separate adaptation rather than direct import.
2. It does not choose measurement instruments; that is handled by the later COMS study.
3. It does not require PEM for case ascertainment, even though post-exertion symptoms are
   included as an outcome domain.
4. It does not adjudicate biological coherence of case definitions.

## Follow-up

Use as the project standard for minimum adult long-COVID dimensional reporting, not as a
replacement for PEM-positive mechanistic definitions or trigger-specific PAIS criteria.
