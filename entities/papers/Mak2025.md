## Key Contribution

This cross-sectional observational study provides the largest to date systematic assessment of immunological imprinting by seasonal coronaviruses in long COVID (LC), using a 47-patient cohort nested in the LARGO randomized-controlled trial alongside 41 fully recovered healthy controls (HC). The central finding is that LC patients show significantly reduced SARS-CoV-2 anti-spike IgG and IgA (driven by the S1 subunit) but elevated anti-spike IgG against the seasonal betacoronaviruses HKU1 and OC43 — the two coronaviruses with the highest S protein homology to SARS-CoV-2 — strongly suggesting immunological imprinting (original antigenic sin) as a mechanism deflecting responses toward conserved rather than SARS-CoV-2-unique epitopes. A second finding is that LC patients exhibit a significantly elevated IgM/IgG ratio for anti-spike antibodies, indicating impaired isotype class switching from IgM to IgG, which would further limit the affinity and effector function of the anti-SARS-CoV-2 response. A third, unexpected finding is that CMV p65-specific IgG was significantly lower in LC patients and inversely correlated with fatigue severity (r = −0.53, p = 0.0165), raising the hypothesis that CMV-related immune experience modulates LC symptomatology. No distinct antinuclear antibody (ANA) profile distinguished LC from HC, and herpesvirus DNA was undetectable in serum of all but one LC patient.

## Methods

**Study design:** Cross-sectional case-control study nested within the LARGO (Lactoferrin Against LC Symptoms) randomized-controlled trial at Franciscus Hospital, Rotterdam. Only participants with a baseline serum/plasma sample available before treatment intervention were included.

**Cohorts:**
- Long COVID (n = 47): 18–70 years; PCR-confirmed SARS-CoV-2; ≥2 WHO-defined LC symptoms persisting < 1 year. Median age 46 (IQR 38–52); 38.3% male. Blood collected at median 280 days (IQR 142–331) post-infection (February–March 2022).
- Healthy controls (n = 41): fully recovered COVID-19 healthcare workers; median age 53 (IQR 42–58); 22.0% male. Blood collected at median 596 days (IQR 558–722) post-infection (November 2021–July 2022). All demographics were non-significantly different between groups except blood-collection timing (p < 0.0001).

**Exclusion criteria (LC group):** ICU admission, abnormal chest X-ray or pulmonary function tests, current acute infection, active systemic immunological or psychiatric disorders, immunosuppressive medications, pregnancy/lactation, milk allergy.

**Assays:**
- In-house ELISAs: IgG, IgA, IgM against SARS-CoV-2 spike (S), S1, S2, nucleocapsid (N), and spike proteins of HCoV-OC43, HCoV-HKU1, HCoV-NL63, HCoV-229E.
- Microblot-Array panels (TestLine Clinical Diagnostics): herpesvirus IgG/IgM/IgA (EBV EBNA-1, EBV VCA, EBV EA-D/R, EBV ZEBRA, EBV Rta, CMV p65/p28, HSV-1+2, HHV-6) and ANA plus panel (44 antigens associated with systemic autoimmune diseases).
- PCR for EBV EBNA, CMV UL75, HSV-1 gG, HSV-2 gD, VZV, SARS-CoV-2 E and N in extracted nucleic acids from serum/plasma.
- Clinical symptom assessments: Fatigue Assessment Scale (FAS, 10–50; severe ≥35) and Cognitive Failures Questionnaire (CFQ, 0–100; severe ≥55).

**Statistics:** GraphPad Prism v10; Mann-Whitney U test for group comparisons; Chi-square or Fisher's exact for categorical variables; Spearman's rank correlation for associations; Benjamini-Hochberg correction for multiple comparisons; two-tailed α = 0.05.

## Key Findings

**1. Reduced SARS-CoV-2 spike-specific IgG and IgA in LC:**
- SARS-CoV-2 S- and S1-specific IgG significantly lower in LC vs. HC (S: p < 0.0001; S1: p = 0.0091). S2-specific IgG comparable between groups.
- SARS-CoV-2 S- and S1-specific IgA significantly lower in LC vs. HC (S: p < 0.0001; S1: p = 0.0091). S2-specific IgA comparable.
- Nucleocapsid (N)-specific IgG and IgA both elevated in LC vs. HC (IgG p = 0.0015; IgA p = 0.0257), potentially reflecting prolonged or repeated antigen exposure to internal viral proteins.
- No SARS-CoV-2 RNA was detected in serum of any LC patient by RT-PCR.

**2. Impaired isotype class switching (IgM/IgG ratio) in LC:**
- SARS-CoV-2 S-specific IgG/IgM ratio significantly lower in LC vs. HC (p < 0.0001), and S1-specific IgG/IgM ratio lower (p = 0.0490).
- S-specific IgA/IgM ratio was comparable, suggesting the defect is specific to IgG class switching.
- SARS-CoV-2 S-specific IgM levels were comparable between LC and HC.

**3. Elevated seasonal betacoronavirus IgG in LC:**
- LC patients had significantly elevated anti-spike IgG against HKU1 (p = 0.0044) and OC43 (p = 0.0040), both betacoronaviruses that share approximately 40% C-terminal and 25% N-terminal S protein sequence identity with SARS-CoV-2.
- Anti-spike IgG levels for alphacoronaviruses NL63 and 229E were not significantly different.
- Within both LC and HC groups, anti-spike IgG levels against HKU1 and OC43 were strongly positively correlated with each other (r ≈ 0.8, p < 0.0001) but weakly correlated with SARS-CoV-2 anti-spike IgG (HKU1/SARS-CoV-2: r = 0.341, p = 0.059 in LC; not significant in HC).
- OC43-specific IgA was elevated in LC (S2 subunit, p = 0.0015); other seasonal CoV IgA levels were comparable.

**4. CMV p65-specific IgG lower in LC and inversely associated with fatigue:**
- CMV p65-specific IgG significantly lower in LC vs. HC (p = 0.0090).
- Among seropositive LC patients, CMV p65 IgG inversely correlated with fatigue severity as measured by FAS (r = −0.53, p = 0.0165): lower CMV IgG → more severe fatigue.
- CMV p65 IgG did not correlate significantly with CFQ cognitive failure scores.
- Herpesvirus seroprevalence rates (EBV, CMV, HSV-1, HSV-2, HHV-6) were comparable between LC and HC.

**5. EBV — trend but no significant difference:**
- EBV-specific IgG levels trended higher in LC for reactivation markers (EBV Rta IgG elevated +139% on average in LC vs. HC) but did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.1548 for Rta IgG).
- HSV-1, HSV-2, and HHV-6 antibody profiles showed no clear pattern between LC and HC.
- No herpesvirus DNA detected in serum of any LC patient, except HHV-6 DNA in one LC patient (2.1%).

**6. No distinct ANA profile in LC:**
- At least one ANA detected in 46.3% of HC and 48.9% of LC patients; comparable overall.
- Seven LC patients vs. zero HC patients had ≥3 positive ANAs, but this difference was not statistically significant after multiple-comparison correction.
- SARS-CoV-2 antibody levels and herpesvirus antibody levels were not associated with fatigue or cognitive failure severity (Supporting Information).

## Relevance

**Immunological imprinting as a PAIS risk determinant:** This paper provides the most systematic evidence to date that prior seasonal betacoronavirus exposure imprints B cell responses in a way that deflects SARS-CoV-2 immunity toward cross-reactive but suboptimal epitopes. The S1-subunit specificity of the reduced IgG (S1 bears SARS-CoV-2-unique epitopes while S2 is more conserved) is mechanistically coherent with imprinting: memory B cells shaped by OC43/HKU1 re-engage conserved S2/NTD epitopes, outcompeting naive B cells that would generate S1-specific responses. This is relevant to `hypothesis:0001` (shared dysregulated attractor) because reduced SARS-CoV-2 IgG → impaired viral clearance → antigen persistence provides a candidate entry path into the attractor.

**Impaired class switching and B cell dysfunction:** The elevated IgM/IgG ratio is consistent with impaired isotype class switching, which the authors attribute to memory B cells competing with naive B cells for T cell help. This links to `hypothesis:0003` (immune exhaustion feedback) because the same immune disorganization that impairs adaptive class switching could reflect or contribute to reduced T cell engagement.

**Nucleocapsid IgG elevation:** The paradox of reduced spike IgG but elevated N-IgG in LC (despite undetectable serum SARS-CoV-2 RNA) is consistent with `hypothesis:0002` (tissue reservoir antigen fragment): N protein may continue to be shed from tissue reservoirs (contributing to elevated N-specific immunity) while spike antibodies are preferentially misdirected or class-switch-impaired.

**EBV as epiphenomenon:** The absence of significant EBV antibody elevation or detectable EBV DNA in serum supports `hypothesis:0015` (EBV reactivation as consequence, not cause), though the paper is underpowered and the measurement (serum only) does not exclude mucosal or tissue EBV reactivation. The null for serum EBV DNA is consistent with the hypothesis that viremia is not ongoing; latent reactivation that occurred earlier (acutely) may still have left an imprint.

**CMV-fatigue association — novel signal:** The inverse CMV p65 IgG–fatigue correlation is not mechanistically explained by the authors and does not fit existing project hypotheses cleanly. The speculation is that stronger prior CMV adaptive immunity reflects a generally more competent adaptive immune baseline that also mounts a more effective SARS-CoV-2 response, linking back to `hypothesis:0020` (host immune baseline reserve). This is a finding that warrants independent replication.

## Limitations

1. **Blood-collection timing mismatch.** HC samples were collected a median 316 days later than LC samples (596 vs. 280 days post-infection), during which different SARS-CoV-2 variants circulated and antibody waning would differentially affect the groups. This is the single most important confound; the authors acknowledge it explicitly.

2. **Small cohort.** n = 47 LC and n = 41 HC provides limited power for subgroup analyses (e.g., by variant, by symptom cluster). Several trends (EBV Rta, ANA count) may reflect real signals that are underpowered.

3. **Cross-sectional design.** Cannot establish whether immunological imprinting preceded LC or was shaped by the post-COVID immune dysregulation itself; cannot establish causality for the CMV–fatigue inverse correlation.

4. **Antibody assays only; no B cell clonotyping.** The immunological imprinting interpretation is inferential — elevated cross-reactive IgG against OC43/HKU1 is consistent with imprinting but does not prove it. Single-cell B cell sequencing with binding assays would be needed to identify the imprinted clones.

5. **Serum PCR misses tissue-level herpesvirus reactivation.** Mucosal shedding, lymph-node reactivation, and tissue-compartment virus would not be detected. The null EBV serum PCR result does not exclude prior or compartmentalized reactivation.

6. **LC cohort recruited from a single RCT (LARGO) at one center.** Franciscus Hospital, Rotterdam. Potential selection bias from RCT enrollment criteria and single-center recruitment.

7. **ANA panel is calibrated for systemic autoimmune diseases.** It may miss LC-specific autoantibodies (e.g., anti-GPCR antibodies, anti-beta-adrenergic receptor antibodies) relevant to dysautonomia — the project's `hypothesis:0007`.
