# Characterizing Long COVID Symptoms During Early Childhood

## Key Contribution

RECOVER-Pediatrics extension to infants/toddlers (0-2 years) and preschool-aged children (3-5 years),
showing that early-childhood long-COVID symptom indices differ from older-child and adolescent indices.

## Methods

- **Design:** multisite longitudinal cohort with infected and uninfected comparison groups.
- **Setting:** more than 30 US health-care and community settings.
- **Participants:** 472 infants/toddlers and 539 preschool-aged children.
- **Exposure:** SARS-CoV-2 infection history.
- **Outcome:** 41 symptoms in infants/toddlers and 75 symptoms in preschool-aged children; age-band
  research indices derived from symptoms most associated with infection history.

## Key Findings

- Infant/toddler index symptoms included poor appetite, trouble sleeping, wet cough, dry cough, and
  stuffy nose.
- Preschool index symptoms emphasized daytime tiredness/sleepiness/low energy and dry cough.
- Probable long COVID by the derived indices was identified in about 14-15% of infected children in these
  age groups.
- Higher indices were associated with poorer overall health, lower quality of life, and perceived
  developmental delays.

## Relevance

This paper adds the strongest constraint for pediatric measurement: symptoms that define long COVID in
young children are largely observable/proxy-reported and differ from adolescent/adult internal symptoms.

## Limitations

- Parent/proxy reporting is unavoidable in this age band but introduces reporting-channel bias.
- The respiratory-heavy index in young children may be vulnerable to common childhood infection
  background rates.
- No mechanistic biology is measured.
