# Immune correlates underlying small fiber neuropathy presenting as vaccine-associated post-acute SARS-coronavirus syndrome

- **Authors:** Alessandro Limongelli, Federica Bozzano, Alireza Hajabbas Farshchi, Margherita Bellucci, Martina Bavastro, Chiara Castellano, Giampaola Pesce, Francesca Antonini, Alex Incensi, Maria Pia Giannoccaro, Antonio Uccelli, Genny Del Zotto, Lorenzo Moretta, Vincenzo Donadio, Luana Benedetti, Andrea De Maria
- **Year:** 2026
- **Journal:** Frontiers in Immunology, vol. 17, p. 1752120
- **DOI:** 10.3389/fimmu.2026.1752120
- **PMCID:** PMC13043346
- **BibTeX key:** Limongelli2026
- **Source:** PDF

## Key Contribution

This study provides the first detailed immunological characterization of small fiber neuropathy (SFN) presenting as post-vaccine PASC (PASC-vac) in a prospective cohort of 71 previously healthy individuals who developed neurological symptoms following SARS-CoV-2 vaccination. The paper demonstrates that PASC-vac SFN is associated with a state of chronic immune dysregulation — specifically elevated circulating innate-like T cells co-expressing NKG2D and other activating NK receptors, and increased CD34+DNAM-1bright inflammatory precursors with accelerated bone marrow egress — a pattern shared with other immune-mediated diseases (SLE, Crohn's, RA) rather than classical neurodegenerative SFN. Anti-idiotype antibody frequencies against ACE-2 and NRP-1 were not significantly elevated versus healthy donors, suggesting these are not the primary driver of SFN in this cohort.

## Methods

- **Design:** Prospective, single-institution observational cohort study; sequential enrollment at the Infections in Immunocompromised Host Unit and Neurology Unit, IRCCS Ospedale Policlinico San Martino, Genova, Italy.
- **Cohort:** 71 previously healthy, immunocompetent adults (47 F, 24 M; mean age 45.5 ± 11.22 years) with neurological/dysautonomic symptoms after SARS-CoV-2 vaccination (Pfizer n=43, Moderna n=15, AstraZeneca n=5, J&J n=2, heterologous n=6). Symptom onset between February 2021 and September 2022. Excluded: prior COVID-19, pre-existing neurological conditions, MRI/EEG/EMG abnormalities suggesting central lesions or radiculopathies.
- **Skin biopsy (n=21):** Calf and thigh punch biopsies; epidermal nerve fiber density (ENFD) assessed by confocal immunofluorescence (PGP9.5 / collagen IV staining); non-length-dependent vs. length-dependent pattern characterized.
- **Anti-idiotype antibodies:** Anti-ACE-2 IgG by ELISA (Eagle Bioscience); anti-NRP-1 by immunofluorescence on monkey cerebellum tissue sections (Euroimmun commercial assay); confirmed by confocal microscopy (Leica Stellaris 8 Falcon TauSTED).
- **Flow cytometry (n=20 patients + healthy donors):** PBMCs isolated by Ficoll-Paque; multicolor flow cytometry (FACS Fortessa BD; 10,000 events) analyzing CD34+DNAM-1bright inflammatory precursors, T-cell subsets (CD4+, CD8+), NK receptor expression (NKG2D, NKp30, NKp46, CD28), and CD38 co-expression on precursors.
- **In vitro functional assay:** CD34+DNAMbright precursors purified (>99%) and seeded in stem cell culture medium; progenies characterized at 14 days by flow cytometry and tested in reverse ADCC cytotoxicity assay (anti-NKG2D + anti-DNAM-1 + anti-CD3 mAbs; FcgR+ P815 targets).
- **Statistics:** GraphPad Prism 9, JMP 9; two-tailed Fisher's exact test; Mann-Whitney U-test; significance threshold p < 0.05.

## Key Findings

**Clinical phenotype:**
- Median time to symptom onset post-vaccination: 3 days (mean ± SD: 8.76 ± 17.4 days); 64/71 patients had symptoms after the first dose.
- Symptom severity at clinical evaluation (mean 382 ± 133 days from onset) remained high: 5.9 ± 1.9 on 10-point Likert scale; 59% reported severity 8–10.
- Predominant symptoms: dysautonomia 87% (orthostatic 35%, vasomotor 88%), paresthesia (lower limb 79%, upper limb 58%, facial/cervical 44%, thoracic 26%), brain fog 41%, fatigue 30%, tinnitus 28%.
- Distinctive feature: non-length-dependent, multi-regional paresthesia distribution (face/trunk frequently involved), deviating from classical "glove-and-stocking" SFN pattern.
- Second dose: 65% reported exacerbation of symptoms often on the same day.

**Skin biopsy / SFN confirmation:**
- 19/21 biopsied patients (90%) showed reduced small fiber density confirming SFN diagnosis.
- Non-length-dependent pattern (suggesting autoimmune etiology) found in 33% of biopsied patients.

**Anti-idiotype antibodies:**
- Anti-ACE-2 antibodies: 9/71 (12%) PASC-vac patients vs. 4/19 (21%) healthy donors — not significantly different (p = NS, Fisher's exact test).
- Anti-NRP-1 antibodies: 14/71 (20%) PASC-vac patients vs. 1/19 (5%) healthy donors — trend but not significant (p = NS).
- Anti-NRP-1 and anti-ACE-2 co-occurrence was not observed in the same patients.

**Flow cytometry — circulating inflammatory precursors:**
- Significant increase in CD34+DNAM-1bright precursors in PASC-vac PBMCs vs. healthy donors (p < 0.05).
- CD38 expression on CD34+DNAM-1bright cells was ~2-fold lower in PASC-vac vs. HD, indicating accelerated bone marrow egress (shorter bone marrow residence time).
- Total CD34+ cell frequencies were comparable between groups.

**T-cell immunophenotype:**
- Decreased CD28+CD8+ T cells in PASC-vac vs. HD (p < 0.01).
- Increased NKG2D+CD8+ T cells (p < 0.001) and NKG2D+DNAM-1+CD4+ T cells (p < 0.01) in PASC-vac vs. HD.
- CD4+ T cells with simultaneous NKG2D, NKp30, and NKp46 co-expression were elevated.
- NK cells did not show significant skewing in PASC-vac patients.

**In vitro functional assay:**
- CD34+DNAMbright precursor-derived progenies were predominantly T cells (90.1 ± 5.4%) with a minority of NK cell progenies (6.8 ± 3.4%).
- T-cell progenies expressed NKG2D and DNAM-1 (innate-like phenotype) but lacked NKp30, NKp46, and CD16.
- CD3+CD8+ progenies co-expressing NKG2D and DNAM-1 showed enhanced cytotoxicity in reverse ADCC assay when combined with anti-CD3 + anti-NKG2D + anti-DNAM-1 mAbs — confirming antigen-independent cytotoxic capacity.

## Relevance

This paper is directly relevant to the project research question (research-question:post-acute-infection-syndromes) and the PAIS frame of failed homeostatic recovery in multiple respects:

1. **Shared immune failure mode:** The peripheral immune dysregulation documented here — elevated innate-like T cells, mobilized inflammatory CD34+ precursors, reduced CD28+CD8+ T cells — is essentially the same pattern observed in SLE, Crohn's disease, and RA. This provides mechanistic support for the project's hypothesis that PAIS (whether post-infection or post-vaccine) represents a convergent immune failure mode, not a disease-specific one.

2. **Persistent inflammation beyond acute phase:** Symptoms persisted at high severity for a mean of 382 ± 133 days (>1 year) in previously healthy individuals. The immunological changes — accelerated bone marrow egress of inflammatory precursors, elevated innate-like T cells — are still measurable at this time point, indicating genuine failure of immune homeostatic recovery, not a transient perturbation.

3. **Dysautonomia as a PAIS signature:** Dysautonomia was present in 87% of patients, consistent with the dysautonomia phenotype documented in Long-COVID and ME/CFS; SFN provides a plausible structural substrate for the autonomic dysfunction commonly reported across PAIS.

4. **Antigen-independent inflammation as a driver:** The demonstration that CD34+DNAMbright-derived innate-like T cells can mediate cytotoxicity independently of canonical TCR-antigen recognition is mechanistically significant for PAIS: it suggests that once triggered, inflammation may be self-sustaining without persistent antigen (consistent with antigen-clearance but symptom-persistence observations in Long-COVID).

5. **PASC-vac vs. PASC-infection overlap:** The paper explicitly positions PASC-vac as a phenotypic analog of PASC, differing mainly in triggering mechanism (vaccine spike protein vs. infection). This supports the project's cross-syndrome framework treating vaccine-related and infection-related PAIS as variants of the same failed-recovery process.

6. **Vaccine-specific caution:** The causal link between vaccination and SFN onset is supported temporally (median 3-day onset) and dose-dependently (exacerbation at second dose), but this is a non-randomized observational cohort without a matched vaccinated-but-asymptomatic comparison arm. The mechanistic pathway from vaccine antigen exposure to SFN onset remains hypothetical; the immune dysregulation documented may be an effect of ongoing SFN rather than its sole cause.

## Limitations

- **No matched vaccinated-asymptomatic control arm:** The study compares PASC-vac patients to healthy donors (HD), but does not compare them to vaccinated individuals who did not develop PASC. This makes it impossible to determine what fraction of the immune dysregulation reflects vaccination per se versus PASC-specific immune failure.
- **Biopsy access bias:** Only 21/71 patients (30%) underwent skin biopsy, selected partly by socioeconomic/logistic factors. Although baseline symptom profiles were reportedly similar between biopsied and non-biopsied groups, histological findings may not be fully representative.
- **No lymphocyte infiltrate assessment:** Studying lymphocyte infiltrates in skin biopsy was outside the original scope; the mechanistic link from circulating innate-like T cells to small fiber damage therefore remains a hypothesis, not a demonstrated pathway.
- **Cross-sectional immunology:** Flow cytometry was performed at a single time point (~382 days from onset). Longitudinal data are absent, so it is unknown whether the immune dysregulation predates, triggers, or persists alongside SFN.
- **No immunogenetic profiling:** HLA or other immunogenetic data not collected; frequency of observed PASC-vac SFN in the vaccinated population cannot be estimated.
- **Heterogeneous vaccine products:** Six different vaccine types/combinations were included; no stratified analysis by vaccine type was reported, which limits attribution of mechanism to specific antigen formats (mRNA vs. adenoviral vector).
- **Anti-idiotype antibody interpretation:** The non-significant trend for anti-NRP-1 (20% vs. 5% in HD; p = NS) may reflect insufficient power rather than a true null effect given n = 19 in the HD group.

- Key open questions this paper raises (for orchestrator consideration):
  1. Do vaccinated-but-asymptomatic individuals show any subclinical elevation in CD34+DNAM-1bright or innate-like T cells, or is this shift specific to those who develop PASC-vac?
  2. Can longitudinal immune profiling distinguish patients who recover from PASC-vac SFN from those who remain symptomatic — i.e., is immune normalization a marker of homeostatic recovery?
  3. Is the non-length-dependent SFN pattern (indicating autoimmune etiology, present in 33% of biopsied patients) associated with specific immunological features (e.g., higher innate-like T cell burden) compared to the length-dependent subgroup?
