# Long-Term Sequelae of COVID-19: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Symptoms 3 Years Post-SARS-CoV-2 Infection

- **Authors:** Masoud Rahmati, Raphael Udeh, Jiseung Kang, Xenia Dolja-Gore, Mark McEvoy, et al.
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal:** Journal of Medical Virology, 97: e70429
- **DOI:** 10.1002/jmv.70429
- **BibTeX key:** Rahmati2025
- **Source:** PDF

## Key Contribution

This is the first systematic review and meta-analysis to quantify the pooled prevalence of persistent long COVID symptoms specifically at 3 years post-SARS-CoV-2 infection. Synthesizing 11 studies across 142,171 individuals, it establishes that 20% (95% CI: 8–43%) of COVID-19 survivors still carry at least one unresolved symptom at the 3-year mark, with pulmonary diffusion impairment (DLCO <80% predicted: 42%; 95% CI: 34–50%), dyspnea (12%), fatigue (11%), and insomnia (11%) as the leading persistent sequelae. The review also identifies key risk factors — hospitalization severity, older age, female sex, and comorbidities — that predict 3-year symptom persistence.

## Methods

- **Design:** Systematic review and meta-analysis following PRISMA 2020 guidelines; pre-registered on PROSPERO (CRD42024581975).
- **Search:** PubMed, MEDLINE (Ovid), CENTRAL, Web of Science, Scopus, and Embase from database inception to July 20, 2024.
- **Eligibility (PICO):** Survivors of COVID-19 (any severity) with symptoms assessed at ≥3 years post-infection; studies reporting multi-follow-up data were restricted to the 3-year time point only to maintain methodological consistency; prospective cohort and cross-sectional designs included; narrative reviews, editorials, and studies without English summaries excluded.
- **Included studies:** 11 articles (9 cohort, 2 cross-sectional), published 2023–2024, from 6 countries (Bulgaria, China [n=5, four from Wuhan], Japan, Italy, Romania, USA [n=2]). Collectively covering 142,171 individuals (range 88–135,161), mean age 36–86 years; 123,675 (87%) male, 18,172 (13%) female. Study quality: medium-to-high (Newcastle–Ottawa Scale scores 7–9).
- **Statistical approach:** One-stage random-effects model for pooled proportions; Cochran's Q and I² for heterogeneity (low: 0–40%, moderate: 30–60%, substantial: 50–90%, considerable: >90%); sensitivity analysis by leave-one-out; meta-analysis conducted in R v4.2.2 ("meta" package); Egger/Begg tests not performed due to n<10 studies per analysis.
- **Symptom categorization:** Eight physiological clusters — neurologic, mental, pulmonary, cardiovascular, musculoskeletal, kidney, gastrointestinal, metabolic.

## Key Findings

**Pooled symptom burden at 3 years**
- At least one unresolved symptom: **20%** (95% CI: 8–43%; I²=100%; 4 studies, n=3,470)
- At least one unresolved respiratory symptom: **35%** (95% CI: 18–56%; 2 studies, n=755)

**Specific symptom prevalences (pooled, random effects)**
| Symptom | Prevalence | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| DLCO <80% predicted | 42% | 34–50% |
| FEV1 <80% predicted | 10% | 8–12% |
| Dyspnea | 12% | 10–15% |
| Fatigue | 11% | 6–20% |
| Insomnia / sleep difficulty | 11% | 2–37% |
| Loss of smell | 7% | 5–8% |
| Loss of taste | 7% | 3–16% |
| Anxiety | 6% | 1–32% |
| Cough | 6% | 2–15% |
| Expectoration | 4% | 1–11% |
| Myalgia | 4% | 2–18% |

**Trends vs. prior time points**
- Dyspnea: slight increase from ~10% at 2 years to 12% at 3 years (not statistically significant); pulmonary diffusion metrics unchanged.
- Fatigue: substantial reduction from ~27–28% at 2 years to 11% at 3 years — suggesting partial but incomplete homeostatic recovery in this domain.
- Sleep disorders, anxiety, smell/taste: worsening trend at 3 years relative to 2-year prevalence (sleep 11% vs. 25%; anxiety 6% vs. 9%; smell 7% vs. 3.5%; taste 7% vs. 4.5%), indicating non-monotonic neuropsychiatric trajectories.

**Mortality and disability at 3 years**
- Non-hospitalized COVID-19 survivors: excess burden 0.22/1000 persons (IRR: 1.01, 95% CI: 0.97–1.04); mortality IRR 1.29 (95% CI: 1.19–1.40), burden 8.16/1000.
- Hospitalized COVID-19 survivors: excess burden 1.29/1000; mortality IRR 1.29; excess burden 8.16/1000.
- Long COVID contributed 9.6 DALYs/1000 persons (non-hospitalized) and 90.0 DALYs/1000 (hospitalized) in year 3.
- Mortality at 3 years: 14.01% (58/414) in patients with neurological COVID vs. 7.84% (94/1196) without.

**Risk factors for 3-year PASC persistence**
- Higher COVID-19 severity score at hospitalization (consistently across studies)
- Older age — associated with dyspnea and reduced daily activity
- Female sex — associated with lung diffusion impairment; higher dyspnea and depression
- ICU admission and mechanical ventilation at hospitalization
- Smoking, allergy, substance use, and comorbidities (congestive heart failure, chronic kidney disease, hypertension, diabetes)
- Three or more COVID-19 vaccine doses positively associated with quality-of-life scores (EQ-VAS) and negatively associated with reduced daily activity

**Functional and work impairment**
- 26% of long COVID patients experienced significant activity limitation relative to pre-COVID baseline (CDC national survey, 2022–2023).
- Return-to-work meta-analysis (cited): 61% returned to work effectively at ≥3 months post-COVID; a substantial proportion required duty or hours adjustments.

**Mechanistic contributors cited in discussion**
1. Severity of acute illness driving greater organ damage.
2. Autoantibody generation triggering ongoing inflammation and neurological/cardiovascular dysfunction.
3. Persistence of SARS-CoV-2 RNA in tissues sustaining immune activation.

## Relevance

This paper directly addresses the project research question on post-acute infection syndromes (research-question:post-acute-infection-syndromes) by providing the highest-quality epidemiological evidence that SARS-CoV-2-triggered long COVID represents a **failed homeostatic recovery** persisting to at least 3 years post-infection — consistent with the PAIS framing.

Key connections to the PAIS frame:

1. **Failed recovery, not temporary perturbation.** The 3-year persistence of symptoms across pulmonary, neuropsychiatric, and cardiovascular domains — for 20% of survivors — confirms that SARS-CoV-2 infection can trigger a durable failure of homeostatic restoration, not a transient disruption with self-limiting trajectory.

2. **Non-monotonic neuropsychiatric trajectories.** The worsening at 3 years of sleep disorders, anxiety, and smell/taste loss (despite general symptom reduction) mirrors the unpredictable immune/CNS re-equilibration dynamics seen in other PAIS conditions (ME/CFS, post-SARS). This is consistent with a model where partial homeostatic recovery enables residual or secondary dysregulation in specific systems.

3. **Shared mechanisms with other PAIS.** The three proposed mechanisms — severity-driven organ damage, autoantibody persistence, and viral RNA persistence — are the same mechanisms proposed for post-SARS, ME/CFS, and post-dengue fatigue. The comparisons to 18-year SARS follow-up data (Li et al., EClinicalMedicine 2023) strengthen the hypothesis that these represent a single pathophysiological class.

4. **Severity and hospitalization as predictors.** The graded relationship between acute-phase severity and 3-year PASC risk supports the hypothesis that the magnitude of the initial immune/inflammatory insult determines the depth of homeostatic failure.

5. **Pulmonary diffusion impairment (DLCO).** The 42% prevalence of impaired DLCO at 3 years, plateaued from 2-year data, suggests a subset of survivors settle into a new, diminished pulmonary homeostatic set point rather than recovering to baseline.

## Limitations

- **Predominantly hospitalized cohort bias.** Nine of 11 studies focused on hospitalized patients; findings may not generalize to patients with mild acute COVID-19, who form the majority of PASC sufferers. The pooled prevalences likely overestimate symptom burden in the general long COVID population.
- **High heterogeneity.** I² values for key outcomes (any symptom: 100%; fatigue: 98%; sleep difficulty: 99%; anxiety: 99%) are extreme, making pooled estimates difficult to interpret and widening CIs substantially. This limits clinical and policy translation.
- **Small number of contributing studies.** Most meta-analyses include only 2–4 studies per outcome, severely limiting statistical power. Egger/Begg publication bias tests could not be run.
- **Geographic concentration.** Five of 11 studies are from Wuhan, China; findings may reflect Wuhan-specific factors (early pandemic variants, healthcare context, population genetics) not representative globally.
- **Sex/gender imbalance.** Across included studies, 87% of participants were male — limiting generalizability to women, who are known to have higher PASC risk in population-based data.
- **Variant-era gap.** All studies span January–December 2020 infection periods (one through November 2021); no Delta or Omicron cohorts. Variant differences in PASC risk are well-documented and limit forward applicability.
- **Self-reported outcomes.** Use of questionnaires for many neuropsychiatric endpoints introduces reporting and recall bias.
- **Lack of uninfected control groups.** Heterogeneous control group definitions across studies (healthy unexposed vs. COVID-19 survivors without long COVID symptoms) limit inter-study comparability.
- **No individual-level data.** Symptom co-occurrence and clustering could not be analyzed; symptom overlap and interaction effects are uncharacterized.
- **Note on Morioka 2024.** This study was listed as "RETRACTED" in the reference list (ref. 8), though results appear to have been included in the analysis. The effect on pooled estimates is unclear.

## Follow-up

- The retraction notice on Morioka et al. 2024 (ref. 8) should be confirmed; if the retraction is confirmed and the data were included in pooled analyses, a sensitivity analysis excluding it would be informative.
- The Cai et al. 2024 (Nature Medicine) study of 135,161 US Veterans provides the most powered 3-year PASC burden estimate (hospitalized vs. non-hospitalized) — worth reading in full for variant- and severity-stratified data.
- Comparison to the 2-year systematic review by the same senior authors (Rahmati et al., Journal of Medical Virology, 2023, cited as ref. 17) would quantify the year-2 to year-3 trajectory directly.
- Given the extreme heterogeneity in pooled estimates, individual-patient data (IPD) meta-analysis would substantially improve precision — particularly for the neuropsychiatric endpoints.
- The finding that ≥3 vaccine doses associate with better QoL outcomes warrants exploration as a potential mechanism for PAIS mitigation strategies across pathogens.
