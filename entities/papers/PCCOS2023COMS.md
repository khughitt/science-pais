# Core outcome measurement instruments for use in clinical and research settings for adults with post-COVID-19 condition

- **Authors:** PC-COS Study Group
- **Year:** 2023
- **Journal:** The Lancet Respiratory Medicine
- **Volume/Pages:** 11(12):1101-1114
- **DOI:** 10.1016/S2213-2600(23)00370-3
- **PMID:** 37926103
- **BibTeX key:** PCCOS2023COMS

## Key Contribution

This follow-on PC-COS Delphi study asks how the 12 adult post-COVID core outcome domains
should be measured. It is important because the answer is much less locked than the COS
domain list: only three instruments reached consensus for the core measurement set.

## Methods

**Design:** Literature review, three-round online modified Delphi process, and online
consensus meeting to develop a Core Outcome Measurement Set (COMS).

**Participants:** 594 participants from 58 countries in round 1; the consensus process
included people with post-COVID condition, family/caregivers, health-care professionals,
and researchers.

**Instrument pool:** Hundreds of candidate instruments were reduced to a smaller set
mapped to the 12 COS domains, then scored through Delphi rounds and consensus meeting.

## Key Findings

Consensus instruments were selected for only three domains:

| Outcome | Consensus instrument |
|---|---|
| Survival | Time until death |
| Recovery | Recovery Scale for COVID-19 |
| Respiratory functioning, symptoms, and conditions | Modified Medical Research Council Dyspnoea Scale |

For the other nine domains, no instrument met the pre-specified consensus threshold. The
paper nevertheless identifies instruments with the greatest support, including FAS/FSS/FACIT-F
for fatigue, DePaul Symptom Questionnaire for post-exertion symptoms, CFQ/MoCA-Blind for
cognition, BPI for pain, WAI/WPAI or a WHO CRF item for work/study changes, and
SBQ-LC/SF-36/EQ-5D-5L/WHO-DAS as candidate multidomain instruments.

## Relevance

For `task:t026`, this paper prevents over-adoption. The project should pre-commit to
PC-COS **domains**, but should not pretend the field has a fully standardized instrument
set for fatigue, PEM, cognition, pain, physical function, mental health, cardiovascular,
or nervous-system outcomes.

## Limitations

1. Most domains lack consensus instruments, so instrument choice remains study-specific.
2. Preferred instruments are not automatically validated for every PAIS subgroup or
   non-COVID trigger.
3. Feasibility drove part of the instrument selection; objective physiology and biological
   assays are outside the minimum COMS.

## Follow-up

Use the COMS as an instrument-ranking source. For project analyses, select one feasible
instrument per available PC-COS domain, record when no consensus instrument exists, and
avoid treating candidate instruments as mandatory standards.
