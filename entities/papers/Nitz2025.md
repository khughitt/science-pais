# Cardiovascular Sequelae of the COVID-19 Vaccines

- **Authors:** James N. Nitz, Kylie K. Ruprecht, Lukas J. Henjum, Andrew Y. Matta, Barnabas T. Shiferaw, Zoie L. Weber, Jalon M. Jones, Raven May, Carmen J. Baio, Kenneth J. Fiala, Alaa A. Abd-Elsayed
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal:** Cureus 17(4): e82041
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.82041
- **PMCID:** PMC12065646
- **BibTeX key:** Nitz2025
- **Source:** Full-text PDF (papers/pdfs/2025_Nitz_cardiovascular-sequelae-of-the-covid-19-vaccines.pdf), read 2026-07-10. Metadata cross-checked via `science paper-fetch --doi 10.7759/cureus.82041` (status: ok, source: europepmc).

> **Scope note — vaccine, not infection:** This paper reviews cardiovascular adverse events following COVID-19 *vaccination*, not post-acute infection sequelae. It is read here as a mechanistic and epidemiological comparator: both vaccination and natural SARS-CoV-2 infection present the spike protein to the immune system, and some downstream CV pathways (myocarditis, thromboinflammation) overlap. However, vaccine-related CV events are uniformly **acute** (onset within days to weeks, resolution within weeks), whereas PAIS by definition persists beyond 12 weeks. **Adjudicated (2026-07-19, `D-009`):** under the *trigger × persistence test* in `specs/scope-boundaries.md`, this paper's acute vaccine adverse events fail **both** scope axes and are **comparator-only** — never a PAIS case and never independent support for `hypothesis:0001`; their value is the infection-vs-vaccination antigen-burden asymmetry (`question:0081`). See `paper:Bellavite2026` for the persistent-PACVS cell of the same test.

## Key Contribution

A narrative review covering 166 articles (PubMed and Cochrane, February 2020 – May 2023, PRISMA 2020) that characterizes the spectrum, incidence, demographic specificity, timing, and proposed mechanisms of cardiovascular adverse events following all COVID-19 vaccine platforms. The primary CV complications are myocarditis (predominantly mRNA vaccines, young males, second dose), pericarditis, vaccine-induced immune thrombosis and thrombocytopenia (VITT, exclusively adenoviral-vector vaccines, young-to-middle-aged females), cerebral venous sinus thrombosis (CVT/CVST as the main VITT complication), and rare events including myocardial infarction, Takotsubo cardiomyopathy, arrhythmias, and new-onset hypertension. The authors stress that all reviewed adverse events are acute in nature, that long-term follow-up beyond six months is absent from the literature, and that the overall CV risk of vaccination remains lower than the CV risk of COVID-19 infection itself.

## Methods

- **Design:** Narrative review with systematic literature search (PRISMA 2020 flow diagram).
- **Search:** PubMed and Cochrane; broad MeSH-term strategy covering vaccine identifiers crossed with 20+ CV outcome terms; date range February 2020 – May 2023.
- **Yield:** 4,419 records identified, 166 included after screening (exclusions: irrelevant to COVID-19 vaccination; no cardiovascular complication; management/risk-factor focused; basic science only; non-English; articles discussing only COVID-19 infection without vaccine arm).
- **Study types included:** RCTs, meta-analyses, observational cohorts, case-control, cross-sectional, case series, case reports — all designs were included to maximize capture of rare events.
- **Data extraction:** Incidence, relative/hazard ratios, event timing, and demographic stratification were extracted from included articles. No formal meta-analysis or risk-of-bias assessment was performed; ranges of quantitative estimates are reported.
- **Scope restriction:** Articles describing events in the context of active SARS-CoV-2 infection (not post-vaccination) were excluded; articles without a clear temporal link to vaccination were excluded.

## Key Findings

### Myocarditis (82 articles)

- **Incidence:** General population 2–17 per million doses; young males aged 16–29: 32–147 per million doses.
- **Demographics:** Strongly male-predominant (79–96% of all cases in young healthy individuals); highest risk in ages 12–29; risk significantly lower for females (<7 per million across all ages).
- **Dose effect:** 35/85 articles reported higher prevalence after the second dose; 67–88% of cases occur after the second dose.
- **Vaccine platform:** Pfizer (BNT162b2) associated with higher prevalence in most studies (~70% of cases) vs. Moderna (mRNA-127), though Moderna showed more excess events in one absolute-risk study (4 vs. 7 excess events per 100,000 vaccinees in 28 days for BNT162b2; 9–28 for mRNA-127).
- **Timing:** Median onset 2–6 days; range 1–90 days.
- **Presentation:** Chest pain (17/85 articles), elevated troponin (17), decreased LVEF (15–50% presenting with reduced LVEF in some studies), ST/T-wave changes (15), myalgia (5), fever (5); female patients more commonly presented with flu-like symptoms.
- **Outcome:** Vast majority showed favorable outcomes with complete resolution; nearly all cardiac dysfunction resolved; late gadolinium enhancement was present in some.
- **Proposed mechanisms:** Immune-mediated response (most supported given 2–3-day onset after second dose); catecholamine excess via spike protein acting on adrenal chromaffin cells; host miRNA release triggering autoimmune cascades; spike-protein–anti-spike-antibody complement activation via the classic pathway; cytokine-mediated inflammation and autoantibody formation; dsRNA contamination.
- **Comparative risk:** Myocarditis risk from vaccination is significantly lower than from COVID-19 infection (risk ratios ~2–3.2 from vaccination vs. ~15–18.5 from infection across studies); for males 16–24 years, one study reported an incidence rate of 15.85 per 100,000 after the second BNT162b2 dose — the highest subgroup.
- **Adenoviral vector vaccine:** Only one article discussed myocarditis following adenoviral vector-based vaccines.

### Pericarditis (17 articles)

- **Demographics:** More common in males; in contrast to myocarditis, pericarditis skews toward older males (median age 21 with 71% males in one study; other articles note older adult predominance).
- **Association:** More strongly associated with mRNA vaccines; trends similar to myocarditis in vaccine-type specificity.
- **Co-occurrence:** Frequently reported together with myocarditis (myopericarditis); similar vulnerable populations.

### Thrombosis, VITT, and Cerebral Venous Thrombosis (84 articles)

- **General thrombosis incidence:** 4–280 per million vaccinations (wide range across study designs and vaccine types); risk ratio for both mRNA and adenoviral vaccines is 1.10 overall; mRNA vaccine-associated thrombosis is rare.
- **VITT (50 articles on adenoviral-specific VITT):**
  - Exclusively associated with adenoviral vector-based vaccines (AstraZeneca CHADOX1/Ad26.COV2.S, J&J); not observed with mRNA vaccines.
  - Incidence: 3–10 per million vaccinations; J&J reported lower (2–3.2 per million) vs. AstraZeneca (7–10 per million).
  - **Demographics:** 70–77% female; 90% under age 60; highest risk group is females under 60 on oral contraceptives.
  - **Onset:** 5–42 days post-vaccination.
  - **Mechanism:** Anti-PF4 (platelet factor 4) antibodies form against cationic PF4–anionic free DNA complexes introduced by the adenoviral vaccine → IgG anti-PF4 complexes interact with platelets via FcγR → platelet activation and aggregation → thrombosis + immune-mediated thrombocytopenia. Mechanistically similar to heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT) but binding a distinct PF4 epitope.
  - **Laboratory signature:** Low platelet count (<150×10⁹/L), elevated D-dimer (>0.5 mg/L), positive anti-PF4 antibodies by ELISA.
  - **Complications:** DIC, intracranial hemorrhage, hemorrhagic stroke, mesenteric ischemia; autopsy cases showed diffuse microthrombi with intracranial hemorrhage as the main cause of death.
- **CVT/CVST:**
  - Incidence: 2.6–10 per million doses; CVT is the primary AIS type in VITT (>50% of cerebral thrombi).
  - Typically occurs in middle-aged women; 14% mortality.
  - Additional thrombotic sites: jugular, hepatic, iliac, renal, portal, splanchnic, carotid, aortic, femoral veins.
  - Hemorrhagic stroke was reported both with and without CVST, and both with and without anti-PF4 antibodies.

### Rare Complications

- **Myocardial infarction (MI):** 3–4 per million doses; higher incidence with AstraZeneca CHADOX1 (+29% risk increase); mRNA vaccines showed no significant MI risk increase. Both classical MI and MINOCA reported; onset typically 24–48 hours post-vaccination; mean age 63–65 years (older than myocarditis patients). Vaccinated individuals have a *decreased* risk of MI during COVID-19 infection (hazard ratio 0.48).
- **Takotsubo (stress) cardiomyopathy:** ~0.17 per million doses; 90% female; median age ~62 years; mRNA vaccines predominantly; elevated troponin and decreased LVEF in all cases; all recovered.
- **Hypertension:** 13–14 per million doses; >10 mmHg increase in average diastolic or systolic BP in the 5 days after administration; hypertensive urgency/emergency much rarer (<0.5 per million).
- **Arrhythmias:** 2–9 per million doses; isolated tachycardia, sinus tachycardia, atrial fibrillation, SVT, palpitations (28–29 per million). POTS noted as a possible complication (autoimmune action on adrenergic receptors or angiotensin-II function proposed). ~7% of vaccine-induced myocarditis cases develop arrhythmia.
- **Cardiac arrest:** 2–3 per million doses; primarily in patients over 75 years; very rare.
- **MIS-C / MIS-A:** One article described pediatric MIS-C following mRNA vaccines; one described MIS-A in adults.
- **Giant cell arteritis:** One study found increased incidence following the third mRNA vaccine dose.

### Public Health and Temporal Findings

- **All events are acute:** Maximum onset window is 90 days; median onset across all adverse event classes is ≤14 days. No study in the corpus had follow-up beyond 185 days for myocarditis/pericarditis.
- **Critical evidence gap:** There is no data on whether COVID-19 vaccination contributes to chronic CV conditions; persistence, recurrence, or delayed-onset complications cannot be assessed from the current literature.
- **Overall mortality:** No significant association of COVID-19 vaccination with all-cause mortality (pooled HR 0.89, 95% CI 0.71–1.10, p = 0.28; Marchand et al.).
- **Infection vs. vaccination risk:** COVID-19 infection carries substantially higher CV morbidity and mortality than vaccination; vaccines significantly reduce severe illness, hospitalization, and death.
- **Comparative vaccine context:** CV complication rates following COVID-19 vaccination are higher than for influenza, MMR, and HPV vaccines, which are widely regarded as safe.

## Relevance

**Scope boundary:** This paper is a boundary case for this project. Its direct subject — acute adverse events after COVID-19 vaccination — does not constitute PAIS, which requires symptom persistence beyond 12 weeks after infection. However, it is directly relevant to the project in the following ways:

**hypothesis:0001 (shared dysregulated attractor):** The finding that both infection and vaccination with the same spike-protein antigen can trigger overlapping CV pathways (myocarditis, thromboinflammation, autoimmune platelet dysregulation) is consistent with the attractor frame's prediction of shared pathophysiology from shared triggers. Crucially, the paper quantifies the asymmetry: infection triggers myocarditis at ~5–6× the rate of vaccination (RR 15–18.5 vs. 2–3.2), suggesting that natural infection is a stronger perturbation of immune homeostasis — which is exactly what the antigen-burden / acute-severity propositions predict. This paper does not test the persistence dimension.

**hypothesis:0007 (autoimmune SFN/dysautonomia):** POTS is explicitly mentioned as a possible vaccine complication with an autoimmune/adrenergic mechanism (autoimmune action on adrenergic receptors). Palpitations and isolated tachycardia are reported post-vaccination. The proposed mechanisms (anti-adrenergic-receptor autoantibodies) overlap directly with `question:0009-functional-autoantibodies-drive-dysautonomia`. This paper does not test IENFD or small-fiber structure, but the autonomic phenotype following vaccination echoes the dysautonomia-as-GPCR-autoantibody hypothesis, and the paper's acute-only follow-up leaves open whether any subset persists.

**hypothesis:0009 (immune set-point shift → autoimmune conversion):** VITT demonstrates that vaccination can trigger genuine de-novo autoimmune responses (anti-PF4 IgG antibodies causing immune-mediated thrombocytopenia). Whether a subset of vaccine-induced autoimmunity persists beyond the acute event to produce chronic set-point shifts is not addressed by this paper (follow-up is ≤185 days) but is a live question.

**hypothesis:0016 (fibrinaloid microclots as non-specific inflammatory marker):** VITT involves widespread microthrombus formation. However, VITT's mechanism (anti-PF4 antibody → platelet-FcγR → aggregation) is distinct from the fibrinaloid microclot hypothesis (amyloid-resistant fibrin deposits driven by spike-protein or LPS). The Nitz2025 thrombosis literature does not engage with the fibrinaloid microclot framework; the two phenomena may co-exist or be distinct compartments of post-SARS-CoV-2 thromboinflammation.

**question:0010 (vascular microclot/endothelial subphenotype):** VITT and CVT/CVST data provide epidemiological denominators and mechanistic detail for the thromboinflammatory arm, though vaccine-specific VITT is mechanistically different from the post-infection endothelial/complement-activation thrombosis discussed in question:0010.

**question:0012 (prevention — does vaccination reduce PAIS?):** The review explicitly notes that COVID-19 vaccines reduce CV risk during COVID-19 infection (vaccinated individuals have HR 0.48 for MI during infection). This is indirect but relevant: vaccines that reduce acute severity also reduce the antigen burden that the attractor hypothesis (`proposition:0021`) implicates in PAIS incidence. However, this paper does not study post-acute outcomes; it does not address whether vaccination reduces long COVID or other PAIS.

**Comparator to `paper:Bellavite2026`:** Both papers concern vaccine-related CV/autoimmune phenomena. Bellavite2026 focuses on persistent PACVS (post-acute COVID-19 vaccination syndrome) with a proposed RAS-autoantibody mechanism; Nitz2025 covers the acute end of the spectrum and is a broader epidemiological survey. Together they bracket the vaccine-related CV phenotype from acute (Nitz) to potentially chronic (Bellavite). Neither paper provides evidence that post-vaccination syndromes are etiologically equivalent to post-infection PAIS, though the mechanistic overlap via spike-protein autoimmunity is real.

## Limitations

**Study-level:**
- Narrative review without formal meta-analysis or risk-of-bias assessment; ranges of incidence estimates are pooled across heterogeneous study designs, populations, and vaccine platforms.
- Search completed May 2023 — does not capture Omicron-era (XBB, BA.4/5 and later) vaccination data, nor bivalent booster data, nor data from the post-Omicron immune landscape.
- No distinction is made between primary vaccination series and booster doses for most adverse events, except where individual studies specified.
- Mixed-design inclusion (case reports through RCTs) without quality weighting means rare adverse events may be over-represented relative to their true population incidence.
- Only 166 of 4,419 screened articles were included; exclusion criteria were applied by two reviewers with a third for disagreements; no formal inter-rater reliability reported.
- No formal meta-regression to test whether incidence estimates varied by geographic region, surveillance system, or time period (early pandemic vs. Omicron era).

**For PAIS project specifically:**
- Follow-up is uniformly short (maximum 185 days; median onset ≤14 days). The key question for PAIS — whether any post-vaccination CV adverse events persist as chronic syndromes beyond 3–6 months — is entirely unanswered by this literature.
- The review does not distinguish syndromes that fully resolve from those with residual subclinical dysfunction (e.g., late gadolinium enhancement after myocarditis).
- No data on long COVID incidence as an outcome in vaccinated vs. unvaccinated individuals.
- The comparison "vaccine CV risk < infection CV risk" is epidemiologically robust for acute events but does not address the chronic-persistence dimension that defines PAIS.

## Follow-up

**Scope adjudication — RESOLVED (2026-07-19, `D-009`, t126):** the single adjudication note now exists in `specs/scope-boundaries.md` (the *trigger × persistence test*), covering both this paper and `paper:Bellavite2026`. Ruling: vaccine-adverse-event reviews stay in this repository as **comparators only** — acute vaccine AEs (this paper) fail both scope axes (comparator-only); persistent PACVS (Bellavite2026) fails only the trigger axis (boundary-monitor per D-003). Neither is a PAIS case.

**Evidence gap — long-term follow-up:** Whether any fraction of post-vaccination CV adverse events persists beyond 6 months (and whether any constitute a chronic syndrome analogous to PAIS) is the single most important unanswered question this review surfaces. Priority: watch for follow-up studies from the Nordic cohort (Karlstad et al., Ref 17 in Nitz2025) and VAERS-linked longitudinal registries.

**Asymmetry signal for antigen-burden frame:** The infection-vs.-vaccination myocarditis ratio (~5–6×) is a usable quantitative anchor for `proposition:0021-acute-antigen-burden-determines-pais-incidence`. Vaccination introduces a bolus of spike-protein mRNA/antigen under controlled immunological conditions; natural infection delivers sustained/higher antigen load across multiple compartments. The risk asymmetry is consistent with antigen burden (rather than spike-protein toxicity per se) driving outcome severity.

**POTS and adrenergic autoantibodies:** The post-vaccination POTS signal (mentioned briefly in Nitz2025 and more centrally in `paper:Bellavite2026`) warrants structured follow-up. Papers documenting POTS incidence after COVID-19 vaccination with mechanistic work (e.g., anti-adrenergic antibody serology) would directly inform `question:0009`.

**Papers to read next:**
- Karlstad et al. 2022 (JAMA; Ref 17 in Nitz2025) — Nordic cohort study, myocarditis risk after BNT162b2 in 23 million residents; the most powered single study on the myocarditis risk.
- Almas et al. 2022 (Biomed Pharmacother; Ref 56 in Nitz2025) — "state-of-the-heart" review on mRNA vaccination-induced adverse CV outcomes, cited as a key comparator.
- Marchand et al. 2023 (Ref 175) — meta-analysis of all-cause and cardiac-related mortality after COVID-19 vaccination (HR 0.89 no significant increase); relevant to net-benefit framing.
- Post-vaccination POTS case series — any study with follow-up >6 months and anti-adrenergic serology would directly link this paper to `question:0009` and `hypothesis:0007`.
