# Outpatient treatment of COVID-19 and incidence of post-COVID-19 condition over 10 months (COVID-OUT): a multicentre, randomised, quadruple-blind, parallel-group, phase 3 trial

- **PMID:** 37302406

## Key Contribution

The COVID-OUT trial is the strongest randomised controlled evidence that early outpatient pharmacologic treatment can prevent long COVID. Among three candidate drugs tested in a quadruple-blind factorial design, **metformin** reduced the incidence of a provider-diagnosed long-COVID outcome by approximately 41% relative risk reduction (RRR) over 10 months (HR 0.59, 95% CI 0.39–0.89; p = 0.012). When started within 3 days of symptom onset the benefit was larger (HR 0.37, 95% CI 0.15–0.95). **Ivermectin** and **fluvoxamine** showed no effect. This establishes proof of concept that a metabolically targeted, globally available, inexpensive generic drug can reduce PASC incidence if given early, and uniquely distinguishes *prevention* from *treatment* of established long COVID. It provides direct human RCT evidence implicating metabolic/mitochondrial disruption during the acute phase as an upstream driver of chronic post-infectious illness.

## Methods

**Design.** Decentralised, multicentre (six US sites), randomised, quadruple-blind (participants, investigators, care providers, outcome assessors all masked), parallel-group, phase 3 trial (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT04510194). 2×3 factorial randomisation: six arms in a 1:1:1:1:1:1 allocation — metformin + ivermectin; metformin + fluvoxamine; metformin + placebo; ivermectin + placebo; fluvoxamine + placebo; placebo + placebo.

**Population.** Adults aged 30–85 years with overweight or obesity (BMI not specified beyond this criterion in the abstract), COVID-19 symptoms for <7 days, and a documented SARS-CoV-2 positive PCR or antigen test within 3 days before enrolment. Enrolled December 30, 2020 – January 28, 2022 (thus spanning pre-Delta and Delta/early Omicron eras). N = 6,602 assessed for eligibility; 1,431 enrolled and randomly assigned; 1,323 received ≥1 dose (modified intention-to-treat, mITT); 1,126 consented for long-term follow-up and completed ≥1 post-day-180 survey (metformin comparison arm: n = 564 metformin, n = 562 placebo). 1,074 (95%) of 1,126 completed ≥9 months of follow-up.

**Key demographic features.** Median age 45 years (IQR 37–54); median BMI 29.8 kg/m² (IQR 27.0–34.2); 56.1% female; 7.0% of women pregnant; predominantly outpatient (by design). ## Key Findings

**Metformin arm (primary result of this paper).**
- Cumulative incidence of long COVID by day 300: 6.3% (95% CI 4.2–8.2%) in metformin vs. 10.4% (7.8–12.9%) in placebo.
- Absolute risk reduction: approximately 4.1 percentage points.
- Relative risk reduction: ~41%.
- HR 0.59 (95% CI 0.39–0.89); p = 0.012.
- Benefit was consistent across all prespecified subgroups.
- Early-treatment effect: when metformin was started within 3 days of symptom onset, HR = 0.37 (95% CI 0.15–0.95), suggesting larger benefit with shorter symptom-to-treatment interval.

**Ivermectin arm.** HR 0.99 (95% CI 0.59–1.64) vs. placebo — no effect on long-COVID incidence.

**Fluvoxamine arm.** HR 1.36 (95% CI 0.78–2.34) vs. placebo — no effect; point estimate in the wrong direction (not statistically significant increase, likely chance).

**Overall long-COVID incidence.** 93 of 1,126 (8.3%) participants reported a provider-diagnosed long-COVID diagnosis by day 300.

## Relevance

**To PAIS research generally.** This is the most rigorous RCT evidence to date that a pharmacologic intervention given during acute COVID-19 reduces long-COVID incidence. It directly tests the hypothesis that acute-phase processes are causally upstream of PASC and shows that intercepting them can alter the trajectory. The null results for ivermectin and fluvoxamine sharpen the inference: the effect is specific to metformin's mechanisms, not attributable to any early outpatient treatment effect or to antiviral activity generically.

**Mechanism implication.** Metformin's principal pharmacological actions include AMPK activation, inhibition of mitochondrial complex I, anti-inflammatory effects (reduced NF-κB signalling, reduced cytokine production), antiviral properties (mTOR suppression reduces viral replication), and reduction of cellular senescence and fibrin-clot formation. The effect cannot disambiguate which of these is operative, but the result is consistent with both: (a) a metabolic/mitochondrial disruption during acute infection priming the PASC state, and (b) metformin reducing viral burden or the immunopathological response that seeds chronic illness. The null ivermectin result rules out a simple antiviral-load explanation for metformin's benefit; the null fluvoxamine result rules out a simple anti-inflammatory/serotonergic explanation.

**Prevention vs. treatment distinction.** COVID-OUT addresses *prevention* of long COVID by early acute-phase treatment — a fundamentally different question from treating established PASC. Results here cannot be extrapolated to treating patients who already have long COVID. This is critical for t004 (therapeutics task): the therapeutic target is the acute window.

**Connects to `question:0012`.** Provides the best current RCT evidence that early acute-phase intervention reduces PAIS incidence, upgrading this question from observational inference to randomised proof of concept for a metabolic agent.

**Connects to `question:0011` and the metabolic/mitochondrial thread.** The metformin effect supports a role for acute metabolic disruption (mitochondrial dysfunction, mTOR/AMPK signalling, energetic stress) as a pathway into PASC. Metformin's complex-I inhibition and AMPK activation are directly relevant to the bioenergetic failure framing in `question:0011-mitochondrial-basis-of-pem`.

## Project Framework Mapping

**Hypothesis 0001 (shared dysregulated attractor).** The trial provides indirect support: if the attractor is entered via acute immunopathological/metabolic events, then a metabolic intervention during that window should reduce attractor-entry probability — which is what the data show. The null ivermectin result (antiparasitic with some antiviral activity) is consistent with the multi-loop attractor framing requiring more than viral clearance alone. The fluvoxamine null challenges a simple serotonergic/anti-inflammatory single-node explanation.

**Metabolic/mitochondrial mechanism thread.** The specificity of the metformin effect is the project's most direct RCT anchor for the claim that metabolic disruption during acute infection is causally upstream of chronic illness. This is the strongest interventional evidence yet in the project's literature for this thread; prior evidence was observational or mechanistic (Che2025, Naviaux2016-class proposals).

**Hypothesis 0004 (acute severity threshold).** COVID-OUT restricted enrolment to mild-to-moderate outpatients with overweight/obesity — a population with elevated PASC risk but not the most severe hospitalised patients. The early-treatment hazard ratio (HR 0.37) vs. later-treatment HR (closer to the overall 0.59) is consistent with a window in which acute-phase processes that set the threshold are still interruptible.

## Limitations

1. **Long-COVID case definition is weak.** The primary outcome is participant-reported receipt of a provider-diagnosed long-COVID label — subject to ascertainment bias (provider diagnostic practices vary), recall bias, healthcare-access differences, and label heterogeneity. The abstract does not specify use of a standardised research case definition (e.g. WHO or CDC definition). This limits confidence in the precision of the measured endpoint.

2. **Population specificity.** Restricted to adults with overweight or obesity, ages 30–85. Metformin's benefit may not generalise to normal-weight individuals, older adults with contraindications, or paediatric populations. Overweight/obese adults are a pre-selected high-PASC-risk group, which may amplify observed effect size.

3. **Enrolment era.** Enrolment spanned pre-Delta through early Omicron (December 2020 – January 2022), with likely heterogeneity in vaccination status, variant, and immune background across this period. The study pre-dated widespread use of antivirals (nirmatrelvir/ritonavir). [INACCESSIBLE: variant-stratified and vaccination-stratified subgroup results.]

4. **Mechanism opacity.** The trial cannot identify which of metformin's pleiotropic mechanisms is responsible. AMPK activation, complex-I inhibition, mTOR suppression, anti-inflammatory effects, and antifibrinolytic effects are all plausible — they are not separable by this design.

5. **Fluvoxamine apparent signal direction.** The HR of 1.36 for fluvoxamine (non-significant) should not be over-interpreted as harm but warrants attention given prior smaller observational/RCT signals for fluvoxamine in COVID severity. This may reflect cohort differences, the long-COVID vs. severity endpoint, or chance.

6. **Active comparator limitation.** Metformin and ivermectin/fluvoxamine were co-randomised in a factorial design; [INACCESSIBLE: full interaction analysis and potential for factorial cell heterogeneity to complicate main-effect estimates.]

7. **Follow-up by self-report survey.** Long-term outcomes are survey-based, and participation in follow-up surveys can introduce selection bias if dropout correlates with outcome.

## Follow-up

- **Mechanism disentanglement.** Which of metformin's mechanisms (AMPK, complex I, mTOR, anti-inflammatory, antifibrinolytic) mediates PASC prevention? Biomarker substudies or dose-response designs needed.
- **Generalisation.** Does metformin's effect hold in normal-weight populations, fully vaccinated cohorts, or different SARS-CoV-2 variants (including Omicron subvariants dominant post-enrolment)? Does it generalise to other PAIS (post-influenza, post-EBV)?
- **Case definition.** Studies using standardised PASC case definitions (symptom-based, functional, or biomarker-anchored) needed to validate the provider-diagnosis endpoint used here.
- **Combination therapy.** Given the attractor-model prediction that no single node is sufficient (hypothesis:0001), does combining metformin with an antiviral (e.g. nirmatrelvir) or immunomodulator further reduce PASC incidence?
- **Treatment window.** The within-3-day HR (0.37) vs. overall HR (0.59) suggests strong time-dependence. Prospective characterisation of the interruptible window length is needed.
- **Connects to `question:0012`:** This trial should be listed as the primary supporting evidence for the possibility that early acute intervention reduces PASC incidence, upgrading that question's evidence tier from observational inference to RCT proof of concept.
