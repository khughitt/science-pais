## Key Contribution

This study applies an explainable neural-network approach (Layer-wise Relevance Propagation, LRP) to publicly available flow cytometry data from COVID-19 patients in order to objectively quantify which immune cell types and activation markers are most predictive of disease severity. The central finding is that innate immune responses are pathologically delayed in severe COVID-19 patients relative to moderate cases, and that this delay — combined with failure to down-regulate IL-17a and IFN-γ on classical monocytes, Tregs, and CD8 T cells — is primarily responsible for progression to severe disease. The paper proposes a concise two-curve dynamic model of immune kinetics distinguishing moderate from severe COVID-19 trajectories.

## Methods

**Data:** Two publicly available flow cytometry datasets from Flow Repository were used:
- FR-FCM-Z36F (96 FCS files): whole blood mass cytometry (CyTOF) from hospitalized COVID-19 patients and healthy controls, characterizing circulating immune cell frequency and phenotype; n = 145 total samples, including Severe (n=36; Early=11, Middle=10, Late=15), Moderate (n=48; Early=28, Middle=13, Late=7), and Healthy (n=12).
- FR-FCM-Z2KP (49 FCS files): PBMC flow cytometry (BD Symphony) measuring cytokine secretion; samples include Severe=17, Severe-to-Moderate=6, Moderate=20, Healthy=6.

**Clustering:** PhenoGraph algorithm (R-based) was used to robustly cluster patient PBMCs into phenotypically distinct immune subpopulations. For Z36F, 38 clusters per patient were identified (including classical monocytes, non-classical monocytes, multiple T-cell subsets, NK cells, neutrophil subsets, basophils, pDCs, and B-cell subsets). For Z2KP, 52 clusters per patient were identified and manually gated into B cells, classical monocytes, CD8 T, Tconv, Treg, and DNT cells, with activation markers including IL-17a, IL-2, GATA3, IFN-γ, IL-4, IL-10, PD_1, CD40L, RORγt, CTLA_4, CCR7, TNF-α, and IL-6.

**Neural network and relevance scores (RS):** A regression neural network (two hidden layers; input = immune cell cluster percentages or activation marker concentrations; output = disease severity score 1–3) was trained using the Pyrenn toolbox. Relevance Scores (RS) were calculated by the LRP algorithm: positive RS indicates a cell type/marker positively correlated with severity; negative RS indicates inverse correlation (lymphopenia-like). K-fold cross-validation (k=5, ~20% validation set) was used. Neural network accuracy: moderate patients ~69.5–72.3%, severe patients ~61.2–67.0% (Z36F); combined accuracy for Z2KP ~73.4% moderate, ~81.5% severe.

**Statistical testing:** Welch's t-test was used to compare RS between HC_W and HC_ICU groups for activation marker analyses.

## Key Findings

**Innate immune response timing:**
- In moderate COVID-19, innate immune responses (classical monocytes, CCR6+ neutrophils, plasma B cells) are activated rapidly and with high RS at the early stage (day 1 since symptom onset), then steadily decline as the adaptive response rises.
- In severe COVID-19, innate immune cell RSs are near-zero at the early stage (no significant correlation with severity), indicating that the early innate response is absent or insufficient. The innate response in severe patients only rises at the middle stage (day 4) and remains elevated through late stage, while the adaptive response is correspondingly delayed.

**Classical monocytes are the most discriminating cell type:**
- Classical monocytes show a strongly positive RS in moderate patients at early and middle stages but show no positive RS (or even slight negative RS) in severe patients at early stage. This continuous decrease in peripheral classical monocyte counts is significantly associated with disease severity, consistent with monocyte apoptosis and tissue migration literature.

**Key activation-marker RS differences (Z2KP, Welch's t-test significant findings):**
- IFN-γ down-regulation on classical monocytes, Tregs, and CD8 T cells is significantly associated with severe disease (high RS for IFN-γ in moderate; significantly lower or absent in severe).
- IL-17a: in moderate patients shows negative RS on classical monocytes (i.e., down-regulation correlates with disease progression for moderate illness); critically, in severe patients IL-17a is NOT down-regulated on classical monocytes — this failure of IL-17a suppression on monocytes and Tregs is strongly associated with severe disease.
- IL-4: markedly positively correlated with disease development in classical monocytes, Tconv, CD8 T, and B cells in moderate patients; this positive correlation is absent in severe patients (IL-4 is not up-regulated).
- TNF-α: negatively correlated with severity in B cells (both moderate and severe); IF_2 also negatively correlated in B cells.
- CTLA_4: markedly positively correlated with disease in classical monocytes (moderate patients).

**Dynamic model of COVID-19 immune responses (Figure 6):**
- Moderate disease: rapid early innate response → primes adaptive response → innate declines, adaptive rises and resolves.
- Severe disease: absent/minimal early innate response → adaptive priming delayed → innate eventually activates at middle stage and remains high through late stage (hyperinflammatory) → adaptive only activates late. This is consistent with SARS-CoV-2 evading early innate immune triggering.

## Relevance

This paper is directly relevant to the PAIS project's framing of severe COVID-19 as a **failure of early homeostatic immune response** that prevents orderly resolution and recovery.

**PAIS frame connection:** The core finding — that innate immune delay at the earliest disease stage causes a cascade of failed immune coordination (no early innate → no timely adaptive priming → late-stage immunopathology) — maps directly onto the project's central question about why some patients fail to recover homeostasis after acute SARS-CoV-2 infection. The dynamic model proposed here provides a mechanistic staging framework: severe COVID-19 does not simply reflect "more inflammation" but rather a fundamentally different *kinetic trajectory* in which the initial homeostatic sensing and response fails, and all downstream immune phases are consequently disordered.

**Connection to persistent immune activation:** The finding that innate immunity remains elevated through the late stage in severe patients, rather than resolving, is a direct precursor to the chronic immune activation phenotypes documented in long COVID. Patients who cannot mount a timely early innate response may never achieve the properly timed innate-to-adaptive handoff required for resolution, setting up the conditions for post-acute persistence.

**IFN-γ and IL-17a dysregulation:** The failure to down-regulate IFN-γ in severe patients' monocytes/Tregs/CD8 T cells, and the failure to suppress IL-17a on monocytes and Tregs in severe patients, align with cytokine storm and immunopathology signatures documented in long COVID literature. These markers are strong candidates for biomarkers that bridge acute severity to PAIS risk.

**Mechanistic specificity:** The paper adds precision to the general "immune dysregulation" frame by identifying specific cell-type/marker pairs (classical monocyte IFN-γ, Treg IL-17a, CD8 IFN-γ) rather than bulk cytokine measurements, supporting the project's emphasis on cell-type-resolved immune profiling as the appropriate level of analysis.

## Limitations

- **Small sample size:** Total n=145 (Z36F) and n=49 (Z2KP) for neural network training; acknowledged as a primary limitation. The regression neural network trades off some accuracy for the ability to compute RS, making accuracy lower than a classifier (~69–81% range).
- **Two datasets only, different staining panels:** The two datasets used different cytometry platforms (CyTOF vs. BD Symphony) and different staining strategies. Merging was infeasible, and the analyses are necessarily separated. Results are internally consistent but cross-cohort generalization is limited.
- **Cross-sectional within each stage bin:** The study bins patients into early/middle/late stage based on days since symptom onset; within each bin, observations are cross-sectional, not truly longitudinal. Patient-level trajectories cannot be tracked.
- **No long COVID or PAIS follow-up:** The study ends at acute-phase discharge; it does not track which patients develop post-acute sequelae. The connection to PAIS is mechanistically plausible but not empirically demonstrated.
- **Regression NN (one output neuron) vs. classifier:** Using a regression architecture (single output neuron representing a continuous severity score) to enable LRP is noted to reduce accuracy relative to a classifier. This is a deliberate methodological trade-off.
- **Flow Repository data only:** Publicly available FCS files often have dozens to hundreds of cases; staining strategy heterogeneity limits the ability to pool data sets for larger-scale validation.
- **Negative RS interpretation:** The biological direction (apoptosis vs. migration) of negatively correlated cells in peripheral blood cannot be determined from peripheral blood counts alone; the authors acknowledge this explicitly.

## Model / Tool Availability

- **Source code:** Neural network optimization and RS calculation pipeline uploaded to GitHub at https://github.com/Zhu-0010/hello_world/branches
- **Neural network library:** Pyrenn (open-source, from Technische Universität München; https://github.com/yabata/pyrenn) was used for the neural network training; connection matrices IW^{1,1}, LW^{2,1}, LW^{3,2} are accessible through Pyrenn's standard interface.
- **LRP implementation:** Layer-wise Relevance Propagation toolbox at https://lrpserver.hhi.fraunhofer.de/
- **Input data:** Publicly available FCS files from Flow Repository (http://flowrepository.org/) under accession FR-FCM-Z36F and FR-FCM-Z2KP.
- **Supplementary data:** S1 File (Cluster_Percentage with group), S2 File (PhenoGraph_Acsinh_Expr activation marker concentrations), S3 File (neural network optimization details) are referenced as supplementary material in the published paper.
- **License:** Not explicitly stated; code appears to be publicly accessible on GitHub.

## Follow-up

- Track which severe-COVID patients in similar cohorts developed long COVID / PAIS, and test whether the innate delay signature at early stage predicts post-acute outcomes.
- Compare the innate-delay dynamic model from this paper to ME/CFS onset studies — does a similar early innate failure accompany infections that trigger ME/CFS?
- The IFN-γ / IL-17a dysregulation on classical monocytes and Tregs identified here should be cross-referenced against long COVID immune profiling studies (e.g., [paper:Bai2023], [paper:Nicolai2023]) to determine whether these markers persist post-acutely.
- Explore whether the PhenoGraph + LRP pipeline could be applied to PBMC flow cytometry data from long COVID cohorts to rank which post-acute immune features most correlate with symptom burden.
- Consider [paper:Ganesh2022] and [paper:Wester2024] (already in entities/) for comparison of acute-phase vs. post-acute immune profiles in PAIS.
