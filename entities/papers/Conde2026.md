# Post-Infectious Fatigue and Depression Following Dengue: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Associated Factors

- **Authors:** Anna Condé, Olivier Maillard, Chaturaka Rodrigo, Antoine Bertolotti, Carolina X. Sandler, Andrew R. Lloyd, Patrick Gérardin
- **Year:** 2026
- **Journal:** Reviews in Medical Virology, vol. 36, no. 4, p. e70164
- **DOI:** 10.1002/rmv.70164
- **BibTeX key:** Conde2026
- **Source:** PDF

## Key Contribution

This is the first systematic review and meta-analysis to jointly characterise and compare factors associated with *both* post-dengue fatigue syndrome (PDFS) and post-dengue depression, quantifying their distinct and shared risk profiles. In a pooled analysis of 9 studies (1470 patients, 2006–2023), female sex (OR 1.69, 95% CI 1.33–2.14), myalgia (OR 3.45, 95% CI 2.10–5.69), dengue haemorrhagic fever / severe dengue (OR 1.53–4.02), emerged as PDFS-specific risk factors, while no factor reached significance for post-dengue depression. Narrative synthesis additionally identifies thrombocytopaenia as a potential shared biomarker for both outcomes — a proposed serotonin-depletion mechanism linking systemic inflammation to both fatigue and depressive symptoms.

## Methods

- **Design:** PRISMA-compliant systematic review and meta-analysis; registered in PROSPERO (CRD420250655004).
- **Search:** PubMed, Science Direct, Web of Science, and Google Scholar from database inception to 6 June 2025, updated 31 December 2025; grey literature via Google Scholar; PRISMA 2020 flow diagram.
- **Inclusion criteria:** Observational studies (prospective, retrospective, cross-sectional) in patients of any age with dengue confirmed by serological or virological methods or clinical diagnosis; outcomes assessed using validated questionnaires (Fatigue Questionnaire, Chalder Fatigue Questionnaire) or clinical/DSM-IV/DASS-21/CESD-20 criteria for depression; English, French, Spanish, Portuguese; follow-up ≥ 1 week post-discharge.
- **Exclusion:** Reviews, protocols, conference abstracts, editorials, letters, case studies, non-peer-reviewed articles, duplicate publications.
- **Selection:** Two independent reviewers (A.C. and P.G.) screened via Rayyan software; disagreements resolved by discussion or third reviewer.
- **Quality assessment:** Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS) for cohort and case-control studies; Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) checklist for cross-sectional and prospective descriptive studies; cut-offs: low (<5), moderate (5–6), high (≥7) points.
- **Statistical analysis:** Random-effects model; between-study variance via REML; pooled odds ratios (OR) when ≥ 2 studies reported a factor; heterogeneity by Cochran Q (significant if p < 0.10) and I² (considerable if ≥ 75%); narrative synthesis for single-study factors. RR-to-OR conversion via Zhang & Yu formula where needed. No publication bias assessment (fewer than 10 studies per outcome, per Cochrane Handbook recommendation).
- **Sensitivity analyses:** Restricted to studies with n ≥ 100 participants and studies with follow-up ≥ 1 month.
- **Study corpus:** 206 records identified → 85 duplicates removed → 121 screened → 9 included in systematic review, 8 in meta-analysis. Studies conducted 2006–2023 across Sri Lanka (n=5), Pakistan (n=2), Singapore (n=1), Colombia (n=1). Total: 1470 dengue patients; mean age 34.5 ± 12.9 years; 45.9% female.

*Adjusted OR.

- Sensitivity analyses confirmed female sex association was robust to restriction by sample size (≥100: OR 1.73, 95% CI 1.36–2.21, p<0.001, 4 studies, n=1164) and follow-up duration (≥1 month: OR 1.81, 95% CI 1.32–2.49, p<0.001, 4 studies, n=743).
- Narrative synthesis additionally identified: unmarried status, presence of chills, mental and physical fatigue at baseline, absence of rashes, low haemoglobin (<11 g/dL), low platelet count (<30×10⁹/L), poor sleep, and experience of stressful life events as single-study associations.

### Post-Dengue Depression — Quantitative Synthesis (1 factor, 3 studies)

- Only female sex could be meta-analysed (2 studies, 169 patients): OR 1.05, 95% CI 0.16–6.79, p > 0.05 — not significantly associated.
- Narrative synthesis: younger patients (Ali et al.) vs. older patients (Gunathilaka et al.) at risk — contradictory. Lower platelet count identified in one case-control study (Gunathilaka et al.). All findings are very low certainty.

### Shared Pathway — Thrombocytopaenia

- Identified through narrative synthesis as an association for both PDFS (one Sri Lanka prospective cohort) and post-dengue depression (one Sri Lanka case-control study).
- Proposed mechanism: platelets are the main peripheral reservoir of serotonin; low platelet counts may reduce serotonin availability, impairing serotonergic transmission — contributing to both depression and fatigue.

### Study Quality

Three studies high quality, five moderate quality, one low quality. Most pooled analyses used unadjusted ORs.

## Relevance

This paper directly advances the project research question (`research-question:post-acute-infection-syndromes`) by providing quantitative risk profiles for two distinct PAIS phenotypes arising from the same pathogen — a design that enables dissection of shared versus syndrome-specific pathways within the PAIS frame.

**Alignment with the PAIS failed-homeostatic-recovery frame:**

1. **Female-sex OR (1.69) for PDFS recapitulates the cross-PAIS female predominance:** Exactly as documented in long COVID, ME/CFS, post-infectious mononucleosis, and post-Q-fever, female sex is the single most consistent and statistically robust predictor of post-dengue fatigue persistence. The proposed mechanisms — sex-linked immune differences (X-chromosome gene escape from inactivation enhancing IFN-I responses, pro-inflammatory low-17β-estradiol milieu in non-reproductive-age women, female bias toward autoimmunity, female hypocortisolism in ME/CFS) — align with the immune dysregulation arm of the PAIS model. The fact that the same OR (~1.65–1.69) emerges independently in both the Hertanti 2025 and Condé 2026 meta-analyses (which partly overlap in primary studies) provides convergent evidence for this cross-PAIS signal.

2. **Myalgia OR (3.45) as a severity-of-immune-activation proxy:** The extremely strong myalgia association (the largest OR in this analysis) closely mirrors the finding in long COVID where acute-phase myalgia predicted subsequent fatigue and post-exertional malaise (PEM). Shared mechanisms proposed by the authors — skeletal muscle myotube infection by DENV driving CXCL-10/IP-10 production, mitochondrial dysfunction (mt-DNA damage, lower ATP production), a shift to non-oxidative glycolytic type I phenotype — are precisely the muscle-signature abnormalities described in ME/CFS and long COVID skeletal muscle biopsies. This cross-PAIS convergence on a shared musculoskeletal failure mode strengthens the "shared PAIS pathophysiology" hypothesis.

3. **Disease severity (DHF/severe dengue ORs 1.53–4.02) as inflammatory priming for PAIS:** Higher initial viral/inflammatory burden → higher PDFS risk, mirroring ICU admission → long COVID and DHF → PIF associations in other literature. Supports a dose-dependent model: the magnitude of acute immune disruption sets the probability of homeostatic failure.

4. **Thrombocytopaenia as a potential cross-outcome biomarker:** The narrative synthesis pointing to thrombocytopaenia for both PDFS and post-dengue depression — via a serotonin-depletion mechanism — is notable as a candidate shared pathway between somatic (fatigue) and psychological (depression) PAIS manifestations, potentially extending to other PAIS conditions where platelet hyper-activation and fibrinoid microclots have been documented.

5. **Null result for post-dengue depression factors:** The absence of pooled significant factors for depression (versus four for fatigue) is not merely a power limitation — it reflects a genuine evidence gap that mirrors the broader PAIS field, where depression is often co-occurrent with fatigue but its specific determinants remain poorly characterised. This is a research gap directly relevant to the project.

6. **Methodological lesson for PAIS research:** The use of validated vs. non-validated instruments for fatigue assessment is a critical confounder across PAIS studies generally. This review, like Hertanti 2025, documents that non-validated clinical assessment underestimates fatigue burden — a cross-PAIS methodological issue.

## Limitations

- **Small corpus:** Only 9 included studies (1470 patients total); post-dengue depression studies only 3 (222 patients), severely limiting power for pooled analyses and moderator exploration.
- **Unadjusted ORs:** Most pooled estimates used unadjusted ORs to maintain consistency across studies, likely introducing confounding; only two factors used adjusted ORs.
- **High heterogeneity for several non-significant factors:** Age (I²=85.3%), fever (87.8%), headache (74.0%), arthralgia (81.3%) — pooled estimates for these factors are unreliable; the meta-analytic model could not formally explore sources of heterogeneity given the small study count.
- **Cannot assess publication bias:** Cochrane Handbook recommends ≥10 studies per funnel plot analysis; this analysis falls below that threshold for all outcomes.
- **Asian-population predominance:** Sri Lanka contributes 5 of 9 studies; one Colombian study is the only non-Asian representation — limits generalisability to other dengue-endemic regions (Latin America, Sub-Saharan Africa).
- **Thrombocytopaenia as confounder of severity:** Thrombocytopaenia co-occurs as a defining feature of DHF; it is unclear whether it is an independent predictor or a severity marker — limits causal inference.
- **No mechanistic data synthesised:** No biomarker, cytokine, or immune-profiling data included; proposed mechanisms (CXCL-10, estrogen receptor, mitochondrial dysfunction, serotonin depletion) are literature-supported hypotheses, not empirically derived from the included studies.
- **Family history of depression not reported:** A major predictor of depression in the general population was absent from all included studies — represents a critical gap for the post-dengue depression evidence base.
- **Overlapping primary studies with `paper:Hertanti2025`:** Both reviews include some of the same primary studies (Sri Lankan and Colombian cohorts from 2006–2023), so the two meta-analyses are not fully independent. This review contributes unique value by (a) including the depression outcome and (b) using OR as the effect measure rather than prevalence estimates.

## Follow-up

- **Hertanti 2025** (`paper:Hertanti2025`): Companion meta-analysis focusing on PDFS *prevalence* (pooled 20%) and some overlapping risk factors; read together these two papers provide complementary views (prevalence vs. associated factors) of the same evidence base.
- **Kalimudki 2022** (`paper:Kalimuddin2022`): Singapore prospective cohort on chronic dengue sequelae — a primary study cited in both reviews; provides longitudinal individual-level data.
- Mechanistic studies needed: immune profiling (cytokine trajectories, IFN-I, CXCL-10/IP-10, CCL-5/RANTES) in post-dengue fatigue cohorts; platelet function and serotonin studies in post-dengue depression.
- Adequately powered prospective cohort studies of post-dengue depression specifically, with family history and psychosocial covariates, are identified as the most critical evidence gap.
- Dengue serotype as a moderator of fatigue/depression outcomes not addressed by any included study — a promising cross-study question.
- The cross-PAIS parallel between dengue myalgia and long COVID myalgia predicting subsequent chronic fatigue/PEM warrants a systematic comparison; the mitochondrial / CXCL-10 muscle mechanism may be a shared PAIS feature.
