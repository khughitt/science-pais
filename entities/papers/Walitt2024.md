# Deep phenotyping of post-infectious myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome

- **Authors:** Brian Walitt, Komudi Singh, Samuel R. LaMunion, Mark Hallett, Steve Jacobson, Kong Chen, et al. (75+ scientists across 15 NIH institutes; senior author: Avindra Nath)
- **Year:** 2024
- **Journal:** Nature Communications
- **DOI:** [10.1038/s41467-024-45107-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-45107-3)
- **PMC:** PMC10881493
- **BibTeX key:** Walitt2024
- **Tier:** Core — single deepest multi-system phenotyping study of PI-ME/CFS to date
- **Source:** Europe PMC full-text XML (OA)

## Key Contribution

The NIH intramural ME/CFS program performed the most comprehensive biological characterization of post-infectious ME/CFS (PI-ME/CFS) yet published, applying 50+ measurement modalities to a small but rigorously adjudicated cohort (n = 17 PI-ME/CFS; n = 21 healthy volunteer controls). The study's central interpretive claim — contested in the field — is that the cardinal motor abnormality of PI-ME/CFS is an **alteration of effort preference** (a reward-effort decision-making deficit mediated by the right temporal-parietal junction) rather than classical peripheral or central fatigue. Alongside this, the study documents autonomic dysfunction, CSF catecholamine reductions, immune alterations consistent with chronic antigenic stimulation (increased naive, decreased switched-memory B-cells; PD-1+ CD8 T-cells in CSF), pronounced sex-differentiated immune and metabolic gene-expression signatures, reduced gut microbiome alpha diversity, and physical deconditioning as sequelae. The study explicitly frames PI-ME/CFS as a centrally mediated disorder with antigen persistence as a candidate upstream driver.

## Methods

**Design:** Cross-sectional exploratory case-control study; NIH Clinical Center inpatient phenotyping (NCT02669212); recruitment December 2016 — February 2020 (halted by COVID-19 pandemic).

**Case ascertainment:** Highly rigorous multi-stage process. Of 484 inquiries, 217 underwent telephone interview and medical-record review; 27 proceeded to week-long in-person evaluation; **17 were adjudicated PI-ME/CFS** by a panel of five recognized ME/CFS clinicians (Bateman, Kogelnik, Komaroff, Natelson, Peterson) who had to reach **unanimous consensus** — both on ME/CFS diagnosis and on infection temporality. Importantly, 20% of cases initially meeting criteria were later reclassified, underscoring the misattribution problem in the broader literature. Eligible participants had to meet at least one of three criteria sets (1994 Fukuda, 2003 Canadian Consensus, IOM 2015), demonstrate moderate-to-severe fatigue (MFI ≥ 13 on general fatigue or ≥ 10 on reduced activity), and show functional impairment (SF-36 thresholds). Infection occurred within the prior five years; no prior explanatory medical or psychiatric illness. All participants were pre-COVID-19 pandemic (triggers: diverse, not SARS-CoV-2).

**Participants:** n = 17 PI-ME/CFS, n = 21 HV; matched on age, sex, and BMI. No group differences in age, sex, or BMI by design; sex distribution was roughly balanced (ME/CFS occurs ~3× more in females but the study sought balance).

**Measurement battery (partial list):**
- Autonomic: 24-hour ambulatory ECG with HRV, tilt-table testing, Valsalva maneuver, baroslope
- Motor/cognitive: handgrip maximal voluntary contraction (MVC), grip endurance, Effort-Expenditure for Rewards Task (EEfRT), 15-battery neuropsychological tests
- Neuroimaging: fMRI during repetitive grip task; conjunction and group-difference analyses of BOLD signal
- Cardiopulmonary: CPET (subset: n = 8 PI-ME/CFS, n = 9 HV)
- Biofluid profiling: CSF and serum catecholamines (LC-MS/MS), CSF and serum metabolomics, plasma lipidomics, aptamer-based proteomics (SomaScan)
- Immunology: PBMC flow cytometry (T/B/NK cell subsets, PD-1, TIGIT, CD244, CD226, CXCR5), CSF flow cytometry, autoantibody discovery panels
- Transcriptomics: PBMC RNA-seq; muscle biopsy RNA-seq; all analyses stratified by sex
- Gut microbiome: shotgun metagenomics of stool
- Small-fiber neuropathy: skin punch biopsy nerve fiber density, CSF neurofilament light chain, GFAP
- Performance validity: standardized tests to rule out confounding by non-cognitive factors
- Qualitative: life narrative and semi-structured qualitative interviews capturing PEM and illness experience

**Case definition in effect:** Fukuda (1994), Canadian Consensus Criteria (2003), or IOM (2015) — any one sufficient, with severity and functional impairment thresholds; unanimous expert adjudication required.

## Key Findings

### Cohort (N)
**The fundamental quantitative caveat: n = 17 PI-ME/CFS.** The study is explicitly designed as exploratory hypothesis-generating deep-phenotyping, not a confirmatory powered study. Post-hoc power calculation shows only effects of d ≥ 0.94 reach 80% power at these group sizes, so only large effects were detectable. The authors acknowledge this and focus on consilience across measurement domains rather than individual p-values.

### Autonomic dysfunction
- Reduced HRV across all time-domain indices (SDNNi, rMSSD, pNN50) and frequency-domain indices (HF and LF power) in 24-hour ECG; non-linear HRV indices also significantly altered (p = 1.1E-12 for one non-linear index)
- Higher heart rate throughout the day (elevated sympathetic tone) and blunted nighttime heart-rate dip
- Decreased baroslope and prolonged pressure-recovery after Valsalva — consistent with parasympathetic deficiency and impaired baroreflex
- No group differences on tilt-table testing for orthostatic hypotension or excessive tachycardia (POTS negative by standard threshold in this cohort)

### Effort preference vs. fatigue
- On EEfRT (Effort-Expenditure for Rewards Task), PI-ME/CFS participants chose fewer hard tasks than HVs when reward and probability were equalized (OR = 1.65 [1.03, 2.65], p = 0.04), reflected in lower Proportion of Hard-Task Choices (PHTC)
- No group differences in response to within-task fatigue accumulation, reward value, or reward probability — isolating the deficit to **effort preference at baseline**, not dynamic fatigue updating
- Grip endurance (time to failure at 50% MVC) was shorter in PI-ME/CFS (p = 0.0002) and correlated strongly with effort preference (32-74% of variance in PI-ME/CFS) — but not with grip strength or arm muscle mass
- fMRI showed decreased BOLD signal in the **right temporal-parietal junction (TPJ)** in PI-ME/CFS during repetitive grip tasks; TPJ is implicated in sense of agency over movement and integration of willed action with outcome
- Peripheral fatigue (high:low EMG ratio) and central fatigue (post-exercise cortical depression) increased in HVs but not in PI-ME/CFS, suggesting PI-ME/CFS participants were stopping before generating classical fatigue signals
- Maximum grip strength (MVC) was equivalent between groups

### Physical capacity
- Peak VO2 significantly lower in PI-ME/CFS (p = 0.004), approximately 3.3 METs below HV group
- Lower peak heart rate (p = 0.07) and peak power (p = 0.08) trends
- Chronotropic incompetence and physical deconditioning interpreted as contributors; ventilatory efficiency (VE/VCO2), muscle oxygenation (NIRS), and mechanical efficiency not different
- All but one PI-ME/CFS participant reached RER ≥ 1.1 (maximal effort confirmed)

### Neurocognitive
- Self-reported cognitive complaints substantially increased across all five domains (attention, verbal memory, visuoperceptual, language, visual memory; p = 0.00006 overall)
- **No significant group differences on any of 15 neuropsychological performance tests** — cognitive complaints were not matched by objective performance deficits in this cohort (possibly insufficient power or self-pacing accommodation)
- Performance validity testing passed in all participants (symptoms are not explained by non-cognitive factors or effort withdrawal)

### CSF catecholamines and metabolites
- Significantly reduced **DOPA** (p = 0.021), **DOPAC** (p = 0.025), and **DHPG** (p = 0.006) in CSF; norepinephrine and dopamine not significantly different
- CSF catechol levels correlated with grip strength and effort preference — linking central dopaminergic/noradrenergic deficiency to the effort-preference phenotype
- Several tryptophan pathway metabolites reduced in CSF (consistent with prior ME/CFS and long-COVID metabolomics)
- Female PI-ME/CFS CSF: tryptophan, butyrate, and TCA-cycle metabolites important in classification; male PI-ME/CFS CSF: different metabolite subset
- The pattern is interpreted as decreased central catecholamine biosynthesis, impacting downstream autonomic function and effort-preference circuitry

### Immune findings
- Increased naive B-cells (p = 0.037) and decreased switched-memory B-cells (p = 0.008) in blood — pattern consistent with chronic antigenic stimulation preventing B-cell class switching / maturation
- Elevated PD-1+ CD8 T-cells in **CSF** (p = 0.033; not in blood) — potential marker of T-cell exhaustion or activation in CNS compartment
- Decreased CD226+ CD8 T-cells in blood (p = 0.055, trend)
- No consistent autoantibody signal: autoantibody discovery assays found low-level antibodies in one PI-ME/CFS and two HVs — **no evidence of a uniform autoimmune mechanism in this cohort**
- NK cell cytolytic function in blood: not different between groups; subset of PI-ME/CFS had elevated NK CD56 bright/dim ratio in CSF

### Sex-differentiated immune and gene-expression signatures
- PBMC RNA-seq: 614 differentially expressed genes overall, but clustering by sex masked disease-status clustering; sex-stratified analysis is essential
  - **Male PI-ME/CFS:** 887 DE genes; enriched in ubiquitin, IL-10, T-cell activation, NF-kB pathways; STAT4-TLR9 interactome upregulated
  - **Female PI-ME/CFS:** 849 DE genes; enriched in B-cell proliferation, cytokine and lymphocyte proliferation processes; B-cell interactome upregulated
  - Only 34 genes (<5%) overlapping between sexes — **immune biology is substantially sex-dimorphic**
- Muscle biopsy RNA-seq: sex-stratified; 593 DE genes (male) / 328 DE genes (female); only 15 genes in common
  - Male: upregulation of fatty acid beta-oxidation and epigenetic/RNA-processing genes; downregulation of hexose metabolism and mitochondrial processes
  - Female: downregulation of fatty acid oxidation and mitochondrial processes (opposite direction for some pathways); upregulation of growth hormone receptor signaling and ubiquitin transferase function
  - Both sexes show increased oxidative stress markers via distinct pathways
- Sex difference confirmed in a publicly deposited ME/CFS monocyte RNA-seq dataset (GSE130353; 638 / 420 DE genes in males/females; only 23 shared) — sex-dimorphism is a replicable feature
- Serum/CSF aptamer proteomics: no FDR-corrected significant differences in full cohort; exploratory multivariate analyses suggested sex-specific protein predictors

### Gut microbiome
- Reduced alpha diversity (observed taxa; p = 0.002); inverse Simpson index not different
- Beta diversity: significant compositional differences by Bray-Curtis dissimilarity (PERMANOVA p < 0.0081)

### Negative / null findings (explicitly catalogued in Supplementary Data S22)
- No differences in: small-fiber nerve density, CSF neurofilament light chain, GFAP, polysomnography, standard clinical labs, tilt-table POTS criteria, PBMC mitochondrial OCR/ECAR (basal extracellular flux), body composition, resting energy expenditure, plasma lipidomics, muscle fiber type (type I:II ratio), NK cell lytic units in blood

### Longitudinal follow-up
- Within four years: **4 of 17 PI-ME/CFS participants had spontaneous full recovery** (~24%); no new explanatory diagnoses emerged in the rest

## Relevance

This paper is the single deepest multi-system reference phenotype for post-infectious ME/CFS. Its relevance to this project operates on several levels:

**For `hypothesis:0001-shared-dysregulated-attractor`:** Walitt2024 provides the most granular characterization of one node in the putative attractor — PI-ME/CFS triggered by non-COVID infection. The hypothetical mechanism diagram (Fig. 10) is directly structured as a cascade of mutually reinforcing loops (infection → immune dysfunction / microbiome → CNS catecholamine depletion → autonomic dysfunction → reduced activity → deconditioning → functional disability), which maps closely to the attractor framework. However, the study is single-trigger (pre-COVID, diverse non-SARS infections) and cannot itself establish cross-trigger molecular convergence. It contributes a detailed biological description of what the attractor "looks like" from one entry point.

**For `question:0001-shared-molecular-signature-across-triggers`:** Walitt2024 supplies the highest-resolution single-trigger reference dataset in the project. The immune signature (naive B-cell expansion, PD-1+ CSF T-cells, sex-dimorphic PBMC transcriptomics, CSF catecholamine depletion, tryptophan metabolite reduction) defines candidate features to check for cross-trigger replication. The absence of a uniform autoantibody signal, and the absence of T-cell exhaustion in peripheral blood (only CSF), are equally important constraints. The extreme sex-dimorphism of gene expression is a major complication for cross-trigger harmonization: studies that do not stratify by sex may wash out real signals or produce spurious ones.

**For `question:0011-mitochondrial-basis-of-pem`:** Walitt2024 explicitly finds **no difference in basal mitochondrial function** (extracellular flux of PBMCs) and no difference in resting energy expenditure — arguing against a resting-state mitochondrial defect. The reduced VO2 peak and exercise intolerance are attributed primarily to deconditioning and chronotropic incompetence (autonomic), not to a primary mitochondrial failure. The null on lipidomics and muscle fiber composition further supports absence of classical mitochondrial myopathy. This is in tension with Che2025 (exercise-provoked metabolic/mitochondrial shift) and Bateman2023; the resolution may lie in whether the provoked vs. resting state is measured.

**For the t001 cross-pathogen track:** Walitt2024 provides the "single-trigger deep-phenotyping anchor" — establishing what PI-ME/CFS looks like at the highest resolution before comparing across triggers. The NIH cohort is pre-COVID, adjudicated, and uses diverse triggers (not all infection-specific triggers are documented in the paper but EBV, enterovirus, and other common ME/CFS precipitants are the expected distribution). This is not a head-to-head cross-trigger study, but it sets the reference specification.

## Limitations

1. **Very small N (n = 17 PI-ME/CFS).** The most important single limitation. Only large effects (d ≥ 0.94) were detectable at 80% power. Effect sizes may be inflated. Many subgroup analyses (sex-stratified, CPET subset n = 8/9) are severely underpowered. The study is appropriately framed as exploratory and hypothesis-generating, but should not be treated as confirmatory.

2. **Cross-sectional design.** Cannot establish causality or temporal ordering of any of the measured alterations. The proposed cascade in Fig. 10 is hypothetical and mechanistically unverified.

3. **Single trigger type (pre-COVID, non-SARS-CoV-2).** The cohort reflects classical ME/CFS triggers. SARS-CoV-2 / long COVID is not represented. Whether findings transfer to COVID-triggered PI-ME/CFS is not established.

4. **"Effort preference" interpretation is controversial.** Framing the hallmark motor finding as "effort preference" rather than "fatigue" has generated significant commentary in the ME/CFS patient and research community. Critics argue the term may imply volitional avoidance, mislabeling an involuntary physiological state as a motivational one. The study design does not rule out that effort-preference behavior is itself downstream of physiological energy limitation (e.g., interoceptive sensing of impending PEM). Authors explicitly note this was not a behavioral finding implying willful avoidance, but the terminology is contested. This must be represented precisely and neutrally in any citation.

5. **No pre-infection baselines.** Baseline biology before ME/CFS onset is unavailable; directionality (e.g., whether autonomic differences preceded or followed the illness) is inferred, not measured.

6. **Misattribution residual.** Even with rigorous adjudication, 20% of included PI-ME/CFS participants were later reclassified during follow-up — suggesting some diagnostic impurity remains.

7. **Performance validity vs. pathophysiology.** The use of performance validity testing to rule out non-cognitive factors is a strength for internal validity, but cannot fully separate symptom-consistency from objective impairment.

8. **No validated biomarker.** Despite 50+ measures, no single biomarker was identified with diagnostic specificity. Most findings are group-level differences, not individual-level classifiers.

9. **Age, sex, and BMI matched but not individually paired.** Group-level matching does not guarantee within-subject confound removal.

10. **CSF catecholamine comparison without exercise provocation.** Measured at rest; whether the pattern worsens post-exertion (relevant to PEM) was not tested.

## Follow-up

- The Map ME/CFS dataset (mapmecfs.org) is the highest-priority external resource to probe for cross-trigger harmonization analyses; it contains the richest available single-cohort PI-ME/CFS multi-omics.
- The "effort preference vs. fatigue" framing warrants reading the primary critiques and author responses published after this paper (Nature Communications; also see patient/research community commentary) before using this terminology in cross-trigger synthesis.
- CSF catecholamine findings should be compared to the long-COVID catecholamine depletion reports cited in the paper (Dunlop group, 2022-2023); if those findings replicate across SARS-CoV-2 and non-COVID ME/CFS triggers, it becomes a candidate cross-trigger convergent signal for `question:0001`.
- The sex-dimorphic immune signature (B-cell in females; T-cell/NF-kB in males) is directly relevant to `question:0007-mechanism-of-female-predominance-in-pais` and should be cross-referenced with the Klein2023 long-COVID immune signature.
- Four spontaneous recoveries in ~4 years (24%) provide a natural longitudinal comparison arm; if the Map ME/CFS dataset includes follow-up omics on recovered individuals, those samples are uniquely valuable for identifying attractor-exit signatures.
- The null on basal mitochondrial function, combined with the reduced VO2 peak attributed to deconditioning + chronotropic incompetence, is a direct empirical challenge to resting-state bioenergetic-deficit theories of PEM; compare with Che2025's exercise-provoked findings (`question:0011`).
