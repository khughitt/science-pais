# Do Long COVID and COVID Vaccine Side Effects Share Pathophysiological Picture and Biochemical Pathways?

- **Authors:** Jean-François Lesgards, Dominique Cerdan, Christian Perronne
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal:** International Journal of Molecular Sciences
- **Volume/Article:** 26, 7879
- **DOI:** 10.3390/ijms26167879
- **BibTeX key:** Lesgards2025
- **Source:** Full-text PDF (papers/pdfs/2025_Lesgards_long-covid-vaccine-side-effects-shared-pathways.pdf), read 2026-06-20.

## Key Contribution

This narrative review proposes that long COVID (post-acute sequelae of SARS-CoV-2 infection, PASC) and post-COVID-19 vaccination syndrome (PCVS/PACVS) share overlapping clinical manifestations and, potentially, a common mechanistic basis rooted in spike-protein-mediated dysregulation of three immuno-inflammatory cascades: the renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system (RAAS), the kininogen–kinin–kallikrein system (KKS), and the lectin complement pathway. The central hypothesis is that the spike protein — whether viral or vaccine-derived — is the "common toxicant" that triggers these three pathways and thereby explains symptom overlap across both conditions. The authors also provide a broad clinical synthesis of long COVID predictors, multi-organ pathophysiology, and proposed therapeutic targets (RAAS/KKS/complement blockade, antioxidants, anti-coagulants, early anti-inflammatory treatment).

**Scope note:** Long COVID is the primary in-scope subject for this project; PCVS is a boundary case (post-vaccination, not post-infection). The shared-pathway thesis is the bridge between the two; the PCVS evidence base is substantially weaker and more contested than the long COVID evidence base (see Limitations).

## Methods

- **Study design:** Narrative review (non-systematic). No registered protocol, no formal PRISMA-type literature search. The authors synthesized literature selectively, drawing on approximately 296 references.
- **Case definitions used:** Long COVID as PASC (post-acute phase: symptoms >3 weeks; chronic phase: >12 weeks post-infection). PCVS/PACVS defined as delayed-onset (days to months) conditions following mRNA or adenoviral vector COVID-19 vaccines; acute vaccine syndromes (anaphylaxis, etc.) explicitly excluded.
- **Long COVID mechanisms covered:** viral persistence, endothelial dysfunction and unrepaired tissue damage, autoimmunity and immune dysregulation (T-cell exhaustion, pro-inflammatory monocytes, autoantibodies), HLA variability and dormant virus reactivation (EBV, HHV-6), gut microbiome dysbiosis, and proposed retrotranscription of viral RNA (last considered very unlikely by the authors themselves).
- **Three shared-pathway framework:** RAAS (ACE2 downregulation → AngII/AT1R overactivation → vasoconstriction, fibrosis, inflammation), KKS (ACE2 downregulation → accumulation of Des-Arg9-bradykinin [DABK] via B1R → pulmonary inflammation and vascular permeability), and lectin complement (MBL/MASP-2 binding to high-mannose glycans on spike protein → C3/C5 activation → microangiopathy, thrombosis, MAC formation). Oxidative stress is described as both a driver and amplifier of all three pathways.
- **Therapeutic proposals:** Blockade of RAAS (telmisartan/AT1R antagonists), KKS (C1 inhibitors, bradykinin receptor antagonists), and complement; antioxidant therapy (Pycnogenol, NRF2 activation, glutathione); early anti-inflammatory and anticoagulant treatment during COVID-19 to prevent long COVID. These are framed as hypotheses for future clinical trials, not as established recommendations.
- **Funding:** Association BonsSens.org (covered publication fees). BonsSens.org is a French vaccine-sceptic advocacy organisation; this association should be noted as a potential source of framing bias, particularly in sections comparing long COVID to vaccine injury.

## Key Findings

### Long COVID epidemiology and clinical picture (well-documented)
- Estimated ~30–50% of COVID-19 cases develop long COVID symptoms; ~18 million US adults affected; ~36 million in Europe during first 3 pandemic years.
- Over 200 documented symptoms spanning nervous, cardiovascular, respiratory, immune, endocrine, renal, and musculoskeletal systems.
- Meta-analysis of 442 studies: pooled prevalence highest in South America (~51%), Europe (~39%), Asia (~35%), North America (~30%).
- Most common symptoms: fatigue (23%, 95% CI 17–30%), memory difficulties (14%), dyspnea (13%), sleep disturbance (11%), arthralgia (10%).
- Key risk predictors (from Arjun et al. 2022, n=371): symptom count during acute phase (aOR=11.24), two vaccine doses prior to infection (aOR=2.32), initial severity (aOR=5.71), hospitalisation (OR=3.89). [Note: The aOR=2.32 for prior vaccination increasing long COVID risk is a contested finding reported by the authors; it contradicts the broader literature showing vaccination reduces long COVID risk overall. Authors use it to suggest a link between vaccine spike and long COVID, which is a selective citation.]

### Proposed shared pathways (mechanistic synthesis; evidence varies widely)
1. **RAAS dysregulation:** Spike protein binds and downregulates ACE2, shifting the ACE2/Ang-(1-7)/MasR protective axis toward ACE/AngII/AT1R pro-inflammatory signalling. AT1R overactivation drives vasoconstriction, oxidative stress, cardiac hypertrophy, fibrosis, and neurological dysfunction. Evidence for this in long COVID is substantial; evidence for vaccine-induced RAAS disruption is largely extrapolated from the shared spike-protein biochemistry [SPECULATION for vaccine arm beyond in vitro data].

2. **KKS activation (des-Arg9-bradykinin / B1R axis):** ACE2 depletion impairs degradation of DABK (bradykinin metabolite), causing sustained B1R activation → cytokine release (CXCL5), neutrophil infiltration, vascular permeability, lung inflammation. A proteomics study (n=outpatients) identified 119 proteins associated with long COVID (Bonferroni p<0.01), with CXCL5 among the most elevated; authors interpret this as consistent with KKS dysregulation. A murine K18-hACE2 model showed elevated B1R expression drives neuroinflammation and cognitive symptoms. Vaccine applicability is proposed by analogy to spike-protein ACE2 binding [SPECULATION for vaccine arm].

3. **Lectin complement pathway:** Spike glycoprotein high-mannose glycans are recognised by MBL/MASP-2, activating the lectin pathway (C4, C2 → C3 convertase → C3a/C5a anaphylatoxins, MAC formation). A longitudinal proteomic study (>6500 proteins, n=268 samples, published in *Science*) found sustained complement activation persisting ≥12 months in long COVID, normalising only in patients with clinical resolution. Marked reduction in MBL and attenuated lectin pathway activity was found specifically in brain-fog-positive patients. Elevated MASP-2 correlated with terminal complement complex (ρ=0.3596, p<0.0001). Post-vaccination, VITT (vaccine-induced immune thrombocytopenia) activates complement indirectly via immune complexes. Direct lectin pathway activation by vaccine-derived spike proteins is proposed by analogy [SPECULATION for vaccine arm].

### Cardiovascular and neurological overlap
- Both long COVID and PCVS show myocarditis, pericarditis, thrombosis, dysrhythmias, and ischemic events (Table 1 in paper).
- Vaccine-associated myocarditis risk is primarily in males 12–30 years, 1–14 days post second mRNA dose; risk is dose-interval dependent (shorter intervals → higher rates: 52.1 vs 9.6 per 1,000,000 doses at <30 days vs >56 days apart for BNT162b2).
- Fibrin amyloid microclots (fibrinolysis-resistant) documented in long COVID and proposed as a diagnostic/therapeutic target; also described after AstraZeneca vaccination in VITT context.
- Neurological: transverse myelitis, Guillain-Barré, venous sinus thrombosis, cognitive impairment documented in both conditions. A comparative analysis of 65 cases found no substantial differences in TM latency, imaging, or recovery profiles between post-infection and post-vaccination cases.

### Oxidative stress
- Reduced plasma thiols (<154 µmol/L) predict ICU admission (sensitivity 79.7%, specificity 64.6%).
- Persistent ROS production is linked to microthrombi, neuroinflammation, and autoantibody stimulation. Pycnogenol supplementation showed improvement in oxidative stress biomarkers and Karnofsky Performance Scale Index in a preliminary investigation [UNVERIFIED — study size and design not specified].

### Post-COVID vaccination syndrome quantification
- 3–5% of vaccine recipients experience symptoms >1 week; 0.2–1.4% >1 month (from cited sources).
- Global adverse events via VigiBase: ~1278 AEs per million doses globally, with wide regional variation likely reflecting surveillance differences.
- Authors note VAERS captures ~10% of actual events — this is a standard pharmacovigilance caveat but is used in a framing that implies substantial underestimation of vaccine harm.

## Relevance

**hypothesis:0001-shared-dysregulated-attractor:** The RAAS/KKS/lectin-complement framework directly supports the shared-failure-mode framing — these three cascades could represent convergent downstream nodes that, once dysregulated, sustain chronic inflammation regardless of whether the initial trigger was infection or (possibly) vaccine spike. However, the evidence is substantially stronger for the infection (long COVID) arm than for the vaccination arm. The hypothesis that vaccine-derived spike protein is a sufficient trigger for PCVS pathology comparable to PASC pathology is mechanistically plausible but not yet experimentally confirmed in controlled human studies.

**question:0001-shared-molecular-signature-across-triggers:** The review's comparison table (Table 1) mapping overlapping symptoms between long COVID and PCVS is directly relevant. The RAAS/KKS/complement triad is a candidate shared signature, but controlled comparative data are absent.

**question:0009-functional-autoantibodies-drive-dysautonomia:** The review notes elevated autoantibodies against ACE2, β2-adrenergic receptors, muscarinic M2 acetylcholine receptors, AT1R, and the Mas receptor in long COVID cohorts. These autoantibodies are directly relevant to autonomic dysfunction. The review does not report equivalent autoantibody data from PCVS cohorts.

**topic:thromboinflammation-and-endothelial-dysfunction:** Strong correspondence. Microclots, VITT, endotheliitis, sustained complement activation, and RAAS-driven coagulopathy are all reviewed with supporting citations.

**topic:post-infectious-dysautonomia-and-autoimmunity:** Autoantibody findings and vagus nerve dysfunction section are relevant; the review frames neurological involvement through spike/ACE2 interaction and RAAS/KKS downstream effects on the CNS.

**topic:shared-failure-mode-across-pais:** The review's core thesis maps cleanly to this framing but is restricted to the COVID/SARS-CoV-2 context (no cross-pathogen comparison to other PAIS).

## Limitations

**Review design and evidence standards:**
- This is a narrative (non-systematic) review with no registered protocol, no formal search strategy, and no quality-of-evidence rating. Citation selection is not transparent and the framing exhibits notable advocacy for viewing vaccine side effects as comparable in severity to long COVID, which reflects both the authors' stated scientific position and the funding source (BonsSens.org).
- The RAAS/KKS/complement triad is mechanistically plausible and supported by primary literature for long COVID; its extension to PCVS rests almost entirely on the assumption that vaccine-derived spike protein behaves identically to viral spike protein in vivo over chronic timescales — this assumption is not directly tested in this paper.

**PCVS evidence base is qualitatively different:**
- PCVS/PACVS lacks the equivalent depth of controlled longitudinal cohort studies that exist for long COVID. Most PCVS data cited are case series, case reports, pharmacovigilance databases (VAERS, VigiBase), and cross-sectional self-report. No randomised comparison of long COVID vs. PCVS biomarkers is presented.
- The claim that vaccine-derived free spike protein reaches levels comparable to SARS-CoV-2 infection (up to 150 pg/mL) and persists for weeks is cited from a small number of studies; these findings have not been widely replicated and their clinical significance for PCVS pathogenesis is uncertain [UNVERIFIED at this level of certainty].

**Selective and contested citations:**
- The aOR=2.32 finding (two prior vaccine doses increasing long COVID risk) is cited without noting that the broader meta-analytic consensus is that vaccination reduces long COVID risk. This is not addressed.
- VAERS and VigiBase data are used to imply underreporting of vaccine harm; the well-known confounding (self-reported, unverified, not age/sex-standardised) is not adequately caveated.
- The funding source (BonsSens.org) is a vaccine-critical advocacy group; this may influence the framing of vaccine-injury evidence in ways that are not solely scientific.

**Case definition heterogeneity:**
- Neither PASC nor PCVS is defined by validated biomarkers or adjudicated case definitions in the cited studies. The symptom overlap table (Table 1) aggregates references without controlling for case ascertainment, time post-exposure, severity, or comorbidities. Apparent overlap in symptom lists does not prove overlap in mechanism.

**No original data:**
- No new clinical, epidemiological, or experimental data are presented. Mechanistic claims (particularly for PCVS) are entirely synthesised from existing literature; primary evidence for vaccine-specific RAAS/KKS/complement activation is indirect.

**Post-vaccination scope boundary for this project:**
- The post-COVID-19 vaccination syndrome (PCVS/PACVS) arm falls outside the core definition of PAIS (post-acute infection syndromes) as used in this project. The shared-pathway thesis is of interest precisely because it might reveal mechanisms that transcend the post-infection frame, but this project's scope-boundaries.md does not currently adjudicate post-vaccination sequelae as in-scope. The long COVID sections of this review are unambiguously in-scope.

## Follow-up

**Immediate:**
- The scope-boundaries.md for this project should be reviewed to adjudicate whether post-COVID-19 vaccination syndrome (PCVS/PACVS) is in-scope as a boundary phenotype, out-of-scope, or handled as "read-across only." The Lesgards review is a natural trigger for this decision: it is the first paper in this project that systematically frames vaccine side effects alongside infection-driven PAIS as sharing mechanisms. Until adjudicated, PCVS content from this paper should be treated as read-across evidence for mechanism, not as a primary subject of synthesis.

**Related papers to read:**
- Lesgards et al. (2023) — the prior paper by the same group on spike protein toxicity (reference [7] in this review; *Arch. Microbiol. Immunol.* 2023, 7, 121–138) is the primary source for the RAAS/KKS/complement framework used here; that paper should be assessed for the original evidence base.
- Scholkmann et al. (2023) — "COVID-19, post-acute COVID-19 syndrome (PACS, 'long COVID') and post-COVID-19 vaccination syndrome (PCVS)" (*Pathol. Res. Pract.* 2023, 246, 154497) — the paper's key reference for defining PCVS.
- Baillie et al. (2024) — complement pathway markers in long COVID (n=166 vs. 79 controls) — directly supports the lectin complement section and proposes complement inhibitors for long COVID.
- The *Science* paper on longitudinal complement proteomics in long COVID (reference [241] in this review) should be prioritised as primary evidence.

**Open questions for this project:**
- Are controlled studies available that directly compare RAAS/KKS/complement activation biomarkers in long COVID vs. PCVS cohorts using matched designs? (No such study is cited in this review.)
- Does the autoantibody profile seen in long COVID (anti-ACE2, anti-β2-AR, anti-M2, anti-AT1R) also appear in PCVS, and if so at what frequency? This is directly relevant to question:0009.
- Is the lectin complement arm (MBL/MASP-2) a tractable biomarker for stratifying long COVID subtypes (e.g., brain-fog positive vs. negative)?
