# A Review of Persistent Post-COVID Syndrome (PPCS)

- **Authors:** Bryan Oronsky, Christopher Larson, Terese C. Hammond, Arnold Oronsky, Santosh Kesari, Michelle Lybeck, Tony R. Reid
- **Year:** 2021 (accepted 28 January 2021, published online 20 February 2021; journal volume 64, 2023)
- **Journal:** Clinical Reviews in Allergy & Immunology, vol. 64, pp. 66–74
- **DOI:** 10.1007/s12016-021-08848-3
- **BibTeX key:** Oronsky2021
- **Source:** PDF
- **Conflict of interest:** EpicentRx, Inc. funds research of the AdAPT-001 product; authors B.O., C.L., and T.R. are employed by EpicentRx, Inc. (AdAPT-001 is a TGF-β inhibitor discussed in the paper as a candidate PPCS therapy).

## Key Contribution

This early narrative review coins the umbrella term "persistent post-COVID syndrome" (PPCS) — now widely known as long COVID — and proposes that PPCS arises from the same SIRS-to-CARS immunological arc seen in post-sepsis syndrome, where the compensatory anti-inflammatory response overshoots, producing a protracted state of immunoparalysis and profibrotic signaling. The central mechanistic hypothesis is that TGF-β overexpression serves as a unifying lynchpin linking post-infectious immunosuppression with multi-organ fibrosis (pulmonary, cardiac, neurological, vascular), making TGF-β inhibition a potentially repurposable therapeutic strategy from oncology. The paper also provides a four-domain diagnostic screening framework (laboratory, radiologic, functional status, symptom/QOL) and a six-point management framework for clinicians encountering PPCS patients.

## Methods

Narrative review with no formal systematic search protocol or PRISMA flow reported. The authors draw on analogy with post-sepsis/post-ICU syndrome to build a mechanistic model, citing supporting primary literature on SARS-CoV-2 pathology, ARDS fibrosis, cardiac and neurological injury, coagulopathy, and TGF-β biology. A table of clinical TGF-β inhibitors in active cancer trials is provided as evidence for repurposability (Table 3). Epidemiological prevalence estimates are cited from individual primary studies rather than pooled. No original patient cohort is presented; this is entirely a synthesis and framework paper.

## Key Findings

**Mechanistic framework: SIRS → CARS → PICS**
- After the acute cytokine storm (dominated by IL-1, IL-6, IL-8, IL-17, IL-1β, MCP-1, TNF-α), an overwhelming compensatory anti-inflammatory response syndrome (CARS) is mounted to prevent maladaptive multi-organ dysfunction.
- If CARS overshoots, the patient enters persistent inflammation, immunosuppression, and catabolism syndrome (PICS) — a state analogous to post-sepsis immunoparalysis, characterized by protracted immunosuppression and susceptibility to secondary infections.
- TGF-β is identified as the central mediator: it is elevated during and after both sepsis and COVID-19; it is profibrogenic, anti-inflammatory, and immunosuppressive; histologic specimens from COVID-19 lung autopsies show fibroblastic proliferation and interstitial fibrosis consistent with TGF-β involvement. TGF-β signaling is mediated by Smad proteins or MAP kinases and Akt.
- By analogy with post-sepsis syndrome (~50% of sepsis survivors develop it, labeled "a hidden public health disaster"), PPCS may occur at sufficient scale to overwhelm health care systems.

**Post-COVID manifestations surveyed**

*Pulmonary:* ~5–8% of COVID-19 patients develop ARDS; ARDS pathologic evolution proceeds through exudative → fibroproliferative → fibrotic phases. Mechanical ventilation amplifies fibrosis through shear-force activation of TGF-β1 and collagen synthesis. ARDS survivors may have exercise limitation and reduced QOL for up to 5 years post-ARDS. Two FDA-approved medications (nintedanib and pirfenidone) slow but do not cure pulmonary fibrosis.

*Cardiac:* COVID-19 patients commonly present with myocardial injury (elevated troponin T, BNP). Six mechanisms of injury are cataloged: (1) pulmonary hypertension and right heart failure; (2) RAS overstimulation → secondary hyperaldosteronism → hypokalemia and arrhythmias; (3) atherosclerotic plaque rupture via proinflammatory cytokines; (4) ACE2-mediated viral myocarditis; (5) myocardial O₂ supply/demand mismatch from ARDS; (6) possible cardiotoxicity of anti-COVID drugs (azithromycin QT prolongation, hydroxychloroquine conduction defects, tocilizumab hypercholesterolemia, lopinavir/ritonavir PR/QT prolongation via CYP3A4 inhibition). Extrapolating from SARS-CoV-1, 40% of 12-year follow-up survivors demonstrated cardiovascular abnormalities.

*Neurological:* In a retrospective case series of 214 patients in Wuhan, 36.4% had neurological symptoms: CNS (24.8%), PNS (8.9%), skeletal muscle (10.7%); most common CNS symptoms were dizziness (16.8%) and headache (13.1%). Neuropsychiatric sequelae — depression, anxiety, psychosis — parallel what is seen after sepsis. TGF-β pathway dysregulation is linked to multiple neurological conditions (Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, ALS, multiple sclerosis, depression, schizophrenia), further implicating it as a therapeutic target.

*Coagulopathy:* Severe COVID-19 can produce a DIC-like coagulopathy with fulminant coagulation activation, low platelets, decreased fibrinogen (<1.0 g/L), and thrombotic complications (pulmonary embolism, stroke). Long-term anticoagulation carries hemorrhage risk; thrombotic after-effects include behavioral and emotional alterations.

**Diagnostic framework (Table 1)**
Four screening domains: (1) laboratory (confirmed active/past infection via RT-PCR or antibodies; abnormal labs vs. baseline); (2) radiologic pathology (pulmonary CT/imaging vs. baseline); (3) functional status deterioration vs. baseline; (4) subjective symptom/QOL parameters (new or worsening symptoms >2 weeks past baseline, or re-emergence of symptoms >2 weeks past baseline).

**Therapeutic candidates**
The paper catalogs six TGF-β inhibitors in clinical trials for cancer as candidates for repurposing to PPCS (Table 3): trabedersen (AP12009, antisense oligonucleotide, Phase 2b), belagenpneumatucel-L (Lucanix, Phase 3 failed), fresolimumab/GC1008 (pan-TGF-β monoclonal antibody, Phase 2), galunisertib/LY2157299 (TβRI small molecule, Phase 2), tasisulam/LY573636 (Phase 2), and BETA PRIME/AdAPT-001 (replicating oncolytic adenovirus encoding TGF-β type II receptor, Phase 1; EpicentRx). Other immunomodulatory candidates mentioned for PPCS include GM-CSF, IVIG, IFNγ, IL-7, PD-L1 inhibitors, IL-3, and checkpoint inhibitors.

## Relevance

This paper connects directly to the project research question (research-question:post-acute-infection-syndromes) — "Why do some individuals fail to recover homeostasis after acute infection?" — in several ways:

1. **PPCS as the prototypical PAIS homeostatic failure.** The paper frames PPCS as a failure of the SIRS-CARS balance to return to immunologic normalcy: the CARS overcorrection that was adaptive during the cytokine storm becomes maladaptive when it persists, locking the system into a state of immunoparalysis and fibrotic remodeling that is the defining signature of failed homeostatic recovery.

2. **SIRS/CARS/PICS as a generalizable post-infectious arc.** By grounding the PPCS model in the post-sepsis/post-ICU syndrome literature, the paper implicitly extends the PAIS frame beyond COVID-19: any severe acute infection that elicits a dominant SIRS response could, in principle, drive the same CARS overshoot and PICS state. This supports the project's hypothesis that PAIS represents a shared failure mode across infectious triggers.

3. **TGF-β as a candidate shared mechanistic node.** The proposal that TGF-β is elevated across sepsis and COVID-19 and drives both immunosuppression and multi-organ fibrosis positions this cytokine as a cross-system molecular mediator — potentially relevant to any PAIS with post-infectious fibrotic or immunosuppressive sequelae. Therapeutic inhibition of TGF-β may be a cross-PAIS strategy worth tracking computationally.

4. **Organ-system breadth of PPCS.** The paper catalogs pulmonary, cardiac, neurological, and vascular sequelae under a single mechanistic umbrella, consistent with the project's multi-system homeostasis framing and its interest in shared failure modes that manifest differently across organ systems.

5. **Diagnostic gap highlighted.** The paper explicitly notes that no pathognomonic test exists for PPCS, which parallels the diagnostic gap the project's computational analysis ambitions aim to address for PAIS more broadly.

**Note on COI:** Three authors are employed by EpicentRx, which is developing AdAPT-001 (listed in Table 3 as a PPCS candidate). The broader TGF-β mechanistic framing is supported by independent evidence, but the specific emphasis on TGF-β inhibition as a therapeutic strategy should be read with this COI in mind.

## Limitations

- **COI:** Three of seven authors are employed by EpicentRx, Inc., which is developing a TGF-β-targeting therapy (AdAPT-001) described in the paper. The emphasis on TGF-β as a unifying mechanism and repurposing candidate should be weighted accordingly.
- **No original data.** This is a narrative review written in early 2021 (accepted January 2021), before systematic longitudinal PPCS cohort data were available; prevalence and mechanism claims rest on very early observational reports, some from news sources and pre-prints.
- **Mechanism is mechanistic hypothesis, not established causality.** The SIRS → CARS → PICS → PPCS causal chain is plausible by analogy with post-sepsis but is not validated in COVID-19–specific controlled studies.
- **Temporal limitations.** Published at pandemic onset; does not account for variant-specific differences, vaccination effects, or the large prospective cohort studies published from 2022 onward.
- **No control for severity.** The paper focuses heavily on ICU/ARDS patients and may not generalize to the large population of PPCS cases following mild or moderate acute COVID-19 — a population now known to comprise the majority of long COVID cases.
- **TGF-β specificity.** TGF-β has pleiotropic roles; inhibiting it carries risks (e.g., immune dysregulation, carcinogenesis) that are not discussed.
- **Extrapolation from SARS-CoV-1 to SARS-CoV-2.** The 40% cardiovascular abnormality figure at 12 years comes from SARS-CoV-1 cohorts and may not transfer.

- Evaluate whether the SIRS/CARS/PICS conceptual model applies to non-COVID PAIS (ME/CFS, post-Q-fever, PTLDS): does the post-sepsis analogy hold, and is PICS documented in mild-acute-infection PAIS?
- Track TGF-β inhibitor clinical progress (galunisertib, fresolimumab, AdAPT-001) specifically in PPCS/long-COVID settings — Table 3 entries are Phase 1–2 in oncology; any PPCS-specific trials would be directly relevant.
- Examine thromboinflammation overlap with Nicolai 2023 (`paper:Nicolai2023`) — DIC-like coagulopathy described here as a PPCS driver maps directly onto that paper's thromboinflammation frame.
- Assess whether immunomodulatory agents tried in post-sepsis with "mixed results" (GM-CSF, IVIG, IFNγ, IL-7, PD-L1 inhibitors) have been tested in long COVID cohorts.
