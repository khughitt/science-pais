## Key Contribution

This prospective, single-center study of 4,882 patients — all hospitalized with moderate-to-severe SARS-CoV-2 pneumonia — reports 18-month cardiovascular (CV) outcomes stratified by sex and COVID-19 variant (Alpha, Delta, Omicron). The central finding is that male sex was an independent predictor of 18-month CV death specifically in the Delta wave (HR 1.676, 95% CI 1.005–2.796, p = 0.048), after multivariable adjustment for age, BMI, and comorbidities. Because enrollment was restricted to hospitalized patients with moderate-to-severe disease throughout, this study provides evidence that the male-biased post-COVID CV mortality signal persists within a severity-restricted stratum and is not entirely a product of men presenting with more severe acute illness on average.

## Methods

**Study design:** Prospective, non-randomized, single-center observational cohort. Enrollment: COVID-Hospital of Bashkir State Medical University (Bashkir State, Russian Federation), 16 April 2020 – 17 March 2022.

**Eligibility / severity restriction:** All enrolled patients were hospitalized with moderate-to-severe SARS-CoV-2-associated pneumonia confirmed by PCR or CT chest imaging (ground-glass opacity / crazy-paving pattern). This enrollment filter constitutes a severity-restricted stratum: all participants had clinically significant acute disease warranting inpatient treatment. No separate within-cohort severity subgroup was analyzed (e.g., ICU vs. floor), so residual severity heterogeneity within the hospitalized tier is not adjusted for in the Cox models.

**Cohort sizes:**
- Total enrolled: n = 4,882
- Total analyzed (post-exclusion): n = 4,509
- Exclusions: 373 patients (9 missing data; 364 died before discharge: 146 Alpha, 107 Delta, 111 Omicron)
- Alpha wave (16 Apr 2020 – 31 Dec 2020): n = 2,275
- Delta wave (1 Jan 2021 – 31 Dec 2021): n = 1,721
- Omicron wave (1 Jan 2022 – 9 Mar 2022): n = 886

**Sex distribution:** 3,032 women, 1,850 men in total; women substantially outnumbered men in all three waves (Alpha: 57.1% women; Delta: 65.9% women; Omicron: 76.7% women). The basis for this sex imbalance is not explained in the paper.

**Outcomes:**
- Primary: CV death at 18 months post-discharge.
- Secondary: CV hospitalization, myocardial infarction (MI), stroke, pulmonary embolism, and a combined CV endpoint (CV death + CV hospitalization + MI + stroke + pulmonary embolism).

**Follow-up:** 18 months via the "ProMed" remote data capture system, supplemented by phone contact and ambulatory/inpatient visits.

**Statistics:**
- Survival analysis: Multiple Kaplan–Meier method; Gehan–Wilcoxon test for sex comparisons within each wave (p < 0.05 threshold).
- Multivariable modeling: Cox proportional hazards models built separately per wave, with covariates: age, sex, BMI, and concomitant conditions (CHD, arterial hypertension, diabetes mellitus, CKD, prior MI, heart failure, prior stroke, COPD).
- Vaccination status: not recorded; acknowledged as a limitation.

## Key Findings

### Overall CV event rates (all patients, across waves)

- CV death at 18 months: 2.48% overall; Delta significantly higher (4.47%) vs. Alpha (1.76%) and Omicron (0.45%), p < 0.001.
- Combined CV endpoint: 10.1% overall; Delta highest (14.6%) vs. Alpha (7.17%) and Omicron (9.93%), p < 0.001.

### Sex differences by wave — Delta wave (load-bearing)

- **CV mortality (primary endpoint):** Men 6.13% vs. women 3.62%, p = 0.017.
- **Combined CV endpoint:** Men 16.87% vs. women 12.61%, p = 0.017.
- CV hospitalization: Men 11.75% vs. women 9.35% (not significant by sex).
- MI, stroke, pulmonary embolism: No significant sex difference.

### Sex differences by wave — Alpha wave

- No significant differences by sex in CV mortality or any CV endpoint.

### Sex differences by wave — Omicron wave

- No significant differences by sex. CV mortality was extremely low: 0 men died vs. 4 women (0.67%), difference non-significant.

### Cox multivariable model — Delta wave

- **Male sex: HR 1.676 (95% CI 1.005–2.796), p = 0.048** — significant independent predictor of 18-month CV death.
- Age: HR 1.085 (95% CI 1.059–1.111), p < 0.001 — also significant in Delta.
- BMI: Not significant in Delta (was significant only in Alpha: HR 0.905, 95% CI 0.863–0.949, p < 0.001).
- Likelihood ratio test for Delta model: LR = 79.49, p < 0.001.

### Cox multivariable model — Alpha wave

- Age: HR 1.048 (95% CI 1.018–1.081), p = 0.002.
- Male sex: Not a significant predictor in Alpha.
- BMI: HR 0.905 (95% CI 0.863–0.949), p < 0.001 (significant and protective).

### Cox multivariable model — Omicron wave

- Only 4 CV deaths in 18 months; model under-powered (LR = 9.51, p = 0.09).
- Prior MI: HR 17.715 (95% CI 1.138–275.697), p = 0.040 — sole significant predictor, but CI is very wide.

## Relevance

This paper is directly load-bearing for the project's working claim that the male post-COVID cardiovascular mortality excess is not entirely attributable to more severe initial presentation in men. The entire enrolled cohort had moderate-to-severe hospitalized disease, so severity confounding at the hospitalized/non-hospitalized boundary is excluded by design. The male HR of 1.676 for Delta-wave CV death survives adjustment for age, BMI, and standard comorbidities within this already-severity-restricted sample.

**Hypothesis links:**

- `hypothesis:0005-reproductive-stage-immune-homeostatic-margin` — the wave-heterogeneous sex effect (Delta significant; Alpha and Omicron not) suggests that the male CV mortality excess is not a fixed biological constant but may interact with variant-specific pathobiology, immune pressure, or vaccination-era context. This is consistent with a sex-hormone or immune-homeostatic mechanism that is modulated by the viral phenotype rather than sex alone.
- `hypothesis:0004-acute-severity-threshold` — the hospitalized restriction controls for the coarser acute-severity axis; the residual male signal within the hospitalized stratum implies the male cardiovascular vulnerability is not simply "men get sicker acutely." However, within-hospitalized severity heterogeneity (ICU vs. floor) is uncontrolled.
- `question:0007-mechanism-of-female-predominance-in-pais` — this paper records a male (not female) CV mortality excess, which is directionally opposite to the female predominance in PAIS fatigue. These two signals may reflect distinct pathophysiological modes: CV thromboinflammatory sequelae (male-biased) vs. neuroimmune/fatigue sequelae (female-biased).

## Limitations

1. **No within-cohort severity adjustment:** ICU admission, mechanical ventilation, oxygen requirement, and clinical severity scores are not reported or adjusted for. The hospitalized restriction controls the hospitalized/non-hospitalized split, but within-hospitalized severity variation remains a potential confounder if men were systematically sicker among the hospitalized.

2. **Survivor-selection bias:** 364 patients who died before hospital discharge were excluded from the 18-month follow-up analysis. If in-hospital death is male-enriched (consistent with the broader literature), the analyzed male survivors may represent a selected subgroup, potentially *attenuating* the true long-term male CV excess relative to an intent-to-treat analysis.

3. **Vaccination status not recorded:** Vaccination was rolled out during the Delta and Omicron enrollment windows and could confound sex differences (if women were more frequently vaccinated or had differing vaccine responses). The authors acknowledge this as a limitation.

4. **Wave-heterogeneous sex effect without causal explanation:** The sex difference is confined to Delta (significant) and absent in Alpha and Omicron. The paper does not offer a mechanism. Plausible explanations include: Delta-specific virulence/tropism, cohort composition differences (the comorbidity profile varied significantly by wave and sex), or vaccination era effects.

5. **Single-center, single country (Russia):** Generalizability to other healthcare systems, ethnic compositions, or treatment-era protocols is uncertain. Vaccination access and timing in Russia may differ substantially from Western cohorts.

6. **Sex imbalance:** Women outnumbered men 3:2 overall and increasingly so by wave (76.7% women in Omicron). The basis for this is unexplained; if it reflects systematic differences in who was admitted, the comparisons may not be representative of the hospitalized population generally.

7. **Combined CV endpoint interpretation:** The significant sex difference in the combined CV endpoint (p = 0.017) is noted to be "likely driven by the significant mortality incidence in men" — meaning the individual component endpoints other than CV death are not independently significant between sexes.

8. **Omicron model under-powered:** Only 4 CV deaths in the Omicron cohort; the Cox model results are unreliable for that wave.

## Follow-up

- Compare with population-based (non-hospitalized-restricted) sex-stratified CV mortality studies to determine whether the male excess in CV death is larger or smaller when severity is not controlled — a severity-dose-response sex comparison.
- Examine whether the Delta-specific sex effect reproduces in other Delta-wave hospitalized cohorts, or whether it is cohort/geography-specific.
- Consider whether the exclusion of in-hospital deaths (male-enriched) induces survivor bias that attenuates the male long-term CV signal; an intent-to-treat analysis including in-hospital deaths would provide an upper bound.
- Cross-reference with `question:0007` — the directional male CV excess here is a different signal from the female PAIS-fatigue excess; do they arise from separable mechanisms (thromboinflammatory vs. neuroimmune) or from a shared dysregulated attractor with sex-dependent phenotypic expression?
