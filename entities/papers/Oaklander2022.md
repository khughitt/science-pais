# Peripheral Neuropathy Evaluations of Patients With Prolonged Long COVID

- **Authors:** Anne Louise Oaklander, Alexander J. Mills, Mary Kelley, Lisa S. Toran, Bryan Smith, Marinos C. Dalakas, Avindra Nath
- **Year:** 2022
- **Journal:** Neurology: Neuroimmunology & Neuroinflammation, vol. 9, no. 3, e1146
- **DOI:** 10.1212/NXI.0000000000001146
- **PMID:** 35232750
- **PMCID:** PMC8889896
- **BibTeX key:** Oaklander2022
- **Source:** Europe PMC full text (XML)

## Key Contribution

This paper is the earliest prospective cross-sectional-plus-longitudinal evaluation of peripheral
neuropathy in a referred cohort of patients meeting WHO long COVID criteria. It demonstrates that
small-fiber neuropathy (SFN) — confirmed by skin biopsy — is the single most common objective
abnormality among evaluated patients, occurring after predominantly mild SARS-CoV-2 infection and
following a prolonged, often disabling course. Symptom onset was rapid (within 1 month of COVID-19
onset), and apparent benefit from immunotherapy (IVIg, corticosteroids) in most treated patients
points toward dysimmune causation, though no definitive immune mechanism is established.

The paper is a small clinical note (n = 17) from a multi-site US referral series, not a population
study, and its primary contribution is establishing the clinical phenotype and providing rationale
for larger investigations. It is a key seeding reference for
`hypothesis:0007-autoimmune-sfn-peripheral-dysautonomia-substrate`.

## Methods

- **Design:** Retrospective analysis of a prospectively enrolled referral cohort ("retrospective
  analysis" per the paper, though participants were enrolled prospectively before record review or
  most testing and treatment). Cross-sectional + longitudinal follow-up (mean 1.4 years).
- **Sample:** 17 patients with SARS-CoV-2 onset between 21 Feb 2020 and 19 Jan 2021, treated
  across 10 US states/territories. Mean age 43.3 ± 3.3 years; 68.8% female; 94.1% Caucasian;
  18.8% Latino.
- **Case definition (long COVID):** WHO Delphi consensus definition — symptom onset within 90 days
  of first day of COVID symptoms, lasting >2 months. SARS-CoV-2 infection confirmed per WHO
  guidelines. COVID severity classified per WHO guidelines; 16/17 had mild COVID.
- **Inclusion/exclusion:** No prior neuropathy history or recognized neuropathy risk factors. No
  evidence of systemic dysimmunity on screening blood work. Brain/spine imaging (when done) was
  unrevealing.

### Skin Biopsy (IENFD) — CRITICAL METHODOLOGICAL DETAIL

**Both lower leg (distal) AND upper thigh (proximal) punch biopsies were performed** — a
paired-site design that allows detection of non-length-dependent patterns. This is explicitly
stated: "62.5% (10/16) of lower leg skin biopsies pathologically confirmed SFN, as corroborated
by 50% of upper thigh biopsies." The proximal-site biopsy rate matches the distal-site rate
closely (50% vs 63%), indicating that proximal involvement was common — consistent with a
**non-length-dependent** distribution. The paper does not state IENFD thresholds or quantitative
density values per site; it reports the biopsy as SFN-confirmatory, using skin biopsy per
established neuropathology standards (staining methodology not specified in this brief report).

- n = 16 lower-leg biopsies; 10/16 (62.5%) confirmed SFN
- n paired upper-thigh biopsies (exact n not stated; 50% abnormal)

### Non-Length-Dependent Pattern

The paper does not provide formal proportions of non-length-dependent vs. length-dependent SFN
per patient (i.e., no per-patient dichotomization is reported in this brief clinical note). The
inference rests on the fact that proximal-thigh biopsy yield (~50%) approached the distal-leg
yield (62.5%), suggesting most patients with distal SFN also had proximal involvement — a
pattern distinct from classic dying-back (length-dependent) neuropathy in which distal IENFD
reduction would substantially exceed proximal. The paper does not explicitly characterize this
contrast as "non-length-dependent" by name, but the dual-site biopsy design was evidently
chosen to demonstrate this pattern.

### Other Diagnostic Tests

- **Electrodiagnostics (large-fiber):** 12 patients; 2/12 (16.7%) abnormal — one critical illness
  sensorimotor polyneuropathy (patient 9, severe COVID), one multifocal demyelinating neuropathy
  (patient 15, 3 weeks post-mild COVID).
- **Autonomic function tests:** 8 patients; 4/8 (50%) abnormal.
- **Autoimmune serology screened:** ANA, ESR, IgG anti-SS-A/SS-B, complement C3 and C4 (the
  "most productive markers of dysimmunity in initially idiopathic SFN" per Ref 7). No Sjögren
  syndrome detected; other inflammatory markers "only occasionally elevated."
- No functional GPCR autoantibodies (anti-β-adrenergic, anti-muscarinic) were measured.
  No specific anti-ganglioside, anti-trisulfatide, or other neuropathy-associated antibodies
  are reported.

### Treatment and Follow-up

- 11/17 (65%) received immunotherapy: corticosteroids 6/17 (35.3%); IVIg 6/17 (35.3%).
- IVIg dose: 5 patients at 2.0 g/kg/4 weeks, 1 at 1.6 g/kg/4 weeks.
- Patients' self-reported recovery: average 51.8 ± 6.7% improvement; none reported complete
  resolution.
- Those receiving sustained IVIg reported benefit; one on prednisone reported 90% improvement.

## Key Findings

### Headline Fractions

| Test | n tested | Abnormal | % |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overall (≥1 test confirming neuropathy) | 17 | 10 | 59% |
| Skin biopsy — distal lower leg | 16 | 10 | 62.5% |
| Skin biopsy — upper thigh (proximal) | ~10–12 | ~5–6 | ~50% |
| Autonomic function tests | 8 | 4 | 50% |
| Electrodiagnostic (large-fiber) | 12 | 2 | 16.7% |
| Received immunotherapy | 17 | 11 | 65% |

### Clinical Phenotype

- 16/17 patients had mild COVID; 1 (patient 9) had severe COVID (ICU, ventilatory support).
- SFN symptom scores at initial evaluation averaged 40.7% of ideal (severely reduced); pain
  scores averaged 4.8/10.
- Neuromuscular examination averaged 77.0% of ideal; reduced distal pin and vibration sensation
  and absent Achilles reflexes were most prevalent.
- SFN symptoms began within 1 month of COVID-19 onset.
- Longitudinal follow-up (~1.4 years mean): 52% average improvement; no complete resolution.

### Neuropathy Subtypes

- Small-fiber neuropathy: the most common diagnosis (≥10 of 17 received SFN diagnoses).
- Multifocal motor neuropathy (demyelinating): 1 patient (patient 15, detailed case report).
- Critical illness axonal neuropathy: 1 patient (patient 9, severe COVID).

### Immune Mechanism Inference

- Standard dysimmunity markers (ANA, SS-A/SS-B, complement) negative.
- No specific pathogenic autoantibody identified.
- Dysimmune mechanism inferred from: (a) slightly delayed onset post-infection, (b) prolonged
  post-infectious course, (c) apparent responses to IVIg/corticosteroids, and (d) prior autopsy
  data showing perivascular macrophage infiltrates without viral antigen in post-COVID neural tissue.

## Relevance

This paper provides the primary empirical seed for
`hypothesis:0007-autoimmune-sfn-peripheral-dysautonomia-substrate` and supplies direct evidence
for propositions:

- **`proposition:0014-pais-small-fiber-structural-lesion-ienfd` (P1):** 63% distal + ~50% proximal
  skin-biopsy SFN confirmation in a WHO-defined long COVID cohort is a concrete instance of the
  peripheral structural lesion claim, albeit in a small referral series. Caution: this is a
  *referred* population, so prevalence is not population-representative.
- **`proposition:0015-pais-sfn-non-length-dependent-pattern` (P2):** The paired distal + proximal
  biopsy design with comparable yield at both sites is the most direct evidence available in this
  paper that the SFN is not purely length-dependent. This is partially supportive but not formally
  reported per patient; see Methodological Gaps below.
- **`proposition:0019-pais-sfn-immunomodulation-modifies-lesion-trajectory` (P3 auxiliary):**
  Apparent IVIg and corticosteroid benefit supports immune-modifiable causation, though this is
  uncontrolled open-label and subject to regression to mean and natural recovery.
- **`proposition:0017-pais-sfn-cross-trigger-convergence` (P4):** Contributes the SARS-CoV-2
  trigger arm for the cross-trigger SFN pattern.

The paper does NOT provide evidence for:
- **`proposition:0018-pais-sfn-anti-gpcr-autoantibody-pathogenicity`:** No functional autoantibody
  serology was performed.
- A comparison to **primary-dysautonomia controls** — the key methodological gap this hypothesis
  tracks. There are no healthy controls and no disease controls of any kind.

## Limitations

- **Critically small sample (n = 17):** A clinical note, not a powered observational study.
  Prevalence and association estimates are not reliable at this size; findings are
  hypothesis-generating only.
- **Referral bias:** All patients were referred for peripheral neuropathy evaluation, enriching
  for sensory/autonomic symptoms and SFN. This is not a random sample of long COVID patients.
  True population-level SFN prevalence in long COVID is unknown from this study.
- **No control group:** No healthy controls, no primary-dysautonomia controls (e.g., idiopathic
  POTS/dysautonomia), and no COVID-recovered-without-long-COVID controls. Absence of controls is
  the key methodological gap for `hypothesis:0007` proposition-testing.
- **Non-length-dependent pattern not formally quantified per patient:** The dual-site (distal + proximal)
  biopsy design enables NLD detection, but the paper does not report per-patient dichotomization of
  NLD vs. LD pattern. The 50% proximal yield vs. 63% distal yield is reported at the group level
  only; the fraction of individuals with NLD-type SFN is not calculable from the reported data.
- **No functional GPCR or anti-ganglioside autoantibody panel:** The autoimmune serology is limited
  to a standard dysimmunity screen (ANA, SS-A/SS-B, complement). The anti-GPCR autoantibody
  hypothesis (`proposition:0018`, `question:0009`) is not addressed.
- **Inconsistent assessment timing:** Patients were evaluated at varying time points during illness
  and treatment, making comparison of symptom scores and test results across patients difficult.
  Longitudinal assessments at standardized intervals would be required for robust trajectory
  inference.
- **Treatment confounding:** Most patients had received symptom-relieving medications before
  evaluation, though the analysis focused on immunotherapies.
- **Predominantly Caucasian sample (94%):** Limits generalizability.
- **Skin biopsy staining and IENFD threshold methodology:** The paper does not specify the staining
  protocol, laboratory, reference range used, or normative database for IENFD cutoffs. These
  details matter for cross-study comparison of biopsy-based SFN rates.

## Methodological Note for `hypothesis:0007` Evidence-Coding

Four evidence-coding questions raised in the ingestion request:

1. **IENFD biopsy methodology — distal-only or proximal+distal?**
   **Proximal + distal** (lower leg + upper thigh). Paired-site design was used. This means the
   design *could* detect a NLD pattern, unlike distal-only studies.

2. **Non-length-dependent vs. length-dependent pattern and fraction?**
   The paper does not formally report per-patient NLD vs. LD classification or a fraction.
   The group-level evidence is: 50% of upper-thigh (proximal) biopsies were abnormal vs. 63%
   of lower-leg (distal). This is strongly suggestive of NLD involvement but the fraction of
   *individuals* with NLD-type SFN is not stated.

3. **Long COVID case definition and confirmed infection?**
   WHO Delphi consensus long COVID definition (onset within 90 days; duration >2 months).
   SARS-CoV-2 infection confirmed per WHO guidelines for all 17 patients.

4. **Primary-dysautonomia or disease controls?**
   **None.** No controls of any type — not healthy, not primary-dysautonomia, not COVID-
   recovered without long COVID. This is the key methodological gap for the hypothesis.

5. **Functional autoantibody / autoimmune serology?**
   Standard dysimmunity screen only (ANA, ESR, SS-A/SS-B IgG, C3, C4); all largely negative.
   No functional GPCR autoantibodies, no anti-ganglioside, no anti-trisulfatide panels.
   Dysimmune mechanism is inferred from clinical course and treatment response, not confirmed
   serologically.

## Follow-up

- **Limongelli 2026** (`paper:Limongelli2026`): Prospective cohort study of SFN in post-vaccine
  PASC with immunophenotyping, skin biopsy (calf + thigh, paired-site), and non-length-dependent
  pattern characterization. A more detailed mechanistic follow-up in the PASC-vac context.
- **Key open questions this paper raises:**
  1. What is the per-patient rate of non-length-dependent SFN in long COVID with paired
     distal+proximal skin biopsies, and how does it compare to primary-dysautonomia controls?
     (`question:0004-convergent-small-fiber-neuropathy-substrate`)
  2. Do functional anti-GPCR autoantibodies correlate with SFN severity or autonomic test
     abnormality in long COVID?
     (`question:0009-functional-autoantibodies-drive-dysautonomia`)
  3. Is the ~50% autonomic-function-test abnormality rate reproducible in larger, less-selected
     long COVID cohorts, and which autonomic circuits are affected (sudomotor, cardiovagal,
     adrenergic)?
  4. Do IVIg-responsive patients have a distinct immunological signature (specific antibodies,
     complement activation) compared to non-responders, and does response correlate with
     IENFD normalization on follow-up biopsy?
