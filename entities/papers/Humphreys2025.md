## Key Contribution

Humphreys et al. map the small literature at the intersection of long COVID and menopause.
The review concludes that symptom overlap is substantial, menopausal status has not consistently shown independent long-COVID risk, and the field needs longitudinal, diverse, biomarker-informed studies.

## Methods

The authors preregistered a PRISMA-ScR scoping review and searched CINAHL Ultimate, MEDLINE, ScienceDirect, Cochrane, and Scopus for English-language studies from 2020 to 2025.
From 387 records, 7 eligible observational studies were included.
The included evidence covered cross-sectional, retrospective, prospective cohort, and longitudinal designs, with total participants reported as 14,810 and 76% female.

## Key Findings

The review identifies recurring overlap between long-COVID and menopause symptoms, especially fatigue, cognitive difficulty, mood change, sleep disturbance, hot flashes, weakness, and exhaustion.
Studies based on self-report suggested that many women perceive long-COVID symptoms as interacting with menstrual or menopausal transitions.
Shah et al. found female sex increased long-COVID risk, but menopausal status did not clearly add independent risk.
One proteomic study highlighted VEGFA overexpression in long COVID and menopausal women, suggesting a possible vascular or angiogenic overlap that remains preliminary.

## Relevance

This is the best batch-level summary source for the menopause-long-COVID intersection.
It supports an open evidentiary posture: menopause may influence symptom experience, diagnosis, and severity, but the current literature does not establish menopause as a direct long-COVID cause.
It also reinforces the need for objective biomarkers, hormone measures, and better phenotype separation.

## Limitations

Only seven studies met inclusion criteria, and most relied on self-reported long-COVID and menopause status.
The review is descriptive rather than meta-analytic.
Ethnicity and socioeconomic reporting were limited across included studies.
The citation metadata are internally inconsistent between import year and article text.
