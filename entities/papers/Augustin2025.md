## Key Contribution

This small cross-sectional controlled study (n=21 post-COVID syndrome [PCS+] vs n=11
PCS− convalescents at ~2 years post-infection) reports co-elevation of ileal activated
mast cells (CD117+CD25+), serum tryptase, serum zonulin, and ileal SARS-CoV-2 spike
protein in PCS+ but not PCS− participants. Spike protein was undetectable in serum
of both groups, yet was detectable in terminal ileum tissue of PCS+ at higher frequency
than PCS−. This makes it, to this project's knowledge, the **first long-COVID cohort
to combine a serum tryptase readout with ileal SARS-CoV-2 spike detection**, linking
the mast-cell activation (MCAS) strand to the gut antigen-persistence frame.

**Evidence-maturity caveat (critical).** These are conference-abstract-level, n=21
findings. The full peer-reviewed version has since been published as `paper:Augustin2026`
(*Mucosal Immunology* 2026, N=43; note the full paper's terminal-ileum antigen readout is
**nucleocapsid**, whereas this abstract reported **spike** — do not conflate the two). The
abstract's observed statistical associations (p-values from small N) should still be
interpreted with caution: small-sample p-values are sensitive to distributional assumptions
and multiple comparisons not reported in an abstract. The co-elevation pattern is
hypothesis-supporting, not confirmatory.

## Methods

**Design:** Cross-sectional, controlled study. Follow-up: ~2 years post-SARS-CoV-2
infection.

**Cohort:** 21 SARS-CoV-2 convalescents with PCS (PCS+) and 11 without PCS (PCS−).
Paired peripheral blood (PB) and terminal ileum (TI) samples obtained from all
participants. PCS case definition and inclusion/exclusion criteria [UNVERIFIED — not
stated in abstract; full methods not available at abstract level].

**Demographics (from abstract):**
- Median follow-up: 22 months (PCS+), 18 months (PCS−)
- Sex: 81% female (PCS+), 73% female (PCS−)
- Pre-existing conditions: 57% (PCS+), 55% (PCS−); most common: arterial
  hypertension, chronic lung disease, diabetes mellitus

**Assays:**
- CD117+CD25+ activated mast cells: fluorescent immunohistochemistry on ileal
  tissue sections
- Residual SARS-CoV-2 spike (S) protein: fluorescent immunohistochemistry (ileal
  tissue sections) and ELISA (serum)
- Tryptase: serum ELISA
- Zonulin (intestinal permeability marker): serum ELISA
- T-cell phenotyping: flow cytometry for CD3+CD4+ (T_CD4), CD3+CD8+ (T_CD8) and
  CCR7±CD27± memory subsets (TCM: T central memory; TEM: T effector memory) in
  both PB and TI

abstract; likely endoscopy, but not confirmed].

## Key Findings

All findings are from a conference abstract (n=21 PCS+, n=11 PCS−) and must be
treated as preliminary.

**Ileal mast cells:** PCS+ had significantly more CD117+CD25+ activated mast cells
in ileal sections compared to PCS− (p<0.0001).

**Serum tryptase:** elevated in PCS+ vs PCS− (p=0.0196). Tryptase is the
canonically accepted serum biomarker of systemic mast cell activation.

**Serum zonulin:** elevated in PCS+ vs PCS− (p=0.024), indicating greater intestinal
barrier dysfunction in PCS+ [UNVERIFIED — zonulin is a widely used but methodologically
contested permeability marker; assay platform not specified in abstract].

**Serum spike protein:** undetectable in PB of both PCS+ and PCS− even at ~2 years
post-infection. Neither group showed circulating spike by ELISA.

**Ileal spike protein:** frequency of tissue-resident S protein-positive cells was
significantly higher in PCS+ vs PCS− (p=0.0135). Spike was detectable in gut tissue
when undetectable in blood. [UNVERIFIED — immunohistochemistry antibody specificity,
staining controls, and method of quantifying "frequency of S protein-infected cells"
not specified in abstract.]

**Tryptase–TCM correlation:** systemic tryptase levels correlated positively with
elevated T central memory cells (TCM) in TI (r=0.7407, p=0.0034; n=15 pairs
[UNVERIFIED — exact n for this sub-analysis not stated]).

**Acute illness severity** and vaccination status [UNVERIFIED — not reported in
abstract; important covariates for interpreting the PCS+/PCS− contrast].

## Relevance

### Link to question:0034 (atopy/MCAS as mechanistically distinct PAIS subgroup)

This abstract provides the first published serum tryptase data in a long-COVID cohort
with matched gut tissue sampling. Elevated tryptase in PCS+ indicates systemic mast
cell degranulation activity persisting ~2 years post-infection, which is the
biomarker-level observation that question:0034 identifies as missing from the
literature. It does **not** confirm that pre-existing MCAS or atopy predisposed
participants to PCS — the cohort's pre-infection atopic status is [UNVERIFIED]; the
finding is that PCS+ patients show ongoing mast cell activation 2 years later, not
that prior MCAS drove this. Pre-existing MCAS as the upstream amplifier remains
inference, not a tested comparison.

The tryptase–TCM correlation (r≈0.74) in ileal tissue is mechanistically suggestive:
mast cell activity may be coupled to T-cell memory responses in gut tissue, potentially
implicating a self-sustaining mast-cell/adaptive immune loop in PCS gut pathology.
This is a post-hoc correlation, not a causal test.

### Thematic link to hypothesis:0002 (antigen/fragment persistence) — indirect, do not overclaim

The ileal spike detection at 2 years in PCS+ but not PCS− is consistent with the
tissue-reservoir antigen-persistence frame in `hypothesis:0002`. However, the abstract
does not characterize whether the ileal spike represents replication-competent virus,
inert protein fragments, or membrane-bound antigen. The mechanism by which ileal spike
coexists with undetectable serum spike is [UNVERIFIED]. This finding is a weak
corroborating signal for h0002's gut compartment hypothesis (as discussed in the
antigen-persistence topic), but at abstract/n=21 maturity it cannot independently
support a causal antigen-persistence claim.

### Coupling the MCAS strand to the gut persistence strand

The most novel conceptual contribution of this abstract is combining both signals —
mast cell activation (tryptase) and antigen persistence (ileal spike) — in the same
cohort. Prior MCAS-long-COVID literature (Weinstock/Afrin thesis) relied on
symptom-pattern matching, not mediator measurement. This abstract adds a group-level
mediator signal (elevated tryptase) in parallel with tissue antigen evidence —
a preliminary mast-cell-activation signal, not confirmation of MCAS (which requires
per-patient consensus diagnostic criteria this abstract does not apply). The
tryptase–ileal-TCM correlation further suggests a gut-tissue immune axis, not just
two independent findings.

### Link to topic:population-boundary-conditions-and-effect-modifiers-in-pais

This paper was originally noted in the topic's "Current State of Knowledge" section
(§D, atopy/MCAS subsection) as the first mediator-level signal in the field. It
does not characterize pre-existing MCAS as a boundary condition per se, but supplies
the biomarker infrastructure (tryptase, ileal MC counts) that future studies
comparing pre-existing vs de novo mast cell activation would need.

## Limitations

1. **Conference abstract level; not peer-reviewed as a full paper.** Methods, statistical
   controls, covariate adjustment, and full sample description are unavailable.

2. **Very small N (n=21 PCS+, n=11 PCS−).** Small samples make p-values highly
   sensitive to distributional assumptions and inflates false-positive risk. Effect
   sizes not reported in abstract.

3. **Cross-sectional design.** Cannot determine whether mast cell activation preceded
   PCS (pre-existing MCAS as risk factor), arose during acute infection, or developed
   as a consequence of persistent PCS-related immune dysregulation. Directionality
   is entirely unresolved.

4. **Pre-infection atopic/MCAS status not reported.** The abstract reports pre-existing
   conditions (hypertension, lung disease, diabetes) but does not state whether
   participants had pre-existing atopy or MCAS diagnosis. This is the pivotal covariate
   for question:0034 and is [UNVERIFIED].

5. **Acute illness severity and vaccination status not reported.** PCS risk and immune
   trajectory are strongly severity-dependent. If PCS+ participants had more severe
   acute COVID-19, the mast cell and antigen findings may primarily reflect greater
   initial viral burden, not a distinct MCAS mechanism.

6. **Ileal spike IHC methodology unverified.** Antibody specificity for SARS-CoV-2 S
   protein vs cross-reactive antigens, staining controls, quantification method, and
   threshold for "S protein-positive cell" classification are not described in the
   abstract. [UNVERIFIED]

7. **Zonulin assay limitations.** Zonulin (as measured by commercial ELISA kits) is a
   methodologically contested biomarker: the protein complement-C3 interference issue
   with the Cusabio kit, for example, generates spurious zonulin-like signal. Without
   knowing which assay platform was used, the zonulin result requires independent
   verification. [UNVERIFIED]

8. **PCS case definition not stated.** Different PCS/long-COVID case definitions produce
   substantially different cohorts; the operational definition used here is [UNVERIFIED].

9. **No multiple testing correction reported.** Five p-values reported across outcomes;
   whether these are raw or corrected is [UNVERIFIED].

10. **No male-stratified analysis.** At 81% female (PCS+) and 73% female (PCS−), the
    cohort is female-predominant; sex-stratified mast cell data are not available from
    the abstract.

## Follow-up

- **Full peer-reviewed publication — RESOLVED (t140, 2026-07-19).** The group
  (Klinik Favoriten / University Hospital Cologne) published the full paper as
  `paper:Augustin2026` (*Mucosal Immunology* 19(3):100328, doi:10.1016/j.mucimm.2026.03.002,
  PMID 41794369), expanding this abstract to N=43 with fuller immune phenotyping. Cite the
  full paper for established findings; this abstract now serves as the precursor record.
- **Pre-infection MCAS/atopy status.** A follow-up study (or full paper) should
  report whether participants had prior MCAS, atopy, or elevated baseline tryptase —
  the critical covariate for question:0034.
- **Acute illness severity adjustment.** Any expansion of this work should adjust for
  acute COVID-19 severity to disentangle mast cell activation as a consequence of
  severe acute illness from a PCS-specific persistent mechanism.
- **Link to Wolff2023:** Wolff2023 (systematic review) established atopy as a
  long-COVID risk factor at the epidemiological level; Augustin2025 begins to supply
  the mediator-level counterpart. These two papers together constitute the current
  (thin) evidence base for question:0034.
- **Tryptase reference range.** Absolute tryptase values not reported in the abstract;
  comparison to clinical MCAS diagnostic thresholds (>20 ng/mL or >11.4 + 2×baseline
  per 2019 consensus criteria) would contextualize the elevation [UNVERIFIED].
- **Zonulin assay platform.** Follow up to identify which commercial zonulin kit was
  used, given known specificity issues in the field.
- See also `topic:antigen-pathogen-persistence` for the broader gut-reservoir literature
  context.
