# Risk of New-Onset Long COVID Following Reinfection With SARS-CoV-2

- **Authors:** Matthew L. Bosworth, B. Shenhuy, A. Sarah Walker, et al.
- **Year:** 2023
- **Journal:** Open Forum Infectious Diseases
- **DOI:** 10.1093/ofid/ofad493
- **BibTeX key:** Bosworth2023

## Key Contribution

Bosworth2023 is a community-sampled reinfection study that estimates new-onset self-reported long COVID
after second infection versus first infection.

## Methods

The study used a large randomly sampled UK community cohort with routine SARS-CoV-2 testing and
self-reported long-COVID assessment 12-20 weeks after infection. Models adjusted for demographics,
health status, vaccination status, infection timing, and calendar date as a variant proxy.

## Key Findings

- Among participants aged 16 or older, self-reported long COVID was 4.0% after first infection and 2.4%
  after second infection.
- Adjusted odds of long COVID after second infection were lower than after first infection (aOR 0.72 for
  any long COVID; aOR 0.66 for activity-limiting long COVID).
- The absolute risk after second infection was still nonzero: about 1 in 40 adults reported long COVID
  after second infection.

## Relevance

This is the clearest warning against treating reinfection as simply another first infection. It supports
the t010 conclusion that risk is cumulative at the population level, but per-infection risk can be lower
after second infection in immune/Omicron-era populations.

## Limitations

The analysis estimates new-onset long COVID after a second infection, not total cumulative burden across
all infections. It relies on self-report at one post-infection visit, is observational, and is dominated
by Omicron-era infections.
