# Persistent Endothelial Dysfunction in Post-Acute COVID-19 Syndrome: A Case-Control Study

- **Authors:** Pasquale Ambrosino, Ilenia Calcaterra, Antonio Molino, Pasquale Moretta, Roberta Lupoli, Giorgio Alfredo Spedicato, Antimo Papa, Andrea Motta, Mauro Maniscalco, Matteo Nicola Dario Di Minno
- **Year:** 2021
- **Journal:** Biomedicines, 9(8):957
- **DOI:** 10.3390/biomedicines9080957
- **PMCID:** PMC8391623
- **BibTeX key:** Ambrosino2021
- **Source:** Full text (Europe PMC XML)

## Key Contribution

This prospective case-control study provides quantitative evidence that endothelial dysfunction — measured by brachial artery flow-mediated dilation (FMD) — persists in convalescent COVID-19 patients within two months of swab-test negativization, and that the impairment is strongly sex-biased: male convalescent patients show severely suppressed FMD while female convalescent patients show values indistinguishable from matched controls. The paper also demonstrates that FMD tracks pulmonary-impairment severity within the case group, raising acute-severity confounding as a critical interpretive problem for the sex effect.

## Methods

**Design:** Prospective case-control study, December 2020 – June 2021, ICS Maugeri IRCCS pulmonary rehabilitation unit, Telese Terme, Benevento, Italy.

**Eligibility:** Convalescent adults with PCR-confirmed SARS-CoV-2 infection, WHO severity class III (severe) or IV (critical), swab-test negativization within the preceding 2 months. Controls were matched for age, sex, and major cardiovascular risk factors from a historical cohort (January 2018 – July 2019) without COVID-19 history.

**Cohort composition:** 133 convalescent COVID-19 patients (81.2% male, mean age 61.6 ± 10.6 years) and 133 matched controls (80.5% male, mean age 60.4 ± 11.5 years). Among cases: 30.8% WHO class III (severe), 69.2% WHO class IV (critical); 70.7% hospitalized; 27.1% required mechanical ventilation.

**FMD measurement:** Standard brachial artery reactivity protocol. Forearm cuff inflation at 70 mmHg above systolic blood pressure for 5 minutes, then 4-minute post-deflation monitoring with automated edge-detection software (Cardiovascular Suite, QUIPU). Operator blinded to clinical and functional status. FMD = percent change in brachial artery diameter from baseline to maximum reactive-hyperemia value.

**Pulmonary function:** PaO2, PaCO2 (arterial blood gas), FEV1%, FVC%, DLCO% (spirometry and diffusion capacity by ATS/ERS protocol). Six-minute walk distance (6MWD), COPD Assessment Test (CAT), Barthel index also collected.

**Statistics:** Paired-sample t-test and Mann–Whitney U for group comparisons; Spearman rho for correlations; stepwise linear regression for independent predictors of FMD after adjusting for major confounders.

## Key Findings

### Primary comparison (cases vs. controls)

- Overall FMD: cases 3.2% ± 2.6 vs. controls 6.4% ± 4.1; p < 0.001. COVID-19 convalescence was associated with markedly impaired endothelial function independent of cardiovascular risk factors.

### Sex-stratified FMD within cases

- **Male convalescent patients:** FMD = **2.5% ± 1.9**
- **Female convalescent patients:** FMD = **6.1% ± 2.9**
- Male vs. female within cases: p < 0.001

### Female cases vs. female controls

- Female cases: 6.1% ± 2.9; female controls: 5.3% ± 3.4; p = 0.362 (not significant)
- Interpretation offered by authors: endothelial dysfunction in post-acute COVID-19 is essentially confined to males; females are not distinguishable from uninfected controls.

### FMD vs. pulmonary-impairment severity (Spearman correlations, within cases)

| Marker | rho | p |
|---|---|---|
| PaO2 (arterial oxygen tension) | 0.247 | 0.004 |
| FEV1% predicted | 0.436 | < 0.001 |
| FVC% predicted | 0.406 | < 0.001 |
| DLCO% predicted | 0.280 | 0.008 |

All correlations are positive: worse lung function = lower FMD = more endothelial impairment.

### WHO severity class comparison

- WHO class III (severe) cases: FMD = 3.2%
- WHO class IV (critical) cases: FMD = 3.1%; p = 0.878 (not significant)
- No FMD gradient between the two severity sub-groups within cases; both WHO strata were heavily impaired relative to controls.

### Multivariable regression

- After adjusting for age, sex, BMI, hypertension, dyslipidemia, diabetes, smoking, and statin use, a recent COVID-19 was an independent predictor of FMD: β = −0.427, p < 0.001.

### Subgroup robustness

- The overall FMD difference between cases and controls was confirmed in subgroup analyses stratified by age (< 65 vs. ≥ 65 years) and by major cardiovascular risk factors (hypertension, dyslipidemia, diabetes, smoking, obesity, history of CV events, statin use). The signal was consistent across all subgroups.

## Relevance

This paper is load-bearing for a project proposition about a **male-biased endothelial/vascular signal in post-acute COVID**, specifically as the **severity-confounded leg** of that argument.

**Hypothesis 0004 (acute-severity threshold):** The strong FMD–pulmonary-impairment correlation (rho 0.247–0.436) demonstrates that residual endothelial dysfunction tracks acute-illness severity within survivors. The WHO-class comparison (severe 3.2% vs. critical 3.1%, p = 0.878) initially appears to contradict a severity gradient, but this null result likely reflects a ceiling effect: all cases in this study had at minimum severe (WHO class III) disease, and the two strata are closely matched in FMD. There is no mild-acute-disease reference arm, so the true severity gradient cannot be read from within-cohort severity subgroups alone. This study supports the proposition that severe acute COVID drives persistent vascular damage, but the design cannot resolve whether the sex difference in FMD reflects a genuine biological sex effect or a severity-confounded proxy.

**Hypothesis 0005 / Question 0007 (sex effects on PAIS):** The sex-stratified FMD result is striking and has been widely cited as evidence for male-biased endothelial susceptibility in post-acute COVID. However, because the cohort is 81.2% male and severity was not stratified by sex before comparing FMD, the male–female FMD difference cannot be cleanly attributed to sex biology. See Limitations section.

**Question 0010 (vascular subphenotype):** This paper provides FMD-based evidence for a vascular dysfunction phenotype in post-acute COVID that is persistent and measurable in a standard rehabilitation referral population, supporting the proposition that vascular injury is a mechanistically distinct axis of PAIS.

## Limitations

### Critical caveat: sex–severity confounding (load-bearing for project proposition)

The male vs. female FMD difference within convalescent patients (2.5% vs. 6.1%, p < 0.001) was **not adjusted for, or stratified by, acute-illness severity**. This is a central confound because:

1. The cohort is 81.2% male. In the acute-COVID literature, males consistently experienced more severe disease, longer hospitalizations, and higher ICU and mechanical-ventilation rates. If male patients in this cohort on average had more severe acute illness than female patients, the male–female FMD gap is partially or fully explainable by differential severity rather than a sex-specific vascular biology.

2. FMD correlates directly with pulmonary-impairment markers (rho 0.247–0.436), which are themselves severity surrogates. This means FMD is not severity-independent in this cohort.

3. The paper does not report the sex distribution of WHO class III vs. class IV cases, nor does it compare FMD between males and females within each severity class. The sex-stratified analysis therefore cannot separate a sex-of-host effect from an acute-severity-mediated effect.

**Conclusion for project use:** Ambrosino2021 establishes (a) persistent endothelial dysfunction in predominantly male, severe/critical convalescent COVID-19 and (b) that FMD tracks pulmonary impairment severity. It does **not** establish that males have intrinsically more endothelial susceptibility to SARS-CoV-2 independent of acute disease severity. This is the severity-confounded leg of any argument that male sex drives worse vascular PAIS biology.

### Additional limitations

- **Rehabilitation-referral selection bias:** All cases were referred to a pulmonary rehabilitation unit, selecting for patients with sufficient residual functional impairment to warrant inpatient rehabilitation. This excludes milder convalescents and the large fraction of COVID-19 cases that never required hospital-level care. Results are not generalizable to the full post-COVID-19 population.

- **Historical controls:** Controls were matched from a pre-pandemic cohort (January 2018 – July 2019). Temporal differences in cardiovascular risk-factor management, medication use, or lifestyle factors across the two periods could influence FMD independently of COVID-19 history.

- **Two-month post-negativization window:** FMD was measured within 2 months of swab-test negativization, not at a fixed interval. Whether endothelial dysfunction persists at 6 or 12 months (the typical long-COVID symptom window) is not addressed.

- **Case definition:** Inclusion required WHO class III or IV severity. This is not a "long COVID" cohort by WHO or standard post-acute-sequelae definitions; it is a post-severe-COVID rehabilitation cohort. Symptom persistence, fatigue, or cognitive impairment were not inclusion criteria.

- **No female-stratified severity analysis:** With only ~25 female cases (~18.8% of 133), the study was insufficiently powered to conduct robust sex × severity interaction tests.

- **Single-centre Italian cohort:** Demographic and healthcare-system-specific factors may limit generalizability.

- **FMD operator dependency:** Although the operator was blinded to clinical status and automated edge-detection software was used, FMD remains technically demanding and susceptible to procedural variation.

## Follow-up

- **Severity-stratified sex comparison needed:** A study measuring FMD in male and female post-COVID patients matched on WHO severity class would be required to disentangle the sex effect from severity confounding.
- **Longitudinal trajectory:** Repeat FMD at 6 and 12 months post-infection would clarify persistence and recovery kinetics.
- **Mild-acute-disease comparator:** A parallel arm of mild-COVID convalescents would establish whether the vascular signature is specific to severe/critical disease or present across the severity spectrum.
- See `question:0010` for the broader vascular-subphenotype evidence synthesis.
- See `question:0007` for the sex-differential PAIS evidence base and the need to disentangle sex from severity in vascular outcomes.
