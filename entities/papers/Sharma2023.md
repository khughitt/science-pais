## Key Contribution

This News & Views commentary synthesizes evidence from two large retrospective cohort studies — Chang et al. (eClinicalMedicine 2023, n = 887,455 matched pairs) and Tesch et al. (medRxiv preprint 2023, n = 640,701 COVID-19 vs. 1,560,357 controls) — establishing that SARS-CoV-2 infection substantially elevates the risk of developing a broad and diverse spectrum of new-onset autoimmune diseases in the months following acute infection. The piece frames this evidence within proposed mechanisms of post-COVID-19 immune dysregulation and calls for systematic national surveillance and future mechanistic investigation.

## Methods

This is a commentary / News & Views piece; the authors do not conduct original analyses. It summarises and contextualises findings from two primary studies:

- **Chang et al.** used the TriNetX global EHR network (>5.9 million adults from 48 healthcare organisations). Propensity score matching produced balanced COVID-19 and non-COVID-19 cohorts of 887,455 individuals each. Study period: 1 January 2020 – 31 December 2021. Only unvaccinated individuals were included to remove vaccine confounding. Autoimmune incidence was assessed at 6-month follow-up.
- **Tesch et al.** (preprint, not yet peer-reviewed at the time of writing) evaluated 640,701 PCR-confirmed COVID-19 cases from Germany in 2020 against 1,560,357 matched controls (matched on age, sex, and pre-existing autoimmune status). Follow-up window: 3–15 months post-infection.
- A third preprint (Syed et al., medRxiv) using England's Clinical Practice Research Datalink Aurum (458,147 infected vs. 1,818,929 uninfected adults, Jan 2020 – Jun 2021) is also described.

All primary studies are retrospective matched-cohort designs; none establish causality.

## Key Findings

**From Chang et al. (887,455 matched COVID-19 vs. non-COVID-19 pairs):**
- Rheumatoid arthritis: adjusted hazard ratio (aHR) 2.98 (95% CI 2.78–3.20)
- Systemic lupus erythematosus: aHR 2.99 (95% CI 2.68–3.34)
- Vasculitis: aHR 1.96 (95% CI 1.74–2.20)
- Inflammatory bowel disease: aHR 1.78 (95% CI 1.72–1.84)
- Type 1 diabetes mellitus: aHR 2.68 (95% CI 2.51–2.85)
- Risk was consistent across all age groups.

**From Tesch et al. (640,701 COVID-19 vs. 1,560,357 controls):**
- 42.6% higher overall likelihood of acquiring an autoimmune condition 3–15 months post-infection.
- Highest incidence rate ratios were found for vasculitis conditions.
- Among individuals with pre-existing autoimmune disease, COVID-19 increased the risk of developing an additional autoimmune disease by 23%.

**From Syed et al.:**
- Type 1 diabetes mellitus, inflammatory bowel disease, and psoriasis were significantly associated with prior SARS-CoV-2 infection.

**Proposed mechanisms** discussed by the commentary authors:
1. Molecular mimicry by viral proteins
2. Widespread ACE2 expression enabling multiorgan involvement
3. Bystander activation of immune cells
4. Release of autoantigens from virus-damaged tissue
5. Superantigen-mediated lymphocyte activation
6. Epitope spreading
7. Host factors: age, comorbidities, genetic predisposition

**Autoantibody evidence:** Multiple autoantibody types detected in COVID-19 patients overlap with autoimmune disease markers: antinuclear antibodies, lupus anticoagulant, cold agglutinins, anti-Ro/SSA antibodies.

## Relevance

This paper directly supports the core PAIS frame in this project — that acute SARS-CoV-2 infection can trigger a durable failure of immune homeostasis that manifests as clinically overt autoimmune disease in the post-acute phase.

**Connections to the project research question (research-question:post-acute-infection-syndromes):**

- **Autoimmunity as a PAIS failure mode:** The aHR estimates (2.68–2.99 for RA, SLE, T1DM; 1.78 for IBD; 1.96 for vasculitis) quantify the magnitude of post-infectious immune dysregulation with population-scale precision. These numbers anchor the autoimmunity branch of the PAIS failure-mode taxonomy.
- **Spectrum of target organs:** The broad organ involvement (joints, kidneys/vasculature, gut, pancreatic beta-cells) echoes the multisystem character of PAIS and is consistent with the ACE2-wide-expression hypothesis.
- **Pre-existing autoimmunity amplifies risk:** The 23% additional risk for individuals with prior autoimmune disease suggests that partial immune dysregulation prior to infection lowers the threshold for post-infectious immune breakdown — mechanistically relevant to the "homeostatic reserve" concept.
- **Shared mechanisms with ME/CFS and long COVID:** Molecular mimicry, bystander activation, and epitope spreading are mechanisms also invoked in ME/CFS pathogenesis, supporting the hypothesis of shared post-infectious immunopathogenic pathways.
- **No vaccine confounding:** Exclusion of vaccinated individuals in the primary studies removes a key confounder, strengthening the attribution to infection per se.

## Limitations

1. **Commentary format:** Sharma & Bayry are synthesising, not generating original data; all quantitative claims are derived from the three primary studies and should be verified in those sources.
2. **Retrospective cohort design of primary studies:** Neither Chang et al. nor Tesch et al. can establish causality. Temporal association with COVID-19 is compelling but confounding by healthcare-seeking behaviour, diagnosis ascertainment bias (increased medical contact post-COVID), and unmeasured covariates remains plausible.
3. **Tesch et al. was a preprint** at the time of this commentary (not peer-reviewed); findings should be treated accordingly.
4. **Unvaccinated-only cohorts (Chang et al.):** While this removes vaccine confounding, it limits generalisability to the current (post-vaccine-rollout) population.
5. **ICD code–based outcome ascertainment:** EHR studies relying on diagnostic codes may misclassify autoimmune conditions, particularly for mild or early-stage disease.
6. **No mechanistic data:** The proposed mechanisms (mimicry, bystander activation, etc.) are not tested in these studies; they are hypotheses supported by prior literature.
7. **No long-term follow-up beyond 15 months:** The trajectory of autoimmune risk beyond 15 months post-infection is unknown from these data.
8. **Single-pathogen focus:** The commentary does not systematically compare COVID-19–associated autoimmune risk against other post-infectious triggers, limiting assessment of SARS-CoV-2 specificity versus shared PAIS mechanism.

- The 166-gene transcriptomic signature from Ghosh et al. (Nat. Commun. 2022) that captures shared host immune responses across viral pandemics warrants a dedicated summary entry.
- Questions raised for the project: (1) Do the autoimmune conditions that emerge post-COVID share pathogenic mechanisms with ME/CFS-associated immune dysregulation, or are they distinct sequelae? (2) Is the 23% excess risk in pre-existing autoimmune patients a ceiling effect, an additive effect, or evidence of distinct susceptibility genetics? (3) How does the post-COVID autoimmune disease incidence curve evolve beyond 15 months — does it plateau, decline, or continue rising?
