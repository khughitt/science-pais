# Insights from an N3C RECOVER EHR-based cohort study characterizing SARS-CoV-2 reinfections and Long COVID

- **Authors:** Emily C. Hadley, Yun J. Yoo, Shishir Patel, N3C and RECOVER consortia, et al.
- **Year:** 2024
- **Journal:** Communications Medicine
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s43856-024-00539-2
- **Article:** 4:129
- **BibTeX key:** Hadley2024

## Key Contribution

Hadley2024 is the main local anchor for the "reinfection does not equal first infection" nuance. It uses
N3C/RECOVER EHR data to characterize reinfection severity and long-COVID diagnosis after initial
infection versus reinfection.

## Methods

The study analyzed more than 3 million N3C patients with EHR data. It characterized reinfections,
severity concordance between initial infection and reinfection, laboratory trajectories, and long-COVID
diagnosis timing.

## Key Findings

- Reinfections were common in the Omicron epoch.
- Severity of reinfection was associated with severity of the initial infection.
- Long-COVID diagnoses occurred more often after initial infection than after reinfection within the same
  variant epoch.

## Relevance

This constrains simple cumulative-risk language. Reinfection can add cases and organ-system burden, but
the per-infection probability of long-COVID diagnosis may be lower after a reinfection than after the
first infection in immune/Omicron-era populations.

## Limitations

The outcome is EHR long-COVID diagnosis, not prospective symptom ascertainment. Reinfections may be
missed, healthcare-seeking and diagnosis intensity vary, and N3C does not provide the cleanest population
sampling frame. Use this as a qualifier, not a standalone null on reinfection risk.
