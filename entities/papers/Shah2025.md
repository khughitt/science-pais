# Sex Differences in Long COVID

- **Authors:** Dimpy P. Shah et al.; RECOVER Consortium
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal:** JAMA Network Open
- **DOI:** 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2024.55430
- **BibTeX key:** Shah2025
- **Source:** PDF: `~/d/health/processes/post-acute-infection/papers/pdfs/2025_Shah_Sex-Differences-in-Long-COVID.pdf`

## Key Contribution

Shah et al. provide large prospective RECOVER cohort evidence that female sex is associated with higher long-COVID risk, and that the association varies by age, pregnancy, and menopausal status.
This is one of the strongest empirical anchors for the project question about female predominance in PAIS.

## Methods

The study analyzed 12,276 RECOVER-Adult participants with SARS-CoV-2 infection enrolled at 83 sites in 33 US states plus Washington, DC, and Puerto Rico.
Long COVID was defined at a visit at least six months after infection using a symptom-based research index.
The authors used propensity-score matching to compare female and male participants, with full and reduced models, and secondary analyses stratified by age and menopausal status.

## Key Findings

Among infected participants, 21% of females and 16% of males met long-COVID criteria at the analysis visit.
Female sex was associated with higher long-COVID risk in the full model (RR 1.31; 95% CI 1.06-1.62) and reduced model (RR 1.44; 95% CI 1.17-1.77).
The association was strongest in ages 40-54 years (RR 1.48; 95% CI 1.19-1.84), present at age 55 years or older (RR 1.34; 95% CI 1.11-1.61), and absent at ages 18-39 years.
Among ages 40-54 years, nonmenopausal females had significantly higher risk than males (RR 1.45; 95% CI 1.15-1.83), while menopausal females had a similar point estimate with wider confidence interval crossing the null (RR 1.42; 95% CI 0.99-2.03).
Adding comorbidities to the propensity score attenuated the sex association, suggesting that female-associated comorbidities may partly mediate or confound risk.

## Relevance

This paper directly updates `question:0007-mechanism-of-female-predominance-in-pais`.
It supports female sex as a robust long-COVID risk factor after adjustment, but it does not identify the mechanism.
The age and menopause analyses argue against a simple menopause-only explanation and point instead to interacting effects of sex hormones, immune response, pregnancy, age, comorbidities, and symptom ascertainment.

## Limitations

Long COVID was defined by a symptom-based research index, and reporting or selection bias may remain.
The study lacked direct sex-hormone levels, menstrual-cycle timing, hormone-related medication details, pregnancy-history detail, and a non-COVID viral control.
Menopausal subgroup analyses had wider uncertainty than the primary sex comparison.

## Model / Tool Availability

No model or code artifact was released in the paper.
The analysis uses RECOVER cohort data subject to RECOVER access procedures.

## Follow-up

Use this as the main empirical citation for female excess risk in long COVID.
The key next step is a causal model distinguishing sex assigned at birth, age, menopausal transition, hormone therapy, pregnancy, comorbidities, and healthcare-seeking/reporting from immune-mediated PAIS susceptibility.
