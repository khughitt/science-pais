# Baseline immune signatures are associated with prospective COVID-19 infection and clinical outcomes in healthcare workers: Evidence from a cohort study in Brazil

- **Authors:** Eliana Teles de Gois, Vitor Hugo Simões Miranda, Gabriela Profírio Jardim-Santos, Heidi Luise Schulte, Maria Eduarda Canellas-de-Castro, Lizandra Moura Paravidine Sasaki, Geraldo Magela Fernandes, Alexandre Anderson de Sousa Munhoz Soares, Laila Salmen Espindola, Patrícia Shu Kurizky, Ciro Martins Gomes, Ismael Artur Costa-Rocha, Joaquim Pedro Brito-de-Sousa, Ana Carolina Campi-Azevedo, Vanessa Peruhype-Magalhães, Andréa Teixeira-Carvalho, Cleandro Pires de Albuquerque, Pedro Henrique de Jesus Diogo, Laurence Rodrigues do Amaral, Marcelo Antônio Pascoal-Xavier, Jordana Grazziela Alves Coelho-dos-Reis, Licia Maria Henrique da Mota, Olindo Assis Martins-Filho, Otávio de Tolêdo Nóbrega

## Key Contribution

This prospective cohort study in Brazilian healthcare workers (n = 386, all SARS-CoV-2-negative at enrollment) demonstrates that pre-infection baseline serum levels of soluble immune mediators — chemokines, pro-inflammatory and regulatory cytokines, and growth factors — are associated with both subsequent SARS-CoV-2 susceptibility and clinical outcome trajectories over a 19-month follow-up. The study establishes that a higher-baseline immune activation state (particularly elevated GM-CSF, CCL11, CXCL10, TNF-α, G-CSF, and IL-5) predicts infection and shapes severity, symptom manifestation, and symptom duration, independent of acute infection itself. This is one of the first reports from a longitudinal 19-month follow-up of hospital staff with pre-infection profiling linking baseline immune milieu to prospective COVID-19 diagnosis and outcome.

## Methods

**Design:** Cross-sectional exploratory observational study initiated July 2020; 19-month prospective follow-up of SARS-CoV-2 non-infected hospital staff at Universidade de Brasília, Brazil.

**Population:** 1,282 healthcare workers screened; 455 volunteered for medical interview; 56 excluded (prior COVID-19 at baseline), 13 lost to follow-up. Final analytical cohort: n = 386 (284 females, 102 males), age 23–67 years. During follow-up, 280 (73%) were prospectively diagnosed COVID-19 (+) and 106 (27%) remained COVID-19 (−).

**Immune profiling platform:** Quantitative measurement of 27 serum soluble immune mediators (7 chemokines: CXCL8, CCL11, CCL3, CCL4, CCL2, CCL5, CXCL10; 8 pro-inflammatory cytokines: IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, IL-12, IFN-γ, IL-15, IL-17; 6 regulatory cytokines: IL-1Ra, IL-4, IL-5, IL-9, IL-10, IL-13; 6 growth factors: FGF-basic, PDGF, VEGF, G-CSF, GM-CSF, IL-2, IL-7) using Bio-Plex Pro Human Cytokine 27-plex microbeads multiplex array (Luminex platform). All samples collected prior to any COVID-19 diagnosis.

**Subgrouping:** COVID-19 (+) subjects stratified by (i) time elapsed to diagnosis (< 4 months [n = 95], 4–16 months [n = 99], > 16 months [n = 86]); (ii) clinical form (asymptomatic [n = 14] vs symptomatic [n = 203] within COVID-19 (+) with outcome data); (iii) disease severity (mild [n = 185] vs severe [n = 32]); (iv) symptom duration (< 4 weeks [n = 154] vs ≥ 4 weeks [n = 77]).

**Prospective diagnosis confirmation:** RT-qPCR (95%), ELISA IgM/IgG seroconversion (4%), antigen testing (1%).

**Statistics:** Mann-Whitney U, Kruskal-Wallis + Dunn's post-test, Fisher's exact test, fold-change analysis, volcano plots, overlaid delta signatures, Spearman correlation-based integrative network analysis (Cytoscape), multivariate ROC/SVM/KNN analysis, decision tree algorithms (WEKA 3.6.11), leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV).

**Ethics:** Approved by CONEP (CAAE 30,846,920.7.0000.0008); waiver of ICF granted due to COVID-19 social isolation context; registered on ReBEC (RBR-62zdkk).

## Key Findings

**Panoramic baseline immune profile:**
- 20 of 27 immune mediators showed significantly higher circulating levels at baseline in COVID-19 (+) vs COVID-19 (−) participants, spanning all major mediator classes (chemokines: CCL11, CCL3, CCL2, CXCL10; pro-inflammatory cytokines: IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, IL-12, IFN-γ, IL-15, IL-17; regulatory cytokines: IL-4, IL-5, IL-9, IL-10, IL-13; growth factors: FGF-basic, VEGF, G-CSF, GM-CSF, IL-2).
- Notably, the COVID-19 (+) group showed a broadly elevated immune activation state before infection, not a targeted single-mediator signal.

**Temporal gradient (time-to-infection):**
- Participants diagnosed < 4 months after baseline showed the largest and most significant differences from COVID-19 (−) comparators, with 22 of 27 mediators significantly upregulated, including CCL11, CCL3, CCL2, CXCL10, IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, IL-12, IFN-γ, IL-15, IL-17, and multiple growth factors.
- The 4–16 month group also showed elevated levels but fewer mediators; the > 16 month group showed attenuation toward COVID-19 (−) levels.
- Interpretation: Baseline immune elevation is not a permanent trait but may reflect a temporarily heightened immunological state that declines over time (or, alternatively, those with highest baseline activation were infected earliest).

**Key discriminating mediators — COVID-19 susceptibility:**
- Multivariate decision tree (GM-CSF, CCL11, CXCL10, TNF-α): 74% overall accuracy, 72% LOOCV (287/386 and 276/386 correct).
- ROC multivariate composite (GM-CSF, CCL11, CXCL10, TNF-α): AUC = 0.70, sensitivity 49%, specificity 81% at cut-off 2.5.
- GM-CSF and CCL11 emerged as primary roots in decision tree classification.
- Mediators with highest fold-change magnitude and significance: GM-CSF, IL-5, VEGF, IL-13, CCL11, IL-10.

**Clinical outcome associations:**
- *Symptomatic vs asymptomatic:* IL-7 and G-CSF best discriminated; decision tree accuracy 93% (201/217), 93% LOOCV; multivariate AUC = 0.75 (Se 56%, Sp 86%, cut-off 1.5).
- *Mild vs severe disease:* IL-1Ra, IL-13, FGF-basic, and G-CSF upregulated in severe cases; basal IL-1β, IL-1Ra, IL-13, FGF-basic, G-CSF higher in those who developed severe disease; decision tree AUC = 0.71 (Se 53%, Sp 80%); 87% accuracy (188/217), 87% LOOCV.
- *Symptom duration (< 4 weeks vs ≥ 4 weeks):* CCL2, IL-15, IL-10, G-CSF elevated in ≥ 4-week group; 67% accuracy (155/231), 67% LOOCV; multivariate AUC = 0.64 (Se 48%, Sp 74%). Symptom persistence showed lowest discriminatory accuracy across all analyses.
- Note: Symptom persistence cut-off used here (≥ 4 weeks) is distinct from WHO long COVID definition (≥ 12 weeks); findings reflect early post-acute symptom trajectories.

**Integrative network analysis:**
- COVID-19 (+) participants showed more complex, highly interconnected baseline immune mediator networks (576 correlations) vs COVID-19 (−) (450 correlations).
- Short time-to-infection and symptomatic groups showed highest network complexity; severe disease and prolonged symptom groups showed fewer, less organized connections — interpreted as immune exhaustion or dysregulation.

**Age-related patterns:**
- Younger HCWs (23–35 years) showed the highest immune mediator elevations across all categories in COVID-19 (+) subgroup.
- Discriminatory performance was highest in 23–35 year group (pro-inflammatory cytokines AUC = 0.70, growth factors AUC = 0.72).
- Older individuals (≥ 46 years) showed progressively diminished differences between COVID-19 (+) and COVID-19 (−).

**Mediator class performance:**
- Growth factors showed highest discriminatory power for prospective COVID-19 diagnosis overall (AUC = 0.58), followed by chemokines (AUC = 0.57); regulatory and pro-inflammatory cytokines AUC = 0.52.
- For severity discrimination, regulatory cytokines (AUC = 0.64) and growth factors (AUC = 0.60) outperformed chemokines and pro-inflammatory cytokines.

## Relevance

**Prospective pre-infection design (rare and high-value):** This is one of very few studies to capture serum immune mediator profiles before any SARS-CoV-2 exposure and link them to subsequent infection risk and clinical outcome. The vast majority of biomarker studies in COVID-19 collected samples at or after symptom onset. The pre-infection design eliminates reverse causality and directly addresses whether a pre-existing immunological milieu predisposes to infection and poor outcomes.

**Hypothesis:0004 / Question:0003 — Acute severity threshold:** The finding that baseline immune state predicts severity (severe vs mild: IL-1Ra, IL-13, FGF-basic, G-CSF elevated pre-infection in those who would later develop severe disease) supports the view that a pre-existing immune configuration sets a threshold for how strongly the acute infection cascades. The regulatory skew (elevated IL-10, IL-13, IL-1Ra) in those who later had worse outcomes suggests that immune suppressive tone prior to infection may blunt antiviral responses, consistent with hypothesis:0004 (a threshold governed by initial immune state). However, the study cannot establish causality — it is exploratory and correlational.

**Hypothesis:0003 — Immune exhaustion feedback:** The integrative network finding that COVID-19 (+) individuals with severe disease or prolonged symptoms exhibited fewer, less organized immune mediator connections at baseline (compared to those with milder outcomes) is suggestive of a pre-existing state of immune disorganization or early exhaustion. This is indirect but compatible with hypothesis:0003 (immune exhaustion as a precondition or amplifier in poor-outcome trajectories).

**Topic:long-covid-immune-dysregulation:** The symptom persistence analyses (though limited by a 4-week rather than 12-week cut-off) implicate G-CSF, CCL2, IL-15, IL-10 as baseline predictors of prolonged symptoms. These overlap with cytokine signatures found in established long COVID cohorts, suggesting continuity between pre-infection immune state and long-term post-infectious trajectory.

**Topic:biomarkers-and-objective-endpoints:** The multivariate decision tree (GM-CSF, CCL11, CXCL10, TNF-α) achieving 74% accuracy for COVID-19 (+) identification provides a concrete example of a pre-infection immune biomarker panel. The discriminatory performance is moderate and would require prospective validation in non-HCW populations before clinical utility, but the panel is an important proof of concept.

**Question:0001 — Shared molecular signature across triggers:** The finding that broadly elevated immune activation (across chemokines, pro-inflammatory, regulatory, and growth factor classes) predicts susceptibility and worse outcomes is consistent with the project's hypothesis of a shared failure mode. Whether analogous pre-infection immune signatures predict PAIS susceptibility after other pathogens (e.g., EBV, influenza, dengue) remains open.

## Limitations

1. **Exploratory, not confirmatory:** Statistical analyses were exploratory; p-values derive from multiple comparisons without strict correction for multiple testing. Results should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating, not as validated biomarkers.

2. **Healthcare worker cohort:** All participants are hospital staff, who have elevated and heterogeneous occupational COVID-19 exposure risk, distinct comorbidity profiles, and potentially different immune states from the general population. Generalizability is limited.

3. **Small subgroup sizes:** Asymptomatic COVID-19 (+) subgroup (n = 14) and severe disease subgroup (n = 32) are small, limiting statistical power and generalizability of subgroup-specific findings.

4. **Symptom persistence definition mismatch:** The study uses ≥ 4 weeks as the symptom duration cut-off for "prolonged" symptoms. This differs substantially from the WHO long COVID definition (≥ 12 weeks after onset). The paper itself notes this discrepancy; findings about symptom persistence should not be taken as directly informing long COVID susceptibility prediction.

5. **No causal inference:** Observational design with no intervention. Baseline immune elevation could reflect pre-existing comorbidities, lifestyle factors, prior exposures (including non-COVID viruses), or confounders not fully characterized. Multivariate analyses did not include adjusted logistic regression to control for covariates like BMI, comorbidities, or job category.

6. **Temporal confounding:** The temporal gradient (higher immune levels → shorter time to infection) is compatible with two very different interpretations: (a) elevated baseline immunity predicts susceptibility, or (b) participants were already in early subclinical COVID-19 infection at baseline but not yet PCR-detectable. The < 4 month time window is particularly susceptible to this confound, as incubation periods and early immune responses can precede diagnosis by weeks.

7. **No vaccination status adjustment:** Enrollment began June 2020 and COVID-19 vaccines were not yet available for all HCWs during the early follow-up period; the paper mentions vaccination screening was part of enrollment but does not report how vaccination during follow-up (after late 2020/early 2021) was handled as a covariate.

8. **Single-center Brazilian cohort:** Findings may not generalize across genetic backgrounds, SARS-CoV-2 variant landscape (pre-Delta/Omicron era), or healthcare systems.

9. **No longitudinal re-profiling:** Immune mediators were measured once at baseline. Serial measurements would allow distinguishing stable trait-like immune profiles from transient pre-infection states.

## Follow-up

- The symptom persistence analyses here (≥ 4 weeks cut-off) are a natural bridge to studies using WHO-defined long COVID (≥ 12 weeks); comparing baseline predictors between these two thresholds would clarify how far pre-infection immune state predicts chronic vs early-resolving symptoms.
- Parallel pre-infection baseline profiling studies should be sought for other PAIS triggers (EBV, influenza, dengue) to test whether the elevated-immune-activation pattern generalizes (directly relevant to question:0001).
- The G-CSF finding as a consistent predictor of severity, persistence, and symptomatic conversion is notable and aligns with published literature on G-CSF in COVID-19 severity; reviewing mechanistic work on G-CSF and emergency granulopoiesis in the PAIS context may be warranted.
- The integrative network complexity finding (more connections in susceptible, fewer in severe/prolonged groups) echoes the "unbalanced network" concept; see related work by Jardim-Santos et al. (ref [34] in this paper) from overlapping authors on disturbed kinetics of serum soluble mediators.
- Authors call for longitudinal immune profiling studies that examine how baseline profiles change with vaccination and prior infections — directly relevant to PAIS project design considerations.
