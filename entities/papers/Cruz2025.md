# Persistence of dysfunctional immune response 12 months after SARS-CoV-2 infection and their relationship with pulmonary sequelae and long COVID

## Key Contribution

This prospective observational study is the first to directly compare the systemic immune and organ-damage protein profiles of post-COVID pulmonary sequelae (PS) and long COVID (LC) patients against fully recovered controls at 12 months post-discharge, demonstrating that PS and LC are biologically distinct conditions sharing a common antiviral inflammatory background but differing in their downstream pathology. PS patients display elevated anti-nuclear autoantibody rates and complement C3, with key cytokines correlating negatively with lung function (DLCO), while LC patients show increased circulating organ-damage markers — a dissociation that argues against a unitary biological model of post-COVID illness. The temporal trajectory in PS also identifies CCL20 and IFN-γ as early biomarkers (elevated at 6 months), and CCL3 and CCL19 as markers of progressive perpetuation (rising from 6 to 12 months), pointing to adaptive immune recruitment as a driver of long-term pulmonary impairment.

## Methods

**Design:** Prospective, observational, controlled cohort study; 113 adults hospitalized for PCR-confirmed COVID-19 pneumonia at Hospital Clinic Barcelona, May–November 2020. All participants were unvaccinated at the time of their acute episode.

**Cohort stratification at 12 months post-discharge:**
- Pulmonary Sequelae (PS): n=51, defined by DLCO <80% reference
- Long COVID (LC): n=31, defined by persistence of symptoms >2 months post-onset without alternative diagnosis and DLCO ≥80% reference (cases with DLCO <80% or known prior pulmonary disease were excluded from LC)
- Recovered (Rec): n=31, asymptomatic with DLCO >80% reference

**Measurements at 12 months:**
- Complement (C3, C4, CH50) by turbidimetric immunoassay
- Immunoglobulin subtypes (IgG, IgA, IgM) by turbidimetric immunoassay
- IgG autoantibodies by indirect immunofluorescence (IFA) on HEp-2 cells (titer ≥1/80 positive); ICAP nomenclature for ANA/ANCA patterns
- Specific autoantibodies panel (anti-U1-RNP, Sm, Ro52, Ro60, SS-B, Scl-70, Jo-1, Cenp-B, Ribosomal-P) by PMAT on Aptiva instrument; anti-IFNα/IFNω by Luminex
- 184 inflammatory and organ-damage proteins by Olink proximity extension assay (Inflammation and Organ Damage panels, 96-plex PEA)
- GDF-15 and WFDC2 by ELISA
- For PS: additional time-course measurements at 6 months

**Statistics:** Kruskal–Wallis with Wilcoxon pairwise post-hoc and FDR adjustment; Spearman correlations; Chi-square; paired t-tests for 6-to-12-month trajectory. Gene Ontology (GO) and KEGG pathway enrichment via ClusterProfiler; STRING protein interaction networks.

## Key Findings

**Autoantibodies and immunoglobulins:**
- IgM total dose and IgG/IgA, IgM/IgA ratios significantly elevated in LC vs. Rec (IgM p=0.032; IgG/IgA p=0.013; IgM/IgA p=0.012); PS showed similar immunoglobulin levels to Rec.
- Anti-nuclear reactivities (any ANA pattern) significantly increased in PS (23.5%) vs. Rec (3.2%), p=0.033; trend in LC (22.6%) did not reach significance (p=0.058).
- No significant difference in any specific autoantibody subtype between groups.

**Inflammatory proteins (Olink Inflammation panel, 96 proteins):**
- 21 of 96 proteins differed across groups.
- Shared elevated in both PS and LC vs. Rec: IFN-γ, IL-8, MCP-4, SIRT2, TNFSF14 — representing a common persistent antiviral/inflammatory state.
- PS-exclusive elevation vs. Rec: CCL20, CCL3, CCL19 — lymphocyte-homing chemokines; C3 complement protein elevated specifically in PS vs. LC.
- LC-exclusive elevation vs. Rec: CXCL5, AXIN1, CXCL1, STAMBP, CXCL6, IL-7.
- IL-6 significantly higher in PS vs. LC.

**DLCO correlation in PS:**
- 6 of 8 cytokines elevated in PS (CCL19, CCL20, CCL3, IFN-γ, IL-8, MCP-4) negatively correlated with DLCO at 12 months; elevated levels → more severe lung function impairment.
- At 6 months, only CCL20 (p=0.035) and IFN-γ (p=0.026) were elevated in PS vs. Rec — these are early biomarkers.
- CCL3 (p=0.006) and CCL19 (p=0.01) increased significantly from 6 to 12 months in PS, suggesting progressive adaptive immune recruitment driving continued pulmonary impairment.

**Organ-damage proteins (Olink Organ Damage panel):**
- No significant difference in organ-damage proteins between PS and Rec.
- LC had 22 proteins with altered plasma levels vs. Rec, 17 of which were also elevated vs. PS — a stark LC-specific signature.
- Several LC-elevated proteins linked to SARS-CoV-2 viral replication or immune evasion: FKBP1B, PRKAB1, CSNK1D (viral replication), KIM1 (SMAD1-related viral entry), FOXO1 (airway inflammation), STX8/YES1 (T-cell activation), ERBB2IP/INPP1L (inflammation), MVK/BANK1 (autoimmune association); 13 of 22 with immune-related functions.
- TNNI3 (Troponin I) and CALCA (calcitonin) were uniquely reduced in LC.
- GDF-15 and WFDC2 (chronic lung disease biomarkers) elevated in both PS and Rec vs. LC; both negatively correlated with DLCO at 12 months (GDF-15: R=−0.34, p=0.004; WFDC2: R=−0.26, p=0.015).

**Pathway enrichment:**
- Shared PS+LC: IL-17 signaling, cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, NF-κB signaling, Toll-like receptor signaling, chemokine signaling.
- PS-specific enrichment: acute inflammation, tissue repair, T-cell-related functional enrichment.
- LC-specific enrichment: antimicrobial immune responses, cytotoxic features; KEGG enrichment includes Chagas disease and viral infection pathways, consistent with a viral reservoir hypothesis.

## Relevance

This paper directly addresses the core PAIS research question (`research-question:post-acute-infection-syndromes`): why do some individuals fail to recover after acute SARS-CoV-2 infection, and do distinct post-acute clinical phenotypes share or diverge in their underlying biology?

**Homeostatic recovery failure framing:** At 12 months, neither PS nor LC patients have returned immune markers to baseline — both retain elevated IFN-γ, IL-8, MCP-4, SIRT2, and TNFSF14. This directly supports the PAIS frame of *failed homeostatic return* after acute infection: the immune system remains activated rather than resolving. The shared antiviral/inflammatory signature (IFN-γ elevation, NF-κB and TLR pathway enrichment in both groups) is consistent with persistent viral antigen driving ongoing innate immune stimulation.

**Two failure modes, one trigger:** The PS vs. LC dissociation provides a rare direct biological contrast within PAIS. PS features complement activation (C3), lymphocyte-homing chemokines (CCL3, CCL19, CCL20), and anti-nuclear autoantibodies — a pattern of immune-mediated pulmonary damage resembling autoimmune fibrotic lung disease. LC features circulating organ-damage markers with many proteins linked to viral replication and immune evasion, supporting the viral reservoir hypothesis for LC. This aligns with the PAIS project's working frame that "partly convergent, partly trigger-specific mechanisms" can feed distinct phenotypes.

**Temporal dynamics:** The 6-to-12-month rise of CCL3 and CCL19 in PS implies that the immune dysregulation in pulmonary sequelae is not merely residual but is progressive — the homeostatic recovery failure is self-perpetuating, driven by adaptive immune recruitment.

**Autoimmunity sub-theme:** The significant enrichment of anti-nuclear autoantibodies in PS (23.5% vs. 3.2% Rec; p=0.033) and the complement C3 elevation represent a molecular autoimmune signature connecting to the `health-immunity` peer project's frame on infection-triggered autoimmunity.

## Limitations

1. **No pre-COVID baseline:** Lung function was not measured before hospitalization; DLCO deficit could predate infection in some patients. Mitigation: patients with known prior pulmonary disease were excluded (only 4 asthma patients excluded).
2. **Sample size:** n=51/31/31 is modest; limits power for subgroup analyses and replication of specific protein-level findings.
3. **Plasma-only biomarkers:** Plasma reflects systemic levels; does not capture local lung tissue biology. Authors acknowledge this is a limitation for interpreting PS lung pathology.
4. **PS group had higher pre-existing cardiovascular comorbidities** (49% vs. 22.6% Rec, p=0.032) — non-pulmonary symptom differences between PS and Rec may partly reflect comorbidities rather than post-COVID biology.
5. **Acute-disease severity confounding:** PS patients had more severe acute COVID (higher WHO scores, more ICU admissions) than LC, so PS vs. LC differences could partly reflect severity of original illness rather than distinct post-acute mechanisms.
6. **Unvaccinated cohort (2020 wave):** All participants were hospitalized in 2020 before vaccination was available; findings may not generalize to vaccinated individuals or to mild/non-hospitalized COVID-19.
7. **Case definition overlap risk:** Long COVID defined as symptoms >2 months; patients with impaired DLCO were excluded from LC, but the boundary between PS-with-symptoms and LC-with-mild-DLCO-dip may not be perfectly clean in practice.
8. **Spanish cohort, Barcelona hospital:** Single-center; ethnic, socioeconomic, and healthcare-system specificities limit generalizability.

- **Peluso2024** (already in project): plasma antigen persistence in post-acute COVID — cross-reference viral reservoir evidence in the LC organ-damage signature.
- Key question this paper raises: do CCL3/CCL19/CCL20 trajectories distinguish progressive vs. stable PS, and can early (6-month) CCL20/IFN-γ levels predict 12-month DLCO outcome? (This would define actionable early biomarkers for PS.)
- Mechanistic gap: what drives CCL3/CCL19 rise from 6 to 12 months in PS — ongoing viral antigen, autoimmune attack, or fibrotic remodeling? Studies combining BAL and plasma proteomics needed.
