# Immune-cell signatures of persistent inflammation, immunosuppression, and catabolism syndrome after sepsis

- **Authors:** Xing-Feng Sun, Wen-Chen Luo, Shao-Qiang Huang, Yi-Jun Zheng, Lei Xiao, Zhong-Wei Zhang, Rong-Hua Liu, Zi-Wen Zhong, Jie-Qiong Song, Ke Nan, Zhi-Xin Qiu, Jing Zhong, Chang-Hong Miao
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal:** Med, vol. 6, article 100569
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.medj.2024.12.003
- **BibTeX key:** Sun2025
- **Source:** PDF

## Key Contribution

This paper delivers the first comprehensive single-cell transcriptomic atlas of peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) in patients with persistent inflammation, immunosuppression, and catabolism syndrome (PICS) following sepsis — a post-acute critical-illness state with ICU mortality of 24.2% vs. 12.3% in sepsis without PICS. Profiling 91,180 high-quality cells from 16 elderly participants (6 PICS, 5 acute sepsis, 5 healthy controls) across 23 immune-cell clusters, the study characterises the specific immune-cell subpopulations driving PICS pathophysiology, identifies prognostic immune signatures distinguishing PICS survivors from non-survivors, and demonstrates that megakaryocytes (MKs) exert previously unrecognised immunomodulatory effects in the PICS milieu. Murine cecal ligation and puncture (CLP) models validated the key single-cell findings.

## Methods

- **Study design:** Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq; 10x Genomics) of PBMCs from 16 elderly (≥65 years) adult participants: 6 PICS patients (further divided into PICS-death, n = 2, PD-01/PD-02; PICS-alive, n = 4, PA-01 to PA-04), 5 acute sepsis patients (AS-01 to AS-05), and 5 healthy controls (HC-01 to HC-05). All PICS cases arose from bacterial infection.
- **Cell atlas:** 91,180 high-quality cells after QC; 23 UMAP clusters assigned to 8 cell types: B cells (CD79A, CD19), dendritic cells (CLEC10A, CD1C), megakaryocytes (MK; PF4, PPBP), monocytes (VCAN, FCN1, CD14, LYZ), NK cells (GNLY, KLRF1), plasmacytoid DCs (SOX4, CLEC4C), plasma cells (CD79A, JCHAIN, IGHA1), and T cells (CD3D, CD3E).
- **Monocyte sub-clustering:** 6 monocyte sub-clusters (Mono1–6) re-resolved from the main atlas; Mono1 and Mono4 characterised as myeloid-derived suppressor cell (MDSC)-like via GO/GSEA and ssGSEA (cytokine-cytokine receptor pathway score comparison).
- **B cell sub-clustering:** 5 sub-clusters: naive B, memory B, IGHA1-plasma, IGHA2-plasma, IGHG1-plasma.
- **T cell sub-clustering:** 10 sub-clusters including 4 CD4 (naive, Tem, Tmix, Treg) and 6 CD8 (naive, MAIT, Tem, Tcm, Cycling, CD8TEMRA) cells.
- **MK sub-clustering:** 1379 MKs stratified into 5 sub-clusters (MK1–MK5).
- **Validation:** Murine CLP PICS model (n = 5–6 mice per group, sham/sepsis/PICS); MK adoptive transfer assay; cytokine/chemokine panel by Luminex; qPCR for IFITM2, IFITM3, S100A9; flow cytometry for MK counts.
- **Cell-cell communication:** CellphoneDB and CellChat analysis of ligand-receptor interactions across PBMC subpopulations.
- **Pseudotime:** Monocle2 trajectory analysis on monocyte scRNA-seq data.
- **Statistical tests:** Two-sided Wilcoxon rank-sum test for DEGs; one-way ANOVA or Kruskal-Wallis for cytokine comparisons; Mann-Whitney for selected pairwise comparisons.

## Key Findings

### Monocyte suppression (Mono1/Mono4 MDSC-like subsets)
- Mono1 and Mono4 were substantially enriched in PICS and sepsis patients (22.5% and 28.3% of monocytes, respectively vs. 10.3% in HCs).
- Both subsets expressed a CD14+, S100A8/9+, HLA-DR−, HLA-DMA−, HLA-DQB1− signature consistent with monocytic MDSCs (M-MDSCs), with low HLA-DR expression indicating immune immaturity and impaired stimulation capacity.
- Immune function (cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction pathway) was significantly lower in Mono1 and Mono4 in sepsis vs. HCs; partially restored in PICS vs. sepsis.
- DEG analysis showed Mono1/Mono4 upregulate PLAC8, RETN, CLU, CD163 in sepsis (worse prognosis markers); S100A8, S100A9, S100A12 are upregulated in PICS vs. sepsis while HLA family is downregulated vs. HCs.
- PICS-death patients showed further reductions in all Mono1–6 subsets compared to PICS-alive, consistent with immunoparalysis predicting fatal outcome.
- Pseudotime analysis: Mono1 and Mono4 predominate in early/medium disease phases (M-MDSC states); trajectory positions consistent with progressive immune suppression.

### B cell and plasma cell dysregulation
- Naive B and memory B cell proportions decreased in both PICS and sepsis vs. HCs; plasma cells proliferated, with the highest plasma cell proportion in sepsis.
- In the PICS-death group, plasma cell counts and all three plasma sub-clusters decreased both quantitatively and functionally, evidencing suppressed humoral immunity.
- PICS-alive patients showed more active memory B cells and IGHA1-plasma cells vs. PICS-death. IGHA1-plasma cells upregulated IGHV3-33, IGLV7-46, IGLV2-8 (PICS-alive vs. PICS-death), indicating enhanced IgA antibody production capacity correlates with survival.
- Naive and memory B cells upregulated IFITM1, IFITM2, IFITM3, and AIM2 in PICS vs. sepsis, indicating enhanced antigen-processing and innate immune response participation in the recovery phase.
- S100A8 and S100A9 were upregulated across all B cell subsets in PICS-alive vs. PICS-death.

### CD8TEMRA dysfunction and Treg dysfunction in PICS-death
- CD8TEMRA cells were more enriched in sepsis than PICS. PICS-death patients showed markedly increased CD8TEMRA proportions and a decrease in Tregs compared to PICS-alive.
- CD8TEMRA in PICS-death upregulated genes related to NK/T cell activation, granzyme-mediated apoptotic signalling, and antigen processing, but downregulated cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction genes — a dual activation/immunosuppression phenotype.
- LAG3 (T cell exhaustion marker) significantly upregulated in CD8 T cell subtypes in PICS, particularly in PICS-death. TIGIT elevated in Tregs across all participants. Pro-apoptotic genes TNFRSF14 and CD27 upregulated in all T cell subtypes in PICS-death.
- Tregs in PICS-death: CD69 expression (activation marker) elevated; DUSP1 and FOS significantly higher (inhibit proliferation, promote apoptosis) — activated but functionally suppressed, associated with poor prognosis.
- Immune-regulation genes HLA-DRB5, S100A8, IGKV3, JCHAIN downregulated in Tregs of PICS-death, indicating potential suppression of Treg function.

### Megakaryocyte immunomodulatory role
- 1379 MKs (5 sub-clusters); MK count was higher in all disease groups vs. HCs, most pronounced in PICS-death.
- MK1 accounted for ~46.5% of total MKs in patients (vs. ~7.1% in non-infectious human bone marrow); MK1 gene sets enriched for antigen processing/presentation, neutrophil degranulation — indicating immune-related function shift.
- IFITM2 and IFITM3 (anti-infective) and S100A9 (inflammatory regulator) were consistently upregulated in MKs from PICS and sepsis vs. sham in both human scRNA-seq and murine qPCR validation.
- MK adoptive transfer experiment: injection of PICS mouse-derived MKs into PICS mice reduced pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α significantly reduced; TGF-β, IL-4, IL-12p40, IL-12p70, IL-17A inhibited; CCL2, CCL3, CCL4, CXCL1, IL-10 decreased). No equivalent effect with septic-mouse-derived MKs — anti-inflammatory capacity is PICS-specific.
- Septic mouse MK injection had no significant effect on TNF-α in septic recipients, confirming the PICS-specific immunomodulatory character.

### Cell-cell communication
- PICS-alive patients had 15,960 inferred ligand-receptor interactions vs. 7,111 in PICS-death (over 2× reduction), with lower overall interaction strength (98.76 vs. 92.91) — generalised immune communication collapse in fatal PICS.
- Mono1 and Mono4 exerted inhibitory effects on naive B and memory B cells via TNFSF13 >> TNFRSF14, FAS, TNFSF10 >> TNFRSF10A pathways; pro-apoptotic effects stronger in sepsis and weaker in PICS.
- Mono1 and Mono4 inhibited CD8T cells more than CD4T cells; CD8TEMRA via TGFBR1+2 in all three groups; CD8TEMRA via TGFBR1 only in PICS.
- TGF-β pathway: TGFB1-(TGFBR1+TGFBR2) signalling weakened in PICS-death (autocrine/paracrine Treg-to-Mono1/4 signal suppressed).
- IFN-I pathway activated only in PICS-alive, suggesting antiviral/adaptive immune responses are reduced in patients who die from PICS.
- GALECTIN, IFN-II, IL16, CD40-related pathways decreased in PICS-alive; CCL, CXCL, CD70, TGF-β, LIGHT, VEGF increased in PICS-alive — highlighting specific pro-recovery communication axes.

## Relevance

This paper is directly relevant to `research-question:post-acute-infection-syndromes` and the PAIS frame of failed homeostatic recovery after acute infection in several ways:

1. **PICS as a prototypical non-viral PAIS.** PICS following sepsis is a structurally identical failure mode to long COVID/ME/CFS: immune dysregulation persists long after the initiating acute insult, systemic inflammation is sustained, and the homeostatic setpoint is not restored. The paper provides the highest-resolution immune-cell map yet of this failure mode from peripheral blood, analogous to (and complementary to) single-cell atlases in long COVID.

2. **Immune paralysis as shared post-infectious failure mode.** The M-MDSC monocyte signature (Mono1/Mono4: HLA-DR−, S100A8/9+) in PICS mirrors findings reported post-COVID and post-trauma: myeloid compartment reprogramming from classically inflammatory to immunosuppressive phenotypes is a recurrent feature across PAIS triggers. The partial restoration from sepsis to PICS is consistent with the hypothesis that PAIS is a recovery trajectory that stalls before homeostatic baseline is reached.

3. **Single-cell immune atlas for PICS enables cross-PAIS comparison.** The 23-cluster PBMC atlas stratified by disease state (HC, sepsis, PICS-alive, PICS-death) provides a reference framework for comparing immune cell composition across different PAIS. Key biomarker contrasts — IGHA1-plasma cell activity, memory B cell antigen-processing signature, LAG3/TIGIT exhaustion markers, MK immunomodulatory expansion — are candidate cross-PAIS signals worth examining in long COVID and ME/CFS datasets.

4. **Prognosis prediction.** PICS-alive vs. PICS-death differences (active memory B / IGHA1-plasma cells, Treg function, CD8TEMRA phenotype, MK count) define a prognostic immune state that could parallel the recovery-vs-non-recovery dichotomy seen in other PAIS. This maps directly onto the project's interest in why some individuals recover and others do not.

5. **Megakaryocyte immunomodulation as a novel PAIS mechanism.** The demonstration that PICS-derived MKs reduce pro-inflammatory cytokines through adoptive transfer is a novel mechanism. If MKs play similar roles in long COVID or ME/CFS (where thrombocytopenia or platelet activation are reported), this adds a non-leukocyte arm to the PAIS failure-mode model.

6. **Age and population specificity.** All participants were ≥65 years; this is a useful anchor for the known age-skew of severe PICS and potentially of other severe PAIS presentations.

## Limitations

- **Very small human cohort.** N = 16 participants (6 PICS, 5 sepsis, 5 HC); PICS sub-groups are n = 2 (PICS-death) and n = 4 (PICS-alive). Single-cell resolution per participant is high, but inter-individual variability and statistical power at the group level are severely limited. All reported DEGs and group differences should be treated as hypothesis-generating.
- **Elderly-only population (≥65 years).** The age restriction was deliberate for cohort homogeneity but limits generalisability to younger PICS or PAIS patients; immune-cell composition changes dramatically with age.
- **Bacterial infection aetiology only.** All PICS cases arose from bacterial infections. The paper explicitly cautions against extrapolating to PICS from cancer or trauma, and findings may not extend to viral-triggered post-acute syndromes (e.g., long COVID).
- **Male mice only.** The murine CLP validation used male mice exclusively; oestrous-cycle effects in female mice are excluded, limiting cross-sex generalisability.
- **Immunocompromised patients excluded.** Patients receiving immunomodulatory medications were excluded; this subgroup (relevant for transplant patients, rheumatic disease) is important but uncharacterised.
- **Single time-point sampling.** PBMC collection at one point in the PICS disease course; longitudinal immune dynamics from sepsis through PICS to recovery or death are not captured.
- **No integration with clinical metadata.** The scRNA-seq resource was not powered to correlate molecular findings with demographic or clinical features due to sample size.
- **MK adoptive transfer is a proof-of-concept mouse experiment.** The specificity and mechanism of PICS MK immunosuppression is not yet characterised at the receptor/ligand level in human tissue.

## Model / Tool Availability

- **scRNA-seq data:** Deposited in NCBI GEO (accession GSE217906), publicly available as of publication.
- **No custom software:** The paper reports no new unique reagents or bespoke computational tools; all analyses used established packages (Seurat, Monocle2, CellphoneDB, CellChat, standard GSEA pipelines).

## Follow-up

- Compare the Mono1/Mono4 M-MDSC signature (S100A8/9+, HLA-DR−) to myeloid cluster profiles in published long COVID scRNA-seq datasets (e.g., Su et al. 2022, Zhang et al. 2020) — is the same immunosuppressive monocyte subset enriched post-COVID?
- Examine GSE217906 (GEO) data directly to extract per-sample cell-type proportions and DEG tables for computational integration with other PAIS datasets.
- Read Reyes et al. (Nat. Med. 2020, ref 15) on MS1 immunosuppressive monocyte state in bacterial sepsis — the M-MDSC framing here extends that finding into the PICS phase.
- Investigate whether megakaryocyte expansion is reported in long COVID or ME/CFS PBMC studies; if so, test whether the PICS MK1 immune gene signature is shared.
- Examine whether the IFN-I activation signal in PICS-alive parallels IFN-I persistence (which is associated with poor outcomes in long COVID in some cohorts) — the direction of IFN-I association may differ across PAIS.
- Ariel et al. (Front. Immunol. 2024, ref 37 in paper) on post-sepsis peripheral myeloid compartment diversity — cross-reference with the MDSC characterisation in this paper.
