# Insights From Invasive Cardiopulmonary Exercise Testing of Patients With Myalgic Encephalomyelitis/Chronic Fatigue Syndrome

- **Authors:** Phillip Joseph, Carlo Arevalo, Rudolf K.F. Oliveira, Mariana Faria-Urbina, Donna Felsenstein, Anne Louise Oaklander, David M. Systrom
- **Year:** 2021
- **Journal:** Chest, vol. 160, no. 2, pp. 642–651
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.chest.2021.01.082
- **PMID:** 33577778
- **PMCID:** PMC8727854
- **BibTeX key:** Joseph2021
- **Source:** web search + Europe PMC abstract + PMC full text

## Key Contribution

This is the first study to systematically assess small-fiber neuropathy (SFN) using skin punch biopsy in a large ME/CFS cohort (n = 160). Using PGP9.5-immunolabeled lower-leg skin biopsies alongside invasive cardiopulmonary exercise testing (iCPET), the study demonstrates that 31% of ME/CFS patients have objectively reduced epidermal innervation (≤5th percentile of sex/age/race-adjusted norms), providing the first skin-biopsy-confirmed prevalence estimate of SFN in ME/CFS. It also identifies two distinct hemodynamic subtypes — preload failure (impaired venous return → low cardiac output) and peripheral oxygen extraction failure (microvascular shunting) — and proposes that small-fiber autonomic neuropathy may underlie both through neuropathic dysregulation of microvascular tone.

## Methods

- **Design:** Retrospective case-control analysis from a prospective invasive exercise testing program at Brigham and Women's Hospital. 1,516 upright iCPETs were screened; 160 ME/CFS patients (right atrial pressure [RAP] < 6.5 mm Hg) with available skin biopsy results were compared to 36 healthy controls with normal iCPET.
- **ME/CFS case definition:** National Academy of Medicine (2015) criteria — three major criteria required: (1) substantial reduction/impairment in activity with fatigue ≥6 months; (2) post-exertional malaise; (3) unrefreshing sleep; plus one of two minor criteria: cognitive impairment or orthostatic intolerance. [NOTE: NAM 2015 criteria do not require post-infectious onset; onset was not systematically characterized.]
- **Post-infectious characterization:** Preceding infection documented in 24% (39/160) of ME/CFS patients vs. 0% of controls. No detailed characterization of trigger organism, duration, or severity.
- **Skin biopsy — DISTAL-ONLY:** Standard single-site, 3-mm punch biopsy from a site 10 cm proximal to the lateral malleolus (lower leg). PGP9.5 immunolabeling. SFN defined as epidermal innervation ≤5th percentile of sex-, age-, and race-adjusted laboratory norms. NO proximal (thigh) biopsies were performed; non-length-dependent vs. length-dependent pattern was therefore NOT analyzed or reported.
- **Biopsy context note:** Authors explicitly acknowledge the limitation of single-site distal biopsy, noting that studies using wider sampling (including upper thigh) identified proximal denervation as more prevalent (46% upper-thigh vs. 32% distal-leg).
- **Autonomic testing:** iCPET measured right heart pressures, cardiac output (Qc), and VO2; Qc/VO2 slope used to assign tertiles (low-, normal-, high-flow). No formal autonomic reflex screen (QSART, tilt table) reported.
- **Controls:** 36 normal-iCPET subjects (no intrinsic cardiac/pulmonary disease). No primary dysautonomia or idiopathic POTS control arm.
- **Autoantibody serology:** Not performed.
- **Statistical approach:** Comparison of ME/CFS vs. controls using t-tests and chi-square; correlation of SFN presence with hemodynamic tertiles tested.

## Key Findings

**SFN prevalence:**
- 31% of ME/CFS patients (n = 160) had skin-biopsy-confirmed SFN by the ≤5th percentile criterion (p < 0.0001 vs. controls — controls were at 0% by design given the normal-iCPET selection).
- This is the largest ME/CFS skin-biopsy cohort reported at time of publication.

**Hemodynamic subtypes:**
- Low-flow tertile (preload failure, impaired venous return): reduced peak cardiac output (88.4% ± 19% predicted) and lower RAP vs. other tertiles.
- High-flow tertile (peripheral extraction failure, microvascular shunting): impaired systemic oxygen extraction (0.74 ± 0.1 vs. 0.86–0.88 in other tertiles; p < 0.0001).
- All three tertiles had similar low RAP overall vs. controls, consistent with the broader preload-failure phenotype in ME/CFS.

**SFN and hemodynamics:**
- SFN denervation severity did NOT correlate with the iCPET exertional measures (including VO2 peak, Qc/VO2 slope). The co-occurrence of SFN and hemodynamic abnormalities is descriptive, not a demonstrated causal path within the data.

**Preceding infection:**
- 24% of ME/CFS patients had documented preceding infection; no formal sub-group analysis of SFN prevalence in infection-triggered vs. non-infection-triggered cases was reported. [UNVERIFIED: whether Fukuda or any other case definition sub-analyses were performed in the supplementary material]

## Relevance

This paper directly supports **P1 of hypothesis:0007** (structural SFN lesion is objectively present in ME/CFS) and is the principal primary-data evidence for the ME/CFS leg of **P4** (cross-trigger SFN convergence: PTLDS, long COVID, ME/CFS). Specific connections:

1. **P1 support (structural IENFD lesion in ME/CFS):** 31% biopsy-confirmed SFN in a 160-patient cohort is the largest and most rigorously controlled ME/CFS skin-biopsy prevalence estimate available. It establishes that objective small-fiber pathology occurs in a substantial minority of ME/CFS patients using NAM criteria.

2. **P4 partial support (cross-trigger convergence):** The SFN finding, combined with the existing literature from Oaklander2022 (long COVID, proximal+distal, ~50–63% SFN) and the PTLDS literature (Adler2024), fills the ME/CFS leg of P4. However, no head-to-head cross-syndrome comparison using one protocol is available, so convergence remains inferential.

3. **P2 gap (non-length-dependent pattern):** This study used distal-only biopsies and explicitly did NOT analyze the non-length-dependent vs. length-dependent distinction. The authors' own footnote acknowledging higher proximal rates (46% thigh vs. 32% leg) in other literature hints at possible non-length-dependent pathology but cannot confirm it in this cohort. This is the **main limitation for hypothesis:0007 P2**.

4. **Dysautonomia–SFN substrate link:** The iCPET data show hemodynamic features (preload failure, microvascular shunting) plausibly driven by autonomic nerve dysfunction, consistent with the hypothesis that SFN disrupts microvascular autonomic regulation. However, the absence of QSART or formal autonomic reflex screen means the autonomic-fiber specificity of the SFN is inferred, not directly measured.

5. **Post-infectious ME/CFS fraction:** 24% had documented preceding infection — a substantial post-infectious subgroup, though not systematically analyzed for SFN enrichment. This is a missed opportunity to directly test whether post-infectious-onset ME/CFS has higher SFN rates.

## Limitations

- **Distal-only skin biopsy:** The single lower-leg site precludes detection of non-length-dependent SFN and means the 31% figure is likely an *underestimate* (authors' own note: proximal thigh biopsies detect higher rates, ~46%). P2 of hypothesis:0007 cannot be assessed from this study.
- **No proximal controls:** 36 "normal iCPET" controls are not representative of a population healthy control group; they share the same cardiopulmonary referral setting, introducing selection bias.
- **No primary-dysautonomia control arm:** Idiopathic POTS, familial dysautonomia, or other primary autonomic disorder comparison is absent, making it impossible to determine whether the SFN rate or hemodynamic pattern distinguishes PAIS from primary causes.
- **Retrospective design with selection bias:** Cohort required both iCPET referral AND available skin biopsy result — likely enriched for more severe or dysautonomia-prominent ME/CFS; the 31% figure may not generalize to broader ME/CFS populations.
- **NAM 2015 criteria (broader than CCC/ICC):** Including patients by NAM criteria (which omit the CCC requirement for autonomic symptoms and the ICC's wider neurological/autonomic symptom burden) may capture a heterogeneous group; SFN rate may differ under stricter CCC/ICC definitions.
- **No autoantibody serology:** P3 of hypothesis:0007 (autoimmune causation) is entirely unaddressed. The SFN could reflect non-immune causes (metabolic, hereditary, toxic).
- **No correlation between SFN and post-infectious onset:** 24% had preceding infections but no sub-group SFN analysis was reported — the post-infectious ME/CFS leg specifically is underpowered in this dataset.
- **SFN denervation severity did not correlate with exercise measures:** The absence of a dose-response between SFN severity and exercise impairment weakens the causal claim that SFN drives exercise intolerance in the proposed microvascular-shunting model.

No computational model, software, or dataset released with this paper. Underlying iCPET data are from a single-institution clinical program; raw data not publicly available per the paper.

## Follow-up

- **Joseph2023** (paper:Joseph2023): A follow-up study from the same group examining neurovascular dysregulation in ME/CFS and post-acute sequelae of SARS-CoV-2 — directly extends the hemodynamic-SFN framework from this paper.
- **Oaklander2022** (cite:Oaklander2022): The long-COVID counterpart study using paired distal+proximal biopsies — the methodological advance needed to answer P2. Directly complements Joseph2021 for the cross-syndrome comparison.
- **Key open questions this paper raises:**
  1. Would adding a proximal (thigh) biopsy site in the same cohort raise the SFN detection rate toward 46% and reveal a non-length-dependent pattern (addressing hypothesis:0007 P2)?
  2. In ME/CFS patients with documented post-infectious onset (24% of cohort), is SFN enriched vs. non-post-infectious-onset patients — directly testing P4?
  3. Would autoantibody serology (anti-GPCR, TS-HDS, FGFR3) in SFN-positive ME/CFS patients reveal an autoimmune etiology (addressing P3)?
  4. Is the 31% SFN rate stable across ME/CFS case definitions (CCC vs. ICC vs. NAM), or does it vary with case-definition strictness?
