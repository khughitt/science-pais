# Interventions for the management of long covid (post-covid condition): living systematic review

- **Authors:** Dena Zeraatkar, Michael Ling, Sarah Kirsh, et al. (20 authors); last: Jason W. Busse
- **Year:** 2024
- **Journal:** BMJ (The BMJ), vol. 387, e081318
- **DOI:** 10.1136/bmj-2024-081318
- **PMID:** 39603702
- **PMCID:** PMC11600537
- **BibTeX key:** Zeraatkar2024
- **Source:** Europe PMC full-text XML (CC BY 4.0)

## Key Contribution

The first iteration of a living systematic review of RCTs testing interventions for long COVID, covering 24 trials (3,695 patients) published through December 2023 across six intervention categories. Using the GRADE framework, the review found **moderate-certainty evidence for only three findings**: online CBT probably reduces fatigue and improves concentration; a combined physical and mental health rehabilitation programme probably increases recovery/meaningful improvement and reduces depression; and intermittent aerobic exercise probably improves physical function more than continuous exercise. All other interventions — including antivirals, dietary supplements, hyperbaric oxygen, and neurostimulation — were supported by only low or very low certainty evidence, or came from trials with integrity concerns. The review serves as the landscape map for the therapeutics space in long COVID as of late 2024, and will be updated for up to two years from publication.

## Methods

**Study design:** Living systematic review and meta-analysis; registered on Open Science Framework (https://osf.io/9h7zm/).

**Search:** Medline, Embase, CINAHL, PsycInfo, Allied and Complementary Medicine Database, and Cochrane CENTRAL from inception to December 2023; supplemented via Epistemonikos COVID-19 Repository and expert solicitation through February 2024. Duplicate screening and extraction in pairs with adjudication.

**Eligibility:** RCTs enrolling adults (≥18 years) meeting WHO long COVID definition (symptoms ≥3 months after confirmed/probable/suspected COVID-19 that persist ≥2 months), randomised to any drug or non-drug intervention vs placebo, sham, or usual care. Minimum 25 participants per arm. Excluded: trials targeting only anosmia/hyposmia; trials where ≥20% of patients had recovered <3 months before randomisation; pseudorandomised designs.

**Long COVID subtypes tracked:** General symptoms (fatigue, reduced functional capacity), respiratory sequelae (dyspnoea-predominant), neurological/cognitive sequelae.

**Outcomes:** Patient-important outcomes informed by a published core outcome set and patient partners: fatigue, pain, post-exertional malaise (PEM), changes in employment/education, cognitive function, mental health, dyspnoea, quality of life, patient-reported physical function, recovery/improvement, and serious adverse events.

**Risk of bias:** Modified Cochrane RoB 2.0 tool (five domains). Trials without blinding of patients/providers/investigators were considered high risk of bias for deviations from intended intervention and outcome measurement, unless two active arms were matched for patient-provider interaction. The TRACT checklist (19 items) was applied to screen for data fabrication, falsification, and analytic errors.

**Synthesis:** Network meta-analysis was planned but not feasible given evidence sparsity and outcome heterogeneity. Frequentist random-effects pairwise meta-analyses were performed where pooling was possible; otherwise individual trial results are described. Effects reported as mean differences (continuous) or absolute risks per 1,000 patients (dichotomous). MIDs (minimal important differences) sourced from published literature for other chronic conditions (no long COVID-specific MIDs exist); 0.5 SD at baseline used as distribution-based MID when anchored estimates unavailable. GRADE certainty ratings applied to each intervention-outcome pair.

**Statistical software:** R, `meta` package v4.1.2. Data and code publicly available on OSF.

## Key Findings

**Overall landscape:** 24 eligible RCTs (n=3,695) from an initial search that also identified 239 registered-but-unpublished trials. Trials were small (median 100 participants, IQR 60–153) and predominantly European. Median follow-up: 8 weeks (IQR 4.8–15.6). Most recruited patients with general long COVID symptoms; 2 trials focused on neurological/cognitive subtypes, 3 on respiratory subtypes.

**Integrity concerns:** 6 of 24 trials (25%) had concerns about data integrity or trial execution (retrospective registration, implausibly large benefits, unusually small variability, or highly similar arm characteristics inconsistent with randomisation). These were excluded from certainty conclusions.

**Risk of bias:** ~50% of results rated high risk of bias, primarily due to lack of blinding and unmatched patient-provider interaction between arms.

**Interventions with moderate-certainty evidence (the only actionable signals):**

| Intervention | Comparator | Outcome | Effect | GRADE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Online CBT ("fit after covid", 17 weeks) | Usual care | Fatigue (CIS fatigue subscale, 8–56) | MD −8.4 (95% CI −13.1 to −3.7); just at MID | Moderate |
| Online CBT | Usual care | Concentration problems (CIS concentration subscale, 4–28) | MD −5.2 (−7.97 to −2.43) | Moderate |
| Combined physical + mental health rehabilitation (online, 8 weeks) | Single-session advice | Recovery or meaningful improvement | 161 more per 1,000 (61 to 292 more) | Moderate |
| Combined rehabilitation | Single-session advice | Depression (HADS-D, 0–21) | MD −1.50 (−2.41 to −0.59) | Moderate |
| Combined rehabilitation | Single-session advice | Quality of life (PROMIS 29+2 PROPr, −0.022–1) | MD 0.04 (0.00 to 0.08) | Moderate |
| Intermittent aerobic exercise (3–5×/week, 4–6 weeks) | Continuous aerobic exercise | Physical function (SF-36 PCS, 0–100) | MD 3.8 (1.12 to 6.48) | Moderate |
| Vortioxetine (8 weeks) | Placebo | Cognitive function | No effect | High |
| Vortioxetine | Placebo | Depression, quality of life | Probably no effect | Moderate |
| Coenzyme Q10 (500 mg/day, 6 weeks) | Placebo | Quality of life | Probably no effect | Moderate |
| SIM01 synbiotic | Placebo | Quality of life | Probably no effect | Moderate |

Effects for CBT and rehabilitation were modest — most just meeting the pre-specified MID threshold.

**Interventions with no compelling evidence (low/very low certainty or integrity concerns):** leronlimab (CCR5 monoclonal antibody), glucosaminyl muramyl dipeptide (Licopid), actovegin, inspiratory muscle training, in-patient rehabilitation with acupuncture, low vs high intensity exercise, multicomponent exercise + physiotherapy, amygdala and insula retraining, telerehabilitation mobile app (ReCOVery), L-arginine + liposomal vitamin C, SIM01 for fatigue/concentration/dyspnoea (high risk of bias due to selective reporting and outcome reclassification post-trial), Brainmax, hyperbaric oxygen, transcranial direct current stimulation, photobiomodulation, active hydrogen therapy.

**Post-exertional malaise (PEM):** The rehabilitation trial (n=585, severe acute COVID-19 history) closely monitored for PEM and reported no instances, suggesting supervised moderate physical activity can be safe in this population. Authors note this requires replication across milder-severity phenotypes.

**Intervention categories and trial counts:**
- Drug interventions: 4 trials, n=708
- Physical activity/rehabilitation: 8 trials, n=985
- Behavioural: 3 trials, n=314
- Dietary supplements: 4 trials, n=794
- Medical devices and technologies: 4 trials, n=309
- Combination treatment: 1 trial, n=585

## Relevance

**t004 therapeutics landscape map:** This review is the foundational certainty-graded map of the long COVID RCT space as of end-2023. The dominant finding — that only behavioural/rehabilitation approaches have moderate-certainty evidence, and no drug intervention reaches moderate certainty of benefit — directly defines the starting point for the planned `topic:therapeutics-and-clinical-trials` synthesis. The extremely thin evidence base (24 trials for a condition affecting tens of millions, with 239 unpublished registered trials) signals a major research gap, not a lack of effective treatments.

**PRO-dependence of endpoints:** All primary efficacy endpoints across every included trial are patient-reported outcomes (fatigue scales, quality-of-life instruments, functional questionnaires). The authors note explicitly that no objective laboratory measures predict patient-reported benefit in long COVID, and that PROs directly capture impairment. This PRO-dependence intersects with `topic:biomarkers-and-objective-endpoints`: the review underscores both why PROs are clinically appropriate and why their absence of objective correlates complicates blinding, bias assessment, and mechanistic interpretation. Trials relying on PROs without active-comparator blinding are systematically downgraded for risk of bias, limiting how far any non-blinded intervention can rise above moderate certainty.

**Mechanistic heterogeneity and phenotype problem:** The paper explicitly states that long COVID may be multiple distinct phenotypes, and no subgroup analyses by pathophysiological mechanism (antigen persistence, autoimmunity, dysautonomia, gut dysbiosis, etc.) were possible because trials did not stratify by presumed mechanism. This absence reinforces the project's working hypothesis that therapeutic trial design in long COVID is premature without better phenotypic and mechanistic stratification.

**Connection to ME/CFS frame:** The review explicitly places CBT and graded physical activity in the context of ME/CFS, where both have also shown efficacy, and addresses the contested nature of these findings (some patients and advocates interpret them as evidence that the condition is "psychological"). The authors carefully note that efficacy of CBT/rehabilitation does not indicate a psychological cause and may simply reflect coping mechanisms for biological symptoms.

**SIM01 / gut dysbiosis signal:** The synbiotic trial (SIM01) raised gut microbiome modulation as a plausible mechanistic avenue (SARS-CoV-2 ACE2 expression in gut epithelium; reduced *Bifidobacterium* in COVID-19 patients) but was rated low certainty due to selective reporting, outcome reclassification, and conflict of interest (trial run by patent holders). Independent replication needed before this signal can inform mechanistic claims in this project.

## Limitations

1. **Search cut-off December 2023.** The long COVID trial landscape is evolving rapidly; the living-review update schedule (up to 2 years from November 2024 publication) means findings will be superseded. The 239 registered-but-unpublished trials at time of search represent potentially much larger evidence to come (including NIH RECOVER trials).

2. **Trial sparsity precludes network meta-analysis.** Planned NMA was not feasible; most comparisons rest on a single trial. Pooling was generally impossible due to outcome measure heterogeneity across trials.

3. **High risk of bias in behavioural/rehabilitation trials.** The two most promising intervention categories (CBT, combined rehabilitation) were at high risk of bias due to unavoidable lack of blinding and asymmetric patient-provider interaction. The "moderate" certainty ceiling may reflect this structural limitation of non-pharmacological RCTs rather than truly uncertain effects.

4. **PRO-only endpoints.** No biomarker or objective functional measure was used as a primary endpoint in any trial. MIDs for long COVID do not exist; the review imported MIDs from other chronic conditions, introducing uncertainty in clinical importance thresholds.

5. **Phenotypic and mechanistic heterogeneity unaddressed.** Pre-specified subgroup analyses by long COVID subtype, acute severity, vaccination status, time since infection, and SARS-CoV-2 variant were not feasible. All effectiveness estimates apply to uncharacterised pools of phenotypically heterogeneous patients.

6. **Covariate reporting was poor.** Vaccination status reported in only 3 trials; diagnostic confirmation of acute COVID-19 not reported for ~50% of patients; symptom description missing in 7 trials. Limits generalisability.

7. **Trial integrity.** 25% of included trials had integrity concerns; methods for detecting fabrication/falsification have limited sensitivity and specificity, so some problematic trials may remain and some flagged trials may be legitimate.

8. **MID sources.** MIDs for continuous outcomes borrowed from other chronic disease populations; distribution-based MIDs (0.5 SD) used when anchored estimates unavailable. Different MID choices by other investigators could change certainty ratings.

9. **Conflict of interest in SIM01 trial.** The trial ran by the intervention's patent holders is a specific concern the review explicitly flags.

10. **Geographic coverage.** 62.5% of trials were European; low/middle income countries substantially underrepresented.

## Follow-up

- The living review will be updated; track future iterations for emerging high-certainty signals, especially from the NIH RECOVER trial portfolio and PANORAMIC/other large platform trials.
- The SIM01 synbiotic finding warrants monitoring for independent replication — if confirmed, it would be the first dietary supplement with moderate-certainty evidence of benefit.
- Pacing programmes (activity management) are currently recommended in many guidelines over graded exercise due to PEM concerns; the finding that supervised rehabilitation did not trigger PEM in the one large trial adds to a contested literature — worth tracking future dedicated PEM monitoring trials.
- The complete absence of drug interventions reaching moderate-certainty efficacy makes this review a strong baseline argument for mechanism-stratified trial design: matching intervention to presumed dominant pathophysiology (e.g., antivirals for antigen persistence cohort, anti-inflammatory for persistent immune-activation cohort) rather than enrolling all long COVID patients.
- Connect to `topic:biomarkers-and-objective-endpoints`: what would it take to include an objective biomarker endpoint alongside PROs in a long COVID RCT? This review provides the negative motivation — no trial has done it yet, and the risk-of-bias consequences of PRO-only measurement in open-label designs are directly visible here.
- Consider cross-referencing against Thaweethai2023 (RECOVER symptom clustering) and any papers establishing long COVID subtype definitions that could be used as stratification criteria in future trials.
