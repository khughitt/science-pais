## Key Contribution

Multi-omic profiling (immunological assays, virology, bulk RNA-seq, plasma proteomics) of two independent cohorts — a 2020–2021 discovery cohort (n = 142) and a 2023–2024 validation cohort (n = 38) — demonstrates that long COVID (LC) is characterized by persistent, chronic activation of proinflammatory pathways (JAK-STAT, IL-6, IFN, complement) and simultaneous immune exhaustion (CD8+ T cell exhaustion, PD-1 upregulation, reduced cytotoxic T cell and NK cell signatures) sustained for more than 180 days after acute SARS-CoV-2 infection, with no evidence of circulating virus in peripheral blood. The findings provide evidence that LC is not driven by ongoing viral replication detectable in blood but by a chronically dysregulated immune state, and they validate the JAK-STAT / IL-6 signaling axis as a candidate therapeutic target — a JAK1 inhibitor (abrocitinib, NCT06597396) clinical trial has been initiated based on these data.

## Methods

**Study design:** Observational cohort study using prospective enrollment from MassCPR (Massachusetts Consortium for Pathogen Readiness) and Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center (BIDMC).

**Discovery cohort (2020–2021):** n = 142 individuals: uninfected controls (UC; n = 35), acutely infected (< 30 days; n = 54), convalescent controls (CC; n = 24), and long COVID patients (LC; n = 28). Median age 63 (UC), 65.75 (acute), 50.05 (CC), 50.50 (LC). LC cohort predominantly female (85.7%), 100% Hispanic/Latino. Median days to first COVID: 200 days for LC vs. 71 for CC. Most LC participants were unvaccinated (72% no vaccine).

**Validation cohort (2023–2024):** n = 38: CC (n = 20) and LC (n = 18) enrolled at NIH RECOVER clinical site at BIDMC between October 2022 and December 2024. Median days to COVID: 286 (CC), 420 (LC). Predominantly Not Hispanic or Latino.

**Assays performed:**
- SARS-CoV-2 neutralizing antibody (nAb) titers via luciferase-based pseudovirus neutralization (WH/2020, Delta, BA.1 variants)
- T cell responses: IFNγ ELISpot with pooled peptides
- Virological: plasma SARS-CoV-2 RT-PCR (genomic N, E; subgenomic sgRNA N, sgRNA E copies/ml)
- Bulk RNA-seq of PBMCs (LC n = 26, CC n = 21; days 90–180; also UCs n = 35 and acute n = 54)
- Plasma proteomics (Olink proximity extension assay, LC n = 22, CC n = 8; days 90–180)
- GSEA for time-course analysis (< 30, 30–100, 100–200, 200–300 days)
- Validation cohort: bulk RNA-seq PBMCs (days 15–700); ELISA and Meso Scale Discovery (MSD) for IL-6R protein levels

**Analytical methods:**
- Differential gene expression (DEG) analysis comparing LC vs. CC (and vs. UC)
- Pathway enrichment (GSEA, normalized enrichment scores / NES)
- Weighted gene correlation network analysis (WGCNA-style co-expression)
- Unsupervised k-means clustering and PCA for cohort stratification
- Supervised random forest (RF) classifiers for LC prediction from pathway modules (ROC/AUC)
- Sex-stratified transcriptomic analysis
- Spearman correlation of pathway scores with clinical symptom counts

## Key Findings

**Transcriptomic profile of LC vs. convalescent controls (days 90–180):**
- Unsupervised clustering of bulk RNA-seq separated LC from all other groups (CC, UC, acute)
- Top upregulated genes in LC: *IL6*, *NLRP3*, *TNF*, *JAK2*, *CSF2*, *IL1B*, *IL10*, and chemokines (*CXCL2*, *CXCL3*, *CCL3*, *CCL10*, *IFNG*)
- Top downregulated genes: NK-cell inhibitory receptors (*KLRC1*, *KIR3DL2*) and T cell activation/differentiation markers (*CD28*, *ICOS*, *TCF7*)
- CD8+ T cell exhaustion signatures (genes: *IFI44*, *PRDM1*, *NR4A3*, *NFKBIA*, *MAFF*) and PD-1 signaling (*PDCD1*) were significantly elevated in LC

**GSEA pathway enrichment in LC (days 90–180, FDR q < 0.05):**
- Upregulated: IL-6 signaling, IL-6-JAK-STAT3 signaling, JAK-STAT signaling, JAK1 signaling, IFNα/IFNβ/IFNγ signaling, IL-1, IL-2, IL-15, IL-17, IL-18 signaling, complement cascade (C4 activators, initial triggering, complement cascade), coagulation (clotting cascade, fibrinolysis, coagulation system), leptin-insulin signaling, steroid hormone biosynthesis, signatures enriched in monocytes/macrophages/neutrophils/DCs/mast cells, FCGR-dependent phagocytosis
- Downregulated: RNA processing, cytoplasmic ribosomal proteins, oxidative stress, amino acid metabolism, circadian rhythm, NK cell signatures, T cell activation and differentiation signatures

**Persistence of JAK-STAT / IL-6 axis:**
- IL-6, IL-6-JAK-STAT3, JAK-STAT, and JAK1 signaling upregulated at both day 90–180 AND more than 180 days after infection (GSEA, Fig. 3a,b)
- GSEA time-course (30-day bins, Fig. 3 + Extended Data Fig. 3) shows proinflammatory pathways elevated starting by day 30 and sustained through day 300
- Hub genes in weighted gene correlation network (Spearman P < 0.05): *IL6R*, *IL6*, *IL1R*, *CD14*, *CSF1*, *CSF3RLEPR*, *IL4R*, *STAT1*, *STAT3*, *JAK2* (Fig. 3c)

**Correlation of IL-6-JAK-STAT3 with other pathways (Fig. 2d):**
- Positively correlated with: complement cascade (r significant, P < 0.0001), coagulation system (P < 0.0001), leptin-insulin signaling (P < 0.0001), steroid hormone biosynthesis (P = 0.0007), JAK-STAT (P < 0.0001), PD-1 signaling (P < 0.0001)
- Negatively correlated with: amino acid transport (P < 0.0001), oxidative stress (P < 0.0001), circadian rhythm (P < 0.004), metabolism of RNA (P = 0.004)

**Symptom correlations (Fig. 2e):**
- Proinflammatory/IL-6/JAK-STAT pathway scores positively correlated with fatigue, shortness of breath, and cognitive complaints (brain fog) in LC participants

**Proteomics (plasma, LC n = 22 vs. CC n = 8, days 90–180):**
- JAK-STAT pathway proteins elevated (STAT5, STAT1, IL6ST, SOS1, RELB)
- NF-κB signaling elevated
- Complement and coagulation proteins elevated (TMPRSS6, F8, C9, C1S, F9, C6, C1R, FN1)
- Leptin-insulin and corticotropin-releasing hormone signaling elevated (TCF4, PRKCA, PRKCB, PLCG1)
- Decreased: DNA damage repair (RAD51D, HUS1, MSH2), cytotoxic T cell markers (granzyme B), telomere maintenance proteins (PCNA, PRIM1, TENI, RUVBL1)

**Acute infection predicts later LC development:**
- Exploratory analysis of subset with both acute (< 30 days) and convalescent (90–180 days) samples
- Individuals who later developed LC had higher proinflammatory pathway activity during acute infection: IFNβ, IFNγ, JAK-STAT, IL-6, innate immune signatures (monocytes, neutrophils, complement, CCL3, CCL20, CD160, FI3A1, F3, IL6, NR4A1, NLRP3, THBS1)
- RF model identified IL-6, JAK-STAT, IL-6-JAK-STAT3, IFNβ, IFNγ signaling pathways during acute infection as top predictors of LC development

**Validation cohort (2023–2024; NIH RECOVER, n = 38):**
- Transcriptomic profiling confirmed upregulation of proinflammatory cytokine signaling, complement activation, T cell exhaustion, monocyte signatures in LC vs. CC at day 90–180 (FDR q < 0.05)
- IL-6, JAK-STAT, JAK1 signaling persistently elevated at day 90–180 and > 180 days (Extended Data Fig. 5c,d)
- IL-6R protein (ELISA and MSD): significantly elevated in LC vs. CC and UC at day 90–180 (Kruskal-Wallis with Dunn post hoc, Padj < 0.05) and more than 90 days and more than 180 days (Fig. 5c,d)

**Random forest prediction of LC status (combined cohorts, days 90–180):**
- AUC values: IL-6-JAK-STAT3 signaling = 0.83, JAK-STAT signaling = 0.85, IFNγ signaling = 0.84, proinflammatory response = 0.84, antigen presentation and activation = 0.82, complement cascade = 0.84 (Fig. 6a)
- Top individual gene markers: JAK1, PIK3CA, CXCL8, BCL2L1, OSM, MAP3K8, STAT3 (Fig. 6b)

**Sex-stratified analysis:**
- Females with LC (n = 29) showed stronger enrichment of inflammatory pathways than females who recovered (CC n = 14; Extended Data Fig. 8), while no significant sex differences were detected within the CC group; sex-stratified findings require confirmation in larger studies

**Virological findings:**
- No detectable plasma SARS-CoV-2 viral load (genomic N, E, subgenomic sgRNA) in any CC or LC individuals at day 90–180 (Fig. 1c)
- No viral read counts for common latent viruses (EBV, HSV-1/2, VZV, CMV, influenza) distinguishable in RNA-seq between LC and CC
- SARS-CoV-2 nAb titers: trend toward higher Spike-specific IFNγ ELISpot in LC compared with CC, but not statistically significant

## Relevance

This paper directly addresses the project research question (research-question:post-acute-infection-syndromes) by providing multi-omic evidence for what a post-acute immune failure state looks like at the pathway level. It is a high-confidence, multi-cohort study anchored in controlled comparisons (LC vs. CC) with a validation cohort, and it quantifies the persistent immune dysregulation that constitutes PAIS in the prototypical case (long COVID).

**Connection to the PAIS frame of failed homeostatic recovery:**

1. **Failure to resolve acute inflammation.** The core finding — JAK-STAT, IL-6, IFN, and complement pathways persistently elevated > 180 days post-infection in LC but not in CC — is a direct molecular signature of homeostatic failure: the acute inflammatory response has not returned to baseline. The time-course GSEA (from day 30 to day 300) documents this as a chronic, not merely delayed, deviation.

2. **Immune exhaustion as secondary failure.** The co-occurrence of proinflammatory activation with CD8+ T cell exhaustion signatures and PD-1 upregulation illustrates a secondary breakdown: the adaptive immune regulatory circuits that should terminate inflammation are themselves exhausted or dysfunctional. This is consistent with a model in which unresolved antigenic stimulation (even without detectable virus in blood) drives progressive T cell dysfunction.

3. **Acute-phase predictors of chronic dysregulation.** The RF analysis showing that inflammatory pathway activity during the acute infection (< 30 days) predicts subsequent LC development 90+ days later links the magnitude of the initial immune insult to the chronicity of the post-acute failure — directly supporting the hypothesis that initial homeostatic perturbation amplitude predicts recovery failure.

4. **Shared PAIS mechanisms.** The JAK-STAT / IL-6 / complement axis identified here overlaps with pathways proposed in ME/CFS, post-Q-fever fatigue, and other PAIS conditions covered in the project (see [cite:Komaroff2023], [cite:Choutka2022]). The metabolic dysregulation (amino acid metabolism, corticotropin-releasing hormone, leptin-insulin signaling) and telomere maintenance deficits also align with cross-condition signatures discussed in [cite:Ryan2022] and [cite:Talla2023].

5. **Therapeutic implications.** The abrocitinib (JAK1 inhibitor) trial (NCT06597396) represents the first direct clinical translation of a pathway-level homeostasis-restoration strategy for LC, making this paper a key translational anchor for the project's interest in recovery-promoting interventions.

## Limitations

1. **Small, demographically skewed cohorts.** Discovery cohort LC group (n = 28) is predominantly female (85.7%) and 100% Hispanic/Latino; validation cohort LC (n = 18) predominantly Not Hispanic or Latino. Cross-cohort validation partially mitigates this, but generalizability to broader populations remains limited. The authors explicitly note larger studies are needed to assess generalizability.

2. **Bulk RNA-seq resolution.** PBMC bulk RNA-seq cannot resolve cell-type-specific transcriptomic changes. The paper acknowledges this directly and calls for single-cell or cell-type-resolved approaches. Cell-type deconvolution was performed indirectly via immune cell gene signatures (monocyte, neutrophil, DC enrichment scores) but these are proxies.

3. **No detectable viral persistence in blood.** RT-PCR of plasma at day 90–180 showed no circulating SARS-CoV-2, ruling out viremia but not tissue-compartment viral persistence (lung, gut, lymph nodes). The study cannot exclude reservoir-driven antigen stimulation as a driver of the observed chronic inflammation.

4. **Observational design.** No causal inference is possible from transcriptomic correlations; pathway co-activation with symptoms is associative. The RF models predict LC status but do not establish mechanistic directionality.

5. **Acute-phase predictor analysis is exploratory, subset-only.** The subgroup with both acute and convalescent samples is small (LC n = 8, CC n = 5 for proteomics; similar for RNA-seq), limiting statistical power for the longitudinal prediction models.

6. **Vaccination and treatment heterogeneity.** The 2020–2021 cohort includes mostly unvaccinated individuals (LC: 0% vaccinated; CC: 28% Moderna/Pfizer), while the 2023–2024 cohort had unknown vaccination status. This limits direct comparison across cohorts and to the current post-Omicron, vaccinated LC population.

7. **Cross-sectional sampling within each time window.** Most time points represent different individuals sampled within a window (e.g., "day 90–180") rather than true longitudinal tracking of the same individuals, limiting conclusions about within-person immune trajectory.

## Model / Tool Availability

No computational models or software tools are released with this paper. Random forest models were trained on pathway-level gene set enrichment scores and are not publicly deposited as standalone tools. Source data, supplementary tables (clinical meta-data, gene lists), and extended data figures are available at the journal landing page (https://doi.org/10.1038/s41590-025-02353-x). Code availability is stated to be in the Online content section of the paper.

Clinical trial based on these findings: abrocitinib (JAK1 inhibitor) for LC, NCT06597396.

**Questions this raises for the project:**
1. Is the JAK-STAT / IL-6 axis upregulation specific to SARS-CoV-2 or does it generalize to other PAIS (ME/CFS post-EBV, post-Q-fever, post-dengue)?
2. What is the cell-type origin of persistent IL-6 / JAK-STAT signaling — monocytes, tissue macrophages, endothelial cells, or a coordinated multi-cellular source?
3. Do the acute-phase predictors of LC (elevated proinflammatory pathways < 30 days) differ from those predicting ME/CFS or other PAIS, enabling a divergent-recovery model?
