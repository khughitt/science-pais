## Key Contribution

A comprehensive review of the mechanistic architecture underlying sex differences in immunity, arguing that two orthogonal axes — (1) sex chromosome complement (X-linked gene dosage and XCI escape) and (2) gonadal steroid signaling (estrogen, androgen, progesterone receptors on immune cells) — jointly program divergent innate and adaptive immune responses in males and females across the lifespan. The review then maps how these mechanisms produce sex-differential outcomes in acute viral infections (HIV, HBV, HCV, influenza, SARS-CoV-2), bacterial infections (Mtb, S. pneumoniae, GI pathogens), post-acute infection syndromes (PASC/long COVID, PTLD, Dengue, West Nile, Chronic Q fever), and vaccine responses. The central claim is that sex differences in immunity are not a single-mechanism phenomenon and differ by pathogen, immune compartment, and life-stage; this heterogeneity is itself a source of mechanistic insight and a prerequisite for precision immunology.

## Methods

Narrative review of preclinical (mouse genetic and pharmacological models) and human studies (observational cohorts, clinical trials, transcriptomics, proteomics, single-cell profiling). Covers: innate immunity (pDCs, macrophages, NK cells, ILC2s), adaptive immunity (CD4+, CD8+, Tfh, Treg, B cells, IgG isotypes), acute viral and bacterial infection outcomes, PAIS cross-trigger comparisons, and sex-differential vaccine immunogenicity and safety. Does not perform a systematic literature search or meta-analysis; references mechanistic papers, epidemiological studies, and GWAS-assisted evidence.

## Key Findings

### Two-axis mechanistic framework

**Sex chromosome complement axis**

- The X chromosome encodes ~800 proteins and ~600 noncoding genes, many regulating immune function (TLR7, TLR8, IL13RA1, IL13RA2, FOXP3, KDM5C, EIF2S3, DDX3X).
- In XX individuals, random X chromosome inactivation (XCI) mediated by the long noncoding RNA XIST silences one X; 15–25% of genes on the inactivated X escape XCI, meaning XX individuals have biallelic expression of these loci. In XY individuals, only one copy exists and there is no inactivation.
- Key immune genes that consistently escape XCI include EIF2S3, DDX3X, KDM5C and KDM6A (UTX). KDM6A affects CD4+ T and NK cells; TLR7 escapes XCI in pDCs and B cells from XX individuals and Klinefelter syndrome (XXY) subjects — a primary driver of stronger type I IFN responses and IgG class switching in females.
- Altered or incomplete XIST function can cause hyperactive immune responses in XX individuals and is hypothesized to be one mechanism mediating female-biased susceptibility to PAIS.

**Gonadal steroid axis**

- Estrogen (E2) signals via intracellular ERα, ERβ and membrane-bound GPCR:
  - Binds NF-κB and TLR7 promoters directly; enhances pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF, IL-1β, IL-6, type I and II IFNs); promotes CD4+ and CD8+ T cell activation; stimulates antibody secretion, class-switch recombination, and somatic hypermutation.
  - Effects are concentration-dependent and tissue-dependent — suppressive at high pregnancy concentrations, stimulatory at moderate concentrations.
- Androgen (testosterone) signals via AR (genomic and nongenomic):
  - Increases inflammasome activation; reduces innate lymphoid cell (ILC2) activity; shifts CD4+ T cells toward Th2 and Th17 phenotypes; reduces CD8+ T cell activity; limits thymocyte development.
  - In menopausal women, age-associated inflammatory monocyte expansion is reversed by menopause hormone therapy, implicating androgen/estrogen balance.
- Progesterone (PR): primarily tolerogenic; suppresses IL-1β–COX-2 via nuclear glucocorticoid receptor; limits inflammatory consequences during pregnancy.

### Innate immunity

- **pDCs:** Female pDCs produce more type I IFNα than male pDCs in response to TLR7 activation (HIV-derived TLR7/TLR8 ligands), due to biallelic TLR7 expression and estrogen-driven TLR7 induction. This effect appears after puberty, implicating both XCI-escape and gonadal steroid axes.
- **Macrophages:** Female macrophages have higher basal expression of TLR2, TLR3, TLR4, MyD88; greater phagocytic activity; better NADPH oxidase–mediated bacterial killing; greater resistance against group B Streptococcus.
- **NK cells:** Higher frequency in males; but cells from females produce more IFNγ, perforin, and granzyme B per cell; sex differences largely explained by KDM6A copy number (higher in females), since eliminating the sex difference in Kdm6a copy number in mice eliminates NK cell number and function differences.
- **ILC2s:** Androgen-limited — fewer in male humans and mice after puberty; female ILC2s produce more IL-5 and IL-13 in response to IL-2/IL-33; female bias in ILC2-driven lung type 2 inflammation. ILC2 sex differences operate via an androgen-ILC2-dendritic cell axis.

### Adaptive immunity

- **CD4+ T cells:** Greater frequency in females (post-puberty, thymus-rate difference); females produce more IFNγ; males produce more IL-4 and IL-17A; androgen receptor (AR) activity limits thymocyte development and checkpoint efficacy.
- **Tfh cells:** More IL-6 production in females → greater germinal center B cell expansion and antibody responses.
- **Treg cells:** Higher circulating proportion in males (androgen-driven); lower in females → mechanistically links to higher female autoimmune susceptibility; confirmed in transgender individuals on testosterone.
- **CD8+ T cells:** More effector function in females (IFNγ, TNF, granzyme B expression); androgen receptor activity in CD8+ T cells limits checkpoint blockade efficacy in cancer models. Sex-biased CD8+ T cell stemness programs affect antitumor immunity.
- **B cells:** Females have more class-switched memory B cells, greater IgG2b (mice) / IgG2 (human) concentrations; TLR7 biallelic expression drives IgG class switching; estradiol enhances somatic hypermutation and class-switch recombination via activation-induced deaminase upregulation. Women exhibit stronger antibody responses to almost all vaccines.

### Sex differences in acute viral infections

- **HIV-1:** Males: higher initial plasma viral RNA; females: more immunopathology at similar viral loads — greater CD8+ T cell activation (CD38+HLA-DR+), more interferon-stimulated gene expression (ISG15, MX1, TNFSF10), faster disease progression to AIDS. On ART: women have more viral reservoir (PD-1+CD4+ T cell pools); estrogen inhibits HIV replication via ERα/β-catenin; exogenous E2 in postmenopausal women enhances TLR7-mediated IFN signaling.
- **HBV:** Male-biased: 5–7x more likely to develop HCC; testosterone → AR → binds TERT promoters → HCC; female sex protected via ER → HNF4α → represses HBV transcription; in male HBV-positive HCC cell lines, AR signaling increases DNA damage, nuclear DNA leakage, and cGAS-STING activation.
- **HCV:** Progesterone response element in HCV genome (directly regulated by PR); female sex predicts acute clearance; chronic disease and HCC more male-biased; castration of male mice raises HBsAg levels (consistent with androgen-mediated HBV persistence).
- **IAV:** Age-dependent reversal of sex-biased severity:
  - Children: Males (H1N1/H3N2) more likely to be hospitalized; pregnancy is a risk factor.
  - Reproductive-age adults: Females have worse morbidity and pulmonary inflammation despite similar viral loads; female mice have greater TNF, IFNγ, IL-6, CCL2 production; estradiol/ERα agonist treatment improves outcomes by suppressing immunopathology; testosterone dampens pulmonary damage.
  - Elderly (≥65): Males at greater risk of severe outcomes.
  - Mechanistic driver: When immunopathology drives severity → XX (stronger immune induction) does worse; when reduced immunity drives severity → XY (androgen-immunosuppressed) does worse.
- **SARS-CoV-2 acute:** Males: worse acute disease, higher IL-6, IL-8, IL-18, more pulmonary CD11b+ myeloid cells, fewer lymphocytes; females: more ISGs, CD4+/CD8+ T cell activation, more intermediate monocytes (CD14+CD16+), stronger SARS-CoV-2-specific B cell responses, stronger ISG induction by PTPRC exon 6 alternative splicing. Transcriptomics and serum proteomics confirm baseline and induced expression differences. The Delta variant increased pulmonary myeloid infiltration more in males; Omicron shows more female CD4+ T cell-associated responses — sex differences in immunity may need updating as variants evolve.

### Sex differences in PAIS (directly relevant to this project)

**Viruses:**

- **PASC/Long COVID:** Females, particularly in the 40–55 age range, are more likely than males to experience PASC. Female individuals have increased symptom prevalence, particularly neurocognitive symptoms; some evidence that PASC results in greater persistence of symptoms up to 3 years in females (depression, fatigue, myalgia, neuropsychiatric complications, difficulty sleeping). Pulmonary sequelae and exercise capacity up to 1 year after SARS-CoV-2 or MERS infections are more often reported in females. Male PASC: more long-term cardiovascular complications (even after mild COVID). Transcriptomic analyses show increased XIST expression in several innate and adaptive immune cell subsets in females with PASC; male PASC: TGFB signaling disrupted, decreased Y-chromosome genes (DDX3Y, UTY, KDMSD, PRKY, USP9Y). Limited evidence for a gonadal-steroid-specific role in PASC outcomes so far.
  - *[Precision note, t125 review, 2026-07-19]* — Against the primary study this transcriptomic result comes from (Hamlin et al. 2025, Stanford IRIS cohort, n=45 longitudinal; PMC12148066): the XIST elevation is an **acute-phase** signal — higher **during acute infection in females who *subsequently* developed LC**, "still present but less prominent" at 3 and 12 months — not a steady-state PASC-immune-cell upregulation. The male Y-linked result is **reduced *expression*** (DDX3Y down-regulated across most subsets at 12 months in males with LC; UTY/PRKY/USP9Y differed too), **not genomic "loss."** The primary study does **not** connect the Y-linked expression finding to the (separately reported) male cardiovascular phenotype; those are two distinct statements and should not be read as one causal chain.
- **West Nile Virus:** Males: more severe acute disease with neuroinvasion; females: more self-reported post-acute symptoms despite comparable plasma viral loads and IFNα induction.
- **Dengue:** Males: more acute disease; females more at risk of post-Dengue fatigue, arthralgia, and post-Chikungunya inflammatory disease. Females 40–55: greater risk of developing fatigue after Dengue infection.

**Bacteria:**

- **PTLD (Post-treatment Lyme disease):** Females 4.7x more likely to meet PTLD criteria; infected female subjects have increased frequencies of central memory CD8+ T cells in a high-fatigue/cognitive symptom patient subgroup — not observed in male subgroups.
- **Chronic Q fever (Coxiella burnetii):** Predominantly male (>40y); ovariectomy increases bacterial load in female mice; treatment with estradiol reduces load to levels comparable to infected male mice; female macrophages have greater TNF and CXCL10 expression than male MDMs after C. burnetii infection.

**Cross-PAIS summary from the paper:** Female individuals appear more likely to experience diverse PAIS symptoms and complications. Identification of common inflammatory and immunological mechanisms of PAIS is needed with consideration of sex chromosome-linked and autosomal gene expression differences. Notably, autoimmune diseases (MS, SLE) that are more common in females have their etiology partly in early-life EBV infection, further highlighting that diverse chronic diseases after viral infection are more likely to occur in female individuals.

### Sex differences in vaccine responses

- **Viral vaccines (childhood):** Girls have greater antibody responses and cytokine secretion to MMR; greater antibody titers to polio, meningitis C; HPV vaccine responses greater in females both in children and young adults.
- **Influenza vaccines:** Women aged 18–49 generate higher hemagglutination inhibition titers than men to trivalent inactivated influenza vaccine regardless of full or half dose; monovalent H1N1 vaccine: females 18–45 years develop greater IL-6 and neutralizing antibody responses; sex difference attenuated in older individuals (≥65y) due to reproductive senescence and frailty in men.
- **COVID-19 vaccines:** Women mount stronger IgG responses to BNT162b2 (Pfizer); CD4+ and CD8+ T cell activation by COVID-19 vaccines is greater in female individuals with sex outweighing age and BMI in predicting cellular responses; faster decline in neutralizing antibody titers at 6 months in men; booster dose eliminates sex and age differences in responses in mice and humans.
- **BCG:** Better protection against Mtb in female mice; female mice show greater CD4+ and CD8+ T cell recruitment to lungs 18 days after BCG vaccination; BCG causes greater reactivity in girls (reduced childhood mortality).
- **Pneumococcal vaccines (PPV23 vs PCV13):** PPV23 results conflicting; for PCV13, women >60y tend to mount stronger opsonophagocytic responses.
- **Adverse reactions:** Female individuals generally have higher immunogenicity and also a greater frequency of adverse reactions; sex differences in adverse and rare severe reactions to vaccines must be considered — particularly relevant for trust in vaccine science.

## Relevance

This is the most directly relevant review to `question:0007-mechanism-of-female-predominance-in-pais` in the literature. It provides a unified mechanistic architecture for why females are disproportionately affected by PASC/long COVID, PTLD, and other PAIS.

**Hypothesis linkages:**

- **`hypothesis:0005` (reproductive-stage immune homeostatic margin):** The gonadal-steroid axis described here (estrogen's immunostimulatory role at moderate concentrations; age-related menopausal decline; androgen effects on ILC2s and Treg pools) is the molecular underpinning of h0005's "margin" concept. Estrogen's dose-dependent immunostimulatory effects provide the mechanism by which reproductive-stage transition could shift innate and adaptive baselines. The female 40–55-year-old PASC peak noted here is consistent with perimenopause-phase transition in that age window. The paper explicitly notes that declining estradiol during reproductive senescence is the mechanism attenuating sex differences in vaccine responses in older women. **Support rating: direct mechanistic corroboration for the hormone-immune mechanism proposition (Proposition 0002).**

- **`hypothesis:0020` (host immune baseline reserve gate):** The two axes described (sex chromosome dosage, gonadal steroids) are literal operational components of the "pre-infection baseline reserve" that h0020's conjecture proposes as a gate for PAIS entry. Sex is now one of the most mechanistically characterized dimensions of this reserve. Specifically: (1) TLR7 biallelic expression → stronger type I IFN baseline → higher reserve against virus establishment, but also higher immunopathology risk; (2) androgen-driven Treg and ILC2 suppression → modified inflammatory baseline in males → different reserve profile. **Support rating: mechanistic basis for biological sex as a reserve-axis component.**

- **`hypothesis:0001` (shared dysregulated attractor):** The sex-differential immune programming described here shapes both the probability of entering the failed-recovery attractor (stronger innate/adaptive activation in females → more immunopathology-type PAIS) and the phenotype within the attractor (female PASC more immune/autoimmune/neurocognitive; male PASC more cardiovascular). The XIST upregulation finding in female PASC immune cells — and the Y-chromosome gene loss in male PASC — suggest sex-chromosome-mediated immune state differences persist into the attractor state. **Support rating: novel molecular marker for sex-biased attractor entry.**

- **`hypothesis:0009` (post-infectious immune set-point shift → autoimmune):** The paper's discussion of autoimmune disease female predominance (SLE, MS linked to EBV early-life infection) and the Treg-depletion axis (lower circulating Treg in females driven by androgen-regulated FOXP3) provides mechanistic context for why females are more susceptible to latent→overt autoimmune conversion after PAIS. The TLR7 biallelic expression → higher IgG class switching axis also directly raises the prior probability of autoantibody generation in females. **Support rating: mechanistic prior for sex as a modifier of autoimmune conversion rate.**

- **`hypothesis:0012` (PAIS fatigue/cognitive symptoms as trigger-nonspecific):** The finding that female excess in PASC, PTLD, and post-Dengue fatigue is consistent across diverse triggers — while male excess in cardiovascular PASC and Chronic Q fever is also trigger-modulated — supports the trigger-nonspecific nature of the neuroimmune/fatigue symptom cluster in females. **Support rating: corroborating cross-trigger sex pattern.**

## Limitations

1. **Narrative review, not systematic:** No systematic search strategy or meta-analysis; coverage may be selective. Effect sizes and replication status of individual studies are not rigorously assessed.
2. **Heavy preclinical reliance for mechanisms:** Many mechanistic claims (KDM6A–NK cell axis, ILC2-androgen axis, estradiol–IAV pathogenesis) are established primarily in mouse models; human translational evidence is cited where available but mechanistic confirmation in human immune cells remains incomplete for several claims.
3. **PAIS section is relatively thin:** The PASC/PTLD/Dengue-PAIS sex-difference section is presented descriptively; the review does not attempt to adjudicate causal mechanisms for sex-biased PASC susceptibility (citing limited evidence for gonadal steroids specifically in PASC), nor does it engage with the ascertainment/symptom-attribution confounds raised by `hypothesis:0008`.
4. **Gonadal steroid evidence in PAIS is weak:** The paper explicitly notes "so far, there is limited evidence supporting a role for gonadal steroids in PASC outcomes" — the bulk of the sex-PAIS link is attributed to X-chromosome dosage effects (XIST, TLR7 biallelic) rather than hormonal mechanisms, which partially conflicts with the hormone-mediated framing of `hypothesis:0005` Proposition 0002.
5. **Gender/behavioral confounders:** The paper acknowledges gender differences in healthcare use, behavior, and occupation affect infection outcomes but explicitly excludes them from scope. These are not ignorable for human PAIS studies.
6. **Life-course perspective is descriptive not predictive:** While age-dependent reversal of sex effects (IAV) is described, no unified model is offered for predicting at which life stage sex differences flip direction for a given pathogen.
7. **Intersex and transgender individuals underrepresented:** Briefly mentioned but not systematically reviewed; limits translation to individuals with non-binary sex chromosome or hormone profiles.

**Questions raised for this project:**

1. Does XIST upregulation in innate/adaptive immune cells represent a passive epigenetic readout of female sex, or an active driver of immune hyperactivation that contributes causally to female-biased PASC? (candidate for new question)
2. Can the sex-biased PAIS susceptibility be decomposed into the X-chromosome-dosage component vs. the gonadal-steroid component in human cohort designs that include genetic sex, hormone measures, and PAIS outcomes? (candidate for new question)
3. The paper notes that the male PASC cardiovascular phenotype may be driven by the DDX3Y/UTY/Y-chromosome gene loss axis — does this provide a mechanistic link to the male-vascular signal questions (question:0019, question:0020, question:0021)?
