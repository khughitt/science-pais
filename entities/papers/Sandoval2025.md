# Two neurocognitive domains identified for patients with myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome and post-acute sequelae of COVID-19

- **Authors:** Ariadna Sandoval, Mingqi Li, Leonard A. Jason
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal:** Frontiers in Neurology, vol. 16, article 1612548
- **DOI:** 10.3389/fneur.2025.1612548
- **BibTeX key:** Sandoval2025
- **Source:** PDF

## Key Contribution

Using exploratory factor analysis (EFA) on self-reported neurocognitive symptom data from large ME/CFS (N = 2,313) and PASC (N = 299) cohorts, this study identifies two consistent and replicable neurocognitive domains: (1) "impairment of concentration and memory" encompassing classic brain-fog symptoms (forgetfulness, slowed thought, attention difficulties, word-finding problems), and (2) "sensory and perceptual disturbances" encompassing hypersensitivity to light, noise, and smells plus twitching and loss of depth perception. The second domain — sensory overload — has been substantially underrepresented in prior neurocognitive assessments of post-viral illness, and the paper argues it should be routinely included in symptom inventories for ME/CFS and PASC.

## Methods

**Design:** Cross-sectional, aggregated multi-cohort observational study using archival survey data.

**ME/CFS cohorts (total N = 2,313):** Eight sub-samples aggregated — DePaul University convenience sample (N = 214, self-identified ME/CFS, online); BioBank via Solve ME/CFS Initiative (N = 500; physician-diagnosed, 8 excluded); Newcastle clinic referrals, UK (N = 95; physician-examined); Norway 1 (N = 173; ME/CFS patient-organisation contacts); Norway 2 (N = 60; outpatient/inpatient ME/CFS centres); Norway 3 (N = 169; tertiary care); Chronic Illness convenience sample (N = 435; online); Japan clinic (N = 124); Spain tertiary centre (N = 182); Amsterdam CFS Medical Centre (N = 356). Demographics: mean age 46.93 years (SD = 13.66), 81.0% female, 76.4% White/Caucasian; 20.8% on disability.

**PASC cohort (N = 299):** Self-reported non-recovery from COVID-19, recruited via social media August 2020. Mean age 45.1 years (SD = 12.88), 81.6% female, 90.3% white.

**Instrument:** DePaul Symptom Questionnaire (DSQ-1), 54-item self-report. Analysis focused on 13 neurocognitive domain items: 8 concentration/memory items (absent-mindedness, forgetfulness, slowness of thought, difficulty understanding, word-finding, sustained attention, focusing on one thing, vision/attention) and 5 sensory/perceptual items (sensitivity to light, noise, smells, twitching, loss of depth perception). ME/CFS participants rated frequency over past 6 months (0–4); PASC participants rated symptoms as experienced during first 2 weeks of illness at current time. Both frequency and severity scores standardised to 100-point scales; composite scores averaged.

**Statistics:** Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA) with principal axis factoring, Promax rotation (kappa = 4), IBM SPSS v25. Number of factors determined by parallel analysis (5,000 permutation iterations); items with rotated loading < 0.3 dropped. One-way ANOVAs compared severe vs. mild/moderate impairment groups on factor scores.

## Key Findings

**Factor structure (ME/CFS sample; N = 2,313):**
- KMO = 0.94, Bartlett's p < 0.001 — EFA appropriate.
- Two-factor solution retained.
- Factor 1 ("Impairment of concentration and memory"): eigenvalue 6.06, explaining 47% of common variance. High loadings: absent-mindedness/forgetfulness (0.893), problems remembering (0.865), slowness of thought (0.808), difficulty understanding (0.806), word-finding (0.788), sustained attention (0.718), focusing on one thing (0.664), vision/attention (0.602).
- Factor 2 ("Sensory and perceptual disturbances"): eigenvalue 0.87, explaining 7% of common variance. Loadings: sensitivity to light (0.844), sensitivity to noise (0.741), sensitivity to smells (0.567), twitching (0.342), loss of depth perception (0.340). Inter-factor correlation r = 0.65.

**Factor structure (PASC sample; N = 299):**
- KMO = 0.94, Bartlett's p < 0.001.
- Two-factor solution retained with near-identical structure.
- Factor 1 eigenvalue 7.41, explaining 56% common variance; loadings 0.69–0.945.
- Factor 2 eigenvalue 0.91, explaining 7% common variance; loadings 0.47–0.90. Inter-factor correlation r = 0.71.

**Severity gradient (ANOVA):**
ME/CFS — severely impaired patients scored significantly higher on both factors vs. mild/moderate:
- Factor 1: F(1, 2,204) = 124.47, p < 0.01 (severe M = 0.14 SD 0.93; mild/moderate M = −0.36 SD 0.97)
- Factor 2: F(1, 2,204) = 165.67, p < 0.01 (severe M = 0.15 SD 0.87; mild/moderate M = −0.38 SD 0.88)

PASC — same pattern:
- Factor 1: F(1, 287) = 54.01, p < 0.01 (severe M = 0.41 SD 0.86; mild/moderate M = −0.36 SD 0.91)
- Factor 2: F(1, 287) = 54.24, p < 0.01 (severe M = 0.37 SD 0.91; mild/moderate M = −0.35 SD 0.76)

**Proposed neural mechanism:** Factor 1 symptoms mapped to dysfunction of the central executive network (working memory, attention); Factor 2 to anterior insula overactivation within the salience network, plausibly driven by low excitability thresholds causing excessive stimulus sensitivity. Authors cite findings of increased salience network activation in ME/CFS (Manca et al., *Brain Struct Funct* 2021) and aberrant resting-state salience network functional connectivity in adolescent ME/CFS (Wortinger et al., *PLoS One* 2016).

## Relevance

This paper is directly relevant to `research-question:post-acute-infection-syndromes` in two ways.

First, it provides empirical evidence of **shared neurocognitive structure** across ME/CFS and PASC — two post-infectious conditions arising from different triggers (diverse infectious agents vs. SARS-CoV-2). The two-factor solution replicates across both populations, consistent with the project's hypothesis that post-acute syndromes share a common phenotypic attractor. Neurocognitive impairment in PAIS is thus not merely a label overlap but has stable internal structure observable in large samples.

Second, the sensory overload domain (Factor 2) specifically implicates **salience network dysregulation** as a candidate shared mechanism in PAIS neurocognition. The salience network mediates switching between internal (default mode) and external attention (central executive), and its persistent overactivation could explain why recovery from acute infection fails to re-establish normal interoceptive and attentional baseline — a direct instance of failed homeostatic recovery at the neural-circuit level. This connects to the project frame of post-infectious failure modes and complements immune-dysregulation and autonomic failure mechanisms with a central nervous system dimension.

## Limitations

1. **Self-reported diagnoses:** PASC sample diagnosed by self-report without medical evaluation; ME/CFS diagnoses confirmed in some samples but not all (notably the DePaul and social-media samples). Misclassification cannot be ruled out.
2. **Self-reported neurocognitive symptoms only:** No objective neurocognitive testing (no neuropsychological battery, no neuroimaging in this study). Subjective symptom counts may reflect illness attribution or mood comorbidities.
3. **Unequal sample sizes:** ME/CFS N = 2,313 vs. PASC N = 299; the PASC sample is substantially smaller, which may affect stability of the second factor (eigenvalue only 0.91).
4. **PASC symptom timing inconsistency:** PASC participants rated symptoms during the first 2 weeks of illness assessed retrospectively, while ME/CFS participants rated the past 6 months. This difference in recall window and illness phase limits direct comparison of factor score magnitudes.
5. **Comorbidity confounding:** ME/CFS and long COVID often co-occur with depression, anxiety, and other conditions with overlapping neurocognitive profiles. The study does not adjust for or characterise psychiatric comorbidities.
6. **Cross-sectional, no longitudinal trajectory:** Cannot determine whether the two-factor structure emerges acutely vs. persists vs. resolves over time, which would be relevant to understanding homeostatic failure dynamics.
7. **Recruitment heterogeneity:** ME/CFS sub-samples span 8 countries and diverse recruitment channels; pooling assumes measurement equivalence which was not formally tested.
8. **Factor 2 low variance explained (7%):** Although retained as meaningful, the sensory/perceptual factor explains only a small proportion of common variance; some items (twitching, depth perception) have loadings ≤ 0.34 in the ME/CFS sample.

## Model / Tool Availability

No computational model or tool released. Raw data stated as available from the authors upon reasonable request. DSQ-1 instrument is publicly documented (Jason LA, Sunnquist M, et al., *Front Pediatr* 2018, doi:10.3389/fped.2018.00330).

## Follow-up

- Are the two neurocognitive domains differentially associated with objective measures of salience network function or cerebrovascular reactivity in ME/CFS or PASC neuroimaging studies?
- Does the sensory overload domain (Factor 2) correlate with dysautonomia severity or orthostatic intolerance, given that hypervigilance is proposed to arise partly from reduced cerebral blood flow?
- Can the two-factor structure replicate in a longitudinal design distinguishing acute recovery trajectories from persistent PAIS — i.e., do both domains persist together or does one resolve while the other persists?
- See `paper:Choutka2022` (broad PAIS mechanistic review already in project) — check whether it covers CNS/salience network mechanisms specifically.
