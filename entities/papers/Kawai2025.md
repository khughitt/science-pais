# Viral Infections and Risk of Cardiovascular Disease

- **Authors:** Kosuke Kawai, Cresencia Felician Muhere, Elkin V. Lemos, Joel M. Francis
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal:** Journal of the American Heart Association
- **DOI:** 10.1161/JAHA.125.042670
- **BibTeX key:** Kawai2025
- **Source:** PDF: `~/d/health/processes/post-acute-infection/papers/pdfs/2025_Kawai_Viral-Infections-and-Risk-of-Cardiovascular-Disease-Systematic-Review-and-Meta-Analysis.pdf`

## Key Contribution

Kawai et al. provide a broad systematic review and meta-analysis of viral infections as risk factors for cardiovascular disease.
The paper is not menopause-specific, but it strengthens the cross-pathogen vascular-risk background for PAIS and long-COVID cardiovascular sequelae.

## Methods

The authors searched MEDLINE, Embase, Web of Science, African-Wide Information, and Cochrane Library from inception to July 2024.
They included 155 observational studies of viral infection and cardiovascular outcomes, excluding cross-sectional and ecological studies.
Random-effects models estimated pooled adjusted risk ratios, with analyses separated by study design.

## Key Findings

SARS-CoV-2 infection was associated with higher risk of cardiovascular disease (11 cohort studies; RR 1.82; 95% CI 1.44-2.30), coronary heart disease (12 cohort studies; RR 1.74; 95% CI 1.44-2.11), and stroke (12 cohort studies; RR 1.69; 95% CI 1.23-2.31).
Self-controlled case-series studies found elevated MI and stroke risk shortly after SARS-CoV-2 infection.
Influenza showed a strong acute MI and stroke risk in the first month, while HIV, hepatitis C, and herpes zoster showed persistent associations with coronary heart disease and stroke.
The authors discuss inflammatory cytokines, endothelial dysfunction, hypercoagulability, neutrophil extracellular traps, and plaque destabilization as plausible pathways.

## Relevance

This paper broadens `topic:thromboinflammation-and-endothelial-dysfunction` beyond SARS-CoV-2.
It supports the idea that viral triggers can produce vascular and thromboinflammatory risk that persists after the acute infection, although it does not specifically address fatigue-predominant PAIS or menopause.

## Limitations

The meta-analysis has moderate to high heterogeneity for many virus-outcome pairs.
Most outcomes are cardiovascular diagnoses, not long-COVID or PAIS syndromes.
Residual confounding from comorbidities, antiviral treatment, coinfections, and study design remains possible.

## Follow-up

Use this as background when asking whether post-infectious vascular risk is a general viral phenomenon or a SARS-CoV-2-specific PAIS mechanism.
It also motivates comparing cardiovascular endpoints by sex and menopausal status in long-COVID cohorts.
