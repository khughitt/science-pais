# Apparent risks of postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome diagnoses after COVID-19 vaccination and SARS-CoV-2 infection

- **Authors:** Alan C. Kwan, Joseph E. Ebinger, Janet Wei, Catherine N. Le, Jillian R. Oft, Rachel Zabner, Debbie Teodorescu, Patrick G. Botting, Jesse Navarrette, David Ouyang, Matthew Driver, Brian Claggett, Brittany N. Weber, Peng-Sheng Chen, Susan Cheng
- **Year:** 2022
- **Journal:** Nature Cardiovascular Research, vol. 1, pp. 1187–1194
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s44161-022-00177-8 (author correction: 10.1038/s44161-023-00339-2)
- **BibTeX key:** Kwan2022
- **Source:** PDF (Unpaywall OA copy)

## Key Contribution

This is the largest controlled EHR-based study to directly quantify new POTS-diagnosis rates in the 90 days after SARS-CoV-2 infection versus after COVID-19 vaccination, using a sequence–symmetry design that acts as its own internal control (each patient's pre-exposure window serves as the comparator). The study establishes that SARS-CoV-2 infection raises crude POTS diagnosis rates approximately 5.35-fold compared with the vaccinated cohort, and that vaccination itself produces a modest but statistically significant elevation above baseline. POTS emerges as one of the most common post-vaccination adverse events by frequency, surpassing myocarditis, and is substantially more frequent after natural infection.

## Methods

**Design:** Two retrospective sequence–symmetry analyses within separate, mutually exclusive cohorts drawn from the Cedars-Sinai Health System EHR (Los Angeles County, 2020–2022). Each analysis compares new diagnosis rates in the 90 days before versus 90 days after an index exposure (first vaccination dose or first documented SARS-CoV-2 infection).

**Vaccination cohort:** n = 284,592 (age 52 ± 20 years; 57% female; 62% Pfizer-BioNTech, 31% Moderna, 6.9% Johnson & Johnson/Janssen; patients with SARS-CoV-2 infection within 90 days of vaccination excluded).

**Infection cohort:** n = 12,460 (age 47 ± 23 years; 50% female; confirmed SARS-CoV-2 infection; patients with vaccination within 90 days of infection excluded).

**Outcome ascertainment:** POTS-associated diagnoses were captured by ICD-9/ICD-10 codes, grouped into a composite: (1) dysautonomia, (2) other specified cardiac dysrhythmias (primary POTS proxy, ICD codes I49.8 and G90.9), (3) mast cell activation syndrome, (4) Ehlers–Danlos syndrome (EDS), and (5) fatigue. No single ICD-10 code existed for POTS at time of study; a unique POTS ICD-10 was added to US coding in October 2022. Clinical adjudication of 50 sequentially identified patients with both I49.8 and G90.9 codes found 80% were formally confirmed or strongly consistent with POTS.

**Comparators:** Common primary care (CPC) diagnoses (20 conditions without direct COVID-19 biological plausibility) as a referent for background healthcare-engagement bias; myocarditis as a benchmark post-exposure condition.

**Statistics:** Post-exposure odds estimated by one-sample proportions testing with continuity correction; odds ratios (ORs) for POTS vs CPC estimated with logistic regression using cluster-robust standard errors; secondary analyses with clustered bootstrapping (2,000 replications). Sex-stratified analyses conducted for both cohorts.

## Key Findings

**Post-vaccination (n = 284,592):**
- POTS (ICD proxy): odds of new diagnosis in 90 days post-vaccine vs 90 days pre-vaccine = **1.52 (95% CI 1.36–1.71)**, P < 0.001.
- Dysautonomia: odds = **2.24 (1.30–3.87)**, P = 0.002.
- Composite POTS-associated diagnoses (n = 4,526): odds = **1.33 (1.25–1.41)**, P < 0.001.
- CPC diagnoses (n = 33,590): odds = **1.21 (1.18–1.23)**, P < 0.001.
- OR of POTS-associated vs CPC post-vaccine = **1.10 (1.03–1.17)**, P = 0.003 (bootstrap-confirmed 1.10 [1.02–1.17]).
- Post-vaccination ranking by odds: myocarditis (2.57) > dysautonomia (2.24) > POTS (1.52) > mast cell disorders (1.37 ns) > UTI (CPC referent, 1.32). POTS is among the highest-odds non-myocarditis post-vaccine adverse events.

**Post-infection (n = 12,460):**
- POTS (ICD proxy): odds = **2.11 (1.70–2.63)**, P < 0.001. Pre-infection rate 987/100,000; post-infection rate 2,087/100,000.
- Composite POTS-associated diagnoses (n = 1,004): odds = **1.52 (1.33–1.72)**, P < 0.001.
- CPC diagnoses (n = 3,325): odds = **1.40 (1.31–1.50)**, P < 0.001.
- OR of POTS-associated vs CPC post-infection = 1.08 (0.93–1.25), P = 0.29 (non-significant, attributed to limited infection-cohort size).

**Cross-cohort comparison:**
- Crude risk rate ratio: post-infection POTS rate was **5.35 (5.05–5.68)** times higher than post-vaccination rate. The paper explicitly cautions that this is a ratio of crude risks from mutually exclusive populations with different baseline POTS prevalence, not a causally adjusted comparison; the infection cohort was older, less female (50% vs 57%), and more racially diverse.

**Sex-stratified analyses:**
- Sex-stratified analyses were conducted and figures are presented separately for male and female patients (Figs. 1b, 1c, 2b, 2c). The paper reports "similar between-sex results for POTS-associated diagnoses" for both the vaccination and infection cohorts. EDS was rarely diagnosed in males (n = 5) vs females (n = 35), but this reflects baseline EDS sex skew, not an infection/vaccination-specific effect.
- No per-sex OR is reported for POTS or POTS-associated diagnoses. The paper does not report an infection-specific sex-amplification effect on POTS incidence. The predominance of young women in the vaccinated cohort (57% female, mean age 52) is consistent with the background POTS demographic, not an infection-specific sex interaction.

**Biological mechanisms discussed (speculative):**
- Shared antigenic component of SARS-CoV-2 spike protein between infection and vaccination may trigger immune-mediated autonomic neuropathy.
- Broad ACE2 expression suggests inflammasome activation by spike protein could produce neurocardiogenic effects and autoimmunity induction.
- Lipid nanoparticle carrier inflammatory effects noted but considered less likely than spike-mediated effects.

## Relevance

This paper provides the most rigorously designed population-level evidence that SARS-CoV-2 infection raises POTS incidence, establishing POTS/dysautonomia as a bona fide PASC subphenotype at scale. Key connections to this project:

1. **Dysautonomia as a PAIS subphenotype (task t018):** POTS is among the best-epidemiologically characterized post-infectious complications documented here. The 5.35-fold crude risk ratio for infection vs vaccination provides a benchmark for the infection-attributable burden of dysautonomia. Task t018 calls for splitting PAIS outcomes by subphenotype; this paper anchors the dysautonomia arm.

2. **Sex and female predominance (question:0007):** The paper is critically informative on what this dataset does *not* show. Sex-stratified figures reveal no infection-specific sex amplification of POTS odds; the authors describe similar between-sex results. The POTS-affected sub-cohort after vaccination (n = 1,924) is 59% female — close to the 57% female background cohort, suggesting that POTS accrual after COVID-19 tracks pre-existing POTS demographic structure rather than exposing a new female-specific mechanism. This is relevant evidence against the hypothesis that SARS-CoV-2 infection creates an *excess* sex differential above baseline POTS demographics, at least in this ICD-coded cohort.

3. **Autoantibody-mediated dysautonomia hypothesis (hypothesis:0009):** The paper invokes spike-protein-triggered immune-mediated mechanisms and autoimmunity as the most biologically plausible explanations for post-COVID POTS. This aligns with and provides epidemiological motivation for the functional-autoantibody-drives-dysautonomia hypothesis.

4. **Infection > vaccination gradient:** The consistently higher post-infection rates across nearly all studied diagnoses (not just POTS) suggests that the degree of systemic immune activation and tissue exposure to viral antigen, rather than spike-protein exposure per se, is a key severity driver for PASC endpoints.

## Limitations

1. **Single-center, single-geography:** Cedars-Sinai, Los Angeles County only. Not externally validated; demographic mix may not generalize.

2. **ICD-code ascertainment, not clinical POTS diagnosis:** No unique POTS ICD-10 code existed during the study; authors used a proxy composite. Adjudication showed 80% concordance in a small subset (n = 50), but remaining 20% represent non-POTS diagnoses. Non-differential misclassification biases toward the null in relative comparisons but inflates absolute rates.

3. **Outpatient-only capture:** Inpatient encounters excluded, minimizing confounding but potentially missing severe cases; patients seeking care outside Cedars-Sinai are unrecorded.

4. **Mutually exclusive cohorts with different baseline risks:** Infected and vaccinated populations are not comparable on demographics (50% vs 57% female; different age distributions; different race/ethnicity composition). The 5.35 crude risk ratio cannot be interpreted as a confounder-adjusted infection-vs-vaccination causal contrast.

5. **90-day window limitation:** Standard POTS diagnostic criteria require symptom duration ≥ 90 days (3 months). The observation window exactly equals the minimum duration; cases captured may include transient autonomic dysfunction that self-resolves, meaning POTS incidence is likely overestimated.

6. **No variant-level or severity stratification:** Delta and Omicron periods not distinguished; acute infection severity not recorded; vaccination timing in relation to infection history not tracked.

7. **No sex × exposure interaction test:** Sex-stratified figures are presented but no formal interaction terms or sex-specific ORs are reported. The conclusion of "similar between-sex results" is descriptive, not statistically confirmed.

8. **Detection/ascertainment bias:** Post-exposure healthcare engagement may increase, inflating all diagnosis rates symmetrically; the CPC referent is designed to capture this but assumes the effect is uniform across conditions.

9. **Author correction (2023):** A published correction exists (DOI 10.1038/s44161-023-00339-2); the nature of the correction is not specified in the main text — verify details if specific numbers are to be used in downstream analyses.

## Follow-up

- **task:t018** — This paper anchors the dysautonomia subphenotype; compare its sex-specific figures against POTS-specific cohort studies (e.g., Eldokla 2022, Finsterer 2022) to determine whether the lack of a sex-amplification signal is an ascertainment artifact or a genuine epidemiological finding.
- **question:0007** — The paper's null sex-interaction result is a key data point: sex-stratified POTS odds are not meaningfully different, suggesting POTS in PASC tracks baseline POTS demographics rather than introducing a novel female excess. This weakens the POTS/dysautonomia arm as evidence for a general PAIS sex-amplification mechanism.
- **question:0009** — Follow up on proposed autoantibody/spike-protein mechanism with functional autoantibody studies (e.g., anti-adrenergic, anti-muscarinic receptor antibody studies in post-COVID POTS).
- Consider whether the 2023 author correction changes any quantitative results before using specific numbers in synthesis.
- Seek external replication studies in non-Cedars-Sinai cohorts with formal POTS diagnosis (tilt-table confirmed) to address the ICD-code ascertainment limitation.
