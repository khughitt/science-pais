# Long COVID in autoimmune rheumatic diseases

- **Authors:** Yuliya Fedorchenko, Olena Zimba
- **Year:** 2023
- **Journal:** Rheumatology International
- **DOI:** 10.1007/s00296-023-05319-0
- **BibTeX key:** Fedorchenko2023
- **Source:** PDF

## Key Contribution

This narrative review synthesises the pathobiology, clinical phenotypes, risk factors, and longitudinal trajectory of Long COVID specifically in patients with pre-existing autoimmune rheumatic diseases (RDs), a population with altered baseline immune regulation that may predispose them to prolonged post-infectious dysfunction. The review catalogues the bidirectional SARS-CoV-2–autoimmunity relationship: RD patients face elevated COVID-19 risk and worse outcomes, while SARS-CoV-2 infection can itself trigger new-onset autoimmunity and rheumatic manifestations, including systemic sclerosis (SSc), systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), and FibroCOVID. It identifies viral persistence, chronic low-grade inflammation, autoantibody production, and endotheliopathy as the core interacting mechanisms sustaining Long COVID in this population, with immune-regulatory drugs (particularly rituximab and glucocorticoids) acting as modifiers of severity and recovery.

## Methods

**Design:** Narrative review; literature search of Medline/PubMed, Scopus, and Directory of Open Access Journals (DOAJ) for articles published through February 1, 2023.

**Search terms:** "post-COVID-19," "post-acute COVID-19 syndrome," "Long COVID," "rheumatic diseases," "immune-mediated inflammatory disease."

**Scope:** Original articles, case studies, and reviews on (1) COVID-19 features and outcomes in RD populations, (2) Long COVID immune mechanisms and clinical phenotypes, (3) autoimmunity arising from SARS-CoV-2 infection, (4) disease-specific associations (RA, SLE, SSc, Sjögren syndrome, fibromyalgia), and (5) treatment and vaccination considerations.

**Limitations of approach:** Narrative (non-systematic) design; no PRISMA flow or formal quality assessment of included studies; heterogeneous study populations, follow-up durations, and Long COVID definitions across cited sources.

## Key Findings

### Elevated risk profile of RD patients

- Patients with immune-mediated inflammatory diseases (IMIDs) had a significantly increased hospitalisation risk compared to non-IMID controls (adjusted OR 1.2; p-value not reported in text but cited from Sattui et al.).
- Specific RD odds ratios for hospitalisation: psoriatic arthritis (PsA) OR 2.0; RA OR 1.4; vasculitis OR 2.1.
- Rituximab (RTX) use conferred markedly elevated severe COVID-19 risk (OR 2.15, 95% CI 1.15–4.01); cyclophosphamide use OR 4.3 (95% CI 1.1–16.8) in ANCA-associated vasculitis (AAV) patients.
- Older age (OR 1.6, 95% CI 1.3–1.9) and RTX in giant-cell arteritis (GCA) patients (OR 1.9, 95% CI 1.3–2.8) associated with worse COVID-19 outcomes.
- Respiratory failure frequency in RD + COVID-19 patients was 38% vs 10% in non-RD controls (p<0.001).

### Long COVID epidemiology

- More than 20% of COVID-19 survivors develop persistent symptoms at ≥1 month post-infection.
- In the COVID-19 Global Rheumatology Alliance Vaccine Survey (patients with systemic autoimmune rheumatic diseases, SARDs): 1 in 4 had symptoms lasting ≥28 days; 1 in 10 for ≥90 days.
- Older age (aOR 6.5, 95% CI 3.0–14.1), comorbidities (aOR 1.1 per comorbidity, 95% CI 1.0–1.2), and osteoarthritis (aOR 2.1, 95% CI 1.0–4.3) were independent predictors of prolonged symptom duration in SARDs patients.
- Risk factors for rheumatic symptoms in general Long COVID: older age (OR 1.2 per 10-year increment, 95% CI 1.1–1.4) and female sex (OR 1.6, 95% CI 1.1–2.2).

### Immune mechanisms driving Long COVID

Four overlapping mechanisms are highlighted:

1. **Viral persistence / impaired clearance:** IgG anti-SARS-CoV-2 S1 antibodies remained significantly elevated from acute phase through Long COVID; SARS-CoV-2 S1 protein detectable in ~65% of Long COVID patients over one year. Viral reservoirs may sustain ongoing immune activation.

2. **Chronic low-grade inflammation:** TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-13 significantly elevated; interferon-γ-induced protein 10 (IP-10) significantly decreased vs acute COVID-19 onset. Upregulation of type I IFN (IFN-β) and type III IFN (IFN-λ1), and high proinflammatory cytokines (IFN-β, IFN-γ, IL-6), linked to Long COVID with 78.5–81.6% accuracy. Upregulation of proinflammatory mediators maintained for ≥8 months post-infection.

3. **Autoantibody production:** Numerous autoantibodies detected in SARS-CoV-2–infected individuals — antinuclear antibodies (ANA), ANCA, lupus anticoagulants, anti-52 kDa SSA/Ro, anti-60 kDa SSA/Ro; anti-52 kDa SSA/Ro (20%), anti-60 kDa SSA/Ro (25%), and ANA (50%) frequencies in COVID-19 patients. 83% of Long COVID patients develop latent autoimmunity; 62% present with polyautoimmunity. >85% had anti-SARS-CoV-2 IgG antibodies positively correlating with autoantibodies, age, and BMI. Mechanism: neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) expose intranuclear antigens, stimulating autoantibody production; extrafollicular B-cell activation correlates with worse prognosis and elevated neutralising antibody levels.

4. **Endotheliopathy:** Markers of endothelial cell activation — von Willebrand factor antigen (VWF:Ag), VWF pro-peptide (VWFpp), and factor VIII — significantly increased in convalescent COVID-19 vs healthy controls. D-dimer at hospital admission associated with COVID-19 mortality (OR 18.4, 95% CI 2.6–128.5). VEGF serum levels significantly elevated in ~50% (n=48) of COVID-19 patients with complications vs fully recovered subjects, suggesting dysregulated NRP-1/VEGF-A signalling.

### Disease-specific associations

**Fibromyalgia/FibroCOVID:** High musculoskeletal pain frequency in COVID-19 survivors; male sex (OR 10, 95% CI 6.02–16.43) and obesity (OR 41.2, 95% CI 18.0–98.9) as strongest FM risk factors in Long COVID. ICAF score increased after SARS-CoV-2 in FM patients.

**Rheumatoid arthritis:** Case report of 74-year-old RA patient retreating positive for SARS-CoV-2 (under prednisone + cyclophosphamide) with devastating disease course.

**SLE:** Lower frequency of Long COVID symptoms in SLE vs other RDs; fatigue and arthralgia predominant.

**Systemic sclerosis (SSc):** Radiological overlap between SSc and COVID-19 pulmonary changes complicates differentiation. RTX-treated SSc patients at increased severe SARS-CoV-2 risk (B-cell depletion → impaired viral clearance). New-onset SSc reported post-COVID in patients without prior autoimmune history.

**Sjögren syndrome (pSS):** ~57% of pSS patients develop COVID-19 symptoms after acute SARS-CoV-2 (mean follow-up 5 months). Hospitalised pSS patients have 8× higher Long COVID risk vs non-hospitalised. Elevated CRP (OR 8.6, 95% CI 1.3–104.4) and HCQ use (OR 2.5, 95% CI 1.0–6.5) are main independent risk factors for Long COVID in pSS.

**Pulmonary impairment:** Meta-analysis of 16 cohorts: 20% of post-discharge patients had pulmonary disturbances at ≥1 month; diffusion impairment most frequent. Cytokine storm may trigger pulmonary fibrosis; ARDS may cause irreversible alveolar fibrosis. Individuals with residual pulmonary injury should be monitored ≥36 months.

### Potential biomarkers

- SARS-CoV-2 S1 spike protein: detectable in ~65% of Long COVID patients at 1 year.
- VEGF: elevated in ~50% of patients with COVID-19 complications.
- Dysregulated NRP-1/VEGF-A pathway may underlie Long COVID course.

### Treatment considerations

- **RTX:** Associated with critical conditions in COVID-19 (effect size 3.3, 95% CI 1.7–6.4) and longer hospital stays (0.6, 95% CI 0.1–0.9). Long-term RTX reduces B cells → impaired viral clearance by antibodies.
- **Glucocorticoids (GC):** Doses ≥10 mg/day prednisone-equivalent associated with increased SARS-CoV-2 positivity (aOR 1.47, 95% CI 1.05–2.03), severe COVID-19 (OR 1.76, 95% CI 1.06–2.96), and COVID-19-related death (OR 3.34, 95% CI 1.23–8.9). Moderate GC doses 7.5–20 mg conferred higher Long COVID risk (RR 1.7, 95% CI 1.1–2.6 by univariate; RR 1.6, 95% CI 1.0–2.5 by multivariate). Avascular necrosis (AVN) of the hip reported in COVID-19 patients treated with prednisolone mean dose 758 mg (400–1250 mg).
- **HCQ:** HCQ did not reduce 28-day mortality in hospitalised COVID-19 patients (RECOVERY trial). Role in Long COVID unclear; proposed utility via TLR signalling inhibition and dampening proinflammatory cytokine production. Independent risk factor for Long COVID in pSS (OR 2.5, 95% CI 1.0–6.5).
- **Antiviral therapy:** Nirmatrelvir/ritonavir reduced Long COVID symptoms in one case report of RA patient; IL-6 blockade + SARS-CoV-2 antiviral in another case resolved Long COVID with antigen persistence.
- **Vaccination:** First vaccine dose reduced post-COVID-19 symptom risk by 13% (median follow-up 141 days); second dose reduced risk by additional 9% (median follow-up 67 days). Italian multicentric cohort showed vaccinated patients less likely to suffer Long COVID.

## Relevance

This paper is directly relevant to the project research question (research-question:post-acute-infection-syndromes) in multiple ways:

**PAIS failure-mode framing:** The paper explicitly frames Long COVID in RD patients as a failure of homeostatic immune recovery after acute SARS-CoV-2 infection — viral persistence drives ongoing immune stimulation, chronic low-grade inflammation prevents return to baseline, autoantibody production represents inappropriate immune activation against self, and endotheliopathy represents organ-level failure of vascular homeostasis. This maps precisely onto the PAIS shared failure-mode concept.

**Pre-existing immune dysregulation as PAIS risk modifier:** RD patients provide a natural experiment in which pre-existing immune dysregulation (altered regulatory T cells, elevated baseline cytokines, immunosuppressive drug exposure) interacts with acute infection to modulate the probability and severity of failed homeostatic recovery. This informs whether PAIS is purely an acute-infection consequence or requires pre-existing immune vulnerability.

**Immunosuppression as a double-edged modulator:** The paradox that immunosuppressive drugs (RTX, GC) both protect against autoimmunity flares but worsen viral clearance and Long COVID severity captures a central tension in understanding PAIS: interventions that restore one component of homeostasis (suppress inflammatory response) can impair another (viral clearance, adaptive immune memory).

**Specific PAIS mechanisms documented:** The review documents four mechanisms core to the PAIS frame — viral/antigen persistence, persistent low-grade inflammation, autoantibody generation, and endotheliopathy — with specific immunological markers (IFN-β, IFN-γ, IL-6, IgG anti-S1, ANA, ANCA, VWF:Ag, VEGF) that are candidate biomarkers of failed recovery.

**Time course of organ damage:** Longitudinal data (2 months to 2 years) document that the failure of recovery is not uniform — pulmonary function, fatigue, and inflammatory arthritis have distinct temporal trajectories, suggesting different component failures in the homeostatic recovery process.

## Limitations

1. **Narrative (non-systematic) design:** No PRISMA protocol, formal search strategy with PICO, or risk-of-bias assessment of included studies; conclusions reflect author synthesis rather than systematic evidence grading.
2. **Heterogeneous Long COVID definitions:** Multiple definitions used across cited studies (3–12 months duration, symptom-based, WHO definition); results may not be directly comparable.
3. **Confounding by disease activity and treatment:** RD cohort analyses rarely adjust for disease activity scores at time of infection or for full immunosuppressive regimen, making it difficult to separate disease-vs-drug effects on Long COVID outcomes.
4. **Predominantly cross-sectional cited studies:** Most longitudinal data come from cross-sectional studies at different time points rather than true prospective cohorts with individual patient follow-up, limiting causal inference.
5. **Limited RD-specific Long COVID data:** For most individual RDs (RA, SLE, SSc), the review cites primarily case reports or small case series; robust controlled studies were largely absent at time of publication.
6. **Publication cutoff:** Literature search through February 2023; does not include subsequent larger cohort studies or mechanistic investigations (e.g., post-COVID immune single-cell atlases).
7. **Geographic bias:** Many cited studies are European or Chinese hospital-based cohorts, limiting generalisability.
8. **Inability to separate pre-existing vs de novo autoimmunity:** The review does not systematically address whether autoantibodies detected in Long COVID represent pre-existing subclinical autoimmunity unmasked by infection or de novo induction.

## Model / Tool Availability

Not applicable. This is a narrative review; no computational models, tools, or datasets are released.

## Follow-up

- Larger prospective RD-specific Long COVID cohort studies with individual follow-up and standardised case definitions needed to test whether RD patients have quantifiably higher PAIS incidence/severity compared to immunocompetent controls.
- Investigate whether specific RD subtypes (SSc vs RA vs SLE) have distinct PAIS phenotypes reflecting their baseline immune dysregulation profiles — testable with registry data.
- The FibroCOVID / post-COVID fibromyalgia phenotype warrants comparison to ME/CFS: are they the same PAIS failure mode expressed at different immune activation thresholds?
- Biomarker candidates (S1 persistence, VEGF, type I/III IFN, ANA/ANCA) should be evaluated together as a panel for predicting PAIS risk in RD patients pre-discharge.
- Mechanistic question: does extrafollicular B-cell activation in severe COVID-19 (already documented to correlate with poor prognosis) represent the immunological transition point from acute to PAIS?
