# Taurine supplementation as a therapeutic strategy for cellular senescence and chronic inflammation in long COVID: a systematic review and meta-analysis

- **Authors:** Kaiming Wang, Chen Hsiang Ma, Mobin Khoramjoo, Janice Y. Kung, Gavin Y. Oudit
- **Year:** 2026
- **Journal:** BMC Infectious Diseases
- **Volume/Pages:** 26:774
- **DOI:** 10.1186/s12879-026-13009-y
- **BibTeX key:** Wang2026b
- **Source:** Full-text PDF (papers/pdfs/2026_Wang_taurine-cellular-senescence-chronic-fatigue.pdf), read 2026-06-20.

## Key Contribution

This PRISMA-compliant systematic review and meta-analysis (27 RCTs and non-randomized trials, n = 1,030) synthesizes clinical trial evidence on oral taurine supplementation across metabolic, cardiovascular, inflammatory, and neurocognitive domains that overlap with PASC pathophysiology. A parallel pooled analysis of six convalescence-phase studies (n = 308) documents significantly lower plasma taurine levels in individuals with persistent PASC symptoms versus recovered, symptom-free counterparts (SMD -0.35, 95% CI: -0.63 to -0.08). The paper argues that this relative taurine deficiency, together with taurine's known anti-senescence and anti-inflammatory properties, positions taurine supplementation (~3,000 mg/day) as a candidate therapeutic strategy to reduce senescence burden and chronic inflammation in long COVID.

**Important caveat — study type and evidence level:** The 27 clinical trials pooled here were **not conducted in PASC patients**; they enrolled populations sharing pathophysiological features with PASC (metabolic syndrome, T2DM, obesity, heart failure, sepsis, cognitive impairment). No direct RCT of taurine in a PASC-defined cohort exists. Evidence level is therefore **indirect/inferential** — the mechanistic rationale is plausible but requires formal validation in dedicated long COVID trials. The taurine-deficiency finding in PASC convalescence is observational and based on only six studies.

## Methods

**Study design:** Systematic review and meta-analysis following PRISMA 2020 guidelines; registered in PROSPERO (CRD420251011508, 16 March 2025).

**Literature search:** Six databases (MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane Library, CINAHL, Web of Science, Scopus) searched through 21 March 2025 for clinical trials of oral taurine supplementation in conditions sharing core PASC pathological features (chronic inflammation, immune dysregulation, endothelial dysfunction, metabolic syndrome). A separate targeted search (through 8 April 2025) identified studies reporting plasma taurine levels during COVID-19 convalescence.

**Inclusion criteria:** Randomized and non-randomized clinical trials, adults ≥18 years; outcomes in neurocognitive, metabolic, cardiovascular, and gastrointestinal domains. Studies co-administering taurine with other nutrients, using non-enteric routes, or evaluating performance enhancement in healthy/athlete populations were excluded.

**Analysis:** Random-effects restricted maximum-likelihood (REML) models, standardized mean differences (SMD) with 95% CIs. Heterogeneity quantified via I², tau², chi-square. Cochrane RoB 2 tool for risk of bias. Meta-regression explored age, sex, daily dose, and treatment duration as moderators. Sensitivity analysis used Cook's distance and one-study-removal. Publication bias assessed by rank correlation and Egger's regression.

**Included trials:** 27 trials (n = 1,030; 50.5% female); median dose 3,000 mg/day (range 1,000–6,000 mg/day), median duration 8 weeks (range 1–16 weeks). Study populations: T2DM (15), neurocognitive (4), gastrointestinal/hepatic (3), cardiovascular (5). Sample sizes ranged from 14 to 120 participants per trial. Twelve studies (44.4%) were low risk of bias; 11 (40.7%) some concerns; 4 (14.8%) high risk.

**Convalescence taurine-level analysis:** Six studies (n = 308; 104 recovered, 204 with persistent PASC) identified comparing plasma taurine between recovered and persistent PASC cohorts.

## Key Findings

**Metabolic parameters:**
- HbA1c reduced: SMD -0.23 (95% CI: -0.42 to -0.03; 10 studies)
- Fasting blood glucose reduced: SMD -0.38 (95% CI: -0.59 to -0.17; 8 studies)
- Fasting insulin reduced: SMD -0.40 (95% CI: -0.63 to -0.16; 7 studies)
- HOMA-IR reduced: SMD -0.36 (95% CI: -0.59 to -0.13; 7 studies)
- Total cholesterol reduced: SMD -0.50 (95% CI: -0.85 to -0.15; 12 studies); significant heterogeneity (I² = 66.75%)
- Triglycerides reduced: SMD -0.50 (95% CI: -0.98 to -0.03; 12 studies); significant heterogeneity (I² = 82.01%)
- LDL reduced: SMD -0.33 (95% CI: -0.55 to -0.11; 8 studies)
- HDL: no significant effect (SMD 0.49, 95% CI: -0.09 to 1.08; 10 studies)

**Inflammation and oxidative stress:**
- CRP reduced: SMD -0.77 (95% CI: -1.01 to -0.54; 7 studies)
- TNF-α reduced: SMD -0.42 (95% CI: -0.65 to -0.19; 6 studies)
- IL-6: trend toward reduction (SMD -0.54, 95% CI: -1.07 to -0.01; 6 studies) — wide CI
- IL-10: trend toward increase, non-significant (SMD 0.62, 95% CI: -0.11 to 1.34; 3 studies)
- MDA (lipid peroxidation/oxidative stress): reduced SMD -0.71 (95% CI: -1.17 to -0.24; 6 studies)
- Meta-regression identified dose-response relationship: TNF-α (β -0.006, p = 0.03), IL-6 (β -0.0008, p = 0.02)

**Cardiovascular / cardiopulmonary:**
- Systolic BP reduced: SMD -0.30 (95% CI: -0.56 to -0.05; 9 studies)
- Diastolic BP reduced: SMD -0.29 (95% CI: -0.49 to -0.10)
- Heart rate: no significant effect (SMD -0.15, 95% CI: -0.59 to 0.30)
- Exercise capacity: suggestive improvement (SMD 0.50, 95% CI: 0.02 to 0.98; 3 studies)

**Neurocognition:**
- No significant effect: pooled SMD 0.07 (95% CI: -0.24 to 0.38; 4 studies); all four studies were in institutionalized elderly or psychosis populations, rated high risk of bias

**Plasma taurine levels in PASC convalescence:**
- After excluding one influential outlier study, PASC individuals had significantly lower plasma taurine than recovered counterparts: SMD -0.35 (95% CI: -0.63 to -0.08; I² = 11.70%; p = 0.01; 5 studies)

**Senescence mechanism (mechanistic rationale cited from preclinical literature):**
- Taurine interacts with CDKN2AIP, a positive regulator of p53, suppressing p53-p21 and p16INK4a signaling axes
- In vitro taurine treatment reduces SA-β-gal activity in irradiated osteoblasts
- Taurine inhibits NF-κB signaling, suppressing SASP cytokines (IL-1β, TNF-α, IL-6, IL-8)
- Upregulates Nrf2-mediated antioxidant enzymes (heme oxygenase-1, glutathione peroxidase, peroxiredoxin, thioredoxin)
- NLRP3 inflammasome suppression via taurine chloramine
- Taurine upregulation of hydrogen sulfide may counteract endothelial dysfunction and microvascular hypercoagulability observed in long COVID

Note: all senescence-mechanism claims above are drawn from preclinical/mechanistic literature cited by the authors, not from the meta-analyzed clinical trials themselves.

## Relevance

This paper is relevant to PAIS as the first systematic evidence synthesis framing taurine as a candidate senolytic/anti-SASP intervention for long COVID. Key links to project themes:

- **Cellular senescence / SASP as PAIS mechanism:** The paper operationalizes the senescence-SASP axis (hypothesis:0001-shared-dysregulated-attractor) as a tractable therapeutic target. Virus-induced cellular senescence producing persistent NF-κB-driven inflammation is proposed as a shared driver across PASC, ME/CFS, and related PAIS.
- **Chronic inflammation markers:** Significant reductions in CRP, TNF-α, and MDA — canonical PAIS biomarkers — are documented across trials (topic:biomarkers-and-objective-endpoints). These map onto hypothesis:0003-immune-exhaustion-feedback: persistent inflammatory signaling that may both result from and perpetuate immune dysregulation.
- **Metabolic and mitochondrial dimension:** Taurine's mitochondrial stabilization role (stabilizing electron transport chain, enhancing bioenergetics, reducing oxidative stress) is directly relevant to question:0011-mitochondrial-basis-of-pem. Metabolic improvements (insulin sensitivity, lipid profile) in pooled trials suggest systemic metabolic rescue that could alleviate PEM-adjacent fatigue.
- **Endothelial and cardiopulmonary outcomes:** Blood pressure reduction and exercise capacity improvement point to endothelial and cardiopulmonary benefit, overlapping with thromboinflammation pathways relevant to this project.
- **Convergence with ME/CFS:** The inflammaging/senescence framing invoked here is shared with ME/CFS mechanistic hypotheses (topic:mecfs-long-covid-convergence). Taurine deficiency during convalescence is proposed as a biomarker distinguishing non-resolving from resolving PASC.
- **Gut microbiome axis:** The paper discusses SARS-CoV-2-induced gut dysbiosis disrupting enterohepatic taurine recycling as a mechanism of PASC-associated taurine depletion — consistent with gut-microbiome dysbiosis as a recurring PAIS candidate mechanism.

## Limitations

1. **No direct PASC trial evidence.** The 27 pooled trials enrolled populations with metabolic or comorbid conditions that share PASC pathophysiological features — none enrolled PASC-diagnosed patients. All inferences about PASC applicability are extrapolative and require dedicated trials. This is the most important limitation for PAIS-framework use.

2. **Substantial heterogeneity** in several analyses (I² > 50% for total cholesterol, triglycerides, HDL), partly attributable to diversity of study populations, dose regimens, and comparators. Treatment effects likely vary by baseline health status and comorbidity profile in ways the meta-regression could not fully resolve.

3. **Small trial sizes.** Individual studies ranged from n = 14 to n = 120 (median ~30). This limits precision of individual estimates and increases vulnerability to publication bias despite negative funnel-plot asymmetry tests.

4. **Risk of bias.** Six of 27 trials were non-randomized (including 2 in metabolic disorders, 2 in elderly women with cognitive impairment, 1 brain injury, 1 sepsis). Fourteen percent rated high risk of bias.

5. **Neurocognition results are unreliable.** The four neurocognition studies were all in institutionalized elderly or early psychosis populations rated high risk of bias; extrapolation to PASC cognitive symptoms (brain fog) is premature.

6. **Convalescence taurine-level analysis is observational and sensitive to a single influential study.** The pooled PASC taurine deficit finding depended substantially on one study (Cook's d = 0.55, ΔSMD = 1.15); after its exclusion, effect was reduced but remained significant. Age and follow-up duration were proposed as confounders.

7. **Senescence mechanism is preclinical only.** The proposed anti-senescence effects of taurine (p53/p21/p16INK4a, SA-β-gal reduction, SASP suppression) are drawn from cell-culture and animal models. No clinical trial in this meta-analysis measured senescence biomarkers (e.g., p16, p21, SA-β-gal, SASP panel).

8. **Co-nutrient synergy excluded by design.** Studies combining taurine with other nutrients were excluded; this limits generalizability to taurine combination therapies prevalent in the literature.

9. **Case definition.** PASC is not defined uniformly across the six convalescence studies; heterogeneity in symptom persistence criteria, follow-up duration (3–24 months), and confirmation of prior SARS-CoV-2 infection should be noted.

## Model / Tool Availability

This is a systematic review/meta-analysis; no computational model, dataset, or tool is released. The R package `metafor` (version 4.8.0) was used for all pooled analyses. Search strategies are provided in Supplemental Tables 2 and 3.

## Follow-up

- A dedicated Phase 2/3 RCT of taurine supplementation in PASC-diagnosed patients is the obvious next step flagged by the authors (none yet exists as of publication).
- Khoramjoo et al. 2024 (PLoS ONE) — cited as reference [24] — documented the plasma taurine/PASC symptom link using multi-omics; read for methodological detail on the convalescence taurine biomarker.
- Wang et al. 2023 (Cell Rep Med) — reference [22] — is the multi-omics study identifying taurine as a top upregulated metabolite 6 months post-COVID-19 infection; this should be cross-checked against Wang2023/Wang2023b already in this project.
- Singh et al. 2023 (Science) — reference [20] — on taurine deficiency as a driver of aging; provides the aging-biology foundation for the senescence hypothesis.
- Question for project: Does the taurine-deficiency signal in PASC covary with other SASP/senescence markers (p16, GDF-15, IL-6)? Existing PAIS biomarker datasets (e.g., UK Biobank, RECOVER) may contain plasma metabolomics to test this.
- The NLRP3 inflammasome pathway is implicated here (ref [88–90]) — relevant to whether inflammasome-targeted therapies (e.g., colchicine, anakinra) and taurine operate on the same node.
- Gut microbiome axis: SARS-CoV-2 disrupting enterohepatic taurine recycling is mechanistically actionable — consider cross-referencing with microbiome dysbiosis papers already in this project.
