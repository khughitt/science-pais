# Sex hormones and immune system

- **Authors:** Marina Averyanova, Polina Vishnyakova, Svetlana Yureneva, Oksana Yakushevskaya, Timur Fatkhudinov, Andrey Elchaninov, Gennady Sukhikh
- **Year:** 2022
- **Journal:** Frontiers in Immunology
- **DOI:** 10.3389/fimmu.2022.928171
- **BibTeX key:** Averyanova2022
- **Source:** PDF: `~/d/health/processes/post-acute-infection/papers/pdfs/2022_Averyanova_Sex-hormones-and-immune-system-Menopausal-hormone-therapy-in-the-context-of-COVID-19-pandemic.pdf`

## Key Contribution

Averyanova et al. review how estrogens, progesterone, and menopausal hormone therapy modulate innate and adaptive immune responses in the context of COVID-19.
The paper supplies mechanistic plausibility for hormone-immune interaction, while acknowledging that COVID-19 outcomes cannot be explained by sex steroids alone.

## Methods

This is a narrative immunology review based on PubMed searches for COVID-19, SARS-CoV-2, sex hormones, estradiol, progesterone, immune response, menopause, and hormone therapy.
It integrates in vitro, animal, observational, and guideline literature.

## Key Findings

The review describes estrogen receptors on immune cells and summarizes anti-inflammatory effects of physiologic estrogen signaling, including reduced TNF-alpha, IL-1beta, and IL-6 production in several experimental systems.
It argues that postmenopausal low estrogen may weaken anti-inflammatory regulation and that menopausal hormone therapy can affect cytokine production, humoral response, vascular endothelium, and hemostasis.
The authors cite observational studies suggesting reduced severe or lethal COVID-19 outcomes among some MHT users, but they also emphasize limitations and the need for further investigation.
They summarize guidance that MHT generally should not be discontinued in mild/moderate COVID-19, with critical illness and thromboembolic risk requiring individualized management.

## Relevance

This paper is a mechanism source, not direct PAIS evidence.
It supports a plausible route from sex hormones to immune-resolution thresholds, but the project should not infer that estrogen deficiency causes long COVID.
It is best used to motivate targeted hypotheses about inflammatory resolution, endothelial protection, and B-cell/humoral effects.

## Limitations

The review is broad and narrative.
Much of the mechanistic evidence is indirect, acute-COVID-focused, or extrapolated from non-COVID systems.
MHT observational findings are vulnerable to healthy-user bias, indication bias, route/dose confounding, and thromboembolic-risk selection.
