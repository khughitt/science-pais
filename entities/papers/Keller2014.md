# Inability of myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome patients to reproduce VO2peak indicates functional impairment

- **Authors:** Betsy A. Keller, John Luke Pryor, Ludovic Giloteaux
- **Year:** 2014
- **Journal:** Journal of Translational Medicine
- **DOI:** 10.1186/1479-5876-12-104
- **BibTeX key:** Keller2014
- **Source:** Europe PMC full-text XML (OA; PMCID PMC4004422)

## Key Contribution

This paper provides one of the clearest objective physiological demonstrations of post-exertional malaise (PEM) in ME/CFS: patients diagnosed by the Fukuda 1994 criteria were unable to reproduce cardiopulmonary exercise test (CPET) results when retested 24 hours after a first maximal test. Because VO2peak is highly reproducible (test-retest difference ≤ 7%, r ≥ 0.95) in healthy and deconditioned adults and in most patient populations, a decline of 13.8% in ME/CFS — achieved under confirmed maximal effort on both days (RER ≥ 1.1, unchanged between tests) — cannot be explained by deconditioning or motivational factors. It constitutes an objective physiological signature of PEM. This grounds a strong counter-argument to the "deconditioning model": simple deconditioning predicts stable VO2peak across trials, not a worse result the next day.

The study also shows that a single CPET systematically over-estimates functional ability in ME/CFS: using the Weber & Janicki classification, 50% of participants were reclassified to a more impaired category when the day-2 result was used.

## Methods

### Participants

- N = 22 ME/CFS patients (17 female, 5 male); illness duration > 6 months.
- **Case definition:** Fukuda et al. 1994 (CDC criteria), applied by referring physicians.
- Mean age 43.7 ± 11.82 years; BMI 27.4 ± 6.59 (overweight cohort).
- Exclusions: high cardiovascular risk (AHA criteria), co-morbidities or orthopaedic limitations affecting cycling.

### Two-Day CPET Protocol

- **Day 1 (CPET 1):** ramp cycle ergometer test to volitional exhaustion.
  - Protocol: 3 min seated rest → 0 W start, +25 W every 2 min, pedal cadence 60–70 rpm (Lode Corival electro-magnetically braked ergometer).
  - Pre-test: 12-lead ECG, supine BP and HR after 5 min rest.
  - Continuous expired-gas collection (breath-by-breath) via open-circuit spirometry (PARVO Medics TrueMax 2400, calibrated before each test).
  - RPE (Borg 6–20), BP, and ECG collected per stage.
- **Day 2 (CPET 2):** identical protocol, 23–24 hours after CPET 1.
- Participant instructions: no food or smoking for 2 h, no caffeine/alcohol for 4 h, no exercise for 24 h before each test; medications used as usual.
- Same seat height on both tests.

### Variables

- Measured at **peak** (maximal) intensity: VO2peak, HRpeak, Work@peak, Ve peak, VCO2peak, O2 pulse@peak, RERpeak.
- Measured at **ventilatory threshold (VT)**: VO2@VT, HR@VT, Work@VT, Ve@VT, VCO2@VT, O2 pulse@VT.
- VT identified via V-slope algorithm (Beaver 1986) with visual confirmation by the same trained investigator.
- Functional impairment classified using the Weber & Janicki four-class system (A: VO2peak > 20; B: 16–20; C: 10–15; D: < 10 ml·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹).

### Statistics

- Repeated-measures ANOVA for VO2, HR, Work, Ve and derived variables; Wilcoxon Signed-Rank for RERpeak (non-parametric). Significance threshold: P < 0.05. SPSS v20.

## Key Findings

### Baseline Aerobic Capacity (CPET 1)

- Mean VO2peak = 21.9 ml·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ = 77.1% of age/sex-matched sedentary predicted values (Bruce norms). Low but within the range attributable to deconditioning on a single test.

### Day-2 Declines at Peak Intensity (CPET 1 → CPET 2)

| Variable | CPET 1 | CPET 2 | % change | P |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VO2peak (ml·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹) | 21.9 | 18.6 | **−13.8%** | < 0.001 |
| %pred VO2peak | 77.1% | 65.2% | — | < 0.001 |
| HRpeak (bpm) | 159.4 | 150.0 | −5.9% (−9 bpm) | 0.001 |
| Work@peak (W) | 122.7 | 105.7 | **−12.5%** | 0.012 |
| Ve peak (L·min⁻¹) | 54.5 | 44.6 | −14.7% | 0.003 |
| VCO2peak (L·min⁻¹) | 1.91 | 1.58 | −16.1% | < 0.001 |
| O2 pulse@peak (ml·beat⁻¹) | 10.48 | 9.46 | −8.8% | 0.003 |
| **RERpeak** | **1.17** | **1.14** | **−1.9%** | **0.157 (ns)** |

All peak variables decreased significantly; RERpeak did **not** differ between tests, confirming maximal effort on both days.

### Day-2 Declines at Ventilatory Threshold (CPET 1 → CPET 2)

| Variable | CPET 1 | CPET 2 | % change | P |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VO2@VT (ml·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹) | 12.2 | 9.9 | **−15.8%** | 0.003 |
| HR@VT (bpm) | 113.5 | 107.9 | −4.9% | 0.086 (ns) |
| Work@VT (W) | 51.4 | 41.4 | **−21.3%** | 0.030 |
| Ve@VT (L·min⁻¹) | 21.2 | 18.8 | −7.4% | 0.035 |
| VCO2@VT (L·min⁻¹) | 0.86 | 0.72 | −11.3% | 0.014 |
| O2 pulse@VT (ml·beat⁻¹) | 8.15 | 7.00 | −12.6% | 0.003 |

HR@VT declined ~5 bpm but did not reach significance (higher coefficient of variation ~19%). All other VT variables declined significantly.

### Individual Patient Heterogeneity

- All 22 patients showed a clinically meaningful decline in either VO2peak or VO2@VT (or both) beyond normative test-retest variability (> 7%).
- Patients whose VO2peak did not decrease on day 2 invariably showed a decrease in VO2@VT, and vice versa — suggesting the physiological impairment can manifest at different intensity thresholds across individuals, but no patient was spared.

### Functional Impairment Re-classification

- Using VO2peak alone: 8 of 22 participants (37%) reclassified to a more impaired Weber-Janicki category by day 2.
- Using VO2@VT alone: 12 of 22 (55%) reclassified.
- Combined: **50% of participants** had worse functional impairment classification when day-2 data were used.
- Single-CPET assessment systematically over-estimates functional ability in ME/CFS.

## Relevance

### PEM as an Objective Physiological Phenomenon

This paper is a foundational source for the claim that PEM in ME/CFS is not subjective symptom amplification or motivational deficit, but a reproducible, measurable failure of aerobic physiology. The RER > 1.1 on both days eliminates sub-maximal effort as a confound. The magnitude of decline (13–21% across variables) far exceeds the ≤ 7% normative test-retest variation documented in healthy inactive adults, children, and most patient populations.

### Counter-argument to Deconditioning

Simple deconditioning predicts stable or improving VO2peak across repeated maximal tests — not a worse result the next day. The day-2 decline is therefore mechanistically specific to ME/CFS physiology and cannot be attributed to inactivity alone. This is a critical boundary condition for any PAIS mechanistic model.

### Candidate Provoked Endpoint for Trials and Diagnosis

The 2-day CPET protocol operationalizes PEM as a quantitative, reproducible endpoint. Key candidate endpoints from this study:
- VO2peak day-2 decline (% or absolute)
- Work@peak day-2 decline
- VO2@VT day-2 decline
- Work@VT day-2 decline (the largest signal, −21.3%)

The VT measures are especially relevant because they reflect the metabolic intensity at which aerobic-to-anaerobic transition occurs — directly tied to bioenergetic efficiency, the focus of question:0011.

### Bioenergetic Implications

The fact that O2 pulse (VO2/HR) also declined significantly on day 2 at both peak and VT (−8.8% and −12.6%) points to reduced oxygen delivery or extraction efficiency, consistent with downstream bioenergetic impairment (mitochondrial or circulatory). The earlier onset of the VT on day 2 (lower Work@VT) means ME/CFS patients cross into anaerobic metabolism at a lower workload after a prior exertion — consistent with hypothesis that a prior exercise bout destabilizes aerobic energy production.

## Project Framework Mapping

- **hypothesis:0001-shared-dysregulated-attractor:** The bioenergetic/metabolic impairment loop is proposed as one of the self-reinforcing axes in the attractor model. Keller2014's day-2 decrement is the sharpest published evidence that an exertional stressor tips an ME/CFS patient's physiology into a measurably worse state — consistent with attractor dynamics (the system does not spontaneously return to baseline within 24 hours).
- **question:0011-mitochondrial-basis-of-pem:** Keller2014 supplies the key provoked physiological phenotype — quantitative VO2 and workload decline at VT — that any bioenergetic/mitochondrial model of PEM must explain. The VT decline is mechanistically closest to mitochondrial efficiency (aerobic-to-anaerobic threshold shift). This study does not address molecular mechanism, but it defines the endpoint that molecular studies need to account for.
- **topic:biomarkers-and-objective-endpoints:** The 2-day CPET protocol is a lead candidate for an objective PEM endpoint in clinical trials and disability assessment. The misclassification finding (50% of patients placed in wrong functional class by a single CPET) has direct implications for both research and medico-legal contexts.
- **topic:mecfs-long-covid-convergence:** If long COVID patients show a similar day-2 CPET decrement, it would strongly support convergent PEM physiology across triggers. Keller2014 is ME/CFS-only (Fukuda 1994); cross-trigger replication is needed.

## Limitations / Caveats

- **Small N = 22**; primarily female (77%), consistent with ME/CFS sex distribution but limits generalizability, particularly for males.
- **Case definition:** Fukuda 1994 (CDC criteria). This is the broadest and most widely used ME/CFS case definition, but it does not require PEM as a mandatory criterion. Results may differ under stricter criteria (CCC, ICC) that require PEM — though the directional implication is that strict-PEM cohorts would show equal or larger day-2 decrements.
- **No healthy control group for the 2-day protocol.** The normative threshold (≤ 7% test-retest variation) is cited from prior literature rather than demonstrated in a matched control arm in this study. Cross-study comparison introduces potential for cohort and protocol differences.
- **No age/sex-matched sedentary controls completing the same 2-day protocol.** Comparison is to sedentary norms for VO2peak (single test), not to deconditioned patients of similar fitness who also perform two tests. A mildly deconditioned non-ME/CFS control group performing the same protocol would further strengthen the argument.
- **Cohort is overweight (mean BMI 27.4)**; weight status may contribute to low baseline VO2peak, though it does not explain the directional day-2 decline.
- **Single exercise mode (cycle ergometry)**; results may differ for treadmill or other modalities.
- **No molecular, immune, or autonomic measures** were collected alongside the CPET. The physiological pattern is documented but the mechanism is not addressed within this study.
- **Single-site study** (Ithaca College / Cornell). Protocol and equipment are clearly specified, facilitating replication, but independent validation is important.
- **Referral bias**: patients were referred by physicians and had already been diagnosed; may not represent unselected ME/CFS patients.

## Model / Tool Availability

None. This is a clinical exercise physiology study; data collection used standard CPET equipment (Lode Corival ergometer, PARVO Medics TrueMax 2400). No software or models are released.

## Follow-up

- Replication in long COVID cohorts using the same 2-day CPET protocol would test whether the day-2 decrement is a convergent PAIS physiological feature (directly relevant to question:0011 and hypothesis:0001).
- Pairing the 2-day CPET with pre/post blood draws for metabolomics, immune profiling, or mitochondrial function assays would link the objective physiological signature to candidate molecular mechanisms. Che2025 (already in this project) does this and should be read alongside Keller2014.
- The Weber-Janicki classification finding is relevant to disability determination in ME/CFS and has policy implications worth tracking.
- Snell et al. (cited as ref 17) and Vermeulen et al. (ref 18) are the prior 2-day CPET studies that Keller2014 builds on; both may warrant their own paper notes if not already summarized.
