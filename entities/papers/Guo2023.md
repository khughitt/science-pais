# Deficient butyrate-producing capacity in the gut microbiome is associated with bacterial network disturbances and fatigue symptoms in ME/CFS

## Key Contribution

Large multi-omic ME/CFS gut-microbiome study showing that the recurrent signal is not only taxonomic
dysbiosis but a functional deficit in butyrate-producing capacity. This is the strongest ME/CFS anchor for
the t007 gut axis because it combines metagenomics, qPCR, fecal SCFA metabolomics, microbial-network
analysis, and fatigue association.

## Methods

- **Design:** case-control, geographically diverse cohort.
- **Participants:** 106 ME/CFS cases and 91 healthy controls.
- **Assays:** fecal shotgun metagenomics, functional metagenomics, qPCR of butyrate-production genes,
  fecal short-chain fatty-acid metabolomics, microbial-network analysis, and clinical phenotyping.

## Key Findings

- ME/CFS cases differed from controls in microbiome diversity, taxa, functional pathways, and interaction
  networks.
- *Faecalibacterium prausnitzii* and *Eubacterium rectale*, two abundant butyrate-producing species, were
  reduced in ME/CFS.
- Functional metagenomics, qPCR, and fecal SCFA metabolomics confirmed deficient butyrate-producing
  capacity.
- Microbiome-based classifiers generalized across geographic variation.
- *Faecalibacterium prausnitzii* abundance was inversely associated with fatigue severity.

## Relevance

This paper supplies the ME/CFS leg of `proposition:0031`: a butyrate/SCFA deficit recurs in a non-COVID
post-infectious illness class, strengthening the long-COVID signal from Liu2022/Su2023 from "COVID-only"
to "LC + ME/CFS convergence."

## Limitations

- Observational case-control design; cannot establish whether butyrate-producing deficits are causal,
  compensatory, diet-mediated, or downstream of illness behavior.
- IBS and dietary factors remain important confounders even when modeled.
- ME/CFS duration and onset-trigger heterogeneity limit direct comparability to long COVID.
