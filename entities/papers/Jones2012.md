# Loss of capacity to recover from acidosis on repeat exercise in chronic fatigue syndrome

- **Authors:** David E. J. Jones, Kieren G. Hollingsworth, Djordje G. Jakovljevic, Gulnar Fattakhova, Jessie Pairman, Andrew M. Blamire, Michael I. Trenell, Julia L. Newton
- **Year:** 2012
- **Journal:** European Journal of Clinical Investigation, 42(2), 186-194
- **DOI:** 10.1111/j.1365-2362.2011.02567.x
- **PMID:** 21749371
- **BibTeX key:** Jones2012
- **Source:** PubMed record plus ME Research UK abstract mirror

## Key Contribution

This is the strongest existing ME/CFS muscle-endpoint result for `task:t056`: a controlled, repeated-exercise 31P-MRS study showing abnormal skeletal-muscle bioenergetic response and recovery in CDC-1994 CFS patients. It directly addresses the missing ME/CFS side of the h0006-A1 vs h0008-M3 comparison, but it is not an Appelman2024 clone: the endpoint is intramuscular pH/acidosis recovery during three bouts of exercise, not before/after muscle-biopsy OXPHOS and SDH after provoked PEM.

## Methods

- **Design:** case-control exercise-provocation study with repeated muscle magnetic resonance spectroscopy.
- **Cohort:** 18 CDC-1994 CFS patients and 12 sedentary controls.
- **Protocol:** maximal voluntary contraction assessment, three bouts of repeat exercise with 31P-MRS, plus cardiorespiratory fitness testing to determine anaerobic threshold.
- **Main muscle readouts:** phosphocreatine depletion as a marker of muscle work, intramuscular pH/acidosis, and time for pH to recover to baseline.

## Key Findings

- The CFS group split into two physiologic subgroups: 8 patients had normal phosphocreatine depletion during exercise, while 10 showed low phosphocreatine depletion consistent with abnormally low generated force/MVC.
- Across the full CFS group, anaerobic threshold, heart rate, VO2, VO2 peak, and peak work were reduced compared with sedentary controls.
- Resting muscle pH was similar in patients and controls, arguing against a simple resting acidosis phenotype.
- In the subgroup that achieved normal phosphocreatine depletion, patients developed greater intramuscular acidosis than controls after comparable work during each of the three exercise periods.
- The normal reduction in acidosis across repeat exercise was absent, and pH recovery to baseline was prolonged almost four-fold.

## Relevance

For `proposition:0030`, this is direct support that ME/CFS has an exercise-provoked skeletal-muscle bioenergetic abnormality. For `hypothesis:0006`, it partially fills the previously empty ME/CFS muscle-endpoint cell: muscle-level pathology is not restricted to long COVID. For `proposition:0011` and h0008-M3, it forces a narrower reading of the endpoint-specific PEM claim: the correct contrast is no longer "ME/CFS has no muscle endpoint datum"; it is "ME/CFS has muscle-endpoint abnormalities, but not yet the same post-PEM OXPHOS/SDH biopsy phenotype under a harmonized protocol."

## Limitations

- Small sample and CDC-1994 CFS definition; PEM status was not phenotyped with modern DSQ/CCC-style criteria.
- The assay captures pH/acidosis and phosphocreatine dynamics, not mitochondrial OXPHOS capacity, SDH activity, fiber-type shift, immune infiltrate, or myopathic injury.
- The exercise protocol is not harmonized with Appelman2024's CPET-plus-day-after-biopsy design.
- The split between normal-PCr-depletion and low-PCr-depletion patients means the muscle phenotype is heterogeneous, not universal.
