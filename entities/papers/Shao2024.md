# Pathogenic mechanisms of cardiovascular damage in COVID-19

- **Authors:** Hong-Hua Shao and Rui-Xing Yin
- **Year:** 2024
- **Journal:** Molecular Medicine
- **Volume/Pages:** 30:92
- **DOI:** 10.1186/s10020-024-00855-2
- **BibTeX key:** Shao2024
- **Source:** Full-text PDF (papers/pdfs/2024_Shao_cardiovascular-damage-mechanisms-covid19.pdf), read 2026-06-20.
- **Evidence level:** Review (narrative literature review; no original data)

## Key Contribution

A comprehensive narrative review synthesizing the pathogenic mechanisms and clinical manifestations of cardiovascular damage in COVID-19, with particular focus on two overarching pathways: direct SARS-CoV-2 cytotoxicity (via ACE2-mediated viral entry into cardiomyocytes, pericytes, and endothelial cells) and indirect hyperimmune responses (cytokine storm, autoimmune myocarditis, complement activation, thromboinflammation). The review covers the full spectrum of cardiovascular complications — myocarditis and pericarditis, hypertension, arrhythmia, myocardial injury and heart failure, coronary heart disease, stress cardiomyopathy, ischemic stroke, coagulopathy, and dyslipidemia — and surveys therapeutic strategies including anticoagulation, immunotherapy, antivirals, and ACE inhibitor/ARB use. It serves as a mechanistic map of the acute cardiovascular injury landscape that underlies some post-acute cardiovascular sequelae.

## Methods

Narrative literature review. PubMed and Web of Science databases searched from 12 December 2019 to 12 April 2024 using MeSH terms and keywords: "COVID-19", "SARS-CoV-2", "cardiovascular damage", "myocardial injury", "myocarditis", "hypertension", "arrhythmia", "heart failure", "coronary heart disease". Search was restricted to English-language publications; articles from 2023–2024 were preferentially included for recency. Salient articles were selected, extracted, and synthesized narratively. No formal PRISMA protocol, meta-analysis, or risk-of-bias assessment is described; this is an expert narrative synthesis.

## Key Findings

**SARS-CoV-2 structure and cardiovascular entry route.** The spike (S) protein S1 subunit receptor binding domain (RBD) binds ACE2 on the cell surface; TMPRSS2 and Furin are required co-factors for priming/cleavage. ACE2 is highly expressed on cardiomyocytes and pericytes (more than on endothelial cells or fibroblasts), making the myocardium directly vulnerable. ACE2 downregulation following viral binding activates RAAS, increasing angiotensin II/Ang-(1-7) imbalance and contributing to inflammation and cardiovascular stress.

**Two core pathogenic mechanisms:**

1. **Direct viral cytotoxicity.** SARS-CoV-2 infects cardiac cells via ACE2, causing direct cardiomyocyte damage. The S protein disrupts endothelial integrity and induces a pro-inflammatory phenotype via NF-κB and MAPK signaling through TLR2 binding; the N protein also contributes. Oxidative stress (NADPH-oxidase activation, superoxide production, mitochondrial damage) amplifies endothelial cell injury. S protein activates the alternative complement pathway (increasing NLRP3), driving further endothelial cytotoxicity.

2. **Indirect hyperimmune response.** Cytokine storm (elevated IL-6, IL-1β, IL-10, TNF-α, CRP) drives systemic inflammation, vascular permeability, platelet activation, and myocardial injury. CD4+ T helper and cytotoxic CD8+ T cells activate inflammatory cascades; macrophage infiltration can cause myocardial fibrosis and chronic low-level inflammation. Molecular mimicry may trigger autoimmune myocarditis; anti-cardiolipin and anti-apolipoprotein A1 antibodies have been found in long COVID patients and are linked to adverse cardiovascular events. ORF8 (a SARS-CoV-2-specific accessory protein) modulates cytokine responses.

**Cardiovascular complications documented:**

- *Myocarditis and pericarditis:* Most common cardiac complications; incidence 15–4,000 cases/100,000 individuals post-COVID vs. 1–10/100,000 pre-COVID. Occurs ~10–15 days after symptom onset; likely driven by combined ACE2-mediated direct injury and hyperimmune response. Approximately 62% of hospitalized COVID-19 patients had acute myocardial injuries.
- *Hypertension:* Present in 15–40% of hospitalized COVID-19 patients; mortality OR 3.05 (95% CI 1.57–5.92) in those with hypertension. RAAS dysregulation and immune dysfunction are implicated.
- *Arrhythmia:* 16.7% of 138 hospitalized COVID-19 patients; higher in ICU (44.4%) vs. non-ICU (6.9%). Mortality significantly higher with arrhythmia (52.4% vs. 24.1%, P=0.005). Mechanisms include metabolic dysfunction, myocardial inflammation, and sympathetic nervous system activation; inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6) are arrhythmogenic.
- *Myocardial injury and heart failure:* 10% of COVID-19 patients develop heart failure (25–35% in hospitalized patients). Elevated hs-cTnI, LDH, CK, and NT-proBNP are common. COVID-19 survivors had approximately 90% additional risk of developing heart failure long-term.
- *Coronary heart disease:* 2.5–15% of COVID-19 patients had CHD; STEMI patients with COVID-19 had higher inpatient mortality and longer stays. DIC found in 71.4% of non-survivors.
- *Ischemic stroke:* 2% pooled prevalence (95% CI 1–2%; P<0.01); driven by hypercoagulability, microthrombosis, and endothelial dysfunction. Recovered COVID-19 patients had 4.40 strokes per 1000 survivors vs. 3.25 per 1000 controls over 9.2-month follow-up.
- *Blood coagulation abnormalities:* DIC very common in non-survivors (71.4%). Deep vein thrombosis in 22.7%, pulmonary embolism widely reported. Elevated D-dimer (>1 µg/L) associated with hospitalization mortality rate. Endothelial injury drives tissue factor and platelet activation, low fibrinolysis, and microvascular thrombosis.
- *Dyslipidemia:* Elevated TG and lower TC, LDL-C, HDL-C in COVID-19 patients vs. controls. Lipoprotein(a) may be responsible for thromboembolic events; PCSK9 inhibitors emerging as potential therapeutic targets.

**Long COVID / post-acute cardiovascular context.** The review notes that approximately 45% of COVID-19 survivors experience persistent symptoms at 4 months; myocarditis and pericarditis may be post-acute cardiac sequelae. TNF-α, IL-1, and IL-6 concentrations were substantially elevated for prolonged periods in long COVID patients. Auto-antibodies (anti-cardiolipin, anti-apolipoprotein A1) in long COVID are linked to ongoing cardiovascular events. However, the review does not systematically analyze post-acute vascular subphenotypes, microclots, or controlled post-acute cohorts — these are noted as requiring further study.

**Therapy summary.** Anticoagulation (LMWH), antiplatelet agents (ticagrelor), immunotherapy (tocilizumab, glucocorticoids, intravenous immunoglobulin), antivirals (remdesivir, nirmatrelvir/ritonavir — the latter reduced long COVID incidence by 26%), and ACEIs/ARBs (generally safe to continue) are discussed. Statins have anti-inflammatory and antithrombotic effects that may reduce COVID-19 composite outcomes.

## Relevance

**Upstream mechanism / background for PAIS:** This review is primarily concerned with cardiovascular damage during the **acute phase** of COVID-19 infection. It is relevant to this project as a mechanistic foundation — the endothelial injury, thromboinflammation, coagulopathy, and immune dysregulation described here are the same processes hypothesized to persist or leave residual damage that underlies post-acute cardiovascular sequelae.

**Direct links to project framework:**

- `topic:thromboinflammation-and-endothelial-dysfunction`: The review provides the most detailed acute-phase mechanistic account of how SARS-CoV-2 drives endothelial inflammation, platelet activation, coagulopathy, microvascular occlusion, and DIC — the upstream cascade that may seed chronic thromboinflammatory states in PAIS.
- `topic:long-covid-immune-dysregulation`: The cytokine storm, autoantibody generation, molecular mimicry, and prolonged cytokine elevation (IL-6, TNF-α) documented here link to persistent immune dysregulation in long COVID.
- `question:0010-vascular-microclot-subphenotype`: The review documents microvascular thrombosis and coagulopathy in acute COVID-19 as context for the post-acute microclot hypothesis. However, it does not directly address fibrin amyloid microclots or post-acute platelet hyperactivation in recovered patients.
- `hypothesis:0001-shared-dysregulated-attractor`: The convergence of direct viral injury and hyperimmune response driving multi-organ cardiovascular damage in acute COVID-19 is consistent with the shared dysregulated attractor hypothesis for PAIS, though this paper does not test that hypothesis.

**Scope note:** The review's primary evidence base concerns **hospitalized COVID-19 patients** during acute infection. Post-acute and long COVID data are mentioned but not systematically reviewed. The mechanisms discussed should be treated as upstream/background for PAIS research, not as direct evidence of post-acute cardiovascular pathophysiology. Extrapolation from acute to chronic post-infectious cardiovascular states requires longitudinal controlled data that this review does not provide.

## Limitations

1. **Narrative review, no formal synthesis protocol.** No PRISMA registration, systematic search strategy with reproducible inclusion/exclusion criteria, or quantitative pooling. Study-level heterogeneity in cohort definitions, severity strata, and follow-up duration is not formally addressed.

2. **Primarily acute-phase focus.** The central evidence base concerns hospitalized COVID-19 patients during the acute illness. Post-acute cardiovascular data are referenced but not the review's main focus; this paper should **not** be cited as evidence for post-acute or long-COVID vascular mechanisms without supplementation by longitudinal PAIS-specific studies.

3. **Case definition and cohort heterogeneity not systematically addressed.** Prevalence estimates across complications vary widely across studies (e.g., myocarditis 1–4,000/100,000; arrhythmia 6.9–44.4%) partly due to differing case definitions, severity selection, and diagnostic criteria — but the review does not formally account for this.

4. **Severity and confounding.** Most mechanistic data come from severe/hospitalized patients; extrapolation to mild/moderate or non-hospitalized COVID-19 (the majority of long-COVID cases) is not addressed.

5. **Causal directionality.** Many associations (e.g., hypertension with worse outcomes, autoantibodies with cardiovascular events) are observational. Pre-existing cardiovascular conditions are strong confounders that the review acknowledges but does not fully disaggregate.

6. **Limited treatment evidence.** Therapeutic section surveys proposed and tested agents but most evidence is from small, observational, or early-pandemic studies; several recommended approaches remain without high-quality RCT support for cardiac outcomes specifically.

7. **Sex, age, and race covariates:** Discussed descriptively but mechanistic heterogeneity across these groups (e.g., ACE2 expression differences by sex, age-related ACE2 downregulation) is noted without formal subgroup analyses.
