## Key Contribution

This paper identifies cyclic GMP-AMP synthase (cGAS) as the cytosolic DNA sensor responsible for detecting double-stranded DNA (dsDNA) in the mammalian cytoplasm and triggering the type I interferon (IFN-I) pathway. The authors demonstrate that cGAS, a member of the nucleotidyltransferase family, synthesizes cyclic GMP-AMP (cGAMP) upon DNA binding, and that cGAMP acts as a second messenger that binds to and activates the adaptor protein STING, leading to downstream TBK1 → IRF3 phosphorylation → IFN-β transcription. This established the cGAS → cGAMP → STING → TBK1 → IRF3 → IFN-I axis as the canonical innate-immune pathway for cytosolic dsDNA sensing.

## Key Findings

- **cGAS is the cytosolic dsDNA sensor.** A previously uncharacterized nucleotidyltransferase-family enzyme synthesizes cGAMP in direct response to cytoplasmic dsDNA; this activity co-purified with the IFN-inducing capacity of the fractions [UNVERIFIED: fractionation step details].
- **cGAMP is the second messenger.** Purified cGAMP is sufficient to activate STING and induce IFN-β without additional protein fractions; this defined cGAMP as a bona fide second messenger in innate signaling [UNVERIFIED: exact concentration ranges tested].
- **Signaling is strictly STING-dependent.** Overexpression of cGAS fails to induce IRF3 activation or IFN-β in STING-null cells, placing STING immediately downstream of cGAMP [UNVERIFIED: specific rescue experiments].
- **cGAS is required for antiviral IFN responses.** Knockdown of cGAS blunts IFN-β induction by both transfected dsDNA and DNA-virus infection, demonstrating physiological relevance beyond synthetic ligands [UNVERIFIED: identity of the DNA virus used].
- **Pathway architecture:** cGAS → cGAMP → STING → TBK1 → IRF3 phosphorylation → IFN-β transcription. This linear cascade is the canonical innate-immune output for cytosolic dsDNA, operating independently of active viral replication — any source of cytoplasmic dsDNA (viral, mitochondrial, micronuclear, or fragmented genomic) can engage it.

## Relevance

**Scope note — this is a general molecular-immunology paper, not a PAIS study.** No post-infectious or chronic-disease data are presented. It is seeded here solely as the primary mechanistic grounding reference for `question:0023` (cGAS-STING as an upstream driver of persistent IFN-I in PAIS) and addresses the `[MISSING_CITATION]` annotation in that question for the claim that *cytosolic dsDNA activates cGAS-STING → IFN-I independent of active viral replication*.

The relevance to PAIS is by general applicability of the mechanism:

- **Sterile / replication-independent IFN-I drive.** Because cGAS recognizes dsDNA structure rather than a pathogen-encoded protein, any source of cytosolic dsDNA — released mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA), micronuclear DNA from viral-induced chromosomal instability, or residual pathogen-derived DNA fragments — can constitutively activate the pathway without ongoing active viral replication. This is the key mechanistic basis for proposals that cGAS-STING sustains IFN-I in PAIS long after viral clearance.
- **Connection to `hypothesis:0003`** (sterile self-sustaining stimulus branch): chronic cGAS-STING engagement by endogenous dsDNA sources provides a candidate mechanism for the "self-sustaining sterile stimulus" arm.
- **Connection to `question:0006`** (JAK-STAT/IFN driver-vs-marker): cGAS-STING is one of the best-supported upstream initiators of the IFN-I signal; establishing whether it is chronically engaged in PAIS patients would move the IFN-I observation from marker to driven output.
- **Pharmacological handle.** The identification of cGAS and cGAMP as discrete enzymatic steps defines druggable nodes (cGAS inhibitors, STING antagonists) that could reduce persistent IFN-I without globally suppressing antiviral immunity.

## Limitations

- **Cell-culture / biochemical study only.** All data are from in vitro systems; no in vivo, animal, or human evidence is presented. Extrapolation to chronic or post-infectious states requires separate experimental support.
- **DNA virus model, not RNA virus.** The antiviral validation used a DNA virus; SARS-CoV-2 is an RNA virus, and its mtDNA-release mechanism engaging cGAS-STING was established in subsequent work (not in this paper).
- **cGAMP structure initially reported as 2′3′-cGAMP, later revised.** Subsequent biochemistry (Wu et al. 2013) established that cGAS produces a non-canonical 2′3′-linkage isomer with higher STING affinity; this paper's initial report of the linkage may reflect the earlier structural assignment [UNVERIFIED: whether the Sun 2013 [@Sun2013] paper characterized the linkage at all].
- **No quantitative dose-response or kinetic data for persistent / low-level engagement.** The paper establishes that cGAS activation occurs; it does not address the threshold of dsDNA needed for sustained tonic signaling, which is the key unknown for PAIS.
- **Not PAIS-specific:** the entire relevance to post-acute infection rests on downstream mechanistic inference, not data from post-infectious cohorts.
