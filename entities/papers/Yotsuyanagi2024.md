# Prevention of post COVID-19 condition by early treatment with ensitrelvir in the phase 3 SCORPIO-SR trial

- **Authors:** Hiroshi Yotsuyanagi, Norio Ohmagari, Yohei Doi, Masaya Yamato, Akimasa Fukushi, Takumi Imamura, Hiroki Sakaguchi, Takuhiro Sonoyama, Takao Sanaki, Genki Ichihashi, Yuko Tsuge, Takeki Uehara, Hiroshi Mukae
- **Year:** 2024
- **Journal:** Antiviral Research
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.antiviral.2024.105958
- **PMID:** 38972603
- **BibTeX key:** Yotsuyanagi2024
- **Source:** Full-text PDF (papers/pdfs/2024_Yotsuyanagi_ensitrelvir-prevention-post-covid-scorpio-sr.pdf), read 2026-06-20 (supersedes earlier read).
- **Note:** Exploratory post hoc analysis of post-COVID-19 condition (PCC) prevention, nested in the double-blind phase-3 SCORPIO-SR RCT. Sponsored by Shionogi.

## Key Contribution

This is an exploratory post hoc analysis of the double-blind phase-3 SCORPIO-SR trial (jRCT2031210350) that assessed whether early oral ensitrelvir — a 3C-like protease (3CLpro) inhibitor — reduces the risk of post COVID-19 condition (PCC/PASC). In patients with mild-to-moderate COVID-19 treated within 120 hours of symptom onset during the Omicron-dominant era, the 125-mg ensitrelvir arm showed nominally lower risk of persistent PCC symptoms at days 85, 169, and 337 compared with placebo under both PCC definitions, though confidence intervals crossed zero at nearly all time points in the overall population. Statistically significant neurological-symptom risk reductions (Fisher's exact test P < 0.05) emerged in prespecified subgroups with high acute-phase symptom burden or BMI ≥ 25 kg/m². This is one of the few randomised controlled data sources for antiviral-based PAIS prevention.

## Methods

**Design:** Double-blind, placebo-controlled, three-arm phase-3 RCT (SCORPIO-SR; jRCT2031210350). The PCC analysis is explicitly described as exploratory and performed in a **post hoc** manner (Methods, Section 2.4), despite an earlier interim report having highlighted the endpoint. The trial was powered for acute-phase outcomes, not for PCC.

**Trial period and setting:** February 10 – July 10, 2022 (Omicron variant BA.1/BA.2 dominant period); 92 institutions in Japan, Vietnam, and South Korea.

**Population:** Adults aged 12 to < 70 years with mild-to-moderate COVID-19, enrolled regardless of vaccination status or risk factors for severe disease, within 120 hours of symptom onset. Mean age 35.6–36.5 years; 53–58% male; >90% vaccinated against SARS-CoV-2; Asian race ~99–100%. Predominantly younger ambulatory patients; fewer than 30% had risk factors for severe disease.

**Interventions:**
- Ensitrelvir 125 mg once daily (375 mg loading dose on day 1) for 5 days
- Ensitrelvir 250 mg once daily (750 mg loading dose on day 1) for 5 days
- Matching placebo for 5 days
- Randomised 1:1:1 within 120 hours of symptom onset

**ITT N:** 603 (125 mg), 595 (250 mg), 600 (placebo). Among these, 379, 345, and 362 agreed to join the extended follow-up (PCC assessment) period. Patients who completed the PCC questionnaire at least once: **341 (125 mg), 317 (250 mg), 333 (placebo)** — the denominator used for the PCC analyses in Table 1 and Figs. 1–5.

**PCC endpoint — two definitions (both used throughout):**

- **Definition 1 (symptom presence, COVID-19-related):** At least one mild, moderate, or severe symptom marked as "COVID-19-related" by the patient on the 14-item acute-phase questionnaire (patients marked a symptom "Yes" or "Not sure" to the question "Symptom: COVID-19 Related?" in the PCC questionnaire).
- **Definition 2 (symptom + functional impact):** At least one mild, moderate, or severe symptom AND general health not returned to the usual pre-COVID level ("Have you returned to your usual (pre-COVID) health?" answered "No").

Definition 2 is the primary framing in the abstract and text. Both definitions assess three symptom pools: any of 14 acute-phase symptoms, any of 4 neurological symptoms (difficulty with concentration and thinking, difficulty in reasoning and solving problems, memory loss, or insomnia), and any of 27 symptoms (14 acute-phase + 13 typical PCC symptoms including palpitations, joint pain, hair loss, skin rash, muscle weakness, dizziness, chest pain, and decreased appetite).

**Assessment timepoints:** Days 85 (week 12), 169 (week 24), and 337 (week 48). Patients remained blinded to treatment until day 337. Complete questionnaires were available for >80% of patients per group at day 337.

**Statistical approach:** Risk reduction (100 × [1 − relative risk], %) with 95% CIs calculated via the Clopper-Pearson method. Statistical significance assessed by Fisher's exact test (unadjusted P < 0.05, two-sided); no multiplicity correction applied across the many comparisons. Relative risk reduction is defined as an unadjusted P value < 0.05. All analyses performed in SAS version 9.4.

**Subgroup analyses (pre-specified):** (1) Patients with median or higher total scores for 14 COVID-19 symptoms at baseline (cutoff = 9, the pooled-population median); (2) patients with baseline BMI ≥ 25 kg/m² (Japanese obesity criterion).

## Key Findings

### PCC Definition 2: any of 14 acute-phase symptoms + general health not returned to usual level (primary framing in abstract)

| Timepoint | 125-mg risk reduction vs placebo (95% CI) | 250-mg risk reduction vs placebo (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|
| Day 85 | 32.7% (−30.6, 66.1) | 10.9% (−67.0, 52.8) |
| Day 169 | 21.5% (−37.3, 55.6) | 9.5% (−56.6, 48.0) |
| Day 337 | 24.6% (−43.7, 60.9) | 30.6% (−36.2, 65.5) |

All CIs include zero in the overall population — no endpoint reached formal statistical significance.

### PCC Definition 2: any of 4 neurological symptoms + general health not returned to usual level (overall population, Fig. 2B)

| Timepoint | 125-mg risk reduction (95% CI) | 250-mg risk reduction (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|
| Day 85 | 41.9% (−29.8, 75.2) | 49.1% (−19.3, 79.9) |
| Day 169 | 34.0% (−22.5, 65.2) | 22.1% (−42.2, 57.9) |
| Day 337 | **63.7% (14.7, 86.1)*** | 40.5% (−26.6, 73.3) |

*P < 0.05 (unadjusted), Fisher's exact test. The day-337 neurological risk reduction for ensitrelvir 125 mg is the only statistically significant finding in the overall population.

### Absolute rates in the PCC questionnaire (Table 2, Definition 2 — reference)

At day 85: COVID-19 symptoms (mild/moderate/severe): 6.3% (125 mg), 7.6% (250 mg), 9.6% (placebo). Physical health poor: 1.7%, 0.9%, 3.5%. Not returned to usual health: 7.5%, 9.8%, 11.8%.

At day 337: COVID-19 symptoms: 6.0%, 6.2%, 6.6%. Physical health poor: 2.5%, 2.1%, 3.6%. Not returned to usual health: 6.0%, 6.5%, 8.2%.

### Subgroup: high baseline symptom burden (total score ≥ 9 for 14 COVID-19 symptoms, Definition 2)

14-symptom endpoint (Fig. 4A):
- Day 85: 125 mg 52.5% (−12.6, 81.3); 250 mg 25.5% (−68.6, 68.5)
- Day 169: 125 mg 17.4% (−66.0, 59.4); 250 mg 10.9% (−83.0, 57.6)
- Day 337: 125 mg 37.3% (−49.2, 74.8); 250 mg 27.7% (−76.3, 72.1)

4-neurological-symptom endpoint (Fig. 4B):
- Day 85: 125 mg **65.5% (−3.1, 90.4)***; 250 mg 38.0% (−67.1, 79.1)
- Day 337: 125 mg **73.1% (14.4, 93.6)***; 250 mg 22.6% (−92.1, 70.4)

*P < 0.05 (unadjusted), Fisher's exact test.

### Subgroup: BMI ≥ 25 kg/m² (Definition 2)

14-symptom endpoint (Fig. 5A):
- Day 85: 125 mg **75.7% (5.5, 95.7)***; 250 mg 57.9% (−46.0, 90.4)
- Day 169: 125 mg **62.0% (−5.2, 88.0)***; 250 mg 27.6% (−75.1, 71.2)
- Day 337: 125 mg 49.3% (−54.0, 84.9); 250 mg 57.6% (−47.0, 90.3)

4-neurological-symptom endpoint (Fig. 5B):
- Day 85: 125 mg **91.9% (43.0, 99.8)***; 250 mg **78.9% (1.2, 97.8)***
- Day 337: 125 mg **90.6% (32.2, 99.8)***; 250 mg 76.5% (−13.8, 97.5)

*P < 0.05 (unadjusted), Fisher's exact test.

The BMI ≥ 25 subgroup produced the strongest signals. Multiple statistically significant (unadjusted) reductions in neurological PCC symptoms were observed in this subgroup with the 125-mg dose.

### Dose-response pattern

Across the overall population, 125 mg consistently showed larger point-estimate risk reductions than 250 mg in the 14-symptom composite at days 85 and 169. By day 337 the two doses converge. The paper attributes the similarity in efficacy between doses to results consistent with findings for acute-phase COVID-19 symptoms and viral load reduction in the SCORPIO-SR acute-treatment results. No pharmacokinetic explanation is offered.

### Individual symptom data (Definition 2, Fig. 3)

Relative risk reductions versus placebo were observed for several individual symptoms under 125-mg treatment. Statistically significant (unadjusted) reductions (P < 0.05) were seen for: shortness of breath (day 85), low energy/tiredness (day 337), difficulty with concentration/thinking (day 337), memory loss (days 85, 169, 337), stuffy nose (day 85), and muscle weakness (day 337). Not all reached significance.

### Safety

Not reported in detail in this PCC-focused analysis paper. Safety data reside in the main SCORPIO-SR trial publication (Yotsuyanagi et al., 2024 — the acute-treatment report).

## Relevance

This trial directly addresses `question:0012` — whether early antiviral treatment of acute COVID-19 reduces PAIS incidence. It provides randomised evidence from a phase-3 trial for a 3CLpro inhibitor (ensitrelvir) on PCC prevention, complementing observational and retrospective data for nirmatrelvir/ritonavir.

Connection to `hypothesis:0001` (antigen/pathogen persistence as upstream driver of PAIS): If early viral replication suppression during the acute phase lowers PAIS risk, this is consistent with a model in which viral antigen burden or viral-driven immune damage during the acute window causally seeds the post-acute syndrome. The trend toward greater effect in patients with high acute symptom scores and BMI ≥ 25 kg/m² further supports the idea that the acute insult magnitude and metabolic context matter.

Comparison with Geng2024 (STOP-PASC): Geng2024 tests nirmatrelvir/ritonavir in patients who already have established PASC (treatment, not prevention). Yotsuyanagi2024 tests ensitrelvir given at the moment of acute infection (prevention). Together they ask: (a) does reducing acute viral burden prevent PAIS, and (b) does late antiviral suppress a viral reservoir sustaining established PAIS? These are distinct mechanistic hypotheses, and a positive on (a) with a null on (b) would favour an "initial hit" model over a persistent-reservoir model.

The neurological-symptom subgroup findings are particularly relevant to dysautonomia and cognitive-symptom hypotheses (brain-fog, memory loss, insomnia), and the BMI-subgroup signals raise questions about metabolic risk moderation of antiviral benefit.

## Limitations

1. **Post hoc, not confirmatory.** The paper explicitly states "All statistical analyses were performed in a post hoc manner" (Section 2.4). The trial was powered for acute-phase outcomes; it is substantially underpowered for PCC. No multiplicity correction was applied across the many comparisons reported. The statistically significant findings should be regarded as hypothesis-generating.

2. **Short treatment window, young vaccinated population.** Mean age ~36; predominantly mild-to-moderate Omicron-era disease; >90% vaccinated. Effect size and generalisability to older, sicker, unvaccinated, or immunocompromised patients (who bear higher PAIS risk) are unknown.

3. **Self-report endpoint only.** PCC defined by self-administered symptom questionnaire with no biomarker, functional, objective, or physician-assessed measurement. Susceptible to recall bias; the authors note that recall of symptom-COVID-19 attribution ~1 year post-infection may be unreliable. The phenomenon of PCC was publicly recognized at time of study, potentially biasing self-assessment.

4. **Non-monotonic dose-response in the overall population.** The 125-mg arm showed larger point-estimate risk reductions than 250 mg for the 14-symptom composite at days 85 and 169. The paper attributes this to consistency with the acute-efficacy findings, but offers no mechanistic explanation. The BMI-subgroup data partially recover a dose-response for neurological symptoms at day 85.

5. **Multi-country, predominantly East Asian cohort.** Japan, Vietnam, South Korea; ~99–100% Asian race; younger, predominantly male cohort may not generalise to populations where PAIS disproportionately affects older women or is enriched in non-Asian ancestry groups.

6. **No pre-specified symptom clusters.** The 14-symptom composite pools heterogeneous PCC manifestations; dimensional analyses that might reveal differential antiviral benefit for fatigue vs cognitive vs autonomic subtypes are not the focus of the report.

7. **Omicron BA.1/BA.2 variant era; >90% vaccinated.** PCC rates in the placebo group (~10% at day 85 under Definition 2) are lower than reported in earlier pandemic waves. Extrapolation to pre-Omicron or future variants is uncertain.

8. **No Kaplan-Meier time-to-resolution curves.** Risk reductions at discrete snapshots (days 85/169/337) may miss dynamic patterns or initial benefit that erodes over time. Indeed, the proportion of patients with PCC symptoms in the placebo group increased from day 85 to day 169, which the authors note is inconsistent with anticipated PCC resolution and may reflect questionnaire recall dynamics.

9. **Conflict of interest.** Shionogi (ensitrelvir manufacturer) sponsored the study, and multiple authors are Shionogi employees or hold stocks. Medical writing support was also funded by Shionogi.

## Model / Tool Availability

None. This is a clinical RCT report; no computational models, tools, or publicly deposited datasets are described. Shionogi states commitment to disclosing trial synopses and sharing data with researchers on reasonable request.

- Compare with Geng2024 (STOP-PASC): prevention (Yotsuyanagi) vs treatment of established PASC (Geng) — together they bound the "acute insult" vs "persistent reservoir" debate.
- Consider updating `question:0012` frontmatter to add `cite:Yotsuyanagi2024` to `source_refs`.
- The BMI ≥ 25 kg/m² neurological-symptom signals (91.9% and 90.6% risk reductions at days 85 and 337, both P < 0.05) are striking even in the context of small subgroup Ns; worth flagging in `hypothesis:0003` (acute severity threshold) if metabolic risk factors are included in that scope.
- The non-monotonic dose-response (125 mg > 250 mg) in the overall population deserves mechanistic follow-up — could reflect CNS penetration differences, PK differences, or noise in an underpowered analysis. The main SCORPIO-SR acute-treatment paper (Yotsuyanagi et al., 2024) may clarify.
- Safety data not available from this analysis; read the main SCORPIO-SR acute-treatment publication for AE data.
