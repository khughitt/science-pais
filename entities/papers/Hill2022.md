## Key Contribution

This large-scale, retrospective case-control study used the National COVID Cohort Collaborative (N3C) EHR data from 31 US health systems — part of the NIH RECOVER program — to identify pre-acute and acute-phase risk factors for PASC/long-COVID (n=8,325 cases matched 1:5 to 41,625 controls). It is one of the first US national-scale EHR studies of PASC risk factors and examines demographics, comorbidities, acute-illness severity, and social determinants of health (SDoH) simultaneously. Middle age (40–69 years), female sex, acute severity (hospitalization, prolonged hospital stay, mechanical ventilation), and comorbidities including rheumatologic disease, chronic lung disease, and depression were independently associated with increased likelihood of PASC diagnosis.

## Methods

**Data source:** N3C data enclave, as of April 14, 2022; data from 59 health systems (4,559,795 potentially eligible patients with COVID-19 diagnosis or positive PCR/antigen test); restricted to 31 systems that documented at least one PASC case via U09.9 or long-COVID clinic between Oct 2021 and Feb 2022 (n=1,490,823 after this restriction; final analytic base n=1,062,661 with index date March 1, 2020 – December 1, 2021).

**Study design:** Retrospective case-control among confirmed COVID-19 patients.

**Case definition (PASC):** ICD-10-CM code U09.9 OR a long-COVID clinic visit flag in the N3C. Earliest COVID-19 positive test or diagnosis was used as the COVID index date. Patients who died within 45 days of index were excluded. Sensitivity analyses used U09.9 only (n=7,512) or long-COVID clinic visits only (n=1,241).

**Control selection — three methods:**
1. *Unrestricted* (Method 1, primary): All non-cases eligible (n=1,054,336); randomly matched 1:5 within health system and COVID index date ±45 days → 8,325 cases vs. 41,625 controls.
2. *Restricted* (Method 2): Excluded individuals with predicted PASC probability ≥0.75 from a computable phenotype (CP) model; 621,374 eligible controls → 8,322 cases vs. 41,610.
3. *More restricted* (Method 3): Included only individuals with predicted PASC probability ≤0.25; 496,073 eligible → 8,322 cases vs. 41,610.

**Risk factors evaluated (all assessed prior to COVID-19 index date):**
- Demographics: age, sex, race/ethnicity
- 17 comorbidities from the Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI), including rheumatologic disease, chronic lung disease, diabetes, depression, peptic ulcer, cardiomyopathy, liver disease, etc.
- Additional CDC-identified COVID-19 severity risk comorbidities (e.g., obesity, corticosteroid use)
- Acute-illness severity: COVID-19-associated hospitalization, length of stay (short 1–7d, long 8–30d, extended 31+d), invasive mechanical ventilation, ECMO, vasopressor use, acute kidney injury, sepsis, remdesivir use
- SDoH (county-level, Sharecare-BU dataset): poverty rate, college education rate, public insurance rate 18–64, physicians per 1,000 residents (all as tertiles)

**Statistical methods:** Multivariable logistic regression, random forest (RF), and XGBoost. Feature importance (RF and XGBoost) and SHAP plots (XGBoost). Three separate regression models for three control cohorts. Secondary analyses added SDoH variables. Stratified analyses by hospitalization status and by sex (supplementary eFigures).

### T078 Narrow Extraction (six focus answers)

**1. EXPOSURE — Pre-existing autoimmune disease as candidate risk factor**

Yes, rheumatologic disease is included as a candidate risk factor, drawn from the 17 Charlson Comorbidity Index comorbidities. No broader "pre-existing autoimmune disease" composite variable is constructed — the term used is "rheumatologic disease," which in the CCI maps to connective tissue disease and rheumatoid arthritis categories.

*Operationalization of "pre-existing":* All comorbidities were identified using EHR data prior to the COVID-19 index date. No explicit lookback window duration is stated in the main text; the eMethods (supplementary) describe the full code sets. [UNVERIFIED — specific ICD code set and lookback window not readable from main-text extraction; supplementary eMethods would need direct inspection]

*Pooled vs. stratified:* Rheumatologic disease is a single pooled category. Organ-specific autoimmune diseases (thyroiditis, type 1 diabetes, inflammatory bowel disease, etc.) are not reported as separate strata. No split into rheumatic/systemic vs. organ-specific autoimmunity.

**2. CONTROL/MATCHING — Comparison design**

PASC-vs-no-PASC among infected individuals (not uninfected controls). All cases and controls had documented SARS-CoV-2 infection. Matching was 1:5 within the same health system and COVID index date within ±45 days of the case's earliest COVID index date.

**3. SEX HANDLING**

Sex was included as a covariate in all multivariable logistic regression models (female OR 1.40, CI 1.33–1.48 in the primary analysis). Additionally, stratified analyses by sex were performed and appear in supplementary eFigures 3 and 4 (published version). The stratified results were described as "similar to main findings." Peak risk age group varied slightly: women peaked at 50–59 years, men at 60–69 years; women older than 70 had decreasing risk while men's risk was stable. No sex × comorbidity interaction terms were tested; sex × rheumatologic disease interaction was not estimated.

**4. ACUTE SEVERITY — Adjustment or stratification**

Acute severity indicators (hospitalization, length of stay, mechanical ventilation, ECMO, vasopressor, acute kidney injury, sepsis) were included as candidate risk factor predictor variables in the same multivariable model alongside comorbidities — they were not adjusted away as nuisance covariates but were evaluated for their independent association with PASC. A stratified analysis by hospitalization status (hospitalized vs. non-hospitalized at COVID index date) was performed as a planned secondary analysis (eTables 8–13 in supplementary), showing different risk-factor profiles for the two subgroups.

**5. HEALTHCARE UTILIZATION — Ascertainment bias adjustment**

County-level physicians per 1,000 residents was included as an SDoH variable and was the only SDoH measure significantly associated with PASC in logistic regression (higher physician density → higher PASC likelihood). The authors explicitly acknowledge this as reflecting ascertainment: "having access to healthcare services increases the likelihood of diagnosis and/or treatment at a long-COVID clinic." In the CP-based control methods, the computable phenotype model restricted eligible controls to individuals with at least one post-COVID healthcare visit (mentioned in Discussion), providing some partial adjustment. Individual-level healthcare utilization intensity was not directly modeled as a confounder adjustment variable.

**6. CASE DEFINITION — PASC computable phenotype**

Primary case definition: ICD-10-CM U09.9 diagnosis code OR a long-COVID clinic visit flag in the N3C database, occurring after the COVID-19 index date. Individuals were required to have COVID-19 index date between March 1, 2020 and December 1, 2021, and to have survived ≥45 days after index. The N3C was restricted to the 31 health systems that actively used U09.9 or had a long-COVID clinic. A separately-developed computable phenotype (CP) ML model for long-COVID (prior publication, reference [23]) was used for control enrichment/restriction — NOT for case identification. Sensitivity analyses: (a) U09.9 only (n=7,512); (b) long-COVID clinic visits only (n=1,241).

## Key Findings

- **8,325 PASC cases** (primary cohort) matched 1:5 to 41,625 controls; all within 31 US health systems using U09.9 or long-COVID clinic.
- **Demographics of cases:** majority >50 years (56.6%), female (62.8%), non-Hispanic White (68.6%).
- **Strongest risk factors for PASC (primary multivariable logistic regression):**
  - Acute severity: COVID-associated hospitalization (OR 3.80, CI 3.05–4.73); extended hospital stay 31+ days (OR 3.38, CI 2.45–4.67); long hospital stay 8–30 days (OR 1.69, CI 1.31–2.17); mechanical ventilation (OR 1.44, CI 1.18–1.74)
  - Age: 40–69 years (OR range 2.32–2.58 vs. reference age group)
  - Female sex: OR 1.40 (CI 1.33–1.48)
- **Top comorbidity risk factors:** tuberculosis (OR 1.65, CI 1.03–2.65); chronic lung disease (OR 1.63, CI 1.53–1.74); rheumatologic disease (OR 1.27, CI 1.11–1.46); peptic ulcer (OR 1.25, CI 1.07–1.46); obesity (OR 1.23, CI 1.16–1.30); depression (OR 1.50, CI 1.40–1.60)
- **Lower PASC risk:** younger age (18–29 years); male sex; non-Hispanic Black, Hispanic, and Asian race/ethnicity; comorbidities including psychosis, cardiomyopathy, metastatic cancer, liver disease, substance abuse, tobacco smoking; COVID-19 primary diagnosis during hospitalization
- **SDoH:** Higher physicians per 1,000 residents in county of residence was associated with increased likelihood of PASC (ascertainment effect acknowledged by authors). Other SDoH measures (poverty, education, insurance) were not significant in logistic regression but appeared as important features in ML models.
- **Stratified by hospitalization:** For hospitalized patients, ECMO, ED visit, and mechanical ventilation were strongest predictors; for non-hospitalized patients, corticosteroid use, depression, peptic ulcer, and coronary artery disease were relatively more important.
- **Sex stratification (supplementary):** Similar results to primary analysis; peak age risk is 50–59 years for women, 60–69 for men; risk declined in women >70 but was stable in men >70.
- **ML model performance:** XGBoost AUC 0.73, logistic regression AUC 0.73, random forest AUC 0.69. Risk factor directions were consistent between ML and LR, though magnitude ordering differed.

## Relevance

This paper is directly relevant to **task:t078** (autoimmune diathesis as sex-conditioned effect-modifier for post-infectious failed recovery). Key relevance points:

- **Rheumatologic disease is a confirmed PASC risk factor** in the largest US EHR study available (OR 1.27, robust across control methods), validating the hypothesis that pre-existing autoimmune/inflammatory states elevate PASC risk.
- **Critical gap for t078**: No sex × rheumatologic disease interaction was estimated, sex stratification in the supplementary shows "similar results," meaning the rheumatologic disease estimate is not sex-stratified. This study cannot answer whether the autoimmune-diathesis risk is sex-conditioned.
- **Female sex is a significant covariate** (OR 1.40), consistent with hypothesis:0005, but the mechanism of the female excess is not addressed.
- **Healthcare utilization (physicians per capita) is acknowledged as an ascertainment proxy** but is not individually adjusted — directly relevant to hypothesis:0008 (measurement-channel ascertainment bias). The PASC outcome is capture-dependent on whether the patient received a U09.9 code or long-COVID clinic visit.
- **Acute severity as strongest predictor** (OR 3.8 for hospitalization) is consistent with hypothesis:0004 (acute-severity threshold for self-sustaining PAIS).

## Limitations

1. **Ascertainment-dependent outcome**: PASC cases are identified only through U09.9 coding or long-COVID clinic visit — patients who had PASC symptoms but never received a formal code or clinic referral are missed. The positive association with physician density explicitly reflects this.
2. **Rheumatologic disease is one pooled CCI category**: No separation into specific autoimmune subtypes (RA, SLE, Sjogren's, IBD, MS, etc.), so effect estimates cannot distinguish between conditions with different immune mechanisms.
3. **No sex × comorbidity interaction terms**: The analysis cannot determine whether rheumatologic disease (or any other comorbidity) modifies risk differently in females vs. males.
4. **No lookback window specified in main text**: The main text does not state the duration of EHR history used to classify pre-existing conditions — confirmed against the full PMC text (PMC10601201), which gives only the index-date eligibility window (March 2020 – December 2021) and the ±45-day case–control index-date matching window. The comorbidity-classification lookback is specified only in the supplementary eMethods (not extracted).
5. **Acute severity as predictor, not nuisance confounder**: Comorbidity effects and severity effects are in the same model; rheumatologic disease OR of 1.27 is adjusted for acute severity, but the directionality of that adjustment is not decomposed.
6. **No vaccination status or therapeutic history**: Paxlovid, remdesivir (other than as acute severity indicator), and vaccine status not evaluated.
7. **No reinfection status**: COVID-19 reinfection not tracked.
8. **Adults only (>18 years)**: Pediatric population excluded.
9. **County-level SDoH proxies**: Individual-level healthcare utilization, insurance, and access were not available.
10. **Pre-Omicron era cohort**: COVID index dates limited to March 2020 – December 2021; Omicron and subsequent variant risk profiles may differ.
11. **Potential circularity in control enrichment**: The CP model was trained on post-COVID features, and some comorbidities may carry forward into the PASC phase, introducing circularity (acknowledged by authors).
