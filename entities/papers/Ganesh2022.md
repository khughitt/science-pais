# The Female-Predominant Persistent Immune Dysregulation of the Post-COVID Syndrome

- **Authors:** Ravindra Ganesh, Stephanie L. Grach, Aditya K. Ghosh, Dennis M. Bierle, Bradley R. Salonen, Nerissa M. Collins, Avni Y. Joshi, Neal D. Boeder, Christopher V. Anstine, Michael R. Mueller, Elizabeth C. Wight, Ivana T. Croghan, Andrew D. Badley, Rickey E. Carter, Ryan T. Hurt
- **Year:** 2022
- **Journal:** Mayo Clinic Proceedings, vol. 97, no. 3, pp. 454–464
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.mayocp.2021.11.033
- **BibTeX key:** Ganesh2022
- **Source:** PDF

## Key Contribution

This is the inaugural clinical cohort report from the Mayo Clinic Post-COVID-19 Care Clinic (PCOCC), characterizing 108 PASC patients by symptom phenotype, inflammatory biomarker profile, and sex. The central finding is a female-predominant (75%; 81/108) syndrome with persistent IL-6 elevation as the dominant detectable inflammatory signal — present in 61% of all patients (69% of women vs 39% of men; P=.046) — far exceeding elevation rates for CRP (17%) and ESR (20%). The paper introduces a "post-COVID syndrome" (PoCoS) concept framed as central sensitization (CS) driven by immune dysregulation, linking PASC to fibromyalgia, ME/CFS, and POTS under a common neuroinflammatory mechanism. A novel "3-hit" model is proposed: genetic susceptibility + psychologic susceptibility + sensitizing event (infection or other systemic stressor).

## Methods

**Study design:** Retrospective chart review of the first 108 consecutive patients seen in the PCOCC between January 19, 2021, and April 29, 2021, approved by the Mayo Clinic COVID-19 Research Taskforce and IRB. Data abstracted from electronic medical records (EMR) into a prospectively maintained REDCap database.

**Case definition:** All patients met the NIH definition of post-acute sequelae of SARS-CoV-2 infection (PASC): at least one symptom developing after COVID-19 and persisting after expected resolution of acute disease. Patients evaluated a median of 148.5 days (IQR 111.5–179.3) after symptom onset.

**Phenotyping:** Patient questionnaires (52 questions covering initial infection, ongoing symptoms, and treatments) reviewed by general internal medicine physicians; assigned to one of six phenotypes — fatigue predominant, dyspnea predominant, myalgia predominant, orthostasis predominant, chest pain predominant, headache predominant. Single-organ-involvement patients were triaged to subspecialty care; multi-system patients evaluated by the general internal medicine and preventive medicine teams.

**Laboratory panel:** Standard battery ordered for all PCOCC patients: CBC, comprehensive metabolic panel, ESR, CRP, ferritin, D-dimer, IL-6, ANA (with reflex), 6-minute walk test, PFTs and chest imaging if dyspnea present. IL-6 lower limit of detection = 3.0 pg/mL (values below threshold entered as 3.0).

**Statistical tests:** Chi-square for categorical variables; ANOVA for continuous variables between phenotypes. P<.05 threshold.

## Key Findings

**Demographics**
- N=108; 75% female (81/108); median age 46 years (IQR 37–55).
- 94% White/Caucasian; 98% non-Hispanic — a recognized generalizability limitation.
- 16% (17/108) admitted for acute COVID-19; majority had outpatient acute illness.
- Most common comorbidities: obesity 39%, anxiety 33%, depression 28%, GI disease 25%.
- Most common symptoms at PCOCC evaluation (chronic): fatigue 89%, shortness of breath 69%, brain fog 69%, anxiety 62%, unrefreshing sleep 58%.

**Phenotype distribution (Table 2)**
| Phenotype | n | Women |
|---|---|---|
| Fatigue predominant | 69 | 58 (84%) |
| Dyspnea predominant | 23 | 11 (48%) |
| Myalgia predominant | 6 | 3 (50%) |
| Orthostasis predominant | 6 | 6 (100%) |
| Chest pain predominant | 3 | 3 (100%) |
| Headache predominant | 1 | 0 (0%) |
Sex distribution across phenotypes was statistically significant (P=.0004).

**Inflammatory biomarkers (Tables 2 & 3)**
- IL-6 elevated in 61% of patients (41/67 with result); CRP elevated 17%; ESR elevated 20%.
- IL-6 elevation in women: 69% vs men: 39% (P=.046).
- IL-6 was highest in myalgia-predominant phenotype (mean 4.9 pg/mL, SD 3.8) and fatigue-predominant (mean 2.5 pg/mL, SD 1.1); lowest in chest pain (mean 1.3 pg/mL, SD 0.3).
- 57% of all patients (62/108) had elevated IL-6 at ≥3 months post-acute infection — a novel finding not previously described in PASC at this chronicity.

**Central sensitization (CS) grouping**
- Fatigue, myalgia, and orthostasis phenotypes collectively categorized as CS phenotypes (n=82).
- CS phenotype had significantly higher IL-6 (mean 2.8 vs 1.6 pg/mL; P=.01) and numerically higher CRP and ESR vs non-CS phenotype (dyspnea + chest pain, n=26), though CRP/ESR differences did not reach significance.
- CS phenotype was 82% women vs 54% in non-CS phenotype (P<.0001).
- Age was not significantly different between CS (mean 45.7 years, SD 13.6) and non-CS (mean 48.7 years, SD 14.8) patients.

**3-hit model for PoCoS (Figure 2)**
The authors propose CS as the shared mechanism for PoCoS, requiring three co-occurring vulnerabilities:
1. **Genetic susceptibility** — candidate genes: SLC6A4, COMT, HTR2A, MYT1L, NRXN3, TAAR1, RGS4, CNR1, GRIA4 (implicated in fibromyalgia and related CS syndromes).
2. **Psychologic susceptibility** — pre-existing personality type, anxiety, depression, OCD, bipolar disorder.
3. **Sensitizing event** — infectious disease, physical trauma, emotional trauma, major medical/surgical event; COVID-19 is one trigger among several.

**Sex-linked mechanism hypothesis**
Post-puberty/pre-menopausal women have higher levels of IL-6, TNF, and IL-1β than adult men; these differences are mediated by estradiol, progesterone, and androgens, which shift as age advances (men's inflammatory cytokine levels rise after middle age). The study hypothesizes this sex-cytokine difference partially explains the female predominance in PASC. Reference cohort data: a study of hospitalized COVID-19-positive patients without immunomodulatory treatment found men had higher innate inflammatory response (IL-8, IL-18) but when women showed elevated innate response, it positively correlated with disease progression.

## Relevance

This paper directly addresses the project research question (research-question:post-acute-infection-syndromes) along three axes:

1. **Failed homeostatic recovery with a specific biomarker signature:** The central finding — persistent IL-6 elevation at a median of ~5 months post-infection in 61% of patients — instantiates the PAIS frame as a measurable failure of immune resolution. The discordance between high IL-6 and normal CRP/ESR demonstrates that standard inflammatory panels miss the dominant signal, which has implications for how this project defines and detects homeostatic failure computationally.

2. **Sex as a PAIS risk axis and mechanistic clue:** The 3:1 female-to-male ratio, strongly linked to IL-6 elevation and the CS phenotype cluster, supports the hypothesis that sex-modulated immune tone (particularly estrogen-driven IL-6 priming) is a biological determinant of PAIS susceptibility — not merely a healthcare-seeking artifact. This connects to the broader immunity project's findings on sex-stratified immune phenotypes.

3. **Central sensitization as a convergent neuroinflammatory mechanism:** By grouping fatigue, myalgia, and orthostasis phenotypes under the CS umbrella and linking them to ME/CFS, fibromyalgia, and POTS, the paper operationalizes the "shared post-infectious failure mode" concept: diverse clinical presentations converge on a common neuroinflammatory attractor state maintained by persistent cytokine signaling (IL-6). This maps directly to the project's "shared dysregulated attractor" working frame from Choutka2022.

4. **PoCoS as a clinical entity:** The paper provides a phenotype taxonomy and a proposed 3-hit model that can anchor future hypothesis testing in this project — specifically hypotheses about genetic susceptibility, pre-existing immune tone, and the nature of the sensitizing event.

## Limitations

- **Referral/selection bias:** Patients were self-referred or provider-referred to a specialized clinic; those with single-organ-system involvement were triaged to subspecialties, enriching the multi-symptom CS profile in this dataset. This means the cohort over-represents CS-like presentations and under-represents isolated anosmia, pulmonary fibrosis, etc.
- **Demographic homogeneity:** 94% White/Caucasian, 98% non-Hispanic — reflects Mayo Clinic's catchment and local demographics; results may not generalize to non-White populations. No patients of Hispanic descent were included.
- **No control group:** No healthy controls or acute-COVID-recovered controls were enrolled; it is not possible to determine what proportion of IL-6 elevation reflects baseline pre-COVID immune tone vs. a PASC-specific elevation.
- **Inconsistent laboratory panel completion:** Standard panel was not ordered in all patients (reflecting referring provider or patient preference), reducing sample sizes for biomarker analyses (IL-6 available in 67/108 patients).
- **Cross-sectional phenotyping:** Phenotypes were assigned at a single visit; longitudinal tracking of symptom evolution and cytokine trajectories was not performed. The 3-hit model and CS framework remain mechanistic proposals, not tested hypotheses in this dataset.
- **Small subgroups:** Myalgia (n=6), orthostasis (n=6), chest pain (n=3), headache (n=1) phenotypes are too small for reliable statistical inference.
- **Absence of imaging or functional data in most patients:** Brain MRI, PET, or detailed autonomic testing were not systematically performed; neuroinflammatory and dysautonomia hypotheses are inferential.
- **Acute COVID-19 severity undercharacterized:** Only 16% were hospitalized; severity spectrum of the acute illness is not fully described, limiting evaluation of severity-PASC relationships.

## Model / Tool Availability

Not applicable. This is a clinical cohort/observational study. No computational model or software tool is released. The PCOCC care model (virtual intake → condensed in-person evaluation → 6-month health coaching follow-up) is described operationally and may serve as a template for PAIS clinic design.

## Follow-up

- **Longitudinal follow-up of this cohort:** The paper reports only the first 108 patients at a single timepoint; subsequent Mayo Clinic PCOCC publications (Vanichelachorn et al., Mayo Clin Proc 2021;96(7):1782–1791 already cited in references) may provide trajectory data.
- **Cytokine mechanism papers:** IL-6 in CS syndromes (Rohleder 2012), sex differences in immune response to COVID-19 (Klein & Flanagan 2020), and tocilizumab trial results (Salama 2021; Stone 2020) are worth reading to evaluate IL-6 as a therapeutic target.
- **Controlled biomarker studies:** The absence of a control group here is the key gap; studies comparing IL-6 trajectories in recovering vs. non-recovering COVID-19 patients (e.g., Davis et al. 2021) would contextualize these findings.
- **Genetics of CS syndromes:** The 3-hit model names specific candidate genes (SLC6A4, COMT, HTR2A, GRIA4, etc.); GWAS and epigenetics papers on fibromyalgia cited here (Arnold 2013, D'Agneli 2019) are worth reading to assess whether these loci are implicated in PASC GWAS data.
- **Link to dysautonomia literature:** The 100% female orthostasis phenotype and overlap with POTS warrants reading the POTS post-COVID case series (Raj et al. 2021) cited in the references.
