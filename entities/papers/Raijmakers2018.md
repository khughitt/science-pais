# Interferon-γ and CXCL10 responses related to complaints in patients with Q fever fatigue syndrome

- **Authors:** Ruud P. H. Raijmakers, Anne F. M. Jansen, Stephan P. Keijmel, Teske Schoffelen, Anja Scholzen, Jos W. M. van der Meer, Leo A. B. Joosten, Mihai G. Netea, Marcel van Deuren, Chantal P. Bleeker-Rovers
- **Year:** 2018
- **Journal:** European Journal of Clinical Microbiology & Infectious Diseases, 37(7):1385–1391
- **DOI:** 10.1007/s10096-018-3265-z
- **PMID:** 29804281 | **PMCID:** PMC6015096
- **BibTeX key:** Raijmakers2018
- **Source:** Europe PMC full-text XML (open access)

## Key Contribution

This study demonstrates that antigen-specific IFN-γ and CXCL10 production — measured by ex vivo whole-blood stimulation with heat-killed _Coxiella burnetii_ — is significantly higher in Q fever fatigue syndrome (QFS) patients with persisting complaints compared to those who recovered. This is notable because it links a cell-mediated immune readout directly to current symptom burden, not merely to serostatus or prior infection, providing a potential objective biomarker for QFS complaint severity and a window into the immunological substrate that distinguishes ongoing disease from resolution.

## Methods

**Design:** Cross-sectional comparison with three groups, nested within and following up the Qure randomized controlled trial (Keijmel et al., Clin Infect Dis 2017).

**Population:**
- QFS patients who had recovered from complaints (n = 8; CIS Fatigue Severity subscale < 35)
- QFS patients with persisting complaints (n = 27; CIS Fatigue Severity ≥ 35)
- Asymptomatic Q fever seropositive controls (n = 10; IgG phase I or II titer ≥ 1:64 but no chronic Q fever diagnosis)

All QFS patients were diagnosed at the Radboud Expertise Center for Q fever, Nijmegen, Netherlands, per the Dutch QFS guideline (criterion: fatigue ≥ 6 months, CIS Fatigue Severity ≥ 35, exclusion of chronic Q fever and other causes, functional impairment SIP ≥ 450). Patients came from the CBT and placebo arms of the Qure study; blood was drawn one year after Qure completion.

**Case definition:** Dutch consensus criteria for QFS. Symptom status at blood draw defined by CIS Fatigue Severity cut-off of 35.

**Assay:** Commercially available Q-detect whole-blood IFN-γ release assay (Innatoss, Netherlands). Whole blood (180 µL) mixed with 20 µL heat-killed _C. burnetii_ (strain 2009-02629, isolated from a goat placenta during the Dutch outbreak) in a 96-well plate; incubated 22–24 h at 37°C. Negative control: RPMI; positive control: PHA.

**Analytes measured:**
- IFN-γ: ELISA (Pelikine compact, Sanquin)
- IL-2, CXCL9, CXCL10, CXCL11: multiplex bead assay (Bio-Rad)

CXCL9/10/11 data were not collected for asymptomatic controls due to an inter-assay coefficient of variability >15% in that run, which was excluded.

**Statistics:** Mann-Whitney U test and Kruskal-Wallis test (non-parametric); Spearman's rank correlation for patient characteristics vs. cytokine/chemokine levels. Significance threshold: p < 0.05.

**Covariates:** Age (mean ~50–53 years across groups, no significant difference), sex (≈60% female, no significant difference), treatment allocation (≈50% placebo, ≈50% CBT across both QFS groups).

## Key Findings

**IFN-γ (primary result):**
After excluding one outlier (QFS-recovered patient with IFN-γ = 533 pg/mL that was confirmed on retest but clinically unremarkable), antigen-specific IFN-γ production was significantly higher in QFS patients with persisting complaints (median 288 pg/mL) than in QFS patients who recovered (median 93 pg/mL; p = 0.041). The full three-group comparison (including asymptomatic controls, median 267 pg/mL) did not reach significance, meaning the effect is specific to recovered vs. persistent within QFS rather than a simple QFS-vs-control contrast.

**CXCL10:**
After the same outlier exclusion, CXCL10 was significantly higher in QFS patients with persisting complaints (median 176 pg/mL) than in recovered QFS patients (median 86 pg/mL; p = 0.045).

**Non-significant analytes:**
IL-2 (persistent: 147 pg/mL; recovered: 114 pg/mL), IFN-γ/IL-2 ratio, CXCL9 (persistent: 803 pg/mL; recovered: 436 pg/mL), and CXCL11 (persistent: 18 pg/mL; recovered: 19 pg/mL) did not significantly differ between persistent-complaint and recovered QFS groups.

**Symptom severity correlation:** No significant correlation was found between any of the five analytes (IFN-γ, IL-2, CXCL9, CXCL10, CXCL11) and CIS Fatigue Severity score or symptom duration within the persistent-complaint group. Thus the biomarker distinguishes the recovered vs. persistent dichotomy but does not grade symptom severity within the symptomatic stratum.

**Directional summary:** Higher IFN-γ and CXCL10 responses to _C. burnetii_ antigen track with ongoing QFS complaints. Recovery is associated with lower antigen-specific Th1/IFN-pathway reactivity. The authors note they cannot determine from this cross-sectional design whether immune modulation drives recovery or complaint persistence drives continued immune activation — the directionality question remains open.

## Relevance

This paper is directly relevant to the PAIS framework on two axes:

1. **Shared molecular signature across triggers (question:0001).** IFN-γ and CXCL10 (a downstream IFN-γ-inducible chemokine) are among the cytokines most consistently elevated in long COVID and ME/CFS immune profiling. Raijmakers2018 provides one of the few instances in which these same analytes are linked to symptom burden in a non-COVID post-infectious fatigue syndrome (QFS), strengthening the case that an antigen-specific Th1/IFN-γ axis is a cross-pathogen PAIS signature candidate.

2. **Immune persistence and antigen-specific T-cell reactivity.** The use of antigen-specific stimulation (not just resting cytokines) is mechanistically informative: it shows that QFS-persistent patients have a more reactive _C. burnetii_-specific T-cell memory compartment at ~7–9 years post-infection (median symptom duration 92 months in persistent group), consistent with either ongoing antigen stimulation or a dysregulated memory T-cell set-point. This aligns with the antigen/pathogen-persistence mechanism hypothesis in this project.

3. **Biomarker potential.** The paper argues IFN-γ and CXCL10 may help differentiate QFS etiology (immunologic vs. psychogenic perpetuation), pointing toward use in stratifying patients for mechanistically targeted interventions — directly relevant to the project's interest in immune stratification for precision therapy.

Closely related project papers: Raijmakers2019, Raijmakers2021, Morroy2016 (from same Dutch outbreak cohort), Galbraith2011 (post-Q-fever fatigue, pre-Dutch-outbreak context).

## Limitations

1. **Small recovered group (n = 8).** The key comparison has very low power; the one outlier exclusion is necessary for significance, raising concerns about robustness. The authors acknowledge inability to recruit more recovered patients.

2. **Cross-sectional design.** Cannot establish whether elevated IFN-γ/CXCL10 drives complaint persistence or is a consequence of ongoing suffering; directionality is genuinely ambiguous.

3. **No symptom-severity gradient within the persisting group.** IFN-γ and CXCL10 did not correlate with CIS Fatigue Severity scores within the persistent group, limiting use as a continuous severity biomarker.

4. **Missing asymptomatic control data for CXCL9/10/11.** An inter-assay CV >15% invalidated the seropositive control run; there is no three-way comparison for chemokines, only a two-group QFS comparison.

5. **Outlier dependency.** The IFN-γ and CXCL10 findings require exclusion of a single outlier in the recovered group. The outlier's IFN-γ of 533 pg/mL retested reproducibly and has no clinical explanation; its inclusion nullifies both p-values.

6. **Cohort overlap with prior treatment.** Patients came from Qure CBT and placebo arms (not doxycycline arm); ~50%/50% split was balanced across recovered vs. persistent, but prior CBT could theoretically influence immune state.

7. **Single assay platform, single antigen.** Commercial Q-detect assay with one proprietary _C. burnetii_ strain; generalizability to other antigen preparations or platforms is uncertain.

8. **Symptom duration confound.** Persistent patients had a median symptom duration of 92 months vs. 29.5 months for recovered patients; it is unclear whether longer symptom duration per se, rather than immune state, modulates the measured responses.

## Model / Tool Availability

No computational model or reusable tool is described. The Q-detect assay (Innatoss, Netherlands) is a commercial product and not freely available for academic reuse without procurement. No data deposition is mentioned.

## Follow-up

**Papers to read:**
- Keijmel et al. 2016 (J Infect) — the predecessor IFN-γ study showing elevated IFN-γ in QFS vs. seropositive controls (cited as CR8); would establish the baseline IFN-γ finding that Raijmakers2018 extends.
- Jansen et al. 2017 (BMC Infect Dis) — CXCL9 as biomarker for chronic Q fever (cited as CR9); distinguishes QFS chemokine findings from chronic Q fever chemokine findings.
- Schoffelen et al. 2013 (Clin Infect Dis) — IFN-γ/IL-2 ratio for discriminating chronic Q fever from QFS (cited as CR13); context for the IFN-γ/IL-2 ratio approach.

**Questions raised for the project:**
- Do IFN-γ and CXCL10 elevation after antigen-specific stimulation generalize across PAIS triggers (e.g., EBV-specific stimulation in ME/CFS, SARS-CoV-2-specific stimulation in long COVID)? If so, this would be a strong candidate shared molecular mechanism.
- Does IFN-γ/CXCL10 elevation at the post-infectious chronic phase reflect antigen persistence, a dysregulated T-cell memory set-point, or both? A longitudinal design starting at acute infection is needed to dissect these alternatives.
- What is the relationship between CXCL10 elevation and endothelial/microvascular dysfunction? CXCL10 is produced by endothelial cells in response to IFN-γ; the same axis may connect to thromboinflammation observed in long COVID (see project topic on endothelial dysfunction).
