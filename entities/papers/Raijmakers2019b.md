# Cytokine profiles in patients with Q fever fatigue syndrome

- **Authors:** Ruud P.H. Raijmakers, Valerie A.C.M. Koeken, Anne F.M. Jansen, Stephan P. Keijmel, Megan E. Roerink, Leo A.B. Joosten, Mihai G. Netea, Jos W.M. van der Meer, Chantal P. Bleeker-Rovers
- **Year:** 2019
- **Journal:** Journal of Infection, vol. 78(5):349-357
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.jinf.2019.01.006
- **PMID:** 30684502
- **BibTeX key:** Raijmakers2019b
- **Source:** Abstract-level only — paper is paywalled with no open-access copy (Unpaywall confirmed); metadata retrieved via Europe PMC and PubMed MEDLINE. Full-text figures, statistical tables, and supplementary details are not available from the accessible sources.

## Key Contribution

This paper provides a dedicated cytokine-omics characterisation of Q fever fatigue syndrome (QFS), comparing immune profiles in QFS patients against chronic fatigue syndrome (CFS) patients, asymptomatic Q fever seropositive controls, and healthy controls. The headline finding is that PBMCs from QFS patients produce markedly elevated IL-6, TNFα, and IL-1β upon ex-vivo stimulation with Coxiella burnetii antigen, and that circulating (unstimulated) IL-6 and IFN-γ are also elevated in QFS relative to controls. This positions QFS as a syndrome with both a hyperresponsive innate inflammatory capacity (stimulation-dependent) and a low-grade chronic inflammatory tone (circulating), distinguishing it from healthy and asymptomatic-seropositive states and placing it alongside CFS in an inflammatory landscape.

## Methods

**Design:** Cross-sectional, case-control study; four comparison groups. Full methods were not retrieved.

**Groups (from MEDLINE record and Europe PMC):**
- QFS patients (n = 20): diagnosed per Dutch national QFS guideline (fatigue ≥ 6 months post-confirmed acute Q fever, functional impairment, Coxiella seropositivity); exact CIS/SIP thresholds are not available from accessible sources.
- CFS patients (n = 19): case definition criteria are not available from accessible sources, likely Fukuda 1994 per other Radboud/Nijmegen studies from this group.
- Asymptomatic Q fever seropositive controls (n = 19): seropositive individuals without fatigue
- Healthy matched controls (n = 25)

**Cytokine measurement — two complementary approaches:**
1. **Ex-vivo stimulation:** PBMCs stimulated with Coxiella burnetii antigen (and likely standard stimuli — LPS, PHA, or similar); cytokine production in supernatant measured. Key analytes: IL-6, TNFα, IL-1β.
2. **Circulating (unstimulated plasma) cytokines:** Measured with Proximity Extension Assay (PEA / Olink-type platform or similar; keyword "Proximity Extension Assay" in MEDLINE). Key analytes: IL-6, IFN-γ. Full panel composition and the specific platform name are not available from accessible sources.

**Statistics:** Specific tests, correction method, and full p-value tables were not retrieved.

## Key Findings

The following findings are derived from the Europe PMC and PubMed MEDLINE abstract; precise effect sizes, confidence intervals, and full cytokine panels are not available from accessible sources.

**Ex-vivo Coxiella antigen stimulation — elevated pro-inflammatory cytokines in QFS:**
- IL-6 production by PBMCs was significantly elevated in QFS patients vs controls (reported P = 0.0001)
- TNFα production was significantly elevated in QFS patients vs controls (reported P = 0.0002)
- IL-1β production was elevated in QFS patients vs controls; the exact P value is not available from accessible sources.
- These three cytokines are all monocyte-derived and reflect a hyperresponsive innate immune state upon re-encounter with Coxiella antigen

**Circulating (unstimulated) cytokines — low-grade systemic inflammation in QFS:**
- Circulating IL-6 was elevated in QFS patients vs controls; exact comparison groups and P values are not available from accessible sources.
- Circulating IFN-γ was elevated in QFS patients vs controls; exact comparison groups and P values are not available from accessible sources.
- The concurrent elevation of both IL-6 (a monocyte/macrophage-derived inflammogen and acute-phase driver) and IFN-γ (a T-cell/NK-cell effector cytokine) in unstimulated plasma supports ongoing, low-grade immune activation rather than a purely stimulus-dependent response

**Comparison to CFS:**
- Both QFS and CFS patients showed evidence of altered cytokine profiles relative to healthy controls; specific direction and magnitude of CFS differences are not available from accessible sources.
- Whether the QFS stimulation-driven elevation was also seen in CFS upon Coxiella antigen stimulation, or was QFS-specific (plausible, given the antigen specificity), is not available from accessible sources.

**Asymptomatic seropositive controls:**
- The profile of this group relative to QFS and healthy controls is not available from accessible sources.

## Relevance

This paper is a core empirical anchor for `question:0001-shared-molecular-signature-across-triggers` and `discussion:0002-cross-pathogen-pais-signature-convergence`. It is the dedicated immunological characterisation of QFS from the Nijmegen/Radboud group — the only team that has produced a systematic molecular series on QFS — and its cytokine findings provide the immunological half of the picture that the mitochondrial-peptide paper (Raijmakers2019) and the neuroinflammation paper (Raijmakers2021) complement.

**Mechanistic implications:**
- Elevated IL-6, TNFα, and IL-1β upon Coxiella antigen stimulation is consistent with the antigen-persistence / immune-reactivation hypothesis (`hypothesis:0002-tissue-reservoir-antigen-fragment`): if residual Coxiella antigen drives episodic innate immune activation, this would produce exactly the ex-vivo pattern observed.
- Elevated circulating IL-6 and IFN-γ at rest points to ongoing immune activity, consistent with the persistent immune activation arm of `hypothesis:0001-shared-dysregulated-attractor`. IL-6 is pleiotropic — it drives acute-phase proteins, hypothalamic fever and fatigue signalling, and activates the HPA axis — so chronic IL-6 elevation would provide a plausible neuro-hormonal mediator of symptom persistence.
- IFN-γ elevation at rest in QFS is compatible with ongoing T-cell or NK-cell activation, potentially indicative of chronic type-1 immune polarisation. IFN-γ is also a known driver of indoleamine-2,3-dioxygenase (IDO) and tryptophan-kynurenine pathway dysregulation, which has been proposed as a sickness-behaviour / fatigue driver in other post-infectious contexts.
- The Proximity Extension Assay (circulating panel) suggests a broader multiplex approach beyond three analytes; the full panel data, if accessible, might reveal additional candidates (e.g., IL-10, IL-17, IL-12, CXCL10/IP-10) that are informative for cross-PAIS comparison.

**QFS as a model PAIS:** Unlike ME/CFS (where the triggering pathogen is often unknown), QFS has a defined bacterial trigger (Coxiella burnetii), confirmed serology, and a well-characterised outbreak cohort from the 2007–2011 Dutch epidemic. The ex-vivo stimulation with Coxiella antigen specifically is particularly informative: if QFS PBMCs over-respond to the original pathogen's antigen years later, this strongly supports either antigen persistence or a durably altered innate immune setpoint following acute infection.

## Limitations

1. **Full text inaccessible.** The specific cytokine panel composition, detailed statistical results (fold-changes, confidence intervals, per-comparison p-values), and figures cannot be verified. All finding summaries derive from the MEDLINE abstract and secondary metadata only.

2. **Small sample size.** Groups of 19–25 subjects are underpowered for multiplexed cytokine panels — false-discovery risk is substantial without stringent correction. Whether FDR correction was applied is not available from accessible sources.

3. **Cross-sectional design.** No pre-infection baseline; cannot establish whether cytokine elevation predates, follows, or co-occurs with symptom onset. Cannot rule out a pre-existing inflammatory tendency.

4. **Case definition for CFS.** The CFS comparator group's case definition is not available from accessible sources, but other papers from this Radboud group use Fukuda 1994 criteria, which are broad and admit patients without post-exertional malaise. If so, the CFS group may be heterogeneous.

5. **Coxiella antigen specificity.** Elevated PBMC response to Coxiella antigen specifically in QFS could reflect antigen-driven memory immune recall (plausible if antigen persists), or could simply reflect a retained adaptive immune memory to the primary infection in seropositive individuals generally. Whether the stimulation response is also elevated to non-Coxiella stimuli (LPS, PHA) would disambiguate persistent activation from antigen-specific memory, but this comparison is not available from accessible sources.

6. **Circulating cytokine panel composition unknown.** Which cytokines beyond IL-6 and IFN-γ were assayed, and whether others were significantly altered, is not available from accessible sources. Selective reporting of positives in the abstract is possible.

7. **All Radboud/Nijmegen QFS cohort.** Findings may not generalise to QFS from other outbreaks or geographies, or to Q fever without an epidemic exposure context.

8. **No longitudinal or treatment-response dimension.** Does cytokine normalisation accompany symptom improvement? This question, relevant to mechanism vs. biomarker status of the cytokine elevations, is not addressed.

## Follow-up

- **Obtain full text** to recover the complete cytokine panel, precise p-values, and any figures showing group distributions. This is the top priority; numbers from the abstract are incomplete.
- **Cross-reference with Raijmakers2019 (mitochondrial peptides):** Do the QFS patients showing elevated IL-6 and TNFα production also show the MT-RNR1/MT-RNR2 downregulation and reduced humanin production? The two papers share an overlapping Radboud QFS cohort — a within-patient correlation between cytokine hyperresponsiveness and mitochondrial-peptide downregulation would be mechanistically informative.
- **Coxiella-specific vs. generic innate stimulation response:** Follow up with data distinguishing antigen-specific recall from general innate hyperactivation. This would help distinguish the antigen-persistence hypothesis from a durably reprogrammed innate setpoint (trained immunity / epigenetic imprinting).
- **IFN-γ / IDO / kynurenine pathway:** Test whether the elevated resting IFN-γ in QFS is linked to IDO activation and kynurenine-pathway metabolites (neurotoxic quinolinic acid, reduced tryptophan → serotonin), which would provide a bridge to the neurobehavioural symptom cluster.
- **Long-COVID and other PAIS cytokine comparison:** Compare this QFS IL-6 / TNFα / IFN-γ elevation profile to published long-COVID and ME/CFS cytokine studies (Calabrese et al., Peluso et al., Seeßle et al.) to evaluate whether the same cytokine triad recurs across triggers — directly relevant to `question:0001-shared-molecular-signature-across-triggers`.
- **`question:0006-jak-stat-il6-driver-vs-marker`**: The persistent IL-6 elevation is exactly the focal question addressed in that QS item. This paper constitutes an important supporting data point that IL-6 is elevated in at least one PAIS (QFS) both at rest and following antigen challenge.
- Adjacent papers: Keijmel et al. (doxycycline/CBT trial in QFS — did treatment arm changes co-occur with cytokine changes?); Roerink et al. (rituximab trial in QFS — B-cell depletion and cytokine outcomes); any Radboud group publications on Coxiella antigen persistence and immunological memory.
