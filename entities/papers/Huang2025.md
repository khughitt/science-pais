## Key Contribution

A PRISMA-compliant systematic review and meta-analysis of 37 studies (N = 2,965,467 patients) quantifies the prevalence and relative risk of three key cardiovascular sequelae — chest pain, palpitations, and hypertension — in post-acute COVID-19 syndrome (PACS). The study establishes that COVID-19 infection significantly and independently increases the odds of developing each of these outcomes compared to uninfected controls, with chest pain showing the highest relative risk (OR 4.0, 95% CI 1.6–10.0). Approximately 15% of COVID-19 survivors experience at least one cardiovascular sequela as a persistent, post-acute manifestation.

## Methods

- **Study type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis; PROSPERO registration CRD42024524290.
- **Search:** PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane Library searched through March 17, 2024 using terms "Post-Acute COVID-19 Syndrome," "long-haul COVID-19," and "adverse cardiac event."
- **Inclusion criteria:** Original studies or follow-ups with at least one cardiovascular PACS outcome; confirmed diagnoses by qualified clinicians with clinical records; patient self-reports via validated tools (Post COVID-19 Functional Status scale, Symptom Burden Questionnaire for Long COVID); minimum median/mean follow-up of 3 months post SARS-CoV-2 infection; WHO-standardized PACS definition; cardiovascular symptom definitions per ICD-10, ACC, and ESC guidelines.
- **Studies included:** 37 studies, with sample sizes ranging from 60 to 2,386,822; follow-up durations 3–12 months; mix of prospective (many) and retrospective designs; countries represented included USA, Korea, UK, Germany, Poland, China, Spain, India, Switzerland, Israel, and others.
- **Quality assessment:** Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS); 23 studies rated high quality (7–9 points), 14 moderate quality (5–6 points).
- **Statistical approach:** Random-effects model (given I² > 60% threshold signaling substantial heterogeneity); pooled odds ratios (ORs) and proportions with 95% CIs reported separately for case-control and case-series subsets. Heterogeneity explored via subgroup analyses (study design, geographic region, age <60 vs. ≥60 years); sensitivity analyses by sequentially removing individual studies; publication bias assessed with Egger's test, Peters' test, and trim-and-fill funnel plots.

## Key Findings

**Case-control analyses (relative risk vs. uninfected controls):**

| Outcome | Studies (N) | Total patients | Pooled OR (random-effects) | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chest pain | 8 | 116,778 | 4.0 | 1.6–10.0 |
| Palpitations | 5 | 543,369 | 3.4 | 1.1–10.2 |
| Hypertension | 9 | 17,317 | 1.7 | 1.6–1.8 |

**Case-series prevalence estimates (PACS patients only):**

| Outcome | Pooled prevalence (random-effects) | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| Chest pain | 22% | 14–33% |
| Palpitations | 18% | 13–24% |
| Hypertension | 19% | 12–31% |

**Subgroup findings:**
- Chest pain prevalence was higher in under-60s (32%, 95% CI 16–47%) than in those ≥60 (18%, 95% CI 12–25%).
- Palpitation prevalence was similar across age groups (~17–20%) but notably higher in European/American cohorts (24%, 95% CI 21–27%) than Asian cohorts (14%, 95% CI 11–18%).
- Study design was a key heterogeneity source: prospective studies for chest pain showed higher ORs (14.2, 95% CI 8.6–23.4) versus retrospective studies (OR 1.6, 95% CI 1.3–2.1).

**Publication bias:** No significant bias detected via Egger's/Peters' tests or trim-and-fill analysis across the primary outcomes.

**Proposed mechanisms discussed:**
1. Persistent viral reservoir in cardiac tissue triggering chronic inflammation via ACE2 downregulation → angiotensin II (Ang II) accumulation → endothelial inflammation, oxidative stress, and apoptosis.
2. Autoimmune response against cardiac antigens developing in later stages of infection, evidenced by elevated cardiac-specific antibodies in COVID-19 patients.
3. Endothelial injury with widespread microangiopathy and persistently elevated endothelial dysfunction biomarkers (including lipid peroxidation markers) in long COVID patients.

## Relevance

This paper directly supports the project's central frame of **failed homeostatic recovery after acute infection** (research-question:post-acute-infection-syndromes). Key connections:

1. **Cardiovascular dysregulation as a PAIS failure mode.** The elevated ORs for chest pain (4.0), palpitations (3.4), and hypertension (1.7) relative to uninfected controls confirm that cardiovascular homeostasis is persistently disrupted long after SARS-CoV-2 clearance, fitting the PAIS model of incomplete physiological recovery rather than transient acute-phase effects.

2. **Scale of burden.** With ~15% of COVID-19 survivors affected and the dataset spanning nearly 3 million patients across dozens of countries, this meta-analysis provides population-level evidence that cardiovascular sequelae are a core — not incidental — feature of PACS, strengthening the case that PACS represents a distinct biological failure state.

3. **Mechanistic pointers for the shared failure mode hypothesis.** The proposed mechanisms — ACE2-mediated endothelial injury, persistent microthrombus formation, and post-acute autoimmunity against cardiac antigens — are consistent with the project's cross-PAIS hypothesis around persistent immune activation and antigen-driven pathology. These mechanisms are not COVID-specific in principle; analogous vascular and immune dysregulation has been described in ME/CFS and post-treatment Lyme disease syndrome, supporting a shared failure-mode framing.

4. **Heterogeneity as a signal.** The very high I² values (93–100% for most outcomes) emphasize that PACS cardiovascular burden is highly context-dependent (study design, geography, age, vaccination/variant era), directly relevant to the project's concern that immune phenotypes are sensitive to host and environmental covariates.

5. **Diagnostic standardization gap.** The paper's critique of inconsistent PACS case definitions across included studies (symptom timeframes of 4, 8, and 12 weeks post-infection conflated) maps onto the project's recognition that case definitions shape measured prevalence — a cross-PAIS methodological concern.

## Limitations

1. **Very high heterogeneity** (I² 93–100% for chest pain and palpitations in case-control analyses) substantially limits the precision of pooled estimates; sensitivity analyses reduced but did not eliminate it.
2. **Predominantly retrospective designs** in many included studies limit causal inference; the prospective-vs-retrospective subgroup showed markedly different ORs for chest pain (14.2 vs. 1.6), suggesting systematic bias.
3. **Short or variable follow-up periods** (3–12 months); the minimum inclusion threshold of 3 months means some "PACS" cases may reflect subacute rather than true long-term sequelae.
4. **Geographic concentration** in Western countries (USA, Europe) with few studies from Africa, South America, or South/Southeast Asia, limiting global generalizability; ethnic disparities in PACS burden are documented but underrepresented.
5. **No stratification by vaccination status or SARS-CoV-2 variant era**, which are known modifiers of PACS risk and cardiovascular severity.
6. **Inconsistent PACS case definitions** across included studies (4-, 8-, and 12-week thresholds conflated), acknowledged by the authors as a source of heterogeneity and potential misclassification.
7. **Potential confounding** by pre-existing cardiovascular risk factors (e.g., hypertension, diabetes) not uniformly controlled across included studies; severity of acute illness also likely a confound.
8. **Limited mechanistic resolution**: mechanisms discussed are proposed hypotheses, not directly tested within this meta-analysis.

## Model / Tool Availability

No computational models, software tools, or datasets released with this paper. Analysis performed in R (version 4.3.1) with the `meta` package (version 6.5.0); code not deposited in a public repository.

## Follow-up

- Examine cardiovascular PAIS sequelae in ME/CFS and post-treatment Lyme disease for analogous vascular/endothelial disruption patterns — a direct test of the shared failure-mode hypothesis.
- Investigate whether ACE2-pathway dysregulation and endothelial dysfunction biomarkers (lipid peroxidation markers, microthrombus indicators) are consistently elevated across PAIS conditions beyond COVID-19.
- Read primary studies with large sample sizes in this meta-analysis (e.g., Hyejin Lee et al. 2022, n=2,386,822; Claire Hastie et al. 2022, n=94,443) for individual-level covariate data on vaccination, variant era, and pre-existing CVD.
- Consider whether the age-stratified finding (younger patients showing higher chest pain prevalence) challenges the naive assumption that cardiovascular PAIS risk increases monotonically with age.
- Link to project hypotheses around dysautonomia as a PAIS cardiovascular manifestation — palpitations in particular may overlap with autonomic dysfunction rather than structural cardiac disease.
