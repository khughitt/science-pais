# Inflammatory rheumatic diseases with onset after SARS-CoV-2 infection or COVID-19 vaccination: a report of 267 cases from the COVID-19 and ASD group

- **Authors:** Francesco Ursini, Piero Ruscitti, Olga Addimanda, et al. (COVID-19 and ASD group; 39 authors total)
- **Year:** 2023
- **Journal:** RMD Open
- **DOI:** 10.1136/rmdopen-2023-003022
- **BibTeX key:** Ursini2023
- **Source:** PDF

## Key Contribution

This is the largest published cohort (n=267) of new-onset inflammatory rheumatic diseases (IRD) arising in close temporal association with either SARS-CoV-2 infection (n=122) or COVID-19 vaccination (n=145), drawn from 23 Italian academic and general hospitals over 12 months (Nov 2021–Oct 2022). The study characterises the spectrum of disease categories — inflammatory joint diseases (IJD), polymyalgia rheumatica (PMR), connective tissue diseases (CTD), and vasculitis — and documents differential phenotypic patterns between the post-infection and post-vaccine cohorts, while explicitly acknowledging that temporal association does not establish causality.

## Methods

**Design:** Multicentric observational cohort study (Italian COVID-19 and ASD collaborative group; >40 physicians, 23 centres spanning northern to southern Italy).

**Inclusion criteria (either/or):**
- Onset of rheumatic manifestations within 4 weeks of RT-PCR/antigen-confirmed SARS-CoV-2 infection, OR
- Onset within 4 weeks of an approved COVID-19 vaccine dose (BNT162b2 [Pfizer] or mRNA-1273 [Moderna]).

**Exclusion criteria:** Prior history of IRD or acrosyndrome; diagnosis of PACS or "long-COVID."

**Data collection:** Web-based Google Forms questionnaire; anonymous; manually de-duplicated.

**Variables collected:** Demographics, infection/vaccination history, clinical rheumatological features, autoantibody panel, ESR, CRP, rheumatologist-assigned diagnosis, first-line treatment.

**Disease activity instruments:**
- IJD: DAS28-CRP (Disease Activity Score 28 joints, CRP-based), assessed at baseline and after ≥4 weeks of treatment.
- PMR: PMR Activity Score (PMR-AS), baseline and after ≥4 weeks.

**Statistical methods:** Student's t-test for normally distributed continuous variables (ln-transformation for skewed); Fisher's exact test for categorical variables; p<0.05 threshold. SPSS v26.0.

**Collection period:** 12 months (November 2021 to October 2022), encompassing the Omicron wave and mass booster vaccination in Italy.

## Key Findings

### Overall cohort (n=267)
- Post-COVID-19: n=122 (45.2%); mean age 54±17 years; 69.7% female.
- Post-vaccine: n=145 (54.8%); mean age 58±16 years; 66.2% female.
- COVID-19 severity in the post-infection group: 85.2% mild, 11.5% severe (WHO criteria), 3.3% asymptomatic; 92.6% managed at home.
- Vaccine doses in post-vaccine cohort: 45.8% after booster (3rd dose); 35.4% after 2nd dose; 16% after 1st dose; 2.1% after 4th dose. Pfizer BNT162b2 in 78.6%, Moderna in 16.6%.
- Mean onset delay after trigger: 14.5±7.8 days (post-COVID-19) vs 13.9±8.5 days (post-vaccine); not significantly different (p=0.59).

### IRD category distribution
| Category | Post-COVID-19 | Post-vaccine | P |
|---|---|---|---|
| IJD | 52.5% | 37.2% | 0.013 |
| PMR | 21.3% | 33.1% | 0.032 |
| CTD | 19.7% | 20.7% | 0.837 |
| Vasculitis | 6.6% | 9.0% | 0.467 |

### Inflammatory joint diseases (IJD; n=64 post-COVID, n=54 post-vaccine)
- Most common category in both groups.
- Predominant diagnosis: peripheral spondyloarthritis (pSpA) — 50% (post-COVID) vs 35.2% (post-vaccine).
- Rheumatoid arthritis (RA): 29.7% (post-COVID) vs 25.9% (post-vaccine); RF+ in ~58-71%; ACPA+ in ~50%.
- Psoriatic arthritis (PsA): significantly higher in post-vaccine group — 29.6% vs 12.5% (p=0.021).
- Baseline DAS28-CRP: 4.3±1.0 (post-COVID) vs 4.4±1.1 (post-vaccine); follow-up 3.0±1.0 vs 3.1±1.1 (approximately 30% drop from baseline in both groups).
- First-line treatments: glucocorticoids ~52-61%, NSAIDs ~41-47%, MTX ~34-41%.

### Polymyalgia rheumatica (PMR; n=28 post-COVID, n=46 post-vaccine)
- Second most common category overall; significantly more common post-vaccine.
- No significant inter-cohort differences in age, inflammatory markers, or PMR-AS at baseline.
- PMR-AS baseline: 28.3 (25.2–33.0) post-COVID vs 24.8 (23.7–27.5) post-vaccine.
- PMR-AS follow-up: 8.44 (5.4–10.0) vs 6.43 (4.6–7.0) — approximately 70% and 74% reduction from baseline, respectively.
- 100% of post-COVID-19 PMR patients received glucocorticoids; 93.5% in post-vaccine group.
- Notably, 7 patients (6 post-vaccine, 1 post-COVID) were aged <50 years (range 33–48) but had elevated CRP (1.15–5.4 mg/dL) and/or ESR (20–110 mm/h), PMR-like clinical presentation, and steroid responsiveness.

### Connective tissue diseases (CTD; n=24 post-COVID, n=30 post-vaccine)
- Range: undifferentiated CTD (UCTD), systemic sclerosis (SSc), idiopathic inflammatory myopathies (IIM), amyopathic dermatomyositis (ADM), SLE, mixed CTD, Sjogren's syndrome, isolated acrosyndrome.
- ANA-positive: 87.5% (post-COVID) vs 93.3% (post-vaccine).
- Only significant inter-cohort difference: isolated acrosyndrome more common post-COVID (20.8% vs 3.3%, p=0.042), consistent with SARS-CoV-2 endothelial tropism.
- No follow-up disease activity data collected (outcome heterogeneity too high).

### Vasculitis (n=8 post-COVID, n=13 post-vaccine)
- Least common category in both groups.
- Spectrum: large-vessel vasculitis (including cranial and LV-GCA), small-vessel vasculitis (ANCA-associated: EGPA, GPA, MPA), variable vessel (Behçet's), skin vasculitis (palpable purpura, urticarial).
- ESR and CRP substantially elevated: ESR 42–67 mm/h, CRP 3.57–5.48 mg/dL.
- No significant inter-cohort differences detected.

## Relevance

This paper is directly relevant to the project's research question on post-acute infection syndromes (PAIS) and the frame of **failed homeostatic recovery after acute infection** in three ways:

1. **Documenting de novo immune dysregulation as a PAIS endpoint.** New-onset IRD arising within weeks of SARS-CoV-2 infection represents one of the most clinically defined downstream consequences of immune homeostasis failure — not merely symptom persistence but frank autoimmune disease induction. The 14-day median onset latency suggests a rapid but delayed (adaptive) immune response, consistent with molecular mimicry, bystander activation, or epitope spreading rather than direct viral damage alone.

2. **Distinguishing post-infection from post-vaccine phenotypes.** The differential enrichment of IJD (especially pSpA/reactive-arthritis-like) post-infection versus PMR post-vaccination points to pathogen-specific versus adjuvant-like immune activation pathways — a mechanistic distinction relevant to understanding which immune perturbations drive which failure modes. The higher acrosyndrome prevalence post-COVID reflects SARS-CoV-2 endothelial tropism as a specific pathogenic mechanism not shared by vaccine-induced events.

3. **Treatment responsiveness as partial recovery signal.** The ~30% DAS28 reduction (IJD) and ~70-74% PMR-AS reduction after 4–8 weeks of first-line therapy indicates that these PAIS-adjacent states are pharmacologically reversible in the short term. This is a useful calibration point: acute immune dysregulation leading to IRD can resolve with targeted intervention, distinguishing it from the more refractory homeostasis failure seen in long-haul ME/CFS-like PAIS presentations.

The study also provides a methodological template (multicentric observational cohort with standardised disease activity instruments pre/post-treatment) directly applicable to future PAIS research designs.

## Limitations

1. **No denominator / incidence estimate.** Total number of exposed individuals (infected or vaccinated) is not tracked, preventing true incidence or relative risk calculations. Authors note Italy had ~25 million COVID cases and ~143 million vaccine doses administered, implying vaccine-exposed population is ~5x larger — coincidental IRD occurrence is therefore expected more frequently in the post-vaccine group without any causal effect.

2. **Temporal association ≠ causation.** The 4-week window is pragmatic but arbitrary; no control group (matched individuals without SARS-CoV-2 or vaccine exposure) is included, so background IRD incidence cannot be distinguished from triggered cases.

3. **Possible misclassification.** Some patients may represent SARS-CoV-2- or vaccine-induced exacerbation of pre-existing subclinical/undiagnosed IRD rather than truly de novo disease. The authors acknowledge this limitation.

4. **Short follow-up (median 8 weeks for IJD/PMR).** Durability of treatment response and rates of remission vs. relapse are unknown. Whether these cases resolve fully or progress to chronic IRD cannot be determined from available data.

5. **Observational design, no randomisation.** Treatment choice was per clinical judgement; no standardised treatment protocol.

6. **Referral/ascertainment bias.** Only cases referred to rheumatology centres within the collaborative network are captured; mild cases managed in primary care are missed.

7. **Incomplete follow-up for CTD and vasculitis.** No post-treatment disease activity data were collected for CTD or vasculitis patients due to outcome measure heterogeneity.

8. **Missing cutaneous domain data for IJD.** Authors note they did not collect psoriasis information, limiting interpretation of the higher PsA prevalence in the post-vaccine cohort.

9. **Italy-specific context.** High vaccine coverage (93% of population >12 years) and predominantly mRNA-vaccine use; findings may not generalise to settings with different vaccine types (e.g., adenoviral vector vaccines) or different population immunological backgrounds.

## Model / Tool Availability

No computational models, tools, or datasets are released. Data are available upon reasonable request to the corresponding author (Dr. Francesco Ursini; francesco.ursini2@unibo.it). ## Follow-up

- Ursini et al., *Ann Rheum Dis* 2022 (ref 25 in paper): earlier report from same group of 66 short-term inflammatory musculoskeletal manifestations post-COVID-19 vaccine — foundational to this larger study.
- Ursini et al., *Clin Rheumatol* 2022 (ref 24): 30-case report of systemic syndromes post-COVID-19 vaccine.
- Mettler et al., *Rheumatology (Oxford)* 2022 (ref 40): global pharmacovigilance study on GCA/PMR risk post-COVID-19 vaccination — should be read for epidemiological complement.
- Investigate: do the post-COVID IRD cases share genetic risk loci (HLA-B27, HLA-DRB1) with spontaneous IRD? This study did not report HLA genotyping beyond B27 in spondyloarthritis subsets.
- A prospective cohort with longer follow-up (≥12 months) is needed to determine whether these post-infection IRD cases remit, relapse, or progress — a key unanswered question for the PAIS recovery frame.
