# Antibodies to β adrenergic and muscarinic cholinergic receptors in patients with Chronic Fatigue Syndrome

- **Authors:** Madlen Loebel, Patricia Grabowski, Harald Heidecke, Sandra Bauer, Leif G. Hanitsch, Kirsten Wittke, Christian Meisel, Petra Reinke, Hans-Dieter Volk, Øystein Fluge, Olav Mella, Carmen Scheibenbogen
- **Year:** 2016
- **Journal:** Brain, Behavior, and Immunity
- **Volume/Pages:** 52:32–39
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.bbi.2015.09.013
- **PMID:** 26399744
- **BibTeX key:** Loebel2016
- **Source:** Europe PMC abstract (blocked_but_oa; full text not agent-accessible); supplemented by Crossref metadata

## Key Contribution

This is the **foundational serology paper** establishing that a substantial subset of CFS patients carry elevated IgG autoantibodies against β2-adrenergic and M3/M4-muscarinic acetylcholine receptors — the specific GPCR targets hypothesized to drive autonomic dysregulation in post-acute infection syndromes. Across a large Berlin cohort (n=268 CFS, n=108 controls), 29.5% of CFS patients were seropositive for one or more of these receptor antibodies. Critically, in a Bergen rituximab trial subcohort (n=25), β2-AR and M4 antibodies declined in clinical responders but not in non-responders to B-cell depletion — providing indirect evidence of pathogenic relevance and establishing these autoantibodies as candidate biomarkers for B-cell-targeting therapy. This paper is the primary citation anchor for the project's `question:0009` thread and for `proposition:0018-pais-sfn-anti-gpcr-autoantibody-pathogenicity`.

## Methods

### Cohort and Case Definition

- **Berlin cohort:** n = 268 CFS patients; case definition is not specified in the PubMed abstract, so CCC/ICC-vs-Fukuda stringency cannot be adjudicated from the extracted source alone.
- **Bergen rituximab subcohort:** n = 25 patients with pre- and post-treatment samples from the KTS-2 rituximab maintenance trial; these patients received rituximab and were assessed as clinical responders vs. non-responders.
- **Healthy controls:** n = 108; matching strategy, age/sex distribution, and exclusion criteria are not characterized in the PubMed abstract.

### Assay Type

**BINDING ELISA (CellTrend GmbH, Luckenwalde, Germany)** — not a functional/receptor-activation bioassay. Serum IgG binding to human G-protein-coupled receptors was detected by ELISA. This is a critical distinction: the assay measures *antibody binding* to receptor epitopes, not receptor agonism/antagonism or downstream G-protein activation. Whether the detected antibodies are functionally active (i.e., activate or block the receptor upon binding) was not tested in this study. See Limitations section.

### Receptors Screened

The panel covered IgG against human:
- α-adrenergic receptors (subtypes not enumerated in the PubMed abstract)
- β-adrenergic receptors (β1, β2)
- Muscarinic acetylcholine receptors M1–M5
- Dopamine receptors
- Serotonin receptors
- Angiotensin receptors
- Endothelin receptors

### Additional Measures

- IgG subclass levels (IgG1–4)
- T-cell activation markers (HLA-DR+ T cells)
- Thyreoperoxidase (anti-TPO) antibodies and anti-nuclear antibodies (ANA) — in the subgroup with high β2 antibodies
- Clinical response to rituximab (responder/non-responder) in the Bergen cohort

## Key Findings

### Seropositive Receptors and Prevalence

| Receptor | Finding in CFS vs. Controls | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **β2-adrenergic (β2-AR)** | Significantly elevated | Primary positive finding; also correlates with immune markers (see below) |
| **M3-muscarinic (M3 AChR)** | Significantly elevated | Positive finding |
| **M4-muscarinic (M4 AChR)** | Significantly elevated | Positive finding; declined with rituximab in responders |
| β1-adrenergic (β1-AR) | Not different from controls | Negative finding |
| α-adrenergic | Not different from controls | Negative finding |
| Dopamine receptors | Not different from controls | Negative finding |
| Serotonin receptors | Not different from controls | Negative finding |
| Angiotensin receptors | Not different from controls | Negative finding |
| Endothelin receptors | Not different from controls | Negative finding |

**Overall seropositivity:** 29.5% of CFS patients (n=268) had elevated antibodies against one or more of M acetylcholine and β-adrenergic receptors.

Exact effect sizes (odds ratios, mean titer differences, confidence intervals) and precise p-values for each receptor comparison are not available from the PubMed abstract; full-text extraction is required.

### Correlation with Immune Markers (β2-AR autoantibodies)

- **IgG subclasses:** High correlation between autoantibody levels and elevated IgG1–3 subclasses; no correlation with IgG4. This pattern is consistent with a T-cell-dependent, class-switched antibody response rather than a natural/innate IgG4-dominated autoantibody.
- **T-cell activation:** Patients with high β2-AR antibodies had significantly more frequently activated HLA-DR+ T cells.
- **Polyautoimmunity:** Patients with high β2-AR antibodies had significantly more frequent anti-TPO and ANA — suggesting an immune milieu predisposed to autoimmunity broadly.

These correlations constitute the "titer ↔ immune marker" arm of the study; they support the view that the autoantibodies arise in the context of active systemic immune dysregulation rather than as isolated natural antibodies.

### Rituximab Subcohort (Bergen, n=25)

In patients receiving rituximab maintenance treatment with prolonged B-cell depletion:
- Elevated β2-AR and M4 receptor autoantibodies **significantly declined in clinical responders**.
- Autoantibodies did **not** decline in non-responders.

This responder-linked decline is the key indirect causal evidence: if B-cell depletion (which depletes the antibody-producing cells) reduces both antibodies and symptoms in the same patients, it implies the antibodies are not merely bystander markers. However, this is correlational and confounded — rituximab has multiple immune effects beyond autoantibody depletion, and responder selection could be driven by the antibody-decline or by other rituximab-sensitive mechanisms.

### No Titer ↔ Symptom Severity Correlation Reported

The abstract does not report a direct correlation between autoantibody titer and symptom severity scores. The immune marker correlations (IgG subclasses, HLA-DR+ T cells) are reported; functional symptom correlations are not confirmed from the abstract-level extraction.

### Subgroup: Infection-Triggered Onset

No subgroup analysis by infection-triggered vs. gradual onset is described in the abstract. The abstract notes that infection-triggered disease onset motivates the autoimmune hypothesis, but whether seroprevalence or titer was stratified by onset type requires full-text extraction.

## Relevance

This paper is the **primary foundational citation** for the project's anti-GPCR autoantibody thread and directly addresses `question:0009-functional-autoantibodies-drive-dysautonomia`. Key relevance:

1. **Establishes prevalence:** ~30% of CFS patients in a large German cohort carry binding anti-GPCR antibodies against the specific receptors (β2-AR, M3, M4) hypothesized to drive dysautonomia — a non-trivial fraction that motivates further investigation.

2. **Specifies receptor targets:** Confirms that β2-adrenergic, M3-muscarinic, and M4-muscarinic — but not β1, α-adrenergic, dopamine, serotonin, angiotensin, or endothelin receptors — are the distinctive targets in CFS. This sharpens the mechanistic hypothesis away from a non-specific pan-autoimmune pattern.

3. **B-cell depletion link:** The rituximab responder data are an indirect interventional signal connecting autoantibody level to clinical state, which advances the causal argument beyond simple cross-sectional association.

4. **Assay standard:** This paper established the CellTrend ELISA as the de facto assay for anti-GPCR antibodies in the ME/CFS/PAIS field. Subsequent papers by the Scheibenbogen group (e.g., Stein2025) use the same platform, meaning findings across studies are directly comparable — but also means all field-wide results share this assay's binding-only limitation.

5. **Immune embedding:** The correlations with IgG1–3 subclasses, HLA-DR+ T cells, anti-TPO, and ANA show these are not isolated natural GPCR antibodies but are part of a broader autoimmune profile — relevant to `hypothesis:0003-immune-exhaustion-feedback` as a candidate self-sustaining loop.

## Limitations

1. **Binding ELISA, not functional assay.** The CellTrend ELISA detects IgG binding to receptor epitopes; it does not measure receptor activation, G-protein coupling, downstream signaling, or physiological autonomic effect. Whether the detected antibodies are agonist, antagonist, or functionally inert is entirely unresolved by this study. This is the single most load-bearing limitation for the `question:0009` causal claim — the entire GPCR-autoantibody → dysautonomia hypothesis requires functional confirmation that this paper does not provide.

2. **Healthy control seropositivity.** While the abstract reports that β2-AR, M3, and M4 antibodies are *significantly elevated* in CFS, natural low-titer anti-GPCR antibodies are known to occur in healthy individuals. Exact seropositive rates in the control group are not reported in the abstract, which makes the threshold for "elevated" and the specificity of the assay difficult to assess independently.

3. **No functional confirmation of receptor pathogenicity.** Neither receptor crosslinking studies, cell-based activation assays, nor passive-transfer experiments (the decisive causal tests) were performed. The paper is purely observational for the pathogenicity question.

4. **No direct symptom-severity correlation reported.** The abstract reports immune-marker correlations (IgG subclasses, T-cell activation) but does not describe a direct titer ↔ symptom severity correlation, leaving the clinical relevance of titer magnitude unclear from abstract-level extraction.

5. **Case definition uncertainty.** The case definition used for the Berlin cohort is not stated in the abstract. If Fukuda 1994 criteria were used (the Charité group's standard at this period), the cohort is broader than CCC/ICC-defined ME/CFS, potentially diluting autoantibody seroprevalence relative to more restrictive case definitions.

6. **Rituximab subcohort is small (n=25) and confounded.** The B-cell-depletion arm is the key interventional-adjacent signal but: (a) n=25 is small; (b) rituximab has multiple immune effects beyond autoantibody reduction (T-cell regulation, cytokine suppression); (c) responder/non-responder classification was clinical, potentially introducing outcome-label subjectivity; (d) this is a subcohort from a trial (KTS-2) not primarily designed to test autoantibody-responder correlations. The rituximab trial program (including RituxME, the Norwegian placebo-controlled RCT) later showed no significant benefit overall — the anti-GPCR-autoantibody responder subset may represent a genuinely enriched population or a small-sample artifact.

7. **Single-centre Berlin cohort.** The main seroprevalence data come from one academic centre in Germany; replication in independent cohorts with different population ancestry and referral patterns is needed.

8. **Conflict of interest.** Harald Heidecke's PubMed affiliation is CellTrend GmbH, the company providing the ELISA platform; this warrants disclosure when interpreting quantitative titer claims.

## Model / Tool Availability

No model or tool artifact. The CellTrend ELISA for anti-GPCR autoantibodies (β1, β2, M3, M4, and others) is a commercial diagnostic product (CellTrend GmbH, Luckenwalde, Germany) — not freely distributable but available as a fee-for-service or kit purchase. This assay is the de facto standard platform referenced across Scheibenbogen-group papers.

## Follow-up

- **Key gap:** Obtain full text to verify: (a) case definition used (Fukuda vs. CCC); (b) exact seropositive rates in healthy controls; (c) effect sizes and p-values per receptor; (d) whether a titer ↔ symptom severity correlation was reported; (e) whether infection-triggered onset was analysed as a subgroup.
- **Functional validation gap:** This paper is foundational but binding-only. The decisive next step for `question:0009` is studies using functional (receptor-activation) assays — cell-based agonist/antagonist assays, or passive-transfer experiments — to confirm that the binding antibodies are also physiologically active. `task:t006` is tracking this literature.
- **Replication:** Check whether independent non-Scheibenbogen groups have replicated the β2-AR / M3 / M4 seroprevalence finding with comparable or different assays.
- **Assay comparison:** The field needs a head-to-head comparison of CellTrend ELISA vs. functional cell-based assays on the same serum samples to quantify binding-vs.-functional concordance — the most direct way to close the assay-type gap.
