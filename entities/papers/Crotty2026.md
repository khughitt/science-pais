# Immunological memory to vaccines

- **Authors:** Shane Crotty
- **Year:** 2026
- **Journal:** Immunity, vol. 59, pp. 813–832
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.immuni.2026.02.019
- **BibTeX key:** Crotty2026
- **Source:** PDF

## Key Contribution

This comprehensive review synthesizes current human data on vaccine-generated immunological memory across four compartments — memory B cells (B_Mem), CD8+ T cell memory, CD4+ T cell memory, and long-lived antibody-secreting plasma cells (B_PC) — and presents a unified "layered tissue and circulating immune memory" model of protective immunity. The central organizing insight is that protection is not a single mechanism but a series of complementary barriers (tissue-resident and circulating) whose individual failures allow viral spread to progress to severe disease. The review provides rigorous, example-rich treatment of hybrid immunity — the qualitative superiority of memory generated by combined infection and vaccination over either alone — and systematically corrects influential misconceptions (notably the claim that mRNA vaccines induce only short-term antibodies). This is the field-defining 2026 synthesis from Shane Crotty's lab, with extensive COVID-19 vaccine data as the primary case study.

## Methods

Narrative review of human immunological data; no new primary data. Organized by memory compartment, with subsections on durability, functionality, tissue/resident memory, and hybrid immunity within each arm. COVID-19 vaccines (mRNA-LNP, Ad vectors, recombinant protein+adjuvant, inactivated virus) are the primary case study due to exceptional data availability; compared throughout to smallpox, HBV, MMR, influenza, pertussis, and other classical vaccines. Mouse-model data used only to frame mechanistic questions where human data are absent.

## Key Findings

### B cell memory (B_Mem)

- B_Mem cells can persist for >50 years after live-attenuated vaccine priming (smallpox antigen-specific B_Mem detected in people vaccinated before 1968; measles-, mumps-, rubella-specific B_Mem stable across decades in cross-sectional adults; HBV B_Mem at 10-15 years).
- COVID-19 mRNA vaccines generate robust B_Mem comparable in frequency to infection, with substantial somatic hypermutation (SHM) and affinity maturation via germinal center activity; B_Mem frequencies increase transiently after a 3rd dose.
- B_Mem maintained by slow antigen-independent homeostatic proliferation (paralleling memory CD8 T cells); intermittent antigen re-exposure (e.g., endemic *B. pertussis* circulation) contributes to maintenance for some specificities.
- B_Mem diversity is a defining functional feature: unlike memory CD8 T cells, which often show large clonal expansions, human B_Mem displays sparse clonality with many distinct BCR sequences — an evolutionary "library" strategy that provides pre-positioned reactivity against future viral variants and related species.
- After a 3rd dose of ancestral mRNA COVID vaccine, B_Mem that had earlier encoded Omicron-cross-reactive specificities expanded and differentiated into B_PC, generating neutralizing antibodies against a variant those individuals had never been directly exposed to. This demonstrates that B_Mem clonal diversity encodes anticipatory breadth beyond the immunizing antigen.
- B_RM (tissue-resident memory B cells): sparse human data; COVID vaccine-elicited spike-specific B_Mem is recruited to infection sites during breakthrough infection and may seed local B_RM, but direct evidence is lacking. wP-primed (whole-cell pertussis) but not aP-primed individuals have CD4+ T_RM in upper airways — consistent with tissue residency requiring local antigen encounter. Overall, parenteral vaccination is not expected to establish B_RM de novo in tissues.
- Hybrid immunity B_Mem: enhanced neutralization breadth and magnitude via continued GC activity post-infection; B_Mem in hybrid immunity retain SHM/affinity maturation over months.

### CD8+ T cell memory

- Elicited robustly by live-attenuated, mRNA, and viral-vectored vaccines; NOT reliably by recombinant protein or inactivated virus vaccines (including inactivated COVID vaccines).
- Durable kinetics largely unknown beyond 1–2 years for most vaccines. Smallpox: measurable memory CD8 T cells in ~50% of vaccinated individuals 20+ years post-vaccination; estimated t½ ~9–15 years (from cross-sectional data). YFV-specific memory CD8 T cells maintained at stable frequencies for ≥2 years.
- Human memory CD8 T cell functional profile: rapid IFN-γ secretion is NOT a consistent feature in humans at baseline (contrast with mouse models); function appears after extended re-stimulation (18h AIM assay or pMHC multimers better reveal responses than acute ICS). Functional diversity greater than previously appreciated.
- CD8 T_RM in tissues: requires local antigen encounter; parenteral vaccination alone does not establish CD8 T_RM in mucosal tissues (lung, upper airway). SARS-CoV-2 infection (apparent or inapparent) creates CD8 T_RM in lung and upper airway. Hybrid immunity: IFN-γ expression is the most enhanced functional feature of memory CD8 T cells.
- Evidence is now strong that CD8 T cells are mechanistically relevant to COVID-19 protection: association with viral clearance speed (HLA-A2 immunogenetics, healthcare worker seroconversion associations); CD8 T cells track with viral clearance in primary SARS-CoV-2 cases and with rapid recall on breakthrough infection.

### CD4+ T cell memory

- Generated by almost all human vaccines (a key difference from CD8+ T cells); even pure polysaccharide vaccines (T-independent) are an exception.
- Multiple subtypes with distinct functions and kinetics: T_H1, T_FH (follicular helper), T_H2, T_H17, CD4-CTL, iTreg — each with distinct migration properties and memory biology.
- Memory T_FH (circulating CXCR5+ CD4+): central to B cell help and high-quality antibody responses; robustly generated by many vaccines; detectable ≥6 months after 2 doses of COVID mRNA vaccine and ≥6 months after HIV vaccines, influenza vaccination, pertussis vaccination, BCG, and tetanus-specific T_FH.
- **Imprinting (critical for PAIS relevance):** The type of initial pertussis vaccine (wP = whole-cell; aP = acellular) administered in infancy shapes CD4 T_H phenotype for *decades*, even after multiple aP booster immunizations. wP → predominantly T_H1 (IFN-γ) CD4 memory; aP → predominantly T_H2/T_H17 (IL-5, IL-4, IL-13) CD4 memory. This T_H imprint persists despite repeated aP boosters in adults and is directly linked to wP-primed adults having CD4 T_RM in upper airways (T_H17 dependent), providing pertussis-specific protection absent in aP-primed individuals. This is the clearest human demonstration of early immune priming durably establishing a functional "set point" resistant to later correction.
- COVID CD4 T cell memory: spike-specific memory CD4 T cells detectable >6 months after 2-dose mRNA series; 3rd and 4th doses had modest impact on frequency but improved quality. Inactivated virus COVID vaccines did generate durable CD4 T cell memory.
- Potential for CD4 T_RM contribution to hybrid immunity: COVID vaccine-induced circulating memory CD4 T cells were associated with rapid recall on breakthrough infection and with lower viral loads in tissues, possibly seeding local T_RM — but this has not been directly demonstrated.

### Antibody memory (B_PC)

- Long-lived B_PC in bone marrow are the primary source of durable circulating antibodies (not circulating B_Mem cells). B_Mem cells' primary antibody role is anamnestic production after recall.
- Smallpox antibodies: near-infinite effective half-life demonstrated — stable circulating titers for ≥88 years in the absence of antigen re-exposure (Swedish population cohort); this establishes that humans can generate truly life-long antibody memory.
- mRNA COVID vaccines generate long-lived B_PC: antibodies 6 months post–2-dose mRNA vaccination comparable to or higher than titers generated by natural SARS-CoV-2 infection alone; 3rd dose substantially elevates peak and slows decay, generating durably higher long-term setpoints. This directly corrects the widespread misconception that mRNA vaccines only induce transient antibodies.
- Kinetics: initial rapid decline (t½ ~21 days for serum IgG protein) reflects short-lived B_PC dying off; plateau after ~6 months reflects long-lived B_PC contribution (much longer t½).
- Key knowledge gap: SARS-CoV-2-specific B_PC are NOT reliably detected in the long-lived bone marrow compartment after mRNA vaccination in humans (Hentenaar2024), in contrast to COVID infection (or hybrid immunity), where durable bone marrow localization appears more robust. This may be a mechanistic explanation for differences in antibody durability between vaccine and hybrid immunity cohorts.
- Influenza vaccines: severely constrained by pre-existing anti-influenza IgG from prior infection/vaccination history, which blunts B_PC differentiation ("original antigenic sin" / immunological imprinting). Most flu vaccine-specific B_PC appear short-lived; influenza vaccine-specific BM B_PC generally undetectable 12 months later. The vast majority of human anti-influenza IgG is infection-induced, not vaccine-induced.
- Inactivated COVID vaccines (CoronaVac, BBIBP-CorV): generated 10- to 100-fold lower B_Mem than COVID mRNA vaccines; antibody titers not durable at 9 months; boosting with more immunogenic vaccines (mRNA, protein+adjuvant) partially rescued responses.

### Hybrid immunity

- Clearly documented for SARS-CoV-2: prior infection + vaccination (in any order) generates greater antibody magnitude, neutralization breadth (including against distant variants), and durability than either alone.
- Mucosal immune component is specific to hybrid immunity: mucosal IgA in saliva and nasal secretions substantially increased in hybrid immunity; immunization alone does NOT induce nasal IgA increase; saliva and nasal IgA appear compartmentalized (mucosal transcytosis from plasma IgG can supply saliva/nasal IgG, but mucosal IgA requires local mucosal plasma cell activity driven by antigen at the mucosal site).
- Tissue-resident memory component: local tissue T cell memory present in hybrid immunity but not vaccination alone — IFN-γ is the most enhanced functional feature of memory CD8 T cells in hybrid immunity context (breakthrough infection studies).
- HPV vaccine illustrates a paradox: >99% efficacy against a mucosal pathogen using a vaccine that generates only systemic IgG (no mucosal IgA). Consistent with passive transudation of systemic IgG to genital mucosa being sufficient for protection against HPV.
- Mechanism of hybrid immunity superiority: due to the combination of high-quality vaccine-induced circulating antibodies/memory and infection-induced tissue-resident memory (B_RM, T_RM) and mucosal IgA — each layer is additive.

## Relevance

This review is relevant to the post-acute-infection project across four threads:

**1. Immune imprinting and PAIS susceptibility (primary link: `hypothesis:0020`).**
The pertussis imprinting example — wP vs aP at 2 months determines CD4+ T_H phenotype for decades despite multiple aP boosters — is the clearest human demonstration that early immune priming durably establishes a functional "set point." The analogous question for PAIS is whether early SARS-CoV-2 immune priming (ancestral vs Delta vs Omicron, vaccination-first vs infection-first) shapes the immunological character of subsequent PAIS presentations. More broadly, the review establishes that vaccine-response competence (magnitude of B_Mem, T_FH, B_PC induction) is substantially shaped by prior immunity history — providing a biologically grounded proxy for the "immune baseline reserve" concept in `hypothesis:0020`. Individuals with depleted or aberrant baseline immune architecture (e.g., blunted flu B_PC due to original antigenic sin, high pre-existing non-specific IgG, frailty-associated thymic output decline) would be predicted to mount qualitatively inferior vaccine responses and to have altered baseline immune states upon next infection.

**2. Tissue-resident memory and antigen clearance (`hypothesis:0002`, `hypothesis:0003`).**
The review establishes that T_RM in mucosal tissues (upper airway, lung) requires local antigen encounter — parenteral vaccination alone does not establish T_RM. Individuals with vaccine-only COVID immunity at the time of initial SARS-CoV-2 infection lack mucosal T_RM compared with those who have had prior infection. Inadequate tissue-level immune surveillance could allow residual antigen or viral material to persist in mucosal compartments, connecting to the antigen-fragment reservoir hypothesis (`hypothesis:0002`). The CD8 T cell functional section also notes that conventional ICS assays undercount functional CD8 T memory in humans — an important methodological caution for interpreting exhaustion or dysfunction claims in PAIS studies supporting `hypothesis:0003`.

**3. Hybrid immunity as a modifier of PAIS risk and phenotype.**
If individuals with hybrid immunity have qualitatively superior tissue-local and mucosal immune responses, they may experience lower PAIS incidence or attenuated PAIS phenotype following breakthrough SARS-CoV-2 infection relative to vaccine-only individuals — both through better initial antigen clearance and through mucosal IgA providing local protection. This represents an unaddressed confound in most PAIS epidemiology (vaccination status is recorded, but hybrid immunity status usually is not), and a specific testable hypothesis about PAIS incidence stratified by immunity type.

**4. Immune set-point maintenance without antigen (`hypothesis:0009`).**
The B_Mem antigen-independent homeostatic proliferation mechanism demonstrates that human immune memory can be maintained for decades without ongoing antigen — a prototype antigen-independent persistence mechanism. This has two implications for PAIS: (a) immune state changes induced by SARS-CoV-2 infection could in principle be maintained without residual viral antigen via analogous maintenance pathways (paralleling trained HSPC innate immunity discussed in the project's h0003 notes); (b) the irreversibility of wP-primed T_H imprinting despite years of antigen-free existence shows that durable immune set-point changes are biologically feasible in humans. Both support the conceptual plausibility of the post-infectious immune set-point shift in `hypothesis:0009`.

## Limitations

- Narrative review; no new primary data; inherits all selection biases of the literature reviewed.
- Human data are sparse for most immune compartments beyond circulating antibodies: direct flow cytometry quantitation of B_Mem is available for few vaccines and few individuals; T_RM data in humans are largely limited to COVID and pertussis; B_RM human data are minimal.
- COVID-19 vaccine data dominate due to pandemic-era investment; generalizability of COVID-specific findings (especially hybrid immunity) to other PAIS-relevant pathogens (EBV, Borrelia, Coxiella, influenza) is assumed in some cases rather than demonstrated.
- The imprinting section focuses on pertussis and influenza; the concept's generalizability to other respiratory pathogens that are relevant to PAIS is not systematically addressed.
- Long-term memory durability data (>10–20 years) exist only for a handful of vaccines (smallpox, MMR, HBV, polio, rabies); for most modern vaccines — including COVID mRNA — durability beyond 2–3 years remains extrapolated.
- No mechanistic data on how PAIS itself affects immunological memory; the review is vaccine-centric and does not discuss how immune dysregulation in PAIS might alter memory maintenance, durability, or functionality.
- The discussion of hybrid immunity is largely observational and cross-sectional; causal attribution of specific immune layers to protection remains incomplete.
