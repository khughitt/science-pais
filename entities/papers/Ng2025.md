# Pre-pandemic leukocyte count is associated with severity of post-acute sequelae of SARS-CoV-2 infection among older women in the Women's Health Initiative

- **Authors:** Ted K. S. Ng, Hind A. Beydoun, Diane Von Ah, and colleagues
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal:** Menopause
- **Volume/Pages:** 32(3):197-206
- **DOI:** 10.1097/GME.0000000000002490
- **PMID:** 39874450
- **BibTeX key:** Ng2025

## Key Contribution

This Women's Health Initiative analysis links pre-pandemic leukocyte count to later PASC
severity among older women. It is a rare PAIS-relevant use of long-running pre-pandemic
cohort data with clinical laboratory measures that could not have been caused by the
subsequent SARS-CoV-2 infection.

## Methods

**Design:** WHI observational analysis of participants with pre-pandemic leukocyte count
and later COVID/PASC survey data.

**Population:** Older/postmenopausal women in WHI.

**Exposure:** Pre-pandemic leukocyte count as a systemic inflammation marker. hsCRP was
available only for a subset and was not the primary supported signal.

**Outcome:** PASC severity among SARS-CoV-2-infected participants; occurrence and
cognitive outcomes were also evaluated.

**Covariates:** Models include demographic and health covariates; hormone therapy use is
reported as an adjustment variable rather than a primary exposure.

## Key Findings

Pre-pandemic leukocyte count predicts PASC severity but not clearly PASC occurrence or
cognitive outcome. hsCRP coverage is much thinner and was not associated in the same way.

## Relevance

For `task:t008`, this is a high-value **pre-pandemic inflammatory-state** anchor. It
supports the design claim that long-running cohorts can test whether baseline biology
precedes post-acute severity, even when the baseline is decades earlier.

For the hormone/HRT line, it does not adjudicate HRT/MHT effects. Hormone therapy appears
as a covariate, not as a staged exposure with route, dose, timing, indication, and
interaction tests.

## Limitations

1. Baseline leukocyte count was measured many years before the pandemic, so exposure
   staleness is real.
2. WHI is women-only and older; generalizability to men and younger PAIS populations is
   limited.
3. The strongest result is PASC severity, not PASC occurrence.
4. This is not a mechanistic immune-panel study; leukocyte count is a broad proxy.

## Follow-up

Use alongside `paper:Neuhouser2024` as the WHI long-COVID baseline-design pair:
Neuhouser2024 screens broad pre-pandemic predictors of long-COVID risk, while Ng2025
anchors pre-pandemic inflammatory burden against PASC severity.
