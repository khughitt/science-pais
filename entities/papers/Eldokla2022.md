## Key Contribution

This cross-sectional study is one of the larger systematic COMPASS-31 characterizations of autonomic symptom burden in long COVID, enrolling 322 patients referred to a post-COVID clinic. The main contribution relevant to this project is a direct test of sex differences in autonomic symptom severity: when COMPASS-31 total score is analyzed as a continuous measure, **male and female patients show statistically equivalent autonomic burden** (median male 28.0 vs female 26.5; p = 0.937). This null sex difference in symptom severity — in a cohort that is 73% female — provides counter-evidence to a hypothesis that the female predominance observed in POTS clinic referrals reflects higher *per-patient severity* of autonomic dysfunction in women.

## Methods

**Design:** Cross-sectional, single-center (post-COVID clinic, Assiut University Hospitals, Egypt).

**Case definition:** NICE long-COVID definition — symptoms persisting or developing ≥4 weeks after acute COVID-19 onset, with confirmed PCR and/or antibody diagnosis, ≥18 years old, symptomatic at acute phase.

**Exclusions:** Known autonomic dysfunction, neurodegenerative brain disorders, cognitive dysfunction diagnosis, or medications with major ANS effects (beta-blockers, TCAs, alpha-1 blockers, central antihypertensives).

**Instrument:** COMPASS-31 questionnaire (Composite Autonomic Symptom Score 31), a validated 31-item self-report instrument covering six domains: orthostatic intolerance (OI), vasomotor, secretomotor, gastrointestinal (GI), bladder, and pupillomotor. Total score 0–100; threshold ≥16.4 used as indicator of initial ANS dysfunction. Arabic translation validated for this study (Cronbach α = 0.85).

**Enrollment window:** August–November 2021; clinical visit for questionnaire completion October–November 2021.

**Sample size:** 356 initially identified; 322 in final analysis after exclusions (see flowchart).

**Demographics:** 73% female (n = 235), 27% male (n = 87); mean age 35.9 years (range 18–74); 90.4% with long-COVID duration >12 weeks (mean 41.9 weeks).

**Statistical approach:** Descriptive statistics; Mann–Whitney test for group comparisons on non-normal scores; Spearman's correlation for continuous associations.

## Key Findings

**Overall burden:** Median COMPASS-31 total score 26.3 (range 0–76.7); 76.7% exceeded the ≥16.4 threshold.

**Domain prevalence (proportion with any positive score in that domain):**
- Gastrointestinal: 91.6%
- Secretomotor: 76.4%
- Orthostatic intolerance: 73.6%
- Pupillomotor: 68.3%
- Bladder: 44.4%
- Vasomotor: 19.9%

**Sex comparison (load-bearing for this project):**
- COMPASS-31 total score: male median 28.0 (range 0–57.0) vs female 26.5 (range 0–76.7), **p = 0.937** — no significant difference.
- COMPASS-31 score ≥16.4 prevalence: 77.0% male vs 76.6% female, **p = 0.938** — no significant difference.
- Neither the continuous score nor the binary threshold showed any sex difference by either metric.

**Duration effect:** Positive correlation between COMPASS-31 total score and long-COVID duration (r = 0.263, p < 0.001). Patients with COMPASS-31 ≥16.4 had significantly longer long-COVID duration than those below threshold (mean 46.2 vs 26.8 weeks, p < 0.001).

**Age effects:**
- COMPASS-31 total score not significantly correlated with age (r = 0.011, p = 0.842).
- OI subdomain positively correlated with age (r = 0.159, p = 0.004).
- GI subdomain negatively correlated with age (r = −0.227, p < 0.001).
- COMPASS-31 ≥16.4 prevalence did not differ significantly between age <40 and ≥40 groups (74.4% vs 81.1%, p = 0.178).

## Relevance

**Direct relevance to question:0007 (mechanism of female predominance in PAIS):** This study is explicit counter-evidence against a "sex-amplified per-patient autonomic severity" hypothesis. Despite a strongly female-skewed clinical sample (73% female), the COMPASS-31 total score is statistically indistinguishable between sexes (p = 0.937). This is consistent with the interpretation that the female skew in post-COVID dysautonomia clinical literature reflects:
1. Who seeks care / gets referred to a post-COVID or dysautonomia clinic (demand-side and referral-pattern effects), and
2. Baseline demographics of established dysautonomia syndromes (POTS historically ~80% female before COVID), rather than
3. A sex-differentiated severity of autonomic symptoms per case.

The null sex difference should be flagged as a **subjective-instrument caveat**: COMPASS-31 is self-report only. It cannot distinguish female symptom-amplification reporting from absence of biological sex differences. Objective autonomic testing (tilt-table, heart rate variability, QSART) would be required to resolve this.

**Relevance to task:t018 (sex and reproductive-stage excess by subphenotype):** Eldokla2022 provides a data point specifically for the dysautonomia subphenotype. Within that subphenotype, sex excess appears to be a prevalence-in-clinic artifact rather than a per-patient severity difference when measured by the available self-report instrument.

**Broader PAIS framework:** The GI domain being the most prevalent (91.6%) and the inverse age-GI correlation warrant attention. GI involvement is compatible with both gut dysbiosis and vagal neuropathy mechanisms discussed in the project's shared-failure-mode frame.

## Limitations

1. **Cross-sectional design:** No pre-COVID baseline autonomic function data; cannot distinguish pre-existing subclinical dysautonomia from post-COVID-onset symptoms.
2. **Self-report instrument only:** COMPASS-31 reflects subjective symptom burden; reporting thresholds may differ by sex, age, education, or cultural background. No objective autonomic testing (tilt table, HRV, QSART). This is the principal caveat for the sex-comparison null result — it cannot rule out a sex difference in reporting rather than biology.
3. **Clinical referral sample:** All patients were referred to a post-COVID clinic with symptoms prompting evaluation; pre-selection bias inflates apparent prevalence relative to a population-based long-COVID cohort. The 73% female composition itself may reflect referral and care-seeking patterns rather than the underlying sex ratio of long-COVID dysautonomia.
4. **Single-center, single-country:** Egypt / Assiut University Hospitals. Demographic and cultural factors (mean age 35.9, largely young adult sample) may limit generalizability.
5. **Enrollment window August–November 2021:** Dominated by Delta-era COVID-19; pre-Omicron viral variant context.
6. **Exclusion of ANS-active medications:** While appropriate for instrument validity, excludes patients managed with beta-blockers or TCAs, possibly removing more severely affected patients.
7. **No healthy control arm:** Baseline COMPASS-31 distribution in the Egyptian population is not established here; the ≥16.4 threshold was borrowed from prior validation studies conducted in other populations.
8. **Author-confirmed case definition (NICE ≥4 weeks):** Includes both subacute (4–12 weeks) and true post-COVID syndrome (>12 weeks); pooling these may dilute findings, though 90.4% had >12 weeks duration.

## Model / Tool Availability

No computational model or code artifact released. The Arabic translation of COMPASS-31 used here has Cronbach α = 0.85 but is not separately published or deposited.

## Follow-up

- **Objective autonomic data needed:** A study pairing COMPASS-31 with tilt-table testing or HRV stratified by sex would directly test whether the null sex difference persists on objective measures.
- **Adjacent paper (Adler2024):** Adler2024 in this project covers autonomic dysfunction in PTLDS; compare domain patterns.
- **Questions raised:**
  - Does the sex-null COMPASS-31 result replicate in population-based long-COVID cohorts (not clinic-referred)?
  - Is the OI subdomain age-correlation driven by reduced cardiovascular reserve in older patients, or by a different post-COVID phenotype in older age groups?
  - Does the GI-inverse-age pattern reflect different exposure history, gut microbiome composition, or age-related differences in how GI symptoms are attributed to COVID vs baseline?
