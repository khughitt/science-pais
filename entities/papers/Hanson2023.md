# The viral origin of myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome

- **Authors:** Maureen R. Hanson
- **Year:** 2023
- **Journal:** PLOS Pathogens, vol. 19, no. 8, e1011523
- **DOI:** 10.1371/journal.ppat.1011523
- **PMCID:** PMC10434940
- **BibTeX key:** Hanson2023
- **Source:** PDF

## Key Contribution

This Pearls-format perspective argues that enteroviruses (EVs) — not diverse pathogens — are the primary viral cause of ME/CFS, marshalling epidemiological (outbreak co-occurrence with polio), clinical (Chia et al. stomach-biopsy series showing persistent EV RNA and VP1 antigen in ME/CFS tissue), and evolutionary (reactivation of latent herpesviruses as secondary phenomena) lines of evidence. The paper explicitly challenges the prevalent multi-pathogen narrative, contending it rests on over-broad case definitions and methodologically weak studies. It also argues that post-COVID ME/CFS-like illness should not be conflated with classical ME/CFS, given that SARS-CoV-2 was absent when the IOM diagnostic criteria were formulated.

## Methods

This is a narrative review / perspective (PLOS Pathogens "Pearls" format). No original data are collected; the author synthesizes published epidemiological cohort studies, clinical case series, seroprevalence data, and virology experiments. Key datasets cited:

- **Dubbo 2006 cohort:** 253 Australians followed 12 months after confirmed EBV, *Coxiella burnetii*, or Ross River virus infection; 9% met a 6-symptom "post-infective fatigue syndrome," but very few would satisfy IOM criteria for ME/CFS.
- **Norwegian influenza cohort:** ME/CFS risk >2-fold higher after flu-like illness compared to vaccinated controls; but the virus identity was unconfirmed in the majority (only 0.3% lab-confirmed versus 2.4% symptom-diagnosed for H1N1).
- **Chia et al. biopsy series (refs 17–19):** Stomach biopsies from 3 longitudinally followed ME/CFS patients with confirmed acute EV infection remained positive for EV RNA years post-onset; a larger case-control biopsy study showed significantly higher EV VP1 and RNA positivity in ME/CFS vs. controls; extracts from ME/CFS biopsies infected immunodeficient mice.
- **Incline Village outbreak (ref 30):** A detailed study documented active replication of newly discovered HHV-6 in blood post-outbreak, providing the strongest evidence for herpesvirus involvement.
- **Long COVID classification study (ref 35):** 9,764 individuals post-COVID-19 classified into 4 subgroups with 12 core symptoms (of 44 candidate); unrefreshing sleep was not among the 12, highlighting divergence from IOM ME/CFS criteria.
- **Prevalence estimates:** US claims-data prevalence 1.7–3.4 million (ref 7); pre-2020 worldwide ME/CFS estimate ~67 million (ref 8); long COVID worldwide estimate ~65 million (ref 25).

## Key Findings

1. **Specificity of the viral trigger:** The claim that "any infection can cause ME/CFS" is challenged. The Dubbo cohort met only a broad 6-symptom fatigue definition, not IOM ME/CFS criteria; the Norwegian influenza data are confounded by unconfirmed pathogen identity. A more defensible conclusion is that experiencing a flu-like illness (vaccinated or not) roughly halves ME/CFS risk compared to getting an unverified flu-like infection.

2. **Enteroviruses as primary candidates:** ME/CFS outbreaks historically co-occurred with polio outbreaks (enterovirus C family); patients lived or worked near polio patients; symptom cluster variability across outbreaks is explained by sequence diversity within the *Enterovirus* genus. EV RNA and VP1 antigen persist in stomach and other tissues years after ME/CFS onset (Chia series); biopsy extracts from ME/CFS patients can infect immunodeficient mice.

3. **Herpesviruses as secondary/reactivation phenomena:** EBV, HHV-6, and other human herpesviruses (HHVs) are reported at ME/CFS onset by many patients. Three mechanistic possibilities: (a) direct HHV causation; (b) primary HHV infection creates susceptibility to a second viral trigger; (c) HHV reactivation perpetuates illness once established. The Incline Village outbreak documented newly active HHV-6 replication, but new HHV infection is inconsistent with the epidemic's epidemiology (most adults already carry latent HHV). Reactivation during immune stress remains the most parsimonious explanation.

4. **Case definition matters critically:** ~13% of adolescents with mononucleosis met ME/CFS diagnostic criteria at 6 months; by 24 months only 4%. ME/CFS prevalence derived from broad criteria (Fukuda 1994) substantially exceeds estimates derived from strict IOM 2015 criteria.

5. **Post-COVID ME/CFS conflation is premature:** Some post-COVID patients fulfil IOM ME/CFS criteria, but SARS-CoV-2 was absent when those criteria were written and cannot be assumed to operate via the same pathways. Viral persistence of SARS-CoV-2 has been documented (refs 36–37), a phenomenon that was underappreciated for RNA viruses pre-pandemic.

6. **RNA virus persistence is under-studied:** Prior to SARS-CoV-2, the capacity of RNA viruses to persist in tissue reservoirs was largely dismissed. EVs are prime candidates for establishing such reservoirs in ME/CFS.

## Relevance

This paper is directly relevant to the project research question (research-question:post-acute-infection-syndromes) on multiple axes:

**PAIS shared failure mode:** ME/CFS exemplifies the project's core frame — a persistent, disabling syndrome that follows an acute viral trigger and does not resolve through normal homeostatic recovery. Hanson's argument that viral persistence (EV reservoirs in gut, heart, muscle, brain) may underlie the chronicity maps precisely onto the antigen/pathogen-persistence mechanism in the project's shared PAIS failure-mode taxonomy.

**Pathogen-specificity vs. shared mechanism:** The paper's challenge to the "any pathogen" narrative introduces a productive tension: if enteroviruses are disproportionately responsible for ME/CFS, the PAIS frame must accommodate both a shared downstream syndrome and possibly pathogen-specific upstream triggers. This informs how the project should model the relationship between trigger identity and failure mode.

**Viral persistence as immune dysregulation driver:** If EVs persist in tissue reservoirs long after acute infection, ongoing innate immune sensing (e.g., pattern recognition of viral RNA) could drive the chronic immune activation and metabolic dysfunction characteristic of PAIS. This connects to the immune-homeostasis frame in the peer project health-immunity.

**Long COVID / ME/CFS relationship:** The paper urges caution about conflating long COVID with ME/CFS, and notes that ~65 million long COVID sufferers roughly mirrors the ~67 million estimated ME/CFS sufferers worldwide — underscoring the project's framing of PAIS as a major, under-resourced public health problem. At the same time, Hanson's call for careful categorical separation is a methodological warning for the project's cross-PAIS comparisons: case definitions must be held constant when comparing syndromes.

**Herpesvirus reactivation:** The reactivation hypothesis (HHVs reactivate under immune stress caused by a primary viral insult) is a secondary-disruption mechanism that fits the project's idea of cascading homeostatic failures — the original infection destabilises immune regulation, which in turn permits latent pathogen reactivation, which further sustains dysregulation.

## Limitations

1. **Narrative review, no systematic methods:** No PRISMA-style search, no quantitative synthesis; evidence selection reflects the author's argument. Studies supporting EV involvement are foregrounded; evidence against is engaged selectively.

2. **Enterovirus causal claim is inferential:** The Chia biopsy series is compelling but small and not independently replicated at scale. Mouse infectivity with biopsy extracts does not confirm that the same pathogen caused the original ME/CFS. Full EV genome sequences from these biopsies are not available.

3. **Absence of cadaveric tissue:** The paper acknowledges the lack of available cadaver samples from ME/CFS patients, which severely limits histopathological investigation of chronic EV infection in brain, spinal cord, and myocardium.

4. **Serological evidence is uninformative:** Because EV infections are near-universal, and because most ME/CFS patients have been ill for years, serology cannot distinguish case from control — a recognised limitation the paper notes but cannot resolve.

5. **Unknown EV variants:** The hypothesis that an "uncommon variant" of an EV drives ME/CFS is plausible but untestable without full genome data from outbreak patients. The same unknown-variant problem applies to sporadic cases.

6. **Conflation risk in EBV/HHV discussion:** Three distinct HHV mechanisms are proposed (direct cause, susceptibility primer, reactivation perpetuator), and the paper does not clearly delineate which evidence supports which. Anti-herpesviral drug trials cited (valacyclovir, valganciclovir) are small and not randomised at scale.

7. **Long COVID section is speculative:** The assertion that pre-2020 ME/CFS patients could not have been infected by SARS-CoV-2 is definitionally true but does not resolve whether the downstream mechanisms overlap; the paper provides no mechanistic analysis of this question.

- **Stein et al. 2022** (ref 36, Nature) and **Chen et al. 2023** (ref 37, eLife): SARS-CoV-2 tissue persistence and long COVID — viral persistence in a well-characterised post-viral syndrome, providing a methodological template for EV persistence studies.
