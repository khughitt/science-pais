# Functional and morphological differences of muscle mitochondria in chronic fatigue syndrome and post-COVID syndrome

- **Authors:** Daniel Alexander Bizjak, Birgit Ohmayer, Jasmine Leonike Buhl, Elisabeth Marion Schneider, Paul Walther, Enrico Calzia, Achim Jerg, Lynn Matits, Juergen Michael Steinacker
- **Year:** 2024
- **Journal:** International Journal of Molecular Sciences, 25(3), 1675
- **DOI:** 10.3390/ijms25031675
- **PMID:** 38338957
- **PMCID:** PMC10855807
- **BibTeX key:** Bizjak2024
- **Source:** PubMed record

## Key Contribution

This is the most direct published CFS-vs-post-COVID muscle-biopsy comparison found in the t056 pass. It measures mitochondrial OXPHOS capacity in permeabilized muscle fibers and mitochondrial morphology/content by electron microscopy. The result is useful for the cross-trigger question because CFS and post-COVID syndrome both show fatigue and muscle-mitochondrial abnormalities, but their specific abnormalities are not identical.

## Methods

- **Design:** case-control muscle-biopsy study comparing healthy controls, CFS, and post-COVID syndrome.
- **Functional readout:** high-resolution respirometry of permeabilized muscle fibers for mitochondrial OXPHOS capacity.
- **Morphology readout:** electron microscopy of subsarcolemmal and intermyofibrillar mitochondria, including cristae integrity, size, perimeter, area, and mitochondrial content.

## Key Findings

- Muscle mitochondrial OXPHOS capacity was impaired across patient groups, with complex-I activity specifically reduced in post-COVID syndrome compared with healthy controls.
- CFS and post-COVID syndrome differed morphologically: post-COVID syndrome showed larger subsarcolemmal mitochondrial measures than CFS, while intermyofibrillar size/content variables did not differ similarly.
- Cristae integrity was higher in post-COVID syndrome than CFS in both intermyofibrillar and subsarcolemmal mitochondria.
- The authors interpret the CFS morphology as more progressed structural alteration, potentially from prolonged inactivity or other molecular causes, whereas the post-COVID complex-I reduction suggests a more direct virus-linked alteration.

## Relevance

For `task:t056`, this shows that ME/CFS/CFS has a direct skeletal-muscle mitochondrial phenotype and that the muscle endpoint can be compared with post-COVID syndrome. But it also cautions against a simple "same lesion" reading: the CFS and post-COVID muscle phenotypes differ in kind, and the study is resting-state rather than exercise-provoked. It therefore supports an interim muscle-localization proposition while preserving h0008-M3's warning that endpoint and protocol choice can change the apparent cross-trigger answer.

## Limitations

- Resting biopsy, not post-exertional biopsy and not a PEM time-course.
- PubMed abstract does not provide all subgroup sample counts and phenotype details; the PMC full text was blocked by reCAPTCHA during this pass.
- CFS-vs-post-COVID morphology is not the same comparison as Appelman2024 long-COVID pre/post-PEM OXPHOS/SDH.
- Interpretation is vulnerable to disease-duration and inactivity confounding, which the authors themselves raise.
