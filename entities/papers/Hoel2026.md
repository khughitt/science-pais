# Charting the circulating proteome in ME/CFS using cross-system profiling to uncover mechanistic insights

- **Authors:** August Hoel, Fredrik Hoel, Sissel Elisabeth Dyrstad, Henrique Chapola, Ingrid Gurvin Rekeland, Kristin Risa, Kine Alme, Kari Sørland, Karl Albert Brokstad, Hans-Peter Marti, Olav Mella, Øystein Fluge, Karl Johan Tronstad
- **Year:** 2026
- **Journal:** Cell Reports Medicine, vol. 7, no. 3, p. 102647
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.xcrm.2026.102647
- **BibTeX key:** Hoel2026
- **Source:** PDF

## Key Contribution

This is the largest aptamer-based serum proteomics study of ME/CFS to date (50 patients vs. 29 healthy controls; 7,326 protein targets via SomaScan v4.1 7k), providing a comprehensive cross-system characterization of the circulating proteome. The study identifies 1,823 aptamers with significant group differences (845 after FDR correction), mapping these to tissue-level effects (reduced intracellular protein release from skeletal muscle, brain, and bone marrow; increased secreted and membrane proteins from brain, liver, intestine, and lymphoid tissue), immune cell signatures (broad reduction in neutrophil/granulocyte-derived proteins, increased lymphoid cell proteins), and secretome changes (elevated coagulation factors, complement components, chemokines, cytokines, and hormones). The findings define a multisystem molecular landscape that supports failed immune and metabolic homeostasis as a core pathophysiology of ME/CFS — a condition that frequently follows acute infection.

## Methods

**Study design:** Cross-sectional case-control comparison. Serum samples from 50 ME/CFS patients (Canadian Consensus Criteria; collected at baseline from two clinical intervention trials: RituxME, NCT02229942, 2014–2017, n=15 rituximab arm; and CycloME, NCT02444091, 2015–2020, n=21 responders + n=14 non-responders) compared to 29 sex- and age-matched healthy controls. Eleven ME/CFS patients were overnight fasted at sampling; analyses were covariate-adjusted for sex, age, BMI, and fasting status.

**Cohort characteristics:** 50 ME/CFS (80% female, mean age 40.4 ± 10.1), 29 HC (72% female, mean age 38.4 ± 9.3). Triacylglycerols (TAG) and non-esterified fatty acids (NEFA) were significantly elevated in ME/CFS (TAG: p=0.0254; NEFA: p=0.0262). Mean SF-36PF 31.2 ± 13.9 in ME/CFS (vs. 16.6 ± 7.7 self-reported PF).

**Aptamer proteomics:** SomaScan v4.1 7k platform targeting 7,326 aptamers → 6,493 protein targets → 6,408 proteins by Entrez gene symbol. For 786 proteins with ≥2 aptamers, Pearson correlation and directional concordance were assessed; 41.6% showed moderate-to-strong correlation (|R| > 0.3); 97.8% of statistically significant aptamer pairs showed directional concordance.

**Statistical analyses:** Univariate (Welch's t test, both uncorrected p-values and FDR q-values), multivariate (PCA, PERM-ANOVA), and covariate analysis. PERM-ANOVA confirmed global group separation (F=14.0, R²=0.15, p=0.001). Logistic regression with AUC, sensitivity, and specificity reported for selected proteins.

**Cross-system annotation:** Subcellular compartment, tissue expression, immune cell type, and secretome annotations applied using Human Protein Atlas (HPA) and a curated human secretome database. MitoCarta 3.0 used for mitochondrial proteins. GO and Reactome gene set enrichment on ranked univariate statistics.

**Secretome analysis:** Protein-centric human secretome database covering 2,100 aptamers (1,657 proteins); functional protein classes classified across 9 secretion compartments and multiple functional subclasses.

**Validation:** Luminex panel of 77 proteins (54 retained after QC) and ELISA measurements of FGF-21, GDF-15, and C-peptide in larger cohorts (n=212 ME/CFS, n=66 HC). Concordance with two independent SomaScan datasets (Germain et al. and Walitt et al.) confirmed.

**Metabotype stratification:** Three previously defined metabolic phenotypes (M1: lipolytic/ketogenic; M2: lipid accumulation; M3: metabolically similar to controls) used as covariate subgroup for selected analyses.

## Key Findings

**Overall scale of proteome changes:**
- 1,823 aptamers (targeting 1,723 unique proteins) differed significantly at p < 0.05; 845 aptamers (811 proteins) remained significant after FDR correction (q < 0.05).
- 60.7% of altered aptamers showed lower abundance in ME/CFS (73.3% at q < 0.05), indicating a net downward shift in the serum proteome.

**Subcellular compartment analysis (of 1,823 altered aptamers):**
- Intracellular proteins (1,146 altered): 74.9% (858) decreased, 25.1% (288) increased.
- Membrane-associated proteins (353 altered): 40.8% (144) decreased, 59.2% (209) increased.
- Secreted proteins (269 altered): 33.5% (90) decreased, 66.5% (179) increased.
- Kolmogorov-Smirnov test confirmed systematic shift: intracellular proteins reduced (D=0.080, p=1.6×10⁻¹⁴; HL=−0.18 [−0.22 to −0.14]), secreted proteins increased (D=0.123, p=7.7×10⁻¹⁵; HL=+0.27 [0.22–0.33]).

**Proteins among the most reduced (intracellular/neutrophil-derived):**
- Nuclear histones H1x, H2.1, H2B and H2A types — markers of neutrophil extracellular trap (NET) formation by activated neutrophils.
- Glycolytic enzymes: GAPDH, ALDOA (fructose-bisphosphate aldolase A), LDHB (L-lactate dehydrogenase B chain).
- Vascular/hypoxia-related: VIM (vimentin), ANTXR2/CMG2 (capillary morphogenesis gene 2), CXADR, ATP5IF1.
- Neuronal/structural: TAGLN3 (transgelin-3), COMP (cartilage oligomeric matrix protein).

**Proteins most elevated (secreted/immune):**
- Immune: MCTS1 (malignant T-cell amplified sequence 1), complement factor D (CFD), complement component C6 (C6), IL-26.
- Metabolic/stress: FABP3, FABP4 (fatty acid-binding proteins 3 and 4), CRABP2 (cellular retinoic acid-binding protein 2), RRARRES2 (chemerin).
- Vascular: LIPG (endothelial lipase).
- Endoplasmic reticulum: ERP29, TMED9.

**Tissue-specific footprints (Figure 4):**
- 1,670 aptamers targeting 1,428 proteins in HPA tissue panels; 20.9% (n=299, p<0.05) differed between groups.
- Skeletal muscle: most pronounced reductions in intracellular proteins (−19.8% overall; −20.7% intracellular), all affected secreted proteins elevated.
- Bone marrow: strong overall reduction (−25.4%), particularly intracellular (−34.6% down) and secreted (−27.3% down) — distinct profile from other tissues.
- Brain, liver, intestine, and lymphoid tissue: modest, bidirectional changes with net increases in secreted proteins.
- Interpretation: reduced intracellular protein release (particularly from skeletal muscle and bone marrow) alongside broadly increased secretion across multiple tissues.

**Immune cell type signatures (Figure 4D):**
- Granulocytes showed 70.3% lowered aptamers; monocytes showed 64.6% lowered aptamers — primarily innate-immune-skewed reduction.
- NK cells: 72.0% elevated aptamers. T cells: 62.5% elevated aptamers.
- Neutrophil-specific validation: 146 of 254 published PMA-activated neutrophil proteins were detected; 52.3% (90 at p<0.05; 62 at q<0.05) differed between groups, with >85% (79 aptamers at p<0.05; 57 at q<0.05) showing lower levels in ME/CFS.
- Key neutrophil proteins reduced: BPI, PADI4, MMP9, ELANE, PRTN3, AZU1, LTF.
- Normal granulocyte/neutrophil counts (3.38 ± 1.03 × 10⁹/L, normal range 1.5–7.3 × 10⁹/L) — the suppressed neutrophil signature is not explained by neutropenia.

**Secretome changes (Figure 5):**
- 454 aptamers at p<0.05 (185 at q<0.05) differed among 2,100 secretome aptamers (1,657 proteins); 61.2% increased at p<0.05; 54.6% at q<0.05.
- Blood proteins (197/838 aptamers significant, 124 elevated): coagulation factors elevated (F7, F5, F9, FGG, PLAU); complement elevated (C3, C30, APOA5, APOD, CHFR1).
- Inflammatory signaling: chemokines/cytokines elevated (CCL13, CCL14, CCL15, IL38A, IL31, IL22, IL26); various cytokines and complement factors up.
- Hormones increased; most enzymes decreased.
- Ligand-receptor interaction analysis: Ephrin subfamily A receptors (EPHA1, EPHA2, EPHA4, EPHA7) and their EFNA protein ligands showed concordant changes, consistent with previously reported EPHA-EFNA signaling involvement in ME/CFS.

**Vascular dysregulation and hypoxia:**
- Reduced VIM, ANTXR2, CXADR, ATP5IF1 indicate impaired endothelial regulation and mitochondrial stress protection under oxygen deprivation.
- Increased LIPG (immune-activation-linked endothelial lipase) and FAM20A (hematopoietic signaling factor).
- Increased WASF3 expression: a target of HIF1A transcription factor, consistent with tissue hypoxia.

**Metabolic stress markers:**
- GDF-15 (growth differentiation factor 15), FGF-21, resistin, leptin, and HGF elevated — these are stress hormones also elevated in mitochondrial myopathies, type 2 diabetes, and hypoxia.
- ELISA in expanded cohort (n=212 ME/CFS, n=66 HC): FGF-21 significantly higher in ME/CFS in women (not men); highest in metabolic subgroup M2. C-peptide and GDF-15 showed no overall group difference but both higher in M2.
- FABP4 elevated in both metabolic subgroups M1 and M2 — functions as fatty acid stress messenger.

**Antibody-based validation:**
- Luminex: 70.4% of 54 proteins showed concordant changes between aptamer and Luminex platforms; 69.1% of platform-level comparisons showed significant positive correlation (p<0.05); none showed negative correlation.
- Cross-study concordance: 122 aptamers overlapped with Germain et al. dataset (391 altered) — all three studies (Germain, Walitt, this study) consistently pointed to immune-related dysregulation.

## Relevance

This paper is directly relevant to the project research question (research-question:post-acute-infection-syndromes) in multiple ways:

**ME/CFS as a PAIS archetype:** ME/CFS is explicitly framed in the introduction as a condition "often triggered by infections" and recognized as part of the broader category of post-acute infection syndromes. 76% of the ME/CFS patients had a documented infection history (Table 1). The molecular landscape documented here thus represents the chronic-phase proteome of a canonical PAIS, providing a benchmark for the "failed homeostatic recovery" state.

**Failed homeostatic recovery:** The serum proteome in ME/CFS shows a pattern of sustained multisystem imbalance rather than resolved, homeostatic biology:
- The net reduction of intracellular proteins (especially from skeletal muscle, bone marrow, and brain) is inconsistent with normal tissue homeostasis and suggests altered tissue integrity or reduced cellular turnover/release.
- The broadly elevated secretome (coagulation, complement, chemokines, hormones) reflects persistently elevated secretory signaling — the opposite of return-to-baseline after acute infection.
- The chronically elevated exerkines (FGF-21, GDF-15) — which normally transiently spike during exercise — indicate a persistent energy stress signal that would be expected to resolve during healthy recovery.

**Immune reprogramming as sustained post-infection state:** The pronounced and specific reduction in neutrophil/granulocyte-derived proteins in the face of normal blood counts indicates functional immune reprogramming that persists into the chronic phase. The paper explicitly states this "may reflect a disturbed immune homeostasis." Elevated complement factors CFD and C6, implicated in vasculitis and autoimmune nephritis, align with autoimmune-like mechanisms proposed for PAISs.

**Shared features with long COVID:** The paper directly references long COVID as a related condition sharing complement activation, immune-vascular dysregulation, mitochondrial pathway changes, and aberrant immune regulation. The serum proteome patterns identified may generalize to other post-infectious states.

**Relevance to project hypotheses:** The data supports multiple PAIS-relevant mechanistic tracks: autoimmune-like neutrophil dysregulation, vascular/endothelial dysfunction, metabolic energy failure, and multi-organ tissue signal dysregulation — each a potential failure mode on the path from acute infection resolution to homeostatic recovery.

## Limitations

- **Sample size:** 50 ME/CFS and 29 HC is modest, though comparable to other ME/CFS omics studies. Replication in larger, independent cohorts is needed.
- **Case definition:** ME/CFS samples from two specific clinical trial biobanks (RituxME, CycloME) — potential selection bias toward patients who were enrolled in intervention trials. The cyclophosphamide responder subgroup (n=21) may not be representative of general ME/CFS.
- **Cross-sectional design:** Cannot establish temporal relationships or distinguish whether protein changes reflect cause, consequence, or epiphenomenon of ME/CFS.
- **Aptamer platform limitations:** SomaScan is semi-quantitative, less precise than mass spectrometry or antibody platforms for isoforms, proteoforms, and post-translational modifications. 41.6% of multi-aptamer proteins showed only weak/no inter-aptamer correlation, reflecting epitope/domain-specific binding.
- **Serum vs. tissue:** Serum proteome reflects a composite of active secretion, passive cell leakage, and washout from multiple organs — direct tissue mechanistic interpretation requires caution.
- **Contextual protein function:** Protein annotations (tissue, compartment) are not strictly specific to one context; alternative interpretations exist.
- **No causal demonstration:** The observed immune, vascular, and metabolic patterns are associative; the paper explicitly does not establish direct causality.
- **Fasting confounder:** 22% of ME/CFS patients were fasted overnight (none of HC); though covariate-adjusted, this asymmetry limits interpretability for some metabolic signals.
- **Sex imbalance:** 80% female ME/CFS vs. 72% female HC; analyses were sex-adjusted but sex differences in the proteome may not be fully captured.

## Model / Tool Availability

- **Proteomics data:** Available as supplemental information; original dataset deposited in PRIDE repository (PAD000026).
- **No novel tool:** The study uses existing SomaScan v4.1 7k platform (SomaLogic) and standard bioinformatics tools. No new software was released.
- **Validated proteins:** Luminex panel data and ELISA data available in supplemental materials.

## Follow-up

- Germain et al. and Walitt et al. SomaScan ME/CFS datasets (reanalyzed in this paper) — read for comparison of proteome findings and any differences in case definition or cohort characteristics.
- Studies on neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) and ANCA-associated vasculitis mechanisms — the neutrophil histone reduction and complement factor elevation point directly to this literature.
- Long COVID proteomics papers (particularly complement activation and coagulation findings cited here) — to compare chronic-phase proteome patterns across PAIS types.
- EPHA-EFNA signaling literature in ME/CFS context cited by the paper — potential mechanistic pathway for vascular and inflammatory dysregulation.
- Whether the metabolic subtype (M1/M2/M3) classification maps to clinical or molecular subgroups in other PAISs (long COVID, PTLDS) — important for cross-PAIS biomarker work.
