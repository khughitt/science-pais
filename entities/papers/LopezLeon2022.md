# Long-COVID in children and adolescents: a systematic review and meta-analyses

## Key Contribution

Early systematic review/meta-analysis establishing that persistent symptoms after SARS-CoV-2 infection
were reported in children and adolescents, while also documenting the severe heterogeneity and
case-definition limitations of early pediatric long-COVID literature.

## Methods

- **Design:** PRISMA systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis.
- **Search:** PubMed and Embase through February 10, 2022.
- **Eligibility:** observational studies with at least 30 participants aged 0-18 meeting a NICE-style
  long-COVID definition.
- **Included corpus:** 21 studies and 80,071 children/adolescents.

## Key Findings

- Pooled prevalence of at least one symptom more than 4 weeks after infection was 25.24%, but
  heterogeneity was extreme.
- Common pooled symptoms included mood symptoms, fatigue, sleep disorders, headache, respiratory symptoms,
  cognitive symptoms, appetite loss, exercise intolerance, and altered smell.
- Compared with controls, persistent dyspnea, anosmia/ageusia, and fever were more clearly elevated than
  many nonspecific symptoms.
- The authors emphasized lack of standardized definitions, recall/selection/misclassification bias,
  nonresponse/loss to follow-up, and heterogeneous controls.

## Relevance

Useful as a baseline map of early pediatric long-COVID reports and a warning that pediatric prevalence
numbers are not portable across definitions, timepoints, and control designs.

## Limitations

- Early-pandemic literature, before RECOVER pediatric indices and longer follow-up.
- High heterogeneity and control-definition inconsistency.
- Prevalence estimates are less reliable than later cohort-based phenotype/trajectory studies.
