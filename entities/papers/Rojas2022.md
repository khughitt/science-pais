# Autoimmunity is a hallmark of post-COVID syndrome

- **Authors:** Manuel Rojas, Yhojan Rodríguez, Yeny Acosta-Ampudia, Diana M. Monsalve, Chengsong Zhu, Quan-Zhen Li, Carolina Ramírez-Santana, Juan-Manuel Anaya
- **Year:** 2022
- **Journal:** Journal of Translational Medicine, vol. 20, article 129
- **DOI:** 10.1186/s12967-022-03328-4
- **BibTeX key:** Rojas2022
- **Source:** PDF

## Key Contribution

This clinical and serological commentary establishes that latent autoimmunity and polyautoimmunity are near-universal features of post-COVID syndrome (PCS), present in 83% and 62% of patients respectively using a broad antigen array (116 IgG + 104 IgM targets). Anti-SARS-CoV-2 IgG antibodies against spike (S1, S2, RBD) were found in >85% of PCS patients and were positively correlated with self-reactive autoantibody levels, suggesting bystander activation as a mechanistic driver. Three of 100 patients developed overt autoimmune diseases (polymyositis, SLE, autoimmune thyroid disease) within seven months of follow-up.

## Methods

Cross-sectional clinical and serological study conducted March 18 – May 20, 2021, at the Clínica del Occidente post-COVID unit, Bogotá, Colombia. **Inclusion:** adults >18 years with PCR-confirmed SARS-CoV-2 infection and persistent or new symptoms after four weeks of acute illness; none vaccinated at time of study. **N = 100 PCS patients** (median age 49 years, IQR 37.8–55.3; 47 male). Pre-pandemic healthy individuals served as control group for the antigen array. Serum antibody profiles were assessed using a commercial antigen array chip measuring 116 IgG and 104 IgM antibodies against self-antigens and infectious agents; a threshold of NFI >2 SD above the pre-pandemic control mean defined positivity. After quality-control filtering, 116 IgG and 104 IgM probes were retained. Correlations were assessed by Spearman test; P < 0.05 was the significance threshold. Latent autoimmunity = ≥1 IgG autoantibody positive; polyautoimmunity (PolyA) = ≥2 IgG autoantibodies positive. Clinical case definition used: symptoms persisting or emerging after four weeks of acute illness (consistent with WHO PCS definition). Median post-COVID time at evaluation was 219 days (IQR 143–258). During acute COVID-19, 65/100 patients were hospitalized, 37% in ICU.

## Key Findings

**Autoantibody prevalence**
- Latent autoimmunity (≥1 IgG autoantibody) found in **83%** of PCS patients.
- Polyautoimmunity (≥2 IgG autoantibodies) found in **62%** of PCS patients.
- Most prevalent IgG autoantibodies (Table 1): Anti-CD8B (18.0%), Anti-IL-2 (10.0%), Anti-Tg (14.0%), Anti-CENP-B/A (9.0%), multiple anti-IFN subtypes (6–9% each).
- IgM positivity was generally low; subsequent analyses focused on IgG.

**Anti-viral vs. self-reactive antibody correlations**
- Anti-SARS-CoV-2 IgG found in >85% of patients for spike S1, S2, and RBD.
- Most IgG autoantibodies were positively correlated with anti-SARS-CoV-2 IgG (Spearman, P < 0.0500; Fig. 1C) — consistent with bystander activation rather than molecular mimicry.
- Fewer autoantibodies were correlated with anti-nucleocapsid protein (NCP) IgG antibodies.

**Effect of age, sex, and BMI**
- Age and BMI were correlated with IgG anti-SARS-CoV-2 (NCP β=1.2041, P=0.0104; S1 β=0.0239, P=0.0239), but had almost no association with autoantibody expression (only a few anti-IFN antibodies were weakly correlated).
- Male sex was associated with higher IgG anti-SARS-CoV-2 NCP and S1 antibody levels, but there was **no effect of sex on the over- or under-expression of autoantibodies**.
- Anti-IFN-λ IgG antibodies were associated with persistence of respiratory symptoms (Fisher's exact test, P=0.0051).

**Overt autoimmune disease**
- Three patients (3%) developed overt autoimmune diseases during seven months of follow-up: one polymyositis, one SLE, one autoimmune thyroid disease.
- The authors note that the transition from latent to overt autoimmunity may take years.

**Interferon autoantibodies**
- Multiple anti-IFN subtypes (IFN-α, -β, -γ, -λ, and others) were detected in 5–10% of patients.
- Anti-IFN-λ IgG specifically associated with respiratory symptom persistence.

## Relevance

This paper directly supports the PAIS framework of **failed immune homeostatic recovery after acute infection**. Key connections:

1. **Immune non-resolution as a PAIS hallmark:** The high prevalence of latent autoimmunity (83%) at a median of 219 days post-infection indicates that acute SARS-CoV-2 triggers self-reactive immune responses that do not resolve within the expected recovery window. This is a concrete immune-level failure of homeostatic recovery — the immune system remains in an abnormal activated state targeting self-antigens well after viral clearance.

2. **Bystander activation as mechanism:** The correlation between anti-SARS-CoV-2 humoral response and autoantibody load (Fig. 1C) points to bystander activation — a mechanism shared across PAIS (implicated in ME/CFS and post-Lyme). This supports the hypothesis that the magnitude of the acute immune response, not just pathogen-specific properties, drives post-infectious autoimmunity.

3. **Latent-to-overt autoimmunity trajectory:** Three of 100 patients developed overt autoimmune disease within 7 months; the authors hypothesize that latent autoimmunity may precede clinical manifestation by years. For the PAIS frame, this suggests that PAIS may not merely be a transient state but a precursor to durable immunopathology.

4. **Anti-IFN antibodies and symptom persistence:** Anti-IFN-λ associated with respiratory symptom persistence provides a mechanistic bridge between autoimmunity and the perpetuation of specific PAIS symptoms — relevant to the research question (research-question:post-acute-infection-syndromes) of why specific symptom clusters persist.

5. **Sex-independence of autoantibody burden:** The absence of sex differences in autoantibody expression (despite sex differences in anti-viral antibodies) is notable because PAIS conditions show female predominance; this suggests autoantibody-mediated pathology is not sufficient alone to explain sex skewing.

## Limitations

1. **No healthy post-COVID control group:** The control group for the antigen array was pre-pandemic healthy individuals, not individuals who recovered fully from COVID-19. This makes it impossible to determine whether the autoantibody profile is specific to PCS or is a general post-SARS-CoV-2 immune response in all infected individuals.

2. **Cross-sectional design (primarily):** Single time-point serology (median 219 days post-COVID) cannot establish whether autoantibodies are rising, plateauing, or resolving. The three overt autoimmune disease cases come from follow-up, but formal longitudinal autoantibody kinetics are not presented.

3. **Small sample, single-center, single-country:** N=100 from one post-COVID clinic in Bogotá, Colombia; selection bias is likely — clinic patients may overrepresent more severe or persistent cases. Generalizability to milder PCS or other populations is unclear.

4. **Commentary format / no pre-registered protocol:** The article is published as a "Commentary" with methods in supplementary files; statistical analysis plan and pre-registration are not described.

5. **Autoantibody threshold definition:** Positivity defined as NFI >2 SD above pre-pandemic healthy controls; this is a generous threshold that may inflate "latent autoimmunity" prevalence, and clinical significance of single low-titer autoantibodies is uncertain.

6. **Vaccination status:** No patients were vaccinated at time of study (early 2021). The autoimmune findings may not reflect the post-vaccination era PCS population.

7. **Overt autoimmune disease event rate:** Only three overt cases in 100 patients over 7 months — numbers are too small to quantify risk or identify predictors.

## Follow-up

- **Acosta-Ampudia et al. (2022)** — companion paper from same group on persistent autoimmune activation and proinflammatory state in PCS (ref [1] in this paper); should be read alongside.
- **Liu et al. (2021)** — J Transl Med, 19:524 — sex-specific patterns of autoantibody response in SARS-CoV-2 infection including in non-severe disease (directly cited for bystander activation evidence).
- **Anaya et al. (2021)** — J Transl Autoimmun, latent rheumatic, thyroid and phospholipid autoimmunity in hospitalized COVID-19 (same group, acute-phase autoimmunity); complements this PCS paper.
- Whether anti-IFN autoantibodies in PCS mechanistically drive symptom persistence (vs. merely correlating) deserves direct experimental follow-up.
- Longitudinal studies needed to track the latent → overt autoimmunity conversion rate over >2 years in PCS.
