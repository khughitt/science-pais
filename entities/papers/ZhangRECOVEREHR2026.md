# Long COVID associated with SARS-CoV-2 reinfection among children and adolescents in the omicron era

## Key Contribution

Large RECOVER-EHR pediatric cohort showing that reinfection during the Omicron era is associated with
higher risk of clinician-coded PASC and PASC-related symptoms/conditions in children and adolescents.

## Methods

- **Design:** retrospective EHR cohort.
- **Setting:** 40 US children's hospitals and health institutions participating in RECOVER.
- **Participants:** 465,717 children/adolescents younger than 21; 407,300 first-infection episodes and
  58,417 second-infection episodes.
- **Exposure:** second SARS-CoV-2 infection at least 60 days after first infection.
- **Outcome:** clinician-documented PASC diagnosis code U09.9 plus 24 PASC-related symptoms/conditions.
- **Analysis:** modified Poisson models with demographic, clinical, and health-care-utilization
  adjustment using matching approaches.

## Key Findings

- PASC diagnosis incidence per million people per 6 months was about twice as high after a second
  infection as after a first infection.
- Reinfection associated with increased PASC diagnosis risk and elevated risks across multiple symptom
  and condition codes, including fatigue/malaise, headache, abdominal pain, POTS/dysautonomia, cognitive
  impairment, myocarditis, thromboembolism, and kidney/cardiovascular outcomes.

## Relevance

This is the pediatric counterpart to the project's reinfection-risk synthesis. It supports the conclusion
that repeated SARS-CoV-2 exposures add pediatric PAIS burden, even though the mechanism remains mixed.

## Limitations

- EHR-coded PASC is specific to documented clinical care and may miss undiagnosed illness.
- Reinfection identification depends on testing/coding capture.
- The design supports risk association, not a mechanism-specific chronic-loop claim.
