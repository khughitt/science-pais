# Global prevalence and risk factors of fatigue and post-infectious fatigue among patients with dengue: a systematic review and meta-analysis

- **Authors:** Nuzul Sri Hertanti, Trung V. Nguyen, Yeu-Hui Chuang
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal:** eClinicalMedicine, vol. 80, p. 103041 (published online 31 December 2024)
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.eclinm.2024.103041
- **BibTeX key:** Hertanti2025
- **Source:** PDF

## Key Contribution

This is the first systematic review and meta-analysis to quantify the global prevalence of both acute-phase fatigue and post-infectious fatigue (PIF) among dengue patients and to identify their risk factors. Pooled across 40 studies (38,406 dengue patients), approximately 59% experience fatigue during the acute phase and 20% go on to develop PIF — fatigue persisting 2 weeks to 6 months or longer after the acute illness. Female sex, dengue hemorrhagic fever (DHF), and preexisting comorbidities emerged as significant risk factors for PIF, framing post-dengue fatigue as a clinically important, under-recognised post-acute infection syndrome.

## Methods

- **Design:** PRISMA-compliant systematic review and meta-analysis; registered in PROSPERO (CRD42024543058).
- **Search:** Five databases (PubMed, Ovid MEDLINE, Web of Science, Embase, CINAHL) searched from inception to 22 June 2024; no language or publication-year restrictions.
- **Inclusion:** Observational studies (cross-sectional, prospective, retrospective) reporting prevalence of fatigue or PIF in dengue patients; case studies, reviews, conference abstracts, protocols, and duplicate publications excluded.
- **Quality assessment:** Hoy's risk of bias tool (10 items; scores: low risk 8–10, moderate 5–7, high 0–4); two independent raters.
- **Fatigue definition:** Subjective tiredness/weakness impacting normal functioning, assessed during the acute phase (typically 2–7 days). PIF defined as fatigue persisting beyond the acute phase, lasting 2 weeks to 6 months or longer.
- **Statistical analysis:** Random-effects model (R v4.3.3, `meta` and `metafor` packages); arcsine square-root transformation for non-normality; heterogeneity assessed via Cochran's Q and I²; subgroup analysis and mixed-effects meta-regression (REML, Hartung-Knapp adjustment) for moderators (continent, study design, fatigue measure); sensitivity analysis by excluding high-risk-of-bias studies and studies with n < 50; publication bias via funnel plots and Egger's test.
- **Risk factor analysis:** Odd ratios (ORs) with 95% CIs pooled only when ≥ 2 studies reported the same factor.

## Key Findings

### Prevalence
- **Acute-phase fatigue:** Pooled prevalence = **59.0%** (95% CI 0.47–0.70; 37 studies, 37,790 patients; Q = 32026.88, I² = 99.90%). Sensitivity analysis excluding high-risk studies: 58.0% (95% CI 0.43–0.71); excluding n < 50 studies: 61.0% (95% CI 0.48–0.74) — consistent.
- **Post-infectious fatigue (PIF):** Pooled prevalence = **20.0%** (95% CI 0.10–0.36; 9 studies, 5045 patients; Q = 649.05, I² = 98.77%). Sensitivity analyses yielded 21.0% (95% CI 0.09–0.41) and a range of 18.0–27.0%, all within the 95% CI of the main estimate.

### Moderators of PIF heterogeneity
- **Study design:** Cross-sectional studies reported higher PIF prevalence (39.7%) than other designs (18.2%).
- **Mean age** (β = 0.24, 95% CI 0.01–0.48, p = 0.043): older participant cohorts reported higher PIF prevalence.
- No significant moderators were found for acute-phase fatigue heterogeneity.

### Risk factors for PIF (pooled ORs from ≥ 2 studies)
| Risk factor | Pooled OR | 95% CI | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Female sex | 1.65 | 1.27–2.14 | 0.00020 |
| Dengue hemorrhagic fever (DHF) | 1.80 | 1.02–3.16 | 0.042 |
| Preexisting comorbidities | 2.14 | 1.36–3.38 | 0.0010 |

- Post-discharge myalgia and headache were each studied in 3 papers but showed no pooled statistical significance (high I²: 81% and 82%, respectively).
- DHF was not a significant risk factor for acute-phase fatigue (pooled OR = 1.29, 95% CI 0.43–3.88, p = 0.65), only for PIF.

### Study characteristics
- 40 included studies; 48.2% female; mean age 40.9 years.
- Geographic distribution: Asia 62.5%, South America 17.5%, Africa 10.0%, Europe 7.5%, North America 2.5%.
- Risk of bias: 7.5% low, 70.0% moderate, 22.5% high.
- Fatigue measurement: 87.5% used clinical symptoms; only 12.5% used validated questionnaires — a major source of heterogeneity.

### Proposed mechanisms (per authors)
- Complex immune response between virus and host, influenced by genetics, gender, dengue severity, comorbidities, and autoimmune responses.
- Females: stronger immune responses (more antibodies, higher CD4+ levels), estrogen-driven B-cell activation, and increased likelihood of autoimmune reactions — proposed contributor to prolonged fatigue.
- DHF patients: intense inflammation and plasma leakage may extend recovery time.
- Comorbidities (diabetes, chronic renal disease, heart disease): complex immunological and neuroendocrine-musculoskeletal-immune interactions that delay recovery.

## Relevance

This paper directly addresses the project research question (`research-question:post-acute-infection-syndromes`) by establishing that **post-dengue PIF is a genuine PAIS phenotype** with a pooled prevalence of 20% — comparable to post-Q-fever fatigue (referenced as ~20% in the paper's Discussion) and substantially lower than post-COVID-19 fatigue (41–47%), suggesting pathogen-specific variation in PAIS burden.

Within the PAIS frame of **failed homeostatic recovery after acute infection**, the findings are interpretable as follows:

1. **Incomplete resolution of immune activation:** The acute-phase fatigue rate (59%) confirms that dengue routinely drives a strong systemic immune response. PIF at 20% marks the subset where immune/physiological homeostasis fails to restore in weeks-to-months, consistent with the shared post-infectious failure mode hypothesis.

2. **Sex-linked immune dysregulation as a PAIS amplifier:** The pooled female-sex OR of 1.65 (95% CI 1.27–2.14) for PIF mirrors the female predominance documented across long COVID, ME/CFS, and post-Lyme — supporting a cross-PAIS mechanism involving sex-hormone-driven immune polarisation (estrogen-mediated B-cell hyperactivation, autoimmune propensity).

3. **Disease severity as a persistence driver:** DHF's association with PIF (OR = 1.80) — but not with acute fatigue — suggests that the *degree* of inflammatory insult (plasma leakage, intense cytokine release) during the acute phase shapes the probability of failed recovery, independently of whether fatigue was present acutely. This is consistent with a "inflammatory priming" model for PAIS.

4. **Comorbidities as homeostatic vulnerability:** The strongest PIF risk factor (OR = 2.14) is preexisting comorbidities, indicating that baseline homeostatic reserve (metabolic, immunological, autonomic) is a key determinant of recovery trajectory — directly aligning with the project's framing of PAIS as multi-system homeostatic failure.

5. **Definition and measurement challenges as cross-PAIS issue:** The heterogeneity in fatigue measurement methods (clinical symptoms vs. validated questionnaires) — and the finding that questionnaire-based studies detected PIF at 29.5% versus 12.6% for symptom-based — is an important methodological lesson applicable across PAIS research.

## Limitations

- **Measurement heterogeneity:** 87.5% of fatigue studies used clinical symptom assessment rather than validated questionnaires, likely underestimating true prevalence and limiting cross-study comparability.
- **Limited PIF studies:** Only 9 studies contributed to the PIF meta-analysis (5045 patients), restricting power for moderator analyses and risk factor pooling.
- **Moderate-to-high risk of bias:** 92.5% of included studies were at moderate or high risk of bias; study populations often not nationally representative; fatigue definitions inconsistent.
- **No IPW weighting:** Lack of population data precluded inverse probability weighting, which may have improved pooled prevalence accuracy.
- **Causal inference impossible:** All included studies are observational; no randomised or quasi-experimental designs. Risk factors are associations, not causal estimates.
- **Insufficient moderator variables:** Limited reporting of potential moderators (e.g., dengue serotype, timing of assessment post-infection) constrained the meta-regression.
- **Cross-sectional studies inflate PIF estimates:** The moderator analysis found cross-sectional studies reported 39.7% PIF vs. 18.2% in other designs; survivor/selection bias likely inflates cross-sectional estimates.
- **No mechanistic data:** No biomarker, cytokine, or immune-profiling data were synthesised; proposed mechanisms remain speculative.
- **Geographic sampling gap:** Only 1 North American study; findings may not generalise to all dengue-endemic regions.

## Follow-up

- **Kalimuddin et al. 2022** (`paper:Kalimuddin2022`): Singapore-based prospective study of chronic sequelae after dengue and acute viral respiratory illness — provides individual-level data on PIF correlates, included in this meta-analysis.
- **Morroy et al. 2016** (`paper:Morroy2016`): Probable post-Q-fever fatigue meta-analysis referenced in Discussion (~20% prevalence) — useful cross-pathogen PAIS comparison.
- Mechanistic studies on immune profiling in post-dengue PIF patients (cytokine trajectories, T-cell exhaustion markers) are flagged by the authors as a critical evidence gap.
- Studies using validated fatigue instruments (e.g., Fatigue Severity Scale, Chalder Fatigue Scale) in dengue cohorts are needed to resolve the clinical-symptoms vs. questionnaire discrepancy.
- Investigation of dengue serotype as a moderator of PIF risk is warranted (not addressed by any included study).
