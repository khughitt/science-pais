# The peptidoglycan of Borrelia burgdorferi can persist in discrete tissues and cause systemic responses consistent with chronic illness

## Key Contribution

This study establishes that polymeric *Borrelia burgdorferi* peptidoglycan (pPG^Bb) — a structurally unique bacterial cell wall PAMP — accumulates specifically in the murine liver and persists for weeks to months after injection or live infection, in contrast to muropeptide fragments and PG from other bacteria (E. coli, S. aureus, D. radiodurans), which are rapidly cleared within 48 hours. The unusual glycan chemistry of pPG^Bb, including a terminal G-G-anhM trisaccharide absent from virtually all other bacteria, partially accounts for this organ-specific retention. Persistent hepatic PG drives liver proteome changes, elevated serum AST/ALT, PBMC energy-metabolism transcriptomic reprogramming, and a cytokine milieu that overlaps with reported protein signatures of chronic post-infectious illness, including Long Covid — providing a mechanistic framework for how post-treatment Lyme disease syndrome (PTLDS) and potentially other PAIS may emerge from lingering non-viable pathogen material.

## Methods

**Animal model.** 6–8-week-old female BALB/cJ mice (Jackson Laboratories). Real-time in vivo fluorescent tracking: 100 µL of cy5- or cy5.5-labeled PG preparations (2 mg/mL) injected retro-orbitally; imaged using Perkin Elmer IVIS system at 2, 6, 12 hours post-injection, then every 24 hours for 1 week, then weekly up to 4+ weeks. Radiant efficiency normalized to a dye-only control.

**PG preparations compared.** Polymeric pPG^Bb vs. digested muropeptide fragments (dPG^Bb) from *B. burgdorferi* (strain B31-A3); polymeric PG from *E. coli* K12 (m-DAP), *S. aureus* FDA 209 (L-Lys), *D. radiodurans* (L-Orn + di-Gly bridge), *B. subtilis* (amidated m-DAP), and *Streptococcus mutans* (L-Lys-Gly5). A dual-labeling tandem system (cy5 and cy5.5 spectral unmixing) allowed simultaneous co-injection to control for individual animal variability.

**Live infection model.** Subcutaneous needle inoculation with 10^4 *B. burgdorferi* cells; livers harvested at 1, 2, and 4 weeks post-infection; PG quantified by PG-specific monoclonal antibody (r-mAb2G10) ELISA.

**Cell studies.** Immortalized murine Kupffer cell line (SCC119) and FL83B hepatocyte line; pulse-chase experiments tracking PG intracellular signal by flow cytometry at 0, 24, and 48 hours. Phagocytosis confirmed and quantified with cytochalasin D and latrunculin A inhibitors. ~10,000 cells per measurement.

**Liver histopathology.** Blind scoring at 35 days post-injection; H&E sections; serum AST and ALT measured longitudinally (weekly).

**Proteomics.** Flash-frozen livers 2 days post-injection; whole proteome analysis; reactome pathway enrichment. Comparison to published SARS-CoV-2 and Long Covid protein signatures (Tables S1, S2).

**Transcriptomics (PBMCs).** Fresh pooled human PBMCs from multiple healthy donors stimulated with equal amounts of polymeric PG from *B. burgdorferi* and 5 other bacterial species; RNA-seq; >10,000 co-expressed genes as PAMP baseline, then differential expression analysis per PG type.

**Human synovial fluid.** Subset of 30 blinded patient samples (Lyme arthritis n ≈ 28 with detectable pPG^Bb, plus rheumatoid arthritis, osteoarthritis, ankylosing spondylitis controls); re-assessed using a newly developed highly specific monoclonal antibody (r-mAb2G10) that distinguishes pPG^Bb from dPG^Bb.

## Key Findings

**1. Polymeric B. burgdorferi PG uniquely persists in the liver.**
- Within 2 days of IV injection, cy5:pPG^Bb signal was ~100-fold higher than dye control in the murine abdomen, concentrated in liver and spleen.
- At >2 weeks post-injection, pPG^Bb signal remained >10-fold above dye control; signal above dPG^Bb was still significant at 4 weeks (p < 0.01).
- Clearance of dPG^Bb fit a one-phase exponential decay (R² = 0.9315), confirming rapid continuous excretion.
- PG from E. coli, S. aureus, and D. radiodurans was cleared to baseline within 48 hours, with liver localization 5–30-fold lower than pPG^Bb.
- The G-G-anhM terminal glycan partially contributes to persistence: pPG from a *chbC* mutant (37% reduction in G-G-anhM) showed significantly reduced signal within 6 hours (p < 0.05) but never reached baseline like other PG types, suggesting G-G-anhM is one of multiple contributing features.

**2. Kupffer cells and hepatocytes phagocytose and retain pPG^Bb differentially.**
- ~90% of Kupffer cells engulfed cy5-labeled PG (all types) within 24 hours.
- Kupffer cells retained significantly more pPG^Bb-derived intracellular signal than pPG^Ec or pPG^Sa at each post-uptake time point (p < 0.001 for both comparisons).
- Hepatocytes preferentially engulfed pPG^Bb over other PG types (>2× more hepatocytes were pPG^Bb-positive on average across 3 experiments); phagocytosis confirmed by dose-dependent inhibition with cytochalasin D and latrunculin A.
- Whole intact *B. burgdorferi* cells were not phagocytosed by hepatocytes (only Kupffer cells), confirming PG specificity.
- Live infection: hepatic PG signal detected as early as 1 week post-infection, plateauing but persisting after 2 weeks (p < 0.0001).

**3. Persistent hepatic PG drives systemic functional signatures overlapping with PAIS.**
- Histopathology at 35 days: minimal gross liver pathology despite prolonged pPG^Bb occupancy (some extramedullary hematopoiesis).
- Serum AST and ALT elevated by day 7 post-injection (p < 0.01 vs. controls), peaked at day 21, remained elevated at day 35 — i.e., liver dysfunction outlasts detectable PG signal.
- Liver proteome (2 days post-injection): ~70% of differentially abundant proteins associated with innate or adaptive immunity. TLR1 and TLR2 signaling dysregulated; enrichment in neutrophil granulation.
- The individual liver proteins dysregulated by pPG^Bb significantly overlapped with proteins dysregulated during SARS-CoV-2 infection and in Long Covid patients (p = 0.00038, Tables S1-S2) — a striking cross-pathogen signature convergence.
- PBMC transcriptomics: pPG^Bb caused the greatest number of significantly differentially expressed genes among all PG types tested (p < 0.01); energy metabolism-related genes (electron transport chain, TCA cycle, longevity-regulating pathway) were among the most differentially expressed, predominantly downregulated; CCL19 and IL-23 transcripts significantly upregulated (both previously implicated in PTLDS; p < 0.0001).

**4. Polymeric pPG^Bb is the predominant form in human Lyme arthritis synovial fluid.**
- The new monoclonal antibody r-mAb2G10 (sensitivity 96.4%) detected pPG^Bb in 27/30 (90%) synovial fluid samples from Lyme arthritis patients who had received oral or IV antibiotic therapy.
- r-mAb2G10 did not interact with control synovial fluid from rheumatoid arthritis, osteoarthritis, or ankylosing spondylitis patients.
- Quantified pPG^Bb concentrations were highly correlated with prior polyclonal antibody measurements (r = 0.8691), validating that previously detected PG is the polymeric form.

## Relevance

This paper is directly and centrally relevant to the PAIS frame. It provides one of the clearest mechanistic demonstrations to date of **antigen/PAMP persistence after pathogen clearance** as an explanatory driver of chronic post-infectious illness, and does so for a named PAIS (PTLDS) with direct comparisons drawn to Long Covid.

Key PAIS connections:

1. **Antigen persistence as a PAIS mechanism.** The paper shows empirically that a non-viable, non-replicating pathogen fragment can accumulate in a specific tissue reservoir (liver), resist clearance via conventional Kupffer cell + hepatocyte phagocytosis, and remain bioactive for weeks to months. This operationalizes the "persistent-antigen hypothesis" for PAIS — moving it from inferential to mechanistically grounded in an animal model.

2. **Cross-syndrome parallels.** The paper explicitly connects its murine data to SARS-CoV-2/Long Covid: the liver proteome signature under pPG^Bb stimulation significantly overlaps with that of Long Covid patients (p = 0.00038). This supports the project's hypothesis that distinct PAIS share a common downstream failure mode regardless of the triggering pathogen, because the ultimate driver may be any sufficiently persistent, immunostimulatory pathogen-derived fragment accumulating in a tissue sink.

3. **Immune reprogramming without overt pathology.** Despite minimal gross liver histopathology at 35 days, pPG^Bb drives prolonged AST/ALT elevation, TLR1/TLR2 signaling dysregulation, and PBMC energy-metabolism suppression — consistent with the "smoldering immune activation" phenotype common to PAIS. The downregulation of mitochondrial/TCA cycle genes in PBMCs is directly analogous to the metabolic dysfunction reported in ME/CFS and Long Covid.

4. **Case definition anchor: PTLDS.** The paper uses the standard clinical definition of PTLDS (symptoms lasting >6 months post-recommended antibiotic treatment), providing a well-characterized PAIS anchor. The human synovial fluid data (pPG^Bb detectable in 90% of Lyme arthritis patients after antibiotics) shift the evidence from mouse model to human disease.

5. **Therapeutic implication.** Beta-lactam antibiotics targeting PG synthesis would theoretically accelerate pPG^Bb release from bacteria, potentially worsening persistence; doxycycline (which arrests protein synthesis, enabling immune lysis) may behave differently. This has direct relevance to understanding why treatment outcomes vary in PTLDS and potentially in other PAIS treated with antibiotic co-administration.

## Limitations

1. **Retro-orbital injection route.** All PG tracking studies required IV retro-orbital injection to facilitate real-time fluorescent imaging. Live infection used subcutaneous needle inoculation (not tick-mediated), so the true route-specific biodistribution after natural tick transmission remains uncharacterized.

2. **Mouse model only; no PTLDS model exists.** BALB/cJ mice develop moderate arthritis upon *B. burgdorferi* infection and are amenable to skin-window fluorescence, but no validated murine model of PTLDS (post-treatment chronic symptoms) exists. The authors note the dysregulated pathways "could generate phenotypes consistent with PTLDS" but cannot confirm the responses truly resemble human PTLDS.

3. **Immortalized cell lines.** Kupffer cell and hepatocyte persistence studies used immortalized lines (SCC119 and FL83B). Primary cell studies may reveal different phagocytic or retention kinetics.

4. **Inbred mouse strain variability.** BALB/cJ is a specific inbred strain; other strains (e.g., C57BL/6) may differ in pPG^Bb retention or immunological responses. Host genetic polymorphisms in innate immune receptors (TLR1, TLR2, NOD1/NOD2) are likely modulators.

5. **PBMC transcriptomics from healthy donors.** The energy-metabolism transcriptome data were collected from healthy human PBMCs stimulated ex vivo, not from PBMCs of PTLDS patients or individuals with any active post-infectious condition. The clinical relevance depends on whether PBMCs are repeatedly exposed to circulating pPG^Bb in vivo, which is modeled but not directly demonstrated in humans.

6. **Human synovial fluid cohort size.** The monoclonal antibody validation used only 30 patient samples; the cohort composition (proportion with oral vs. IV antibiotics, disease duration, symptom severity) is not detailed in the main text.

7. **Causation vs. association for PTLDS symptoms.** The paper demonstrates pPG^Bb persistence and associated molecular changes but cannot establish that these changes cause PTLDS symptoms in individual patients. The mouse model lacks behavioral or neurocognitive outcome measures analogous to fatigue or cognitive impairment in human PTLDS.

8. **G-G-anhM biosynthesis mechanism unknown.** Complete ablation of the G-G-anhM modification (which may eliminate PG persistence) is not yet achievable in wild-type *B. burgdorferi*; the biosynthetic pathway for G-G-anhM is uncharacterized.

## Model / Tool Availability

- **r-mAb2G10 monoclonal antibody:** Developed in this study; capable of distinguishing polymeric from digested pPG^Bb by ELISA. Not yet commercially available; inquire with the Jutras lab (Northwestern University / Virginia Tech). This antibody has diagnostic potential for detecting pPG^Bb in human clinical samples.
- **Fluorescent PG tracking system:** cy5/cy5.5 dual-labeling + IVIS imaging protocol described in full in Materials and Methods; methodology is reproducible from the paper.
- No code, genomic datasets, or computational models deposited for public reuse based on the main text.

## Follow-up

- **Komaroff2025** (`paper:Komaroff2025`) — for cross-referencing the ME/CFS / Long Covid metabolic dysfunction frame and how pPG^Bb-driven PBMC energy-metabolism suppression fits.
- What is the identity of the tissue reservoir for SARS-CoV-2 spike protein persistence? Do Kupffer cells also retain SARS-CoV-2 antigen, and if so, does the liver proteome response overlap as predicted by the p = 0.00038 convergence result found here?
- Does blocking TLR2 signaling reduce pPG^Bb-driven proteome changes and AST/ALT elevation in mice? This would test the mechanistic link between PG recognition and downstream liver dysfunction relevant to PAIS.
- Is G-G-anhM biosynthesis essential for pPG^Bb liver accumulation, and can complete genetic ablation prevent PTLDS-like phenotypes in mouse infection models?
- Human prospective cohort: measure pPG^Bb in serum/synovial fluid at treatment completion and at 6-month follow-up in Lyme disease patients; test whether pPG^Bb concentration at follow-up predicts PTLDS diagnosis.
