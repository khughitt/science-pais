# Altered interferon-γ response in patients with Q-fever fatigue syndrome

- **Authors:** Stephan P. Keijmel, Ruud P.H. Raijmakers, Chantal P. Bleeker-Rovers, Jos W.M. van der Meer, Mihai G. Netea, Teske Schoffelen, Marcel van Deuren
- **Year:** 2016
- **Journal:** Journal of Infection, vol. 72(4):478–485
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.jinf.2016.01.004
- **PMID:** 26820634
- **BibTeX key:** Keijmel2016
- **Source:** Abstract only (Elsevier paywalled; OA copy at Radboud repository returned HTTP 403; findings extracted from structured Europe PMC abstract)

## Key Contribution

This is one of the earliest studies to directly assess antigen-specific immune function in Q-fever fatigue syndrome (QFS). Using ex-vivo whole-blood stimulation with *Coxiella burnetii* Nine Mile phase I antigen, the study demonstrates that QFS patients mount a significantly elevated IFN-γ response compared to seropositive-but-recovered controls, yet display an IFN-γ/IL-2 ratio that is lower than in active chronic Q-fever. The pattern points to an altered cell-mediated immune state in QFS that is qualitatively distinct from both recovered infection and chronic active disease, suggesting ongoing dysregulation of the *Coxiella*-specific T-cell response rather than immune quiescence.

## Key Findings

**IFN-γ production (Coxiella-stimulated)**
- QFS patients: median IFN-γ = 319.5 pg/mL
- Seropositive controls: median IFN-γ = 120 pg/mL → QFS significantly higher (p < 0.01)
- Chronic Q-fever patients: median IFN-γ = 2,846 pg/mL → QFS substantially lower; comparison statistics [INACCESSIBLE]

**IFN-γ/IL-2 ratio**
- QFS ratio: similar to seropositive controls (p-value [INACCESSIBLE])
- QFS ratio: lower than chronic Q-fever patients (p < 0.01)

**Symptom duration correlations**
- Longer symptom duration positively correlated with IL-2 production
- Longer symptom duration negatively correlated with IFN-γ/IL-2 ratio

**Demographics**
- QFS patients were younger than seropositive controls and chronic Q-fever patients (p < 0.001)
- Sex distribution was similar across all groups

**Interpretation:** The authors conclude that QFS represents altered cell-mediated immunity — an intermediate state in which Coxiella-specific IFN-γ is elevated above recovered controls but the IFN-γ/IL-2 cytokine balance differs from that seen in chronic active infection. The longitudinal correlation of symptom duration with IL-2 and the ratio suggests that immunological dysregulation tracks clinical trajectory.

## Relevance

This paper is directly relevant to the project's core frame of post-acute infection syndromes as failed immune homeostasis recovery. Key connections:

1. **QFS as a canonical PAIS with measurable immune dysfunction:** The finding of elevated Coxiella-stimulated IFN-γ in QFS, relative to seropositive recovered controls, provides one of the earliest functional immunological demonstrations that QFS is not simply a psychological or residual-fatigue syndrome — it has a detectable cell-mediated immune correlate tied to the original pathogen. This supports the antigen-persistence / sustained immune activation axis in the project's hypotheses.

2. **IFN-γ axis as a shared cross-PAIS signal:** Elevated or dysregulated IFN-γ production is a recurring finding across long COVID, ME/CFS, and post-Lyme fatigue. Documenting the same axis in QFS strengthens the hypothesis (question:0001-shared-molecular-signature-across-triggers) that persistent *Coxiella*-specific T-cell activation or antigen stimulation underlies QFS, in a pattern broadly analogous to what is seen in other PAISs.

3. **Immune state distinct from chronic active disease:** The IFN-γ/IL-2 ratio differentiation between QFS and chronic Q-fever suggests that QFS is not simply subclinical chronic infection; it is a distinct immunological phenotype. This distinction is important for the project's framing: post-infectious fatigue is not merely milder chronic disease — it is a different homeostatic failure mode.

4. **Symptom duration-cytokine correlations:** The finding that longer QFS duration correlates with higher IL-2 and lower IFN-γ/IL-2 ratio is consistent with a time-dependent immunological trajectory, potentially reflecting evolving exhaustion, antigen clearance dynamics, or regulatory counter-responses over time.

## Limitations

- **Small QFS cohort (n = 20):** Underpowered for subgroup analyses; confidence intervals on cytokine medians and correlation estimates are likely wide.
- **Cross-sectional design:** Cannot establish whether immune alterations precede, accompany, or follow QFS symptom onset; no pre-infection baseline available for most patients.
- **Age confounding:** QFS patients were significantly younger than both comparator groups (p < 0.001); age is an important determinant of T-cell function and IFN-γ production. Whether the elevated IFN-γ in QFS is a QFS-specific signal or partly an age effect is not addressed. [INACCESSIBLE whether age-adjusted analyses were performed.]
- **Case definition not fully reported in abstract:** The QFS diagnostic criteria applied are [INACCESSIBLE] in the abstract. Dutch national QFS criteria require serological confirmation of prior acute Q-fever plus fatigue; whether additional exclusion criteria (depression, other medical causes) were applied is unknown.
- **Seropositive controls (n = 135) not matched to QFS patients:** The large seropositive control group was apparently used from a previously collected cohort (wording: "compared to those previously determined"), introducing potential batch effects and differences in sampling conditions for the cytokine assay.
- **No healthy (seronegative) controls:** The study compares QFS to seropositive recovered and to chronic Q-fever patients, but not to individuals without prior *Coxiella* infection. It is therefore not possible to determine whether the QFS cytokine profile is elevated relative to population background or merely relative to the seropositive-recovered level.
- **IFN-γ measurement: circulating vs. stimulated ambiguity:** The abstract describes "ex-vivo stimulated whole blood" IFN-γ — this is antigen-induced production, not resting/circulating plasma IFN-γ. The distinction matters for comparing to studies measuring circulating IFN-γ.
- **Symptom characterization not described in abstract:** Fatigue severity, functional impairment, co-morbidities, and treatment history of QFS patients are [INACCESSIBLE]; heterogeneity within the QFS group cannot be assessed.
- **Single time-point cytokine measurement:** No longitudinal follow-up data; correlation of symptom duration with cytokine levels is cross-sectional and subject to survivorship/selection bias.
