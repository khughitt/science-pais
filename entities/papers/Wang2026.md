# Predicting Trajectories of Long COVID in Adult Women

- **Authors:** Jing Wang, Jie Shen, Yiming Luo, Amar Sra, Qiaomin Xie, Jeremy C. Weiss
- **Year:** 2026
- **Journal:** arXiv
- **DOI/URL:** arXiv:2603.17722
- **BibTeX key:** Wang2026
- **Source:** PDF: `~/d/health/processes/post-acute-infection/papers/pdfs/2026_Wang_Predicting-Trajectories-of-Long-COVID-in-Adult-Women-The-Critical-Role-of-Causal-Disentanglement.pdf`

## Key Contribution

Wang et al. propose a causal-disentangled LLM regressor for predicting future PASC severity in adult women while suppressing menopause and comorbidity features treated as confounders.
The useful contribution for this project is not the claim that the model establishes causality, but the explicit recognition that menopause, diabetes, sleep, cardiovascular disease, and wearable physiology can produce signal-to-noise problems in women long-COVID prediction.

## Methods

The study uses NIH RECOVER data from 1,155 women with static clinical profiles and four weeks of aggregated wearable data.
The authors encode clinical and wearable histories as structured narratives and use a Qwen-2.5-0.5B backbone with an attention-based disentanglement layer, environment mixing, an InfoNCE-style contrastive mix loss, and multi-head regression.
They compare against XGBoost across five random seeds.

## Key Findings

The causal inference network achieved classification accuracy of 0.867 +/- 0.044 and precision of 0.836 +/- 0.116, while XGBoost had better regression error (RMSE 4.76 +/- 0.16 vs 5.35 +/- 1.17).
Saliency analysis routed direct pathology tokens such as breathlessness and malaise to the causal head with maximum saliency, while menopause and diabetes were assigned lower saliency (<0.27).
The cohort was older and mostly menopausal: median age 61 years, 68.91% menopause, 59.92% heart conditions, 27.45% sleep disorders, and a high burden of baseline confounders.

## Relevance

The paper is relevant as a methodological prompt for `question:0007-mechanism-of-female-predominance-in-pais`.
It highlights a modeling problem this project will face: hormone-status variables can be causes, confounders, mediators, or symptom-overlap artifacts depending on the estimand.
It should not be treated as biological evidence that menopause is non-causal, because the model architecture encodes menopause as suppressible baseline noise by design.

## Limitations

This is an arXiv preprint and should be treated as unreviewed.
The causal interpretation depends heavily on design choices; menopause is treated as a confounder rather than tested as a mediator or effect modifier.
The paper reports limited clinical detail about the RECOVER subset, lacks external validation, and the causal network has higher variance than XGBoost.

## Follow-up

If this project builds prediction models, define the causal estimand before suppressing hormone variables.
A useful follow-up is to compare three DAGs: menopause as confounder, menopause as mediator of age/sex effects, and menopause as effect modifier of immune-dysregulation risk.
