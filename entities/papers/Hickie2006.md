# Post-infective and chronic fatigue syndromes precipitated by viral and non-viral pathogens: prospective cohort study

- **Authors:** Ian Hickie, Tracey Davenport, Denis Wakefield, Ute Vollmer-Conna, Barbara Cameron, Suzanne D. Vernon, William C. Reeves, Andrew Lloyd (Dubbo Infection Outcomes Study Group)
- **Year:** 2006
- **Journal:** BMJ, vol. 333, no. 7568, p. 575
- **DOI:** 10.1136/bmj.38933.585764.AE
- **PMID:** 16950834
- **PMCID:** PMC1569956
- **BibTeX key:** Hickie2006
- **Source:** Europe PMC abstract + PMC full text (WebFetch)

## Key Contribution

The Dubbo Infection Outcomes Study is one of the foundational multi-trigger PAIS cohort studies. By enrolling patients prospectively from the time of acute infection with three etiologically distinct pathogens — Epstein-Barr virus (EBV), *Coxiella burnetii* (Q fever), and Ross River virus (RRV) — and applying a uniform Fukuda 1994 CFS case definition at 6 months, it demonstrates that (1) a stereotyped post-infective fatigue syndrome (PIFS) emerges at a similar rate after each pathogen, and (2) the syndrome is predicted primarily by acute illness severity rather than pathogen type, demographic characteristics, or pre-existing psychological factors. This establishes the trigger-nonspecific, severity-gated character of post-infectious fatigue at the prospective-cohort evidence tier, two decades before SARS-CoV-2 long COVID dominated the field.

## Methods

**Design:** Prospective cohort study. Patients were enrolled at time of acute presentation with one of three confirmed infections and followed at baseline, 3 weeks, 6 weeks, 3 months, 6 months, and 12 months.

**Setting:** The region surrounding Dubbo township, rural New South Wales, Australia (200 km radius, ~104,400 residents).

**Participants:** 253 patients enrolled total. Confirmed pathogen groups: EBV/glandular fever (n=68), Q fever/*C. burnetii* (n=43), Ross River virus epidemic polyarthritis (n=60). Remaining participants (n≈82) had clinically suspected but microbiologically unconfirmed infections.

**Outcome definition:** Fukuda et al. 1994 CDC/CFS diagnostic criteria applied at 6-month physician assessment with medical, psychiatric, and laboratory evaluation to exclude alternative explanations.

**Instruments:** SPHERE 34-item somatic/psychological health report (SOMA subscale); Brief Disability Questionnaire; Composite International Diagnostic Interview (CIDI) for psychiatric history; Eysenck Personality Inventory (neuroticism); locus-of-control scale; structured clinical interview for symptom factor scores (fatigue, musculoskeletal pain, neurocognitive disturbance, mood disturbance).

**Statistical approach:** Kaplan-Meier survival analysis for time-to-onset and between-group comparison; linear regression for symptom factor predictors (β coefficients reported).

## Key Findings

### Incidence and phenotype at 6 months

- **29 of 253 (12%) had prolonged illness** characterised by disabling fatigue, musculoskeletal pain, neurocognitive difficulties, and mood disturbance at 6 months.
- **28 of 253 (11%) met Fukuda 1994 CFS criteria** at 6 months (one patient had prolonged illness but did not fulfil full CFS criteria).
- CFS cases by pathogen group: EBV n=5 (~7%), Q fever n=3 (~7%), RRV n=13 (~22%), unconfirmed infection n=8. Point estimates vary — RRV appears higher — but the difference across infection groups did not reach statistical significance (Kaplan-Meier χ²=3.45, df=3, P>0.05), consistent with the interpretation that incidence rate is not pathogen-determined.

### Symptom phenotype

The PIFS phenotype was described as "stereotyped" across pathogens: persistent fatigue, musculoskeletal pain, neurocognitive difficulties (concentration and memory impairment), and mood disturbance with minimal variation in symptom composition across the three infection groups.

### Predictors of post-infective fatigue syndrome

**Acute illness severity** was the dominant predictor. Symptom factor scores during acute illness strongly predicted later PIFS status:
- Fatigue factor score: β=0.50 (P<0.01) at 3 months; β=0.35 (P<0.01) at 6 months
- Musculoskeletal pain factor: β=0.27–0.30
- Neurocognitive disturbance factor: β=0.24

**Premorbid and intercurrent psychiatric disorder did not show predictive power at any time point.** Premorbid psychiatric diagnoses were present in 21% of PIFS cases vs. 17% of recovered controls (not significantly different); intercurrent psychiatric disorders in 21% of cases vs. 10% of controls (not significantly different in the multivariate model). The paper explicitly states the syndrome was "predicted largely by the severity of the acute illness rather than by demographic, psychological, or microbiological factors."

**Demographic characteristics** did not generally predict symptom factor scores at baseline or follow-up.

**Microbiological factors** (pathogen type, antibody titres) did not predict persistence.

## Relevance

This paper provides tier-2 prospective-cohort evidence for two central project hypotheses:

**`hypothesis:0001` (shared-dysregulated-attractor):** The stereotyped PIFS phenotype emerging at statistically indistinguishable rates after three etiologically distinct pathogens (bacterial intracellular, arthropod-borne alphavirus, herpesvirus) directly supports the multi-trigger convergence premise — that the post-infectious syndrome reflects a host-determined failure mode rather than a pathogen-specific endpoint. The paper's authors frame PIFS as "a valid illness model for investigating one pathophysiological pathway to CFS," implying a shared route regardless of trigger.

**`hypothesis:0004` (acute-severity-threshold):** The finding that acute illness severity — operationalised as symptom factor scores during the acute phase — is the strongest predictor of 6-month PIFS, while pathogen type and psychological factors are not, provides the most direct prospective-cohort anchor for the severity-gating claim. The non-linearity claim (threshold vs. gradient) is not directly tested here, but the prominence of severity as the dominant predictor is consistent with the threshold frame.

The explicit null on psychological and demographic predictors is also important for the project's scope and credibility framing: this 2006 prospective multi-pathogen study directly contradicts the psychosomatic/biopsychosocial model that was dominant at the time.

## Limitations

1. **2006 cohort; pre-SARS-CoV-2:** CFS (Fukuda 1994) was the endpoint, not modern ME/CFS criteria (CCC, ICC) or long-COVID case definitions. Apparent incidence and phenotype may not be directly comparable to contemporary PASC studies that use different definitions and measurement intervals.

2. **CFS case definition at 6 months only:** The primary PAIS endpoint was assessed at 6 months. Longer follow-up at 12 months was included for some analyses, but the proportion meeting CFS criteria at 12 months vs. 6 months is not the headline result; natural history beyond 12 months is not characterised.

3. **Per-pathogen RRV rate appears higher (22% vs. ~7% for EBV/Q fever):** The chi-square test was not significant (P>0.05), but with modest per-group Ns (EBV 68, Q fever 43, RRV 60), the study was underpowered to detect true moderate differences across groups. The "no difference" conclusion should be read as "no statistically significant difference given sample size," not as "rates are identical."

4. **Rural Australian setting:** The Dubbo region cohort — predominantly presenting to primary care in a rural township — may not represent urban or more heterogeneous settings. Pathogen exposure routes (e.g., RRV is mosquito-borne; Q fever is occupationally clustered) may shape who gets enrolled and how severely.

5. **Acute severity is symptom-based, not biomarker- or hospitalization-based:** The severity predictors are symptom factor scores during the acute phase (SPHERE/structured interview), not objective measures like hospitalization, organ dysfunction markers, or viral load. This is a high-ecological-validity operationalisation, but it does not establish the molecular substrate of the severity effect.

6. **Unconfirmed infection group (n≈82):** Approximately one-third of the enrolled cohort had unconfirmed microbiological diagnoses. Their inclusion in the χ² test (df=3, not df=2) means the "no difference" finding includes a heterogeneous comparison group. The three confirmed pathogen groups alone (EBV, Q fever, RRV) represent ~68% of the enrolled cohort.

7. **No non-infected contemporaneous control:** The study follows infected patients; there is no uninfected community comparator to estimate background fatigue prevalence or secular trends over the follow-up window. This precludes a clean "attributable fraction" estimate.

8. **Pre-dates ME/CFS immune phenotyping and PEM objectification:** No systematic immune profiling, autonomic testing, or cardiopulmonary exercise testing was performed. The biological mechanisms linking acute severity to PIFS persistence are inferred, not measured.

## Model / Tool Availability

No computational model or dataset released. The SPHERE instrument (Somatic and Psychological Health Report) used for symptom measurement is a validated research tool but not specific to this paper.

## Follow-up

- **Cross-pathogen chronic fraction:** The ~10-20% non-recovery rate across triggers in this cohort (and in Morroy2016, Kalimuddin2022, Bai2023) is a key empirical regularity for `question:0042` (is this fraction an artefact of shared case definitions?). The Dubbo study used Fukuda criteria prospectively, which partially guards against this concern.
- **Acute severity operationalisation:** The symptom-factor-score approach to severity measurement should be compared against hospitalization-based severity proxies (Cai2024, Rahmati2025) to assess whether the threshold effect holds across different severity operationalisations. This addresses `question:0003`.
- **EBV-specific follow-up:** `question:0051` (prior symptomatic EBV mononucleosis as PAIS risk amplifier) is the forward inquiry from this study's EBV arm. The Dubbo EBV arm predates the modern EBV-PAIS field; the 5/68 (~7%) rate at 6 months in 2006 is informative context for interpreting EBV reactivation hypotheses.
- **Psychological null replication:** The finding that premorbid and intercurrent psychiatric disorders did not predict PIFS should be noted in the context of the broader ME/CFS aetiological debate; this result has been replicated in other post-infectious cohorts and is an important counterpoint to psychosomatic models.
