# Vascular Pathogenesis in Acute and Long COVID: Current Insights and Therapeutic Outlook

- **Authors:** Arneaux Kruger, David Joffe, Graham Lloyd-Jones, Muhammed Asad Khan, Špela Šalamon, Gert J. Laubscher, David Putrino, Douglas B. Kell, Etheresia Pretorius
- **Year:** 2024
- **Journal:** Seminars in Thrombosis and Hemostasis, Vol. 51 No. 3/2025, pp. 256–271
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0044-1790603
- **BibTeX key:** Kruger2024
- **Source:** Full-text PDF (papers/pdfs/2024_Kruger_vascular-pathogenesis-acute-long-covid.pdf), read 2026-06-20.

## Key Contribution

This narrative review, from the Pretorius/Kell group (Stellenbosch / Liverpool), argues that persistent thrombotic endothelialitis — endothelial dysfunction (ED) combined with fibrinaloid microclot formation and platelet hyperactivation — is a primary pathological driver of Long COVID chronicity. The paper synthesizes molecular mechanisms spanning acute-phase vascular injury through PASC, evaluates noninvasive diagnostic tools for ED (flow-mediated dilation, peripheral arterial tonometry, nailfold video capillaroscopy), and surveys a broad therapeutic landscape including anticoagulants, antiplatelets, statins, and other mechanistic agents, while acknowledging that no completed RCTs for anticoagulation in PASC yet exist.

## Methods

Narrative review with no systematic search protocol reported. The paper draws on the authors' own prior proteomics and microscopy work on microclots (Pretorius/Kell group) as well as a broad secondary literature spanning imaging studies, biomarker cohort studies, in vitro experiments, case series, and observational treatment reports. No independent meta-analysis is conducted. The review is structured as: (1) mechanisms of ED and endothelialitis in acute COVID-19; (2) persistence into Long COVID; (3) the role of microclots; (4) diagnostic modalities; (5) therapeutic agents.

Key evidentiary sources drawn upon include:
- Autopsy and histological studies showing pulmonary vasculopathy, microinfarcts, and endothelial necrosis in acute COVID-19.
- Proteomics of fibrin amyloid microclots (the authors' own prior work, Refs 27–28) showing entrapment of VWF, serum amyloid A, and α2-antiplasmin (α2AP) rendering microclots trypsin-resistant.
- Observational/cross-sectional cohort data for FMD impairment in Long COVID (≥12 studies, meta-analysis MD −2.31%; 95% CI −3.19, −1.44; p < 0.0001 by Ambrosino et al, Ref 128).
- A published preprint (not yet completed RCT) for triple anticoagulant therapy (Ref 132, Laubscher et al).

## Key Findings

**Acute COVID-19 vascular pathology**
- Radiological features of acute COVID-19 lung disease reflect a vasculopathic rather than conventional pneumonia pattern: ground-glass opacities with dilated vessels, dual-energy CT (DECT) perfusion defects present universally in hospitalised patients, macroscopic thrombi (7.5%) and perfusion defects (87%) persisting post-acute.
- Two phases of endothelial cell (EC) activation: Type I (rapid, reversible — release of stored vWF, P-selectin, thrombin, histamine) and Type II (hours-days, sustained — synthesis of CRP, fibrinogen, vWF, tissue factor, E-selectin, ICAM-1, VCAM-1, MCP-1).
- SARS-CoV-2 S1 subunit drives IL-6, MCP-1, ICAM-1, and PAI-1 expression via ACE2/NF-κB independently of viral replication. PAI-1 elevation correlates with disease severity and neutrophil activation; elevated PAI-1 impairs fibrinolysis by inhibiting plasmin.
- Direct platelet activation via ACE2 and TMPRSS2 receptors; platelet-platelet and platelet-leukocyte complex formation promoting hypercoagulability.

**Microclots (fibrinaloid microclots)**
- Microclots are small blood clots containing fibrin(ogen) molecules that have undergone amyloid structural transformation, trapping inflammatory molecules (VWF, serum amyloid A, α2AP) and resisting fibrinolysis. Detected by thioflavin T staining (amyloid marker).
- α2AP incorporated into fibrin by FXIIIa inhibits tPA-induced fibrinolysis; higher α2AP cross-linking explains fibrinolysis resistance.
- Microclots observed in acute COVID-19 and Long COVID; also detected inside circulating blood in PASC patients. The Omicron variant causes fewer microclots than earlier variants, consistent with its lower vasculopathic severity.
- Proposed mechanism: microclots block (or partially block) the microcirculation → ischaemia/hypoxia in tissues → hypoxia-dependent reactivation of latent viruses (e.g., EBV).
- **Evidence-status note:** The fibrinaloid microclot hypothesis is primarily advanced by the Pretorius/Kell group from their own proteomics and microscopy work. Thioflavin-T staining detects amyloid-like structures but does not constitute proof of pathogenic causality. Independent replication is limited. The review does not engage with critiques of the microclot hypothesis or alternative explanations for the same clinical phenomena. This claim should be held at the level of a mechanistically plausible but contested hypothesis, not established pathophysiology.

**Long COVID — persistence of vascular dysfunction**
- Persistent endothelial cell apoptotic tendencies months after acute infection; impaired intercellular connexin signalling; glycocalyx shedding (Syndecan-1 elevation confirmed at ~88 days in convalescent mild-disease patients vs healthy controls, but not vs hospitalised patients).
- Vascular biomarkers elevated in Long COVID: VWF/factor VIII, angiopoietin-1 (AGP-1), P-selectin, endothelin-1 (ET-1, elevated at 5 months), VEGF-A (via spike-NRP-1 axis disrupting VEGF-A binding).
- Autoantibodies to AT1R and ETAR (angiotensin II type 1 and endothelin A receptors) found in Long COVID patients, associated with vasoconstrictive and proinflammatory endothelial signalling.
- Viral persistence: SARS-CoV-2 antigen positivity in plasma up to 14 months post-infection (Peluso et al). Oral dysbiosis as a potential vascular viral entry pathway.
- FMD meta-analysis (12 studies, 622 control / 644 convalescent): Long COVID group had significantly lower FMD (MD −2.31%; 95% CI −3.19, −1.44; p < 0.0001). FMD abnormality demonstrable at 12 months post-infection. Inverse correlation with serum TNF-α.

**Diagnostic modalities for ED**
- Flow-mediated dilation (FMD): noninvasive brachial artery ultrasound; validated ED marker; two consensus papers published; reference intervals established. Recommended as a routine assessment tool for Long COVID clinicians.
- Peripheral arterial tonometry (EndoPAT device): validates microvascular function; one small COVID study (n=6, no control group); requires larger Long COVID evaluation.
- Nailfold video capillaroscopy: identifies microvascular structural abnormalities; both symptomatic and convalescent patients show capillaroscopic changes; clinical significance in COVID context still under study.
- Perfusion imaging (SPECT/VQ, DECT, MRI): detects residual clot burden and microvascular injury; preferred over conventional CT for pulmonary vasculopathy.

**Therapeutics**
- No completed RCTs specifically for anticoagulation in PASC reported.
- "Promising" agents (per Table 1): NOACs (anticoagulant; clinician-initiated regimens suggest utility; trials initiated), aspirin and clopidogrel (antiplatelet; found useful in acute COVID-19; clinical trials needed), statins (endothelial protective, anti-inflammatory; established), sulodexide (anticoagulant/antithrombotic; significantly improves ED in Long COVID per TUN-EndCOV study, n reported in cited reference).
- Triple anticoagulant therapy (NOACs + aspirin + clopidogrel): a published preprint (Laubscher et al 2023, Ref 132) showed symptom improvement; this is not yet a completed RCT.
- Additional "possible" agents: ivabradine and midodrine (POTS-associated Long COVID), metformin (reduced risk of developing Long COVID in diabetics), melatonin (endothelial protection, sleep), SSRIs (possible anti-platelet effect), colchicine (pericarditis/pleurisy), probiotics (gut dysbiosis), 5HT-1 antagonists/mast-cell stabilisers (MCAS-associated Long COVID).
- Warfarin: possibly useful as anticoagulation but not yet tested in PASC (Table 1).
- The review explicitly notes "no standard of care currently exists for the condition."

## Relevance

**topic:thromboinflammation-and-endothelial-dysfunction** — This is the primary mechanistic focus. The paper provides the most comprehensive current articulation of the Pretorius/Kell thromboinflammation framework: Type I/II EC activation → hypercoagulability → fibrinaloid microclots → capillary rarefaction → tissue hypoxia → chronic PAIS symptoms. It is a key reference for this topic.

**question:0010-vascular-microclot-subphenotype** — The review directly addresses whether microclot pathology defines a vascular subphenotype of Long COVID. It argues for microclots as a central mechanism in a subset of patients with persistent vascular/clotting abnormalities, and notes that the Omicron variant's lower microclot burden correlates with lower PASC severity. The contested evidence status (Pretorius/Kell group as primary producers) should be reflected in any subphenotype framing.

**topic:therapeutics-and-clinical-trials** — The therapeutic table (Table 1) and clinical-pointers summary (Table 2) make this a useful reference for the current landscape of proposed interventions. The key gap — no completed anticoagulation RCT for PASC — is explicitly stated, providing a direct anchor for the "evidence maturity" dimension of the therapeutics topic. The sulodexide finding (TUN-EndCOV) is one of the few positive signals from an actual trial-adjacent study.

**topic:biomarkers-and-objective-endpoints** — FMD meta-analysis (MD −2.31%; p < 0.0001 at 12 months) and the panel of vascular biomarkers (AGP-1, P-selectin, ET-1, VEGF-A, SDC-1, VWF/FVIII, AT1R/ETAR autoantibodies) directly populate this topic's biomarker inventory. FMD emerges as the best-validated noninvasive objective endpoint for vascular Long COVID.

**hypothesis:0001-shared-dysregulated-attractor** — The review's framing of persistent thrombotic endothelialitis as a shared attractor state (self-reinforcing cycle: ED → hypercoagulability → microclots → hypoxia → viral reactivation → further ED) is compatible with the shared-dysregulated-attractor hypothesis. The proposed mechanism would constitute one implementation of a disease-sustaining positive feedback loop.

**paper:CerviaHasler2024** — Both papers address vascular/coagulation dysfunction in long COVID from different angles; CerviaHasler2024 may contribute complementary data on platelet and complement activation or specific patient cohort evidence. Cross-referencing is warranted when synthesising the thromboinflammation topic.

## Limitations

1. **Narrative review without systematic methods.** No PRISMA flow, no search protocol, no quality assessment of included studies. The paper synthesises selectively and is not positioned to give unbiased effect-size estimates.

2. **Pretorius/Kell group conflict of interest on microclots.** The fibrinaloid microclot section draws almost exclusively on this group's own proteomics and microscopy work. E.P. is named as an inventor on a patent for fluorescence-based microclot detection. Independent replication of key microclot findings is not comprehensively reviewed. The review does not engage with methodological critiques of thioflavin-T staining for fibrin or with alternative mechanistic accounts.

3. **Evidence asymmetry across mechanisms.** Some claims are supported by multiple independent cohort studies (FMD, glycocalyx markers, autoantibodies); others rely on single small studies, preprints, or in vitro data (EndoPAT assessment: n=6, no control group; triple anticoagulant therapy: one preprint). The review does not clearly stratify evidence levels, making it easy to conflate well-supported and weakly-supported claims.

4. **Therapeutic claims lack RCT grounding.** The therapeutic table lists "clinician-initiated treatment regimens suggest it to be useful" as evidence for NOACs, aspirin, and clopidogrel in Long COVID — these are anecdotal or open-label. The paper acknowledges no standard of care exists, but the juxtaposition with "standard of care" level evidence for other indications could mislead readers.

5. **Case definition / cohort heterogeneity not addressed.** The paper does not discuss which WHO/CDC Long COVID definition is applied in cited studies, whether cohorts are post-hospitalisation vs mild acute illness, or how severity, variant, or vaccination status affects the evidence. The FMD meta-analysis populations and the microclot cohorts are not characterised in terms of these covariates.

6. **Causality vs. association not distinguished.** ED and microclots are consistently framed as drivers of Long COVID symptomatology, but most evidence is cross-sectional or observational. The paper does not engage with the possibility that these vascular markers are epiphenomena of an upstream immune process rather than primary drivers.

7. **Oral dysbiosis / viral persistence sections are speculative.** These are presented as plausible upstream causes of ongoing ED, but the cited evidence is largely indirect (associative studies in non-COVID contexts or small COVID cohorts).

## Model / Tool Availability

No computational model, software tool, or dataset is released with this review. The EndoPAT and E4-diagnose devices referenced are commercially available clinical instruments (not developed by these authors).

## Follow-up

- **CerviaHasler2024** (paper:CerviaHasler2024): Cross-reference for complementary vascular/coagulation evidence in long COVID from an independent group.
- **Laubscher et al 2023 preprint (Ref 132):** Triple anticoagulant therapy report — track for peer-reviewed publication and any follow-up RCT registration.
- **Ambrosino et al 2022 (Ref 128):** The FMD meta-analysis is a key methodological anchor — read for inclusion criteria, cohort definitions, and heterogeneity assessment.
- **Peluso et al 2024 (Ref 90):** Plasma-based antigen persistence up to 14 months — relevant to antigen-persistence topic; cross-reference.
- **Independent replication of microclot proteomics:** Seek any studies outside the Pretorius/Kell group confirming fibrinaloid microclot presence and fibrinolysis resistance in PASC, which would substantially strengthen question:0010.
- **RCT tracker:** Monitor ClinicalTrials.gov for completed anticoagulation/antiplatelet trials in PASC — the paper notes trials have been initiated but none completed at time of writing (2024-09-30).
- **Questions raised:**
  - Does microclot burden co-segregate with specific Long COVID symptom clusters (e.g., exercise intolerance, cognitive impairment) or is it diffuse? (Relevant to question:0010.)
  - What is the minimum effective anticoagulation regimen in PASC, and what are the bleeding risks in this population?
  - Can FMD or SDC-1 serve as stratification biomarkers for anticoagulation trial enrollment?
