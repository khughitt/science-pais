# Longitudinal Landscape of Long Flu and Long COVID

- **Authors:** Ming Zheng
- **Year:** 2026
- **Journal:** COVID (MDPI), vol. 6, no. 1, article 21
- **DOI:** 10.3390/covid6010021
- **BibTeX key:** Zheng2026
- **Source:** PDF

## Key Contribution

This paper provides the first large-scale, temporally stratified disease-wide association study (DWAS) of long flu alongside long COVID, using the nationwide Finnish FinnGen registry (9,204 influenza cases, 4,258 COVID-19 cases, 420,005 unexposed comparators; follow-up 1998–2021). It demonstrates that influenza, like SARS-CoV-2, triggers durable multi-organ sequelae extending well beyond the respiratory tract — including persistent cardiovascular, neurological, metabolic, and renal signatures that survive strict FDR correction (p < 0.001) and persist over 1-, 5-, and 15-year horizons. The study reframes influenza as a systemic event embedded in a chronic disease continuum and positions "long flu" as a clinically meaningful PAIS parallel to long COVID.

## Methods

- **Design:** Nationwide registry-based cohort study using the FinnGen infrastructure (Finland), linking three national registries: HILMO (all inpatient/outpatient specialist care), the Population Register Centre + National Death Registry (demographics, mortality), and Kela (prescription medication records).
- **Cohorts:**
  - Influenza exposed: 9,204 individuals with ICD-10 J09–J11 recorded in specialist inpatient/outpatient care, 1 January 1998 – 31 December 2021.
  - COVID-19 exposed: 4,258 individuals with ICD-10 U072.
  - Unexposed comparator: 420,005 individuals with no recorded influenza or pneumonia (J09–J18) across their entire available medical history.
- **Disease endpoints:** 110 harmonized clinical endpoints spanning ICD-10 and ICD-O-3 categories, covering circulatory, neurological, musculoskeletal, digestive, endocrine/metabolic, mental/behavioural, ocular, genitourinary, skin, respiratory, and oncologic domains.
- **Statistical approach:** Bidirectional (pre- and post-infection), temporally stratified DWAS using age- and sex-adjusted Cox proportional hazards models with FDR correction (threshold: FDR-adjusted p < 0.001). Time windows: pre-infection (predisposition analysis) and post-infection at 1, 5, and 15 years.
- **High-risk criterion:** Associations meeting all three: FDR-adjusted p < 0.001, HR > 2.0 or < 0.5, and minimum clinical prevalence > 0.5% in exposed cohort.

## Key Findings

### Pre-existing risk factors (predisposition analysis)
- Chronic multimorbidity — concentrated in cardiometabolic disease — strongly predicts clinical influenza diagnosis.
- Pre-existing CVD diagnoses showing the highest predisposition: heart failure, hypertension, coronary atherosclerosis, atrial fibrillation and flutter, pulmonary embolism, major coronary heart disease, stroke, myocardial infarction, venous thromboembolism. All associated with significantly elevated probability of future influenza diagnosis.
- Additional predisposing conditions: COPD, type 2 diabetes, rheumatoid arthritis, migraine, Alzheimer's disease, depression, chronic kidney disease, iron deficiency anaemia.
- The same pattern holds for COVID-19: pre-existing CVD is the most prominent risk category, with overlapping contributions from neurological, metabolic, and renal conditions.

### Post-infection sequelae — influenza (long flu)
- **Cardiovascular:** Elevated post-influenza risk for thromboembolic events (pulmonary embolism, venous thromboembolism) acutely in year 1, then attenuating; sustained elevated risk (1–5 years) for heart failure, atrial fibrillation and flutter, stroke, myocardial infarction, and major coronary heart disease events.
- **Neurological/psychiatric:** Durable increased incidence of migraine (with and without aura), Alzheimer's disease, dementia, and depression; evident within year 1 and persisting through the 5-year follow-up window. Suggestive but less-established signal extending toward 15 years for neurodegenerative outcomes.
- **Metabolic/renal:** New-onset type 2 diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and osteoporosis identified as multi-year sequelae. Type 2 diabetes signal parallels that seen in long COVID, consistent with shared chronic-inflammation/insulin-resistance mechanisms.
- **Musculoskeletal:** Arthrosis, osteoporosis, spinal stenosis among significant long-term incident outcomes.
- **Ocular:** Age-related macular degeneration signal detected post-influenza.
- **Oncologic:** Basal cell carcinoma shows a post-influenza association (mechanism unknown; may reflect immune surveillance disruption or healthcare detection bias).

### Post-infection sequelae — COVID-19 (long COVID)
- Broad system-spanning disease signatures confirmed across circulatory, musculoskeletal, nervous system, digestive, endocrine/metabolic, mental/behavioural, and oncologic domains.
- Both infections share cardiovascular and neurological sequelae but exhibit distinct organ-specific profiles; SARS-CoV-2 exerts a broader and more pronounced extrapulmonary toll (consistent with Xie & Al-Aly 2024).

### Comparative landscape
- Long flu and long COVID share cardiovascular and metabolic sequelae (type 2 diabetes, heart failure, stroke) but differ in breadth: long COVID has more extensive extrapulmonary involvement.
- The bidirectional design demonstrates a "vicious cycle": chronic disease increases infection susceptibility, and infection in turn drives further chronic disease accumulation.

## Relevance

This paper directly addresses the project research question (research-question:post-acute-infection-syndromes) on multiple levels:

1. **Shared PAIS failure mode:** The DWAS demonstrates that influenza triggers a post-acute syndrome phenotypically similar to long COVID — persistent multi-organ morbidity with cardiovascular and neurological dominance — establishing long flu as a genuine PAIS and not merely an analogy. This is strong population-level evidence for the project's central frame: failed homeostatic recovery after acute infection as a convergent, pathogen-independent failure mode.

2. **Bidirectional vulnerability:** The predisposition analysis reveals that pre-existing chronic disease (particularly cardiometabolic) concentrates infection risk and amplifies PAIS susceptibility — consistent with a model of "reduced physiological reserve" where homeostatic buffers are already depleted before the acute insult.

3. **Temporal persistence:** Risk elevations persisting over 5–15 year windows are inconsistent with simple acute inflammation and imply mechanisms of durable immune or physiological dysregulation (endothelial dysfunction, chronic low-grade inflammation, pro-thrombotic state, neurotropic viral damage) that fail to resolve — a hallmark of PAIS as homeostatic failure.

4. **Cross-pathogen generalizability:** By conducting parallel DWAS for influenza and SARS-CoV-2 in the same registry framework, the paper provides strong evidence that the PAIS failure mode is not unique to SARS-CoV-2 but extends to at least one other respiratory RNA virus, supporting the project's hypothesis of shared post-infectious failure mechanisms.

5. **Pre-existing multimorbidity as effect modifier:** The concentration of risk in individuals with substantial prior chronic disease aligns with the project frame that PAIS severity reflects the degree of pre-infection homeostatic strain.

## Limitations

- **Observational design:** Cox models adjusted only for age and sex; no adjustment for vaccination status, socioeconomic status, smoking, physical activity, or baseline comorbidity burden. Residual confounding is a primary concern.
- **Specialist-care ascertainment only:** Influenza cases required a specialist inpatient/outpatient ICD code (J09–J11), which excludes the large majority of influenza managed in primary care or self-managed at home. The exposed cohort is therefore enriched for more severe, clinically significant influenza — findings may not generalize to mild community-acquired influenza.
- **Selection bias / immortal-time bias:** The study acknowledges potential immortal-time bias and competing risks but did not implement sensitivity analyses (propensity score matching, landmark analyses, competing risks models) due to complexity and desire to maintain statistical power.
- **Influenza virological confirmation absent:** ICD J09–J11 includes clinically diagnosed influenza without mandatory laboratory confirmation; case misclassification is possible, though specialist-care coding reduces this risk relative to primary care.
- **No individual-level vaccination data:** Vaccination substantially modifies both susceptibility and post-infection sequelae (a ~34% reduction in major cardiovascular events cited in the paper); the inability to adjust for this likely attenuates the post-infection effect estimates (vaccinated individuals may have less severe PAIS).
- **COVID-19 cohort small and era-specific:** 4,258 COVID-19 cases from specialist care, limited to 2020–2021 (pre-Omicron); may not reflect current long COVID burden.
- **Single author / single-centre analysis:** Single-author study from the Human Disease Continuum Project; independent replication and external peer validation of analytic choices would strengthen confidence.
- **No mechanistic data:** The DWAS design identifies associations; it cannot distinguish direct viral pathogenesis from immune-mediated sequelae, from healthcare-detection bias, or from shared risk-factor confounding.

## Follow-up

- **Xie Y et al. (Lancet Infect. Dis. 2024):** Direct comparison of long-term outcomes following hospital admission for COVID-19 versus seasonal influenza — cited as evidence of distinct organ-tropism profiles; should be read alongside this paper.
- **Mechanistic follow-up:** Kenney et al. (Sci. Adv. 2022) on direct influenza virus replication in cardiomyocytes driving cardiac fibrosis — provides mechanistic underpinning for the cardiovascular sequelae reported here.
- **Vaccination and PAIS:** Behrouzi et al. (JAMA Netw. Open 2022) meta-analysis linking influenza vaccination to reduced cardiovascular risk — relevant to whether vaccination attenuates long flu phenotype.
- **Project question:** Do the pre-existing multimorbidity patterns that predict long flu susceptibility overlap with those predicting ME/CFS susceptibility or long COVID susceptibility? A cross-PAIS predisposition comparison using FinnGen or UK Biobank could test the "shared vulnerability" hypothesis directly.
- **Project question:** Is the 15-year cardiovascular risk elevation in long flu explained by chronic low-grade inflammation (persistent CRP elevation, immune activation markers) or by direct structural cardiac/vascular remodelling from viral cardiomyocyte invasion?
