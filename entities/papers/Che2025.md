# Heightened innate immunity may trigger chronic inflammation, fatigue and post-exertional malaise in ME/CFS

- **Authors:** Xiaoyu Che, Amit Ranjan, Cheng Guo, Keming Zhang, Rochelle Goldsmith, Susan Levine, Kegan J. Moneghetti, Yali Zhai, Liner Ge, Nischay Mishra, Mady Hornig, Lucinda Bateman, Nancy G. Klimas, Jose G. Montoya, Daniel L. Peterson, Sabra L. Klein, Oliver Fiehn, Anthony L. Komaroff, W. Ian Lipkin
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal:** npj Metabolic Health and Disease, vol. 3, no. 1, p. 34
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s44324-025-00079-w
- **BibTeX key:** Che2025
- **Source:** PDF

## Key Contribution

This multi-omics study (Columbia, Harvard, UC Davis, and 10 other sites) provides a mechanistic model of ME/CFS pathophysiology by integrating plasma metabolomics (913 analytes), plasma proteomics (6,969 proteins via SomaScan), and ex-vivo immune responses to microbial superantigens and pattern-recognition receptor agonists, measured before and 24 hours after a standardised cardiopulmonary exercise challenge. The central finding is that ME/CFS subjects exhibit an exaggerated innate immune response to microbial antigens that, together with secondary metabolic, mitochondrial, and gut-barrier failures, creates a self-reinforcing disease state manifesting as chronic inflammation, fatigue, and post-exertional malaise (PEM). The paper explicitly positions ME/CFS as a post-infectious failed-recovery syndrome and offers candidate biomarkers and therapeutic targets across at least seven convergent pathobiological pathways.

## Methods

**Study design and cohort.** Prospective case-control, 56 ME/CFS and 52 healthy controls (HC), 47 case-control pairs matched for sex, age (±5 years), race/ethnicity, geography, date of exercise, and blood sampling (±12 weeks). Diagnosed by CDC/Fukuda and Canadian Consensus Criteria. Subgroup of 36 ME/CFS and 34 HC enrolled for proteomics. All subjects underwent a cardiopulmonary exercise test (CPX) on an upright ergocycle (ramp protocol per consensus guidelines); blood drawn pre-exercise (same day) and 24 h post-exercise after a 12 h fast.

**Clinical assessments.** Multidimensional Fatigue Inventory (MFI; general/mental/physical fatigue, reduced activity, reduced motivation scored 0-100), Short Form 36 (SF-36), DePaul Symptom Questionnaire (DSQ), Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI).

**Immune stimulation.** TruCulture ex-vivo whole-blood system: null, LPS, *Staphylococcus aureus* enterotoxin B (SEB), heat-killed *Candida albicans* (HKCA), polyinosinic:polycytidylic acid (poly I:C) stimulation; supernatant cytokines measured by TruCulture OptiMAPTM assay (CXCL5, GM-CSF, IFN-γ, IL-1β, IL-10, IL-12p70, IL-13, IL-17, IL-2, IL-23, IL-6, IL-8, TNF-α). PBMC in-vitro stimulation with LPS (0.01, 0.1, 1, 100 ng/ml) and poly I:C (0.2, 2, 20 μg/ml) for IL-6 measurement at 12, 24, 48 h.

**Metabolomics.** Untargeted multi-platform MS: GC-TOF MS for primary metabolites (mono/disaccharides, hydroxyl- and amino acids); HILIC-QTOF MS for biogenic amines including microbial compounds; LC/QTOF MS for complex lipids; CSH-QTOF MS for targeted oxylipins. Total 935 metabolic analytes; 22 excluded (>50% below detection). 220 metabolites cross-validated against an independent Germain et al. cohort (60 ME/CFS, 45 HC, two maximal exercise challenges). Bayesian LMM framework with ChemRICH enrichment and IPA pathway analysis.

**Proteomics.** SomaScan Assay v4.1 (SomaLogic): 7,285 proteins assayed; 6,969 passing QC. Linear mixed-effects models with Bayesian analysis, FDR adjusted at 0.05.

**Statistics.** LMMs adjusted for age, sex, race/ethnicity, geography, BMI, sr-IBS. Bayesian analysis using rstan and bayestestR packages; BayesFactor (BF) ≥ 3, 95% HDI not covering 0, plus FDR p < 0.05 as significance thresholds.

## Key Findings

**Cohort characteristics (Table 1).** MFI scores were dramatically higher in ME/CFS across all subscales at all three time points (pre-exercise general fatigue 69.4 ± 12.0 vs. 19.0 ± 14.6, p < 0.001; physical fatigue 84.2 ± 17.4 vs. 11.7 ± 11.6, p < 0.001). Higher sr-IBS comorbidity in ME/CFS (18% vs. 2%, p = 0.016).

**1. Exaggerated innate immune cytokine responses.**
- Following SEB stimulation, CXCL5, GM-CSF, IL-1β, IL-2, IL-6, IL-8, IL-23, and TNF-α were all elevated in ME/CFS blood before exercise (BF and FDR criteria met).
- SEB drives T-cell expansion via MHC class II superantigen pathway; cytokine profiles indicate elevations in superantigen-induced T-cell mediated immune responses.
- After HKCA stimulation, IFN-γ and TNF-α elevated in ME/CFS before exercise; IL-1β elevated (p_adj = 0.073, borderline).
- Female-specific and age-stratified effects: female ME/CFS < 45 years had significantly higher CXCL5, IL-1β, IL-6, and IL-8 vs. female HC < 45 years (Fig. 1B). Plasma testosterone negatively associated with age in females (p = 0.015), suggesting gonadal steroid modulation of immune tone.
- Post-exercise: female ME/CFS showed decreased GM-CSF, IFN-γ, IL-1β, IL-6, IL-10, TNF-α in response to HKCA — interpreted as immune exhaustion or dysregulation by subpopulations of immune cells following exertion.
- No case-control difference in LPS/poly I:C IL-6 responses; attributed to saturation of TLR cognate receptors at stimulant concentrations used.

**2. Proteomic correlates of innate immune activation.**
- Colony stimulating factor 1 (CSF1) and low-density lipoprotein receptor-related protein 8 (LRP8) were lower in ME/CFS baseline, consistent with impaired DC/monocyte maturation and inadequate inflammation resolution.
- Protein C (proC) and BATF3 elevated pre- and post-exercise. proC is anticoagulant and contributes to vascular and epithelial barrier integrity; BATF3 is essential for CD8a+CD103+ dendritic cell development.
- Endogenous retrovirus group V member 1 (ERVV1) elevated post-exercise; associated with inflammation and neurological disorders.

**3. Disrupted ECM homeostasis and cell adhesion.**
- IPA pathway analysis: integrin cell surface interactions, VEGF signaling, leukocyte adhesion and diapedesis, ECM degradation pathways all reduced before exercise.
- L-selectin (SELL), CDH5 (VE-cadherin), ADGRD1, CLEC3B, COMP reduced at baseline.
- HTRA1 (serine protease degrading ECM) elevated; CLEC3B (tenascin), CD93, COMP lower.
- Post-exercise: SELL, ADGRD1, CLEC3B, COMP remained low; CDH1 elevated. IL-6, BMP1, WNT5A elevated in long-duration ME/CFS (>3 years).
- Interpretation: reduced cell-cell adhesion promotes translocation of microbial products from gut into systemic circulation, triggering innate immune responses and impairing tissue-resident innate lymphoid cells.

**4. Mitochondrial dysfunction.**
- GDF15 (biomarker of mitochondrial disease) higher post-exercise in ME/CFS vs. HC; positively correlated with MFI scores (physical fatigue and reduced activity).
- Citric acid (TCA cycle intermediate) increased post-exercise in ME/CFS but decreased in HC; authors interpret this as reduced mitochondrial flux with citrate accumulation, consistent with impaired TCA cycle.
- Phosphate decreased post-exercise in HC but not ME/CFS, suggesting failure of mitochondrial phosphate utilisation for ATP production (OXPHOS failure signal).
- Acylcarnitines (especially CAR 12:0) reduced after exercise in ME/CFS, indicating reduced fatty acid flux into mitochondria for β-oxidation; previously validated in two independent ME/CFS cohorts.
- 12,13-diHOME (cold-induced lipokine that promotes brown adipocyte FA uptake) lower at baseline in ME/CFS vs. HC; both 12,13-diHOME and 12,13-diHOME precursor linoleic acid increased post-exercise in ME/CFS (borderline significance for 12,13-diHOME, significant for LA).
- EIF1 (translational initiation factor) elevated: may indicate cellular leakage into plasma due to errors in translational initiation.
- UBE2D3 (ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme) elevated: consistent with protein misfolding burden and UPS activity.

**5. Abnormalities in lipid metabolism.**
- Triglycerides and diglycerides elevated in ME/CFS before and after exercise (ChemRICH and IPA; Fig. 2A, B).
- Carnitines, particularly acylcarnitines (CAR 12:0), lower after exercise, indicating depleted mitochondrial FA transport capacity.
- Accumulated triglycerides and diglycerides can trigger inflammation, cellular stress, insulin resistance, and further mitochondrial dysfunction.

**6. Urea cycle disruption.**
- Ornithine:citrulline (ORN:CIT) and arginine:citrulline (ARG:CIT) ratios elevated before exercise in ME/CFS, reflecting impaired initial steps of urea cycle.
- Following exercise, ORN:CIT and ARG:CIT ratios decreased; this is consistent with impairments in later steps where CIT reacts with aspartate to form argininosuccinate, converted to ARG.
- Disruptions primarily driven by alterations in CIT levels, possibly due to reduced activity of CIT transporters or urea cycle enzymes.

**7. Tryptophan-serotonin-kynurenine pathway dysregulation.**
- KYN:TRP ratios lower in ME/CFS before and after exercise (irrespective of antidepressant medication); increased KYN:TRP after exercise suggests post-exercise overactivation of KYN pathway.
- KYNA:KYN ratios higher in ME/CFS before exercise, decreased after exercise. Post-exercise KYNA reduction relative to KYN indicates reduced kynuramine aminotransferase (KAT) activity.
- Elevated 3-methoxyanthranilate (3-MA) post-exercise correlated with general fatigue (Fig. 3B) — 3-MA reflects activated KYN pathway, leading to neurotoxicity and neuroinflammation.
- Indole-3-lactate/indole lactic acid (ILA) depleted at baseline; ILA is an anti-inflammatory metabolite produced by Bifidobacterium, and its reduction reflects gut dysbiosis.
- TRP diversion from serotonin (5-HT) synthesis toward KYN pathway leads to serotonin deficiency, contributing to cognitive dysfunction and mood symptoms.

**8. GI mucosal integrity and dysbiosis.**
- 2,6-Diaminopimelic acid (DAP; bacterial cell wall component) decreased post-exercise in HC but not ME/CFS, suggesting impaired xenobiotic detoxification or altered microbiome composition.
- Leucate (2-hydroxy-4-methylpentanoic acid, microbial metabolite) lower in ME/CFS.
- Citrulline lower in ME/CFS (trending; CIT produced by enterocytes and important for intestinal mucosal integrity).
- TFF1 (trefoil factor secreted by mucus-producing cells) reduced in ME/CFS, indicating impaired gut-barrier restitution.
- Plasma propionic acid (PPA) elevated after exercise in ME/CFS; PPA can disrupt mitochondrial biogenesis and induce neuroinflammation.
- Glucuronic acid higher in ME/CFS at baseline and after exercise; elevated glucuronic acid reflects dysbiosis and dysregulated xenobiotic metabolism (gut β-glucuronidases from elevated Bacteroidetes cleave glucuronide conjugates).

**9. Calcium signaling and complement activation.**
- Calcitonin receptor (CALCR) higher in ME/CFS before and after exercise.
- RAMP3 and S100A8 higher post-exercise. S100A8 accelerates inflammation during infection or hypersensitivity and activates NLRP3 inflammasome.
- C1R and complement factor H-related protein 4 (CFHR4) elevated in ME/CFS after exercise. C1R activates classical complement pathway; CFHR4 regulates alternative pathway.
- Complement activation can lead to fatigue, systemic inflammation, and cognitive impairment (consistent with PEM).

**10. Redox imbalance (copper-dependent antioxidant pathways).**
- Retina-specific copper amine oxidase (AOC2) and copper homeostasis protein CutC homolog (CUTC) elevated post-exercise in ME/CFS. Elevated AOC2 and CUTC indicate increased oxidative stress from reactive oxygen species (ROS).
- MAPK6 also elevated post-exercise; induced by oxidative stress, leads to IL-8 production and epithelial-derived factor secretion in chemotaxis.
- These findings are consistent with a prolonged oxidative state and increasing oxidative stress following exercise.

**11. Analyte-symptom correlations (Fig. 3).**
- Lower baseline 12,13-diHOME in ME/CFS correlated with higher MFI physical fatigue and reduced activity scores.
- Higher post-exercise GDF15 positively correlated with MFI general fatigue, physical fatigue, and reduced activity.
- Reduced CNTN4 at baseline correlated with higher general fatigue scores.
- Reduced CLEC3B pre- and post-exercise correlated with higher physical fatigue and reduced activity/motivation.
- Higher post-exercise S100A8 and C1r correlated with MFI reduced activity/motivation.

## Relevance

This paper is directly central to the project's core research question — "Why do some people fail to recover after acute infection, and what shared mechanisms link PAIS?" — on multiple levels:

**1. Multi-omics mechanistic map of PAIS chronicity.** The study provides the most comprehensive published multi-omics characterisation of ME/CFS, a paradigmatic PAIS, identifying at least seven converging pathobiological pathways (innate immunity, ECM/cell-adhesion, mitochondrial dysfunction, lipid metabolism, urea cycle, tryptophan-kynurenine, and gut dysbiosis) each of which is individually consistent with failed homeostatic recovery after infection. The convergence across orthogonal measurement modalities (cytokine, metabolomics, proteomics) substantially increases mechanistic credibility.

**2. Exercise challenge as a PEM provocateur.** The design uses a cardiopulmonary exercise test to elicit PEM and measures the multi-omic response at 24 h, directly capturing the physiological signature of failed recovery from an acute energy demand. This is methodologically analogous to the project's broader frame of "failed recovery from an acute challenge" (where the challenge is infectious rather than exertional, but both probe the same homeostatic circuits).

**3. Innate immune priming as the initiating failure mode.** The exaggerated cytokine responses to SEB, HKCA superantigens — but not LPS/poly I:C TLR agonists — suggest that ME/CFS involves heightened responses specifically to complex microbial antigens that engage innate-adaptive immune crosstalk (superantigen + MHC class II), rather than a broad TLR hyperreactivity. This is consistent with an infection-primed innate immune dysregulation as the primary lesion sustaining chronicity.

**4. Gut-barrier failure as a link between dysbiosis and systemic innate activation.** The combined evidence of reduced cell adhesion proteins, reduced TFF1, depleted ILA and leucate, and elevated glucuronic acid supports a model in which reduced epithelial integrity allows microbial products to reach systemic circulation, where they chronically stimulate innate immune cells — a mechanism that bridges gut dysbiosis (frequently observed in post-COVID and ME/CFS) to persistent innate immune activation. This directly supports the project's hypothesis of a self-reinforcing homeostatic failure attractor.

**5. Metabolic-immune vicious cycle.** Mitochondrial dysfunction reduces energy for immune resolution; triglyceride accumulation from impaired β-oxidation worsens inflammation; oxidative stress worsens mitochondrial function. The mutual reinforcement of these pathways is precisely the "stable failure-mode attractor" frame the project uses to explain why PAIS does not spontaneously resolve.

**6. Exercise exacerbation as a model for infection-triggered relapse.** The finding that many abnormalities are worse after exercise (complement activation, oxidative stress, mitochondrial signals, tryptophan diversion) provides a template for understanding how acute triggers — including recurrent infections — perpetuate chronicity rather than resolving it.

**7. Serotonin depletion and cognitive symptoms.** Tryptophan diversion from the serotonin pathway to kynurenine after exercise, with elevated neurotoxic KYN metabolites (3-MA, KYNA:KYN shifts), directly ties metabolic failure to the cognitive and mood symptoms that are a core feature of PAIS and points to measurable neurochemical endpoints for future study.

## Limitations

- **Cross-sectional case-control at a single exercise time point.** Causal direction of the metabolic/proteomic abnormalities cannot be established; whether innate immune hyperreactivity precedes or follows metabolic dysfunction is not resolved.
- **Single post-exercise blood draw at 24 h.** The kinetics of PEM recovery typically extend 24-72 h; the 24 h snapshot may miss the peak or resolution of some analytes. No 48 h omics data (clinical MFI collected at 48 h but no matched blood draw for omics).
- **No LPS/poly I:C case-control differences.** Authors attribute this to TLR saturation at the stimulant concentrations used; however, it limits conclusions about TLR-4 and TLR-3-mediated innate immunity specifically. PBMC sample size for the PBMC stimulation experiment was very small (n = 8 ME/CFS, n = 7 HC).
- **Subgroup-specific effects may be underpowered.** Age- and sex-stratified analyses within ME/CFS subgroups are exploratory; many did not reach statistical significance (p_adj < 0.10) in the TruCulture analyses.
- **External validation limited to metabolomics only.** The 220-metabolite cross-validation against Germain et al. is reassuring, but no independent validation exists for the proteomic findings or cytokine exaggeration results.
- **Mostly female cohort.** 66% female in metabolomics/TruCulture arm; male-specific findings are underpowered and some sex-stratified comparisons are not significant.
- **No functional mechanistic experiments.** The paper describes associations; it does not demonstrate that innate immune blockade, carnitine supplementation, or TRP pathway intervention corrects any measured abnormality in ME/CFS cells or animal models.
- **sr-IBS imbalance.** ME/CFS group had significantly higher sr-IBS (18% vs. 2%, p = 0.016), which may confound gut-related metabolite findings; authors included sr-IBS as a covariate in LMMs but the imbalance remains a concern.
- **No longitudinal recovery data.** The study does not follow participants to remission or partial recovery; it cannot address which pathways normalise in improvers vs. non-improvers.

## Follow-up

- Read Germain et al. (JCI Insight 7, e157621, 2022) — the independent metabolomics cohort used for cross-validation; 60 ME/CFS / 45 HC with two maximal exercise challenges, providing the prior distributions used in Bayesian analyses here.
- Read Humer et al. (Front. Immunol. 16, 1483764, 2025) and Paul et al. (Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. USA 118, e2024358118, 2021) — cited for trained innate immunity and redox imbalance in ME/CFS and long COVID, which the Discussion explicitly positions this paper alongside.
- Read Shankar et al. (Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. USA 122, e2426564122, 2025) — cited for oxidative stress as a shared characteristic of ME/CFS and long COVID, a direct complement to the copper-pathway findings here.
- Assess whether the seven-pathway convergent model (innate immunity, ECM/adhesion, mitochondrial dysfunction, lipid metabolism, urea cycle, tryptophan-kynurenine, gut dysbiosis) maps onto similar multi-omic signatures in long COVID or post-Lyme cohorts — a key question for the project's shared-failure-mode frame.
- Investigate whether the exercise-triggered immune exhaustion phenotype (reduced HKCA-induced cytokines post-exercise in female ME/CFS) can be used as a stratification signal to identify ME/CFS subgroups with distinct recovery trajectories.
- Examine whether the GDF15-MFI correlation and the 12,13-diHOME-MFI correlation together define a "metabolic severity index" that could serve as an objective PEM severity measure in clinical trials.
- Consider whether the superantigen-specific (SEB/HKCA) but not TLR-specific (LPS/poly I:C) innate immune hyperreactivity pattern is also present in long COVID — this would support a shared mechanism that is upstream of pathogen-specific differences.
