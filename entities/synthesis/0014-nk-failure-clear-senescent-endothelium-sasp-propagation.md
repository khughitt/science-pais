## State

This hypothesis proposes that NK-cell and macrophage cytotoxic failure to clear virus-induced senescent endothelial cells is the rate-limiting determinant of PAIS persistence, operating through SASP-mediated paracrine propagation of senescence to neighboring endothelium. The central reframing is that persistence is a clearance-capacity problem rather than an initial injury-magnitude problem — a distinction that generates the stratified prediction that senolytics benefit NK-low individuals preferentially.

The epistemic status is proposed and one-source. The anchoring reference is `cite:Nunes2026`, a mechanistic review and model linking virus-induced endothelial senescence and dysfunctional immune clearance to ME/CFS and long COVID; this hypothesis sharpens that framing by naming NK/macrophage cytotoxic failure as the specific clearance determinant and adding the endotype-stratified senolytic prediction.

No direct measurement of NK or macrophage clearance of senescent endothelial cells in a PAIS cohort exists. The NK-cytotoxicity deficit substrate invoked from ME/CFS literature is inconsistent across studies and assay-dependent. Persistent senescent endothelial burden has not been demonstrated in PAIS tissue; the SASP-propagation claim is inferred from general senescence biology.
