# Hypothesis: PAIS fatigue/cognitive symptoms are a trigger-nonspecific neuroimmune sickness-behavior response, not trigger-specific immune pathology

## Organizing Conjecture

The shared PAIS core cluster — fatigue, cognitive slowing, unrefreshing sleep, malaise — is the
**stereotyped cytokine-induced sickness-behavior program** (Dantzer), a conserved, **trigger-nonspecific**
central response to inflammation, sustained by low-grade neuroinflammation *after* the acute trigger has
resolved. Under this reading, the project's convergence across triggers (`question:0001`,
`hypothesis:0001`) is real but **shallow**: it reflects a **single downstream common path** (the
neuroimmune sickness program), not convergence of many *upstream* trigger-specific mechanisms. The diverse
candidate drivers (antigen persistence, autoimmunity, microclots, EBV reactivation) would then be, for the
fatigue phenotype, **coincidental correlates of initial inflammatory magnitude** rather than distinct
causal routes — each merely a different way to switch on the same central program.

## Proposition Bundle

### Core Propositions

- The PAIS fatigue/cognitive core cluster **matches the cytokine-induced sickness-behavior phenotype** and
  is mediated centrally (neuroimmune), not by trigger-specific peripheral pathology.
- It is **trigger-nonspecific**: the same central program is reachable from any sufficiently inflammatory
  acute insult, so the phenotype is more similar across triggers than trigger-specific models predict.
- It is **sustained by persistent low-grade neuroinflammation** after the acute trigger resolves, decoupled
  from ongoing peripheral antigen/autoimmune/vascular processes.

### Supporting Or Auxiliary Propositions

- The various trigger-specific mechanisms are, for the *fatigue phenotype*, downstream correlates of the
  **magnitude** of the initiating inflammatory response rather than independent causes.
- This is distinct from the project's dauer/torpor conserved-response framing: this hypothesis is
  specifically the **Dantzer sickness-behavior / neuroinflammation** mechanism, a proximate CNS program,
  not an evolutionary hypometabolic-state analogy.

## Current Uncertainty

The hypothesis is a **downstream-common-path** claim, and its main fragility is that it is *compatible with*
`hypothesis:0001` rather than a clean rival — a shared attractor could operate *through* a sickness-behavior
final common pathway. It also risks being unfalsifiable-by-vagueness: "neuroinflammation sustains it" needs
a measurable neuroimmune correlate to be testable. Direct human evidence for *persistent* CNS
neuroinflammation driving chronic (not acute) PAIS fatigue is indirect (PET/CSF studies are small and
mixed), so the claim leans on the strength of the acute sickness-behavior model extrapolated to the chronic
phase.

## Predictions

**Strong / discriminating:**

- The fatigue/cognitive phenotype is **more similar across triggers** (COVID, EBV, Lyme, dengue, Q-fever)
  than trigger-specific mechanisms would predict — low between-trigger variance on the core cluster.
- A **common neuroinflammatory signature** (central cytokine/microglial markers, CSF/PET correlates)
  tracks the fatigue phenotype **independent of** trigger-specific peripheral markers.
- Trigger-specific peripheral markers (antigen, autoantibody, microclot burden) **do not add** predictive
  power for the fatigue phenotype once the central inflammatory signal is accounted for.

**Weaker / corollaries:**

- Fatigue-cluster severity scales with the **magnitude** of the initial inflammatory response across
  triggers.
- Anti-inflammatory / neuroimmune-targeted interventions affect the fatigue cluster **regardless of
  trigger**.

## Falsifiability

Confidence would be materially reduced if:

- The fatigue/cognitive phenotype shows **robust trigger-specific differences** (distinct symptom
  structure, biomarkers, or treatment response by trigger) beyond shared-inflammatory-magnitude effects —
  i.e. the convergence is *not* nonspecific.
- **Trigger-specific peripheral markers independently predict** the fatigue phenotype after adjusting for
  central inflammation (antigen/autoimmune/vascular arms carry non-redundant causal signal).
- No **persistent central neuroinflammatory correlate** is found in chronic PAIS fatigue despite adequate
  assays, while peripheral mechanisms *do* correlate — undercutting the "sustained by neuroinflammation"
  core.
- A trigger-specific therapy (e.g. antigen clearance, `question:0002`) resolves the fatigue phenotype in
  its trigger but not others — evidence for a trigger-specific, not common, driver.

## Supporting Evidence

- **Komaroff2023 (paper, literature):** synthesizes the ME/CFS–long-COVID convergence and the
  neuroinflammatory / central-fatigue framing consistent with a shared sickness-behavior final common
  pathway (`source_refs`).
- **Dantzer sickness-behavior program (literature, mechanistic):** cytokine-induced sickness behavior
  reproduces the exact fatigue/cognitive/anhedonia cluster and is a conserved, trigger-nonspecific central
  response — the mechanistic backbone of this hypothesis.

## Disputing Evidence

- Evidence for **trigger-specific** mechanistic signatures (e.g. distinct autoantibody or antigen profiles
  associated with particular post-infectious syndromes) argues against a purely nonspecific common path.
- The hypothesis's **compatibility** with `hypothesis:0001` means convergence evidence does not
  discriminate between "shared attractor" and "shared sickness-behavior endpoint" — an interpretive, not
  empirical, weakness that keeps this from being a decisive null.

## Evidence Needed To Shift Belief

- **Most efficient upward (toward this hypothesis):** a cross-trigger study showing low between-trigger
  variance on the fatigue cluster, a shared central neuroinflammatory correlate, and no incremental
  prediction from trigger-specific peripheral markers.
- **Most efficient downward:** demonstration that trigger-specific peripheral markers independently drive
  the fatigue phenotype, or robust phenotypic differences by trigger.
- **Most discriminating next test:** a harmonized cross-pathogen cohort (cf. `question:0001`) measuring the
  fatigue cluster, central neuroinflammatory markers, and trigger-specific peripheral markers together —
  testing whether the central signal is common and sufficient while the peripheral signals are redundant.
