# Post-Infectious Dysautonomia, Organ-Specific Sequelae, and Autoimmunity

## Summary

Beyond fatigue and cognitive impairment, PAIS produce a recurrent set of organ-level and autonomic abnormalities — dysautonomia (notably POTS), small-fiber neuropathy (SFN), microvascular endothelial dysfunction, mast-cell activation, and a broad post-infectious autoimmune signature. This topic asks whether these are a convergent peripheral/autoimmune substrate shared across triggers or trigger- and organ-specific phenomena. The evidence supports that (1) post-infectious autoimmunity is real and broad after SARS-CoV-2 (Sharma2023 large cohorts; Rojas2022 near-universal latent autoimmunity), and (2) SFN/dysautonomia recurs across long COVID, PTLDS, and ME/CFS and is a leading candidate "final common pathway" for autonomic symptoms (Adler2024, Limongelli2026). What is contested is causality and mechanism heterogeneity: dysautonomia has at least three distinct substrates (Finsterer2022), some of it is pharmacologically reversible (Salvucci2023), and most "autoimmunity" is latent and of uncertain prognostic weight (Rojas2022).

## Key Concepts

**Post-infectious autoimmunity at scale.** Sharma2023 synthesizes two large retrospective cohorts (Chang et al. n=887,455 matched pairs; Tesch et al. n=640,701 vs 1,560,357 controls) showing elevated new-onset autoimmune disease after SARS-CoV-2 (RA aHR 2.98, SLE 2.99, T1DM 2.68, IBD 1.78, vasculitis 1.96), invoking molecular mimicry, bystander activation, epitope spreading, and autoantibody induction. Rojas2022 (100 PCS patients) finds latent autoimmunity in 83% and polyautoimmunity in 62% at ~7 months, with autoantibody load correlated with anti-spike response — consistent with bystander activation scaling with the antiviral response. Anti-IFN-λ associated with respiratory-symptom persistence [@Sharma2023; @Rojas2022].

**Small-fiber neuropathy as a convergent substrate.** Adler2024 argues dysautonomia (chiefly POTS) is an under-recognized PTLDS complication and proposes SFN — particularly a *non-length-dependent, autonomic* pattern targeting dorsal root ganglia — as a final common pathway shared with PASC and ME/CFS. Limongelli2026 (post-vaccine PASC SFN, n=71) finds innate-like NKG2D+ T cells and mobilized CD34+DNAM-1bright precursors with antigen-independent cytotoxicity — a profile shared with SLE/Crohn's/RA, and ~33% non-length-dependent (autoimmune-pattern) SFN [@Adler2024; @Limongelli2026].

**Mechanistic heterogeneity of dysautonomia.** Finsterer2022 stresses that post-COVID dysautonomia is not one entity: it includes peripheral SFN, abortive GBS/AMSAN residual, central HPA-axis disruption (hypophysitis), and drug-induced neuropathy — distinguishable only by objective autonomic testing and requiring different management.

**Pharmacologically reversible mast-cell-driven subtype.** Salvucci2023 (n=14 treated vs 13 controls) reports dual H1/H2 blockade improved cardiovascular, fatigue, cognitive, and GI symptoms in MCAS-attributed long COVID (29% complete remission vs 0%), and 57% had HR normalize within 20 days — evidence for a reversible, mediator-driven dysautonomia subtype distinct from structural autonomic damage [@Salvucci2023].

**Endothelial/vascular sequelae.** Stahlberg2025 (n=92 PACS, median 31 months) finds peripheral microvascular endothelial dysfunction (RHI <1.67) in 41% and rising NT-proBNP over ~16 months only in the impaired/dysfunctional groups — linking vascular homeostatic failure to progressive subclinical cardiac stress [@Stahlberg2025].

**Musculoskeletal/rheumatic phenotypes.** Ciaffi2023 (54 studies) documents post-acute arthralgia (2-65%), new-onset inflammatory arthritis/PMR-like presentations, fibromyalgia in 31-40%, and inconsistent ACPA/RF — framing SARS-CoV-2 as a tolerance-loss trigger via mimicry, bystander activation, and epitope spreading [@Ciaffi2023].

## Current State of Knowledge

### What the evidence supports

- New-onset autoimmune disease incidence rises measurably after SARS-CoV-2 across many diagnoses in very large cohorts (Sharma2023).
- Latent autoimmunity/autoantibody breadth is near-universal in symptomatic PCS and scales with the antiviral humoral response (Rojas2022).
- SFN and dysautonomia recur across long COVID, post-vaccine PASC, PTLDS, and ME/CFS, with a recurrent non-length-dependent autoimmune-pattern subtype (Adler2024, Limongelli2026).
- At least one dysautonomia subtype (mast-cell-mediated) is pharmacologically reversible (Salvucci2023).
- Durable microvascular endothelial dysfunction links to progressive subclinical cardiac stress (Stahlberg2025).

### What is contested or unresolved

- **Causation of autoimmunity.** Sharma2023 and Ciaffi2023 emphasize that temporal association and mimicry are hypotheses; most autoantibody findings are latent, and only ~3% of Rojas2022 patients had overt disease at 7 months. Long-term conversion rate is unknown.
- **Which dysautonomia substrate dominates.** Finsterer2022's three-substrate model is under-tested; the fraction that is SFN vs GBS-residual vs central HPA is unmeasured.
- **Cause vs correlate of the immune SFN signature.** Limongelli2026 lacks a vaccinated-asymptomatic comparator, so the innate-like T-cell shift may be cause, consequence, or correlate.

### Tensions between papers

Salvucci2023's reversible mast-cell-mediated framing contrasts with the structural/autoimmune-neuropathy framing of Adler2024 and Limongelli2026 — implying dysautonomia is mechanistically plural, and that cross-PAIS "convergence" at the symptom level (POTS) may conceal divergent substrates. This recapitulates the project-wide phenotype-vs-mechanism caution.

## Controversies and Open Questions

- Do PTLDS, PASC, and ME/CFS share a non-length-dependent autonomic SFN signature distinguishing them from primary dysautonomia (Adler2024, Limongelli2026)?
- What fraction of post-COVID dysautonomia is SFN vs GBS-residual vs central HPA disruption (Finsterer2022)?
- What fraction of latent post-infectious autoimmunity converts to overt disease over 5-10 years, and are anti-cytokine autoantibodies the highest-risk subset (Rojas2022, Sharma2023)?
- Is peripheral microvascular endothelial dysfunction (low RHI) a cross-PAIS feature or COVID-specific (Stahlberg2025)?
