# Gut Microbiome, Barrier Function, and Gut-Brain Axis in PAIS

## Summary

The PAIS gut-axis evidence is now strongest as a **recurring subsystem**, not as a standalone causal
explanation. Long COVID and ME/CFS both show gut dysbiosis and reduced SCFA/butyrate-producing capacity or
related microbial-metabolite abnormalities. The most defensible shared signal is functional: depleted
beneficial butyrate/SCFA ecology, barrier/metabolite stress, and plausible gut-immune-brain signaling.

The causal position remains unresolved. Gut dysbiosis could be a baseline susceptibility factor, an acute
infection injury, a chronic-loop amplifier, a consequence of reduced activity/diet/medication, or different
combinations in different disease stages.

## Key Concepts

**Long-COVID dysbiosis persistence.** Liu2022 followed acute COVID-19 patients to 6 months and found that
non-PACS patients moved back toward non-COVID microbiome profiles, while PACS cases retained dysbiosis
with higher *Ruminococcus gnavus* / *Bacteroides vulgatus* and lower *Faecalibacterium prausnitzii*.
Butyrate-producing taxa had the largest inverse correlations with PACS. Su2023 extends the signal beyond
1 year in the same CUHK research line.

**ME/CFS butyrate-capacity deficit.** Guo2023 shows reduced *F. prausnitzii* and *Eubacterium rectale*,
deficient butyrate-producing capacity by functional metagenomics/qPCR/fecal SCFAs, and association with
fatigue severity. Xiong2023 supports reduced butyrate-related host-microbiome signaling but shows strong
duration dependence: short-term ME/CFS is more microbiome-dysbiotic, while long-term ME/CFS can remain
metabolically/clinically abnormal after overt microbial dysbiosis partly resolves.

**Gut barrier and microbial-metabolite integration.** Che2025 places gut dysbiosis markers inside a wider
ME/CFS multi-omic state: reduced indole lactic acid, leucate, citrulline/TFF1 signals, elevated glucuronic
acid and propionic acid, and exaggerated responses to microbial antigens. Walitt2024 adds reduced gut
microbiome alpha diversity in a small, rigorously adjudicated PI-ME/CFS cohort.

**Gut-brain signaling.** Wong2023 links viral persistence and type-I-interferon inflammation to reduced
intestinal tryptophan absorption, peripheral serotonin depletion, vagal impairment, and memory effects.
This is a gut-brain mechanism, but it is not direct evidence that microbiome composition causes PAIS.

**Intervention signal.** Lau2024 tests SIM01, a synbiotic microbiome-modulating preparation, in 463 PACS
patients. Several symptoms improved and the microbiome shifted, but objective functional endpoints did
not clearly move and the product is mechanistically broad [@Lau2024].

## Current State of Knowledge

### What the evidence supports

- Gut dysbiosis and reduced SCFA/butyrate-producing capacity recur in long COVID and ME/CFS.
- The long-COVID signal can persist beyond acute infection and tracks with persistent symptoms.
- ME/CFS evidence independently supports deficient butyrate ecology, not only nonspecific "dysbiosis."
- Microbiome modulation can improve some long-COVID symptoms in at least one randomized trial.

### What is unresolved

- Driver versus consequence: diet, antibiotics, reduced activity, GI comorbidity, acute severity, and
  geography are all serious confounders.
- Timing: Xiong2023 implies the gut axis may be stronger near onset than in long-duration disease.
- Specificity: no comparable mature microbiome evidence was identified for PTLDS, QFS, post-dengue, or
  post-SARS fatigue in this pass.
- Mechanism: stool composition, barrier injury, microbial metabolites, viral persistence, and vagal
  signaling are related but distinct measurements.

## Relevance to This Project

This topic supplies a concrete loop node for `hypothesis:0001`: gut dysbiosis/SCFA depletion can connect
immune activation, epithelial barrier failure, microbial antigen exposure, neuroendocrine signaling, and
cognitive/autonomic symptoms. It also contextualizes `hypothesis:0002`, since gut tissue and gut-resident
immune cells are plausible reservoir/barrier interfaces. The current evidence remains local to the gut
axis and should not promote either hypothesis without longitudinal or interventional target-engagement
data.
