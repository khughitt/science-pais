# Thromboinflammation, Microclots, and Endothelial Dysfunction in Long COVID

## Summary

A convergent body of evidence frames the vasculature as a shared effector organ in long COVID: persistent complement activation, platelet hyperreactivity and a fibrin(aloid) microclot burden, and durable endothelial injury together constitute a self-amplifying *thromboinflammatory* state that fails to resolve months to years after acute SARS-CoV-2 infection. The strongest evidence is for persistent complement dysregulation (CerviaHasler2024, a Science adg7942 prospective proteomics study) and for objectively measurable endothelial dysfunction (Stahlberg2025, EndoPAT reactive-hyperemia index abnormal in 61% of patients at ~31 months). These vascular abnormalities plausibly connect to the cardiovascular symptom burden documented at population scale (Huang2025) and to dysautonomia/POTS-like presentations (Stahlberg2025, Salvucci2023, Ganesh2022), suggesting a candidate *vascular subphenotype* of long COVID. The framing is mechanistically attractive and partly biomarker-supported, but the most distinctive single claim — circulating fibrinaloid microclots as a causal, treatment-responsive driver — remains promising yet contested, lacking controlled interventional confirmation [@CerviaHasler2024; @Stahlberg2025; @Huang2025; @Salvucci2023; @Ganesh2022; @Nicolai2023].

## Key Concepts

**Persistent complement dysregulation and terminal complement complex (TCC) imbalance.** CerviaHasler2024 establishes complement as the dominant blood-protein signature of active long COVID at 6 months: decreased soluble C7-containing TCCs (C5b-7/8/9) with elevated C5bC6, indicating a shift from soluble clearance toward membrane insertion (membrane attack complex assembly) and cell lysis. A C5bC6/C7 complex ratio and a vWF/ADAMTS13 ratio were the top two machine-learning biomarkers (AUROC >= 0.74). Complement activation is positioned as a self-perpetuating loop — tissue injury activates complement, complement drives further injury — that normalizes in recovered patients but persists in those who remain ill [@CerviaHasler2024].

**Microclots / fibrinaloid deposits and platelet hyperactivation.** Nicolai2023 synthesizes the thromboinflammation field: platelet hyperreactivity is the predominant platelet finding in PASC, with increased platelet binding to vWF/collagen, platelet-neutrophil aggregate (PNA) formation, neutrophil extracellular trap (NET) release, and a CD10-low hyperreactive neutrophil phenotype. CerviaHasler2024 independently finds CD41-high monocyte-platelet aggregates highest in 12-month long COVID. Imaging correlates (SPECT perfusion defects without structural lesion) are consistent with microvascular occlusion. The Pretorius fibrinaloid-microclot model — fibrinolysis-resistant amyloid microclots entrapping inflammatory cargo — is the most prominent and most contested expression of this axis.

**Endothelial injury and dysfunction.** A consistent endotheliopathy signature recurs across studies: elevated vWF and factor VIII, reduced ADAMTS13 (a prothrombotic vWF:ADAMTS13 imbalance), elevated thrombomodulin, low antithrombin III with increased thrombin-mediated cleavage, hemolysis markers (low hemopexin, increased free heme), and elevated D-dimer (Nicolai2023, CerviaHasler2024). Stahlberg2025 makes the dysfunction *functionally* measurable: peripheral microvascular endothelial dysfunction (reactive-hyperemia index < 1.67) in 41% and impaired function in a further 20% of a non-hospitalized PACS cohort at a median of 31 months, with NT-proBNP rising over follow-up specifically in the dysfunctional groups — evidence of ongoing, not merely residual, vascular and cardiac deterioration [@Nicolai2023; @CerviaHasler2024; @Stahlberg2025].

**Connection to cardiovascular symptoms and dysautonomia.** Huang2025 (meta-analysis, ~3 million patients) quantifies elevated odds of chest pain (OR 4.0), palpitations (OR 3.4), and hypertension (OR 1.7) versus uninfected controls, with ACE2-mediated endothelial injury, microangiopathy, and post-infectious cardiac autoimmunity as proposed mechanisms. Stahlberg2025 reports POTS in 27% of its cohort; Salvucci2023 attributes a tachycardia/palpitation phenotype to mast-cell activation reversible with H1/H2 blockade; Ganesh2022 (Mayo PCOCC) documents an orthostasis-predominant, female-predominant phenotype with persistent IL-6 elevation. These place the vascular axis in direct contact with the dysautonomia arm of long COVID [@Huang2025; @Stahlberg2025; @Salvucci2023; @Ganesh2022].

**A candidate vascular subphenotype.** Across these papers, complement/coagulation/endothelial abnormalities co-occur and may define a discrete, biomarker-identifiable subgroup whose dominant features are exertional intolerance, dyspnea, chest pain, palpitations, and orthostatic symptoms — distinct from purely neurocognitive or fatigue-dominant presentations.

## Current State of Knowledge

### What the evidence supports

- Complement is genuinely and persistently dysregulated in active long COVID, with a specific, replicated TCC-imbalance signature and quantitative biomarker ratios (CerviaHasler2024).
- Endothelial dysfunction is objectively measurable and durable (>2 years) even after mild, non-hospitalized acute illness, and tracks a worsening cardiac-stress biomarker (Stahlberg2025).
- A coherent endotheliopathy/coagulation biomarker cluster (vWF up, ADAMTS13 down, D-dimer up, antithrombin III down, factor VIII up) recurs across independent cohorts (Nicolai2023, CerviaHasler2024).
- Cardiovascular symptom burden is elevated at population scale and is not explained by acute severity alone (Huang2025; Stahlberg2025's mild-acute cohort).
- Platelet hyperreactivity and monocyte/neutrophil-platelet aggregation provide a cellular substrate linking inflammation to clotting (Nicolai2023, CerviaHasler2024).
- Population-register hard endpoints now support persistence and sex-effect heterogeneity: Spetz2025 reports elevated DVT/PE risk after mild/non-hospitalized COVID-19, elevated DVT/PE risk in the 91-180 day window overall, and a larger COVID-associated thromboembolic increment in men than women against an uninfected comparator.

### What is contested or unresolved

- **Microclots specifically.** Fibrinaloid microclots are a promising but contested entity: detection methods are not standardized, healthy controls can show some signal, and no controlled trial has shown that clearing microclots (e.g. triple anticoagulation) reverses symptoms. The construct should be reported as a hypothesis, not an established mechanism.
- **Causality and directionality.** CerviaHasler2024's cross-sectional snapshots cannot resolve whether complement drives coagulation, coagulation drives complement, or herpesvirus immune complexes initiate both. Stahlberg2025 cannot exclude pre-existing vascular vulnerability as a predisposing rather than acquired trait.
- **Treatment response.** Anticoagulation is not guideline-recommended post-discharge absent prior VTE indications; the STIMULATE-ICP rivaroxaban arm and complement-modulator trials are the decisive tests and have not yet returned definitive positive results.
- **Cardiovascular meta-analytic heterogeneity.** Huang2025 reports very high I-squared (93-100%), with prospective vs retrospective designs giving markedly different chest-pain ORs (14.2 vs 1.6), so pooled magnitudes are imprecise.

### Tensions between papers

CerviaHasler2024 centers complement/TCC imbalance as the driver, whereas Nicolai2023 foregrounds platelet/neutrophil/endothelial thromboinflammation and treats viral persistence and immune dysregulation as alternative upstream causes — complementary but not identical causal emphases. Salvucci2023 attributes the same cardiovascular/dysautonomia phenotype to mast-cell activation (histamine-driven), a partly distinct mechanism from complement/microclot biology, illustrating that "vascular/cardiovascular long COVID" is itself mechanistically heterogeneous. Ganesh2022's central-sensitization/IL-6 framing locates the cardiovascular symptoms partly in neuroinflammation rather than primary vasculopathy.

## Controversies and Open Questions

- Are circulating fibrinaloid microclots a reproducible, quantifiable, and *causal* feature of long COVID, or a partly artefactual correlate (Nicolai2023, Pretorius model)?
- Does a complement/microclot/endothelial axis define a discrete vascular subphenotype, and does membership predict response to anticoagulant or complement-modulating therapy (CerviaHasler2024, Stahlberg2025)?
- Is the endothelial dysfunction in PACS acquired post-infection or an unmasked pre-existing vulnerability (Stahlberg2025)?
- Is the male-biased hard-endpoint thromboembolic signal explained by baseline male vascular risk, COVID-added sex-differential thromboinflammation, or both? Spetz2025 weakens pure baseline carryover but does not report the decisive sex×infection×31-180-day×non-hospitalized interaction.
- How much of the cardiovascular/dysautonomia symptom burden is primary vasculopathy versus mast-cell activation versus central sensitization (Salvucci2023, Ganesh2022, Huang2025)?
- Do the same complement/coagulation/endothelial abnormalities appear in non-COVID PAIS (ME/CFS, post-Lyme), as required for a cross-PAIS shared vascular mechanism (CerviaHasler2024, Nicolai2023)?

## Relevance to This Project

Thromboinflammation supplies a concrete, biomarker-supported effector axis for the project's failed-homeostatic-recovery frame: the vasculature is where persistent complement, coagulation, and endothelial dysregulation converge to produce durable symptoms. It bears directly on `hypothesis:0001-shared-dysregulated-attractor` by furnishing a candidate self-amplifying loop (tissue injury -> complement/coagulation activation -> further injury) that could lock in a pathological attractor state, and it sharpens `topic:long-covid-immune-dysregulation` by tying the immune signature to a vascular endpoint. It also defines a candidate vascular subphenotype that the project's computational subphenotyping aims to test, and connects to the dysautonomia literature via shared cardiovascular and orthostatic presentations. Reproducible vascular readouts (RHI, vWF/ADAMTS13, C5bC6/C7, NT-proBNP dynamics) are candidate objective endpoints for cross-PAIS comparison and trials.
