# Pediatric Long COVID and MIS-C

## Summary

Pediatric long COVID is part of the PAIS landscape, but it is not simply adult long COVID with lower
prevalence. The phenotype and measurement surface change by developmental stage. RECOVER derives separate
research indices for infants/toddlers, preschool-aged children, school-age children, and adolescents; the
salient symptoms shift from observable/proxy-reported appetite/sleep/cough/energy signals in young
children toward cognitive, pain, fatigue, smell/taste, orthostatic, headache, and GI features in older
children and adolescents.

MIS-C is adjacent but distinct. It is a delayed post-SARS-CoV-2 hyperinflammatory syndrome in children,
often severe during the acute/subacute episode, but MUSIC shows generally strong cardiovascular and global
recovery by 6 months. For this project, MIS-C is a recovery-contrast syndrome: it can help distinguish
post-infectious inflammation that resolves from chronic PAIS in which homeostatic recovery fails.

## Key Concepts

**Age-stratified pediatric PASC.** Gross2024 (RECOVER ages 6-17) shows school-age children and adolescents
need different PASC research indices and have different symptom clusters. Gross2025 extends the same
logic to ages 0-5, where symptoms are necessarily more observable and proxy-reported.

**Longitudinal impairment matters.** Early pediatric prevalence reviews such as LopezLeon2022 confirmed
that persistent symptoms occur after SARS-CoV-2 infection but also showed extreme heterogeneity. CLoCk's
24-month follow-up demonstrates the issue directly: broad symptom reports remain common across infection
status groups, while persistent, impairment-aware PCC across repeated follow-up is a smaller and more
biologically meaningful target.

**Reinfection adds risk.** ZhangRECOVEREHR2026 (RECOVER-EHR) reports higher pediatric PASC diagnosis and PASC-like
condition risk after reinfection during the Omicron era, reinforcing the prevention/risk-modification
branch identified in t010.

**MIS-C as contrast.** Truong2025/MUSIC shows MIS-C can be severe but often resolves quickly, with most
participants back near baseline by 6 months. This differentiates MIS-C from chronic pediatric PASC while
making it useful for studying successful immune resolution.

## Current State of Knowledge

### What the evidence supports

- Pediatric PASC exists and can be associated with poorer health, quality of life, and function.
- Symptom profiles differ by age band; adult or adolescent instruments cannot be blindly applied to young
  children.
- Reinfection increases pediatric PASC-coded outcomes in a large RECOVER-EHR cohort.
- MIS-C is post-infectious and immune-mediated, but most children in the MUSIC cohort had reassuring
  6-month recovery.

### What is unresolved

- Mechanism: current pediatric anchors are stronger for phenotype/trajectory than for immune-metabolic
  mechanism.
- Attribution: broad pediatric symptoms are common in background populations and pandemic disruption;
  longitudinal impairment and controls are essential.
- Reporting channel: parent/proxy report, child self-report, and school function can diverge.
- Generalization: MIS-C biology may illuminate immune resolution, but its acute hyperinflammatory course
  should not be treated as the same chronic PAIS state.

## Relevance to This Project

This topic expands the PAIS scope across the lifespan and tightens the case-definition discipline needed
for any pediatric analysis. It supports the project's shared-failure-mode framing only as compatible
context: pediatric PASC has multisystem overlap with adult PAIS, but direct mechanism bridges remain
thin. MIS-C is most valuable as a comparator for why some post-infectious immune activation resolves.
