# Proposition: The small-fiber-neuropathy substrate recurs across distinct infectious triggers (PTLDS, long COVID, ME/CFS)

## Claim

The same peripheral small-fiber-neuropathy substrate (`proposition:0014`) recurs across **distinct
post-infectious syndromes reached from distinct infectious triggers** — *Borrelia*-triggered PTLDS,
SARS-CoV-2-triggered long COVID, and **infection-associated ME/CFS** (ME/CFS is a post-infectious
*syndrome*, not itself an infectious agent; the relevant antecedents are infectious triggers such as EBV
and other documented onsets) — with a comparable signature. Subject = the SFN substrate; predicate =
*recurs across*; object = multiple, etiologically distinct post-infectious syndromes. This is an
`empirical_regularity` supporting a **shared end-organ failure mode reachable from many infections**, and
it is the peripheral-lesion-level instantiation of the project's umbrella conjecture
(`hypothesis:0001-shared-dysregulated-attractor`): the convergence is claimed at the level of a concrete
structural lesion, not only at the symptom/review level. **Q-fever and post-dengue fatigue are candidate
additional triggers** to fold in once SFN / autonomic small-fiber data exist for those cohorts.

## Evidence Summary

`literature_evidence`, **not yet deposited as graph evidence-lines** (`task:t049`). Small-fiber /
autonomic involvement is separately documented per syndrome in corpus-resident summaries:
`paper:Adler2024` (dysautonomia as a core PTLDS component), `paper:Walitt2024` (deep-phenotyped
infection-associated ME/CFS with autonomic assessment), and `paper:Limongelli2026` (post-COVID SFN) —
the empirical seed for convergence. But no study in the corpus compares these syndromes **head-to-head
under one standardized protocol**, so "the same substrate" is currently an aggregation of separate
single-syndrome reports, not a demonstrated cross-syndrome equivalence. Coded support is absent pending
ingestion.

## Caveats

The convergence claim is vulnerable to the same deflationary rival that pressures `hypothesis:0001`: a
shared *symptom* (dysautonomia) reached by trigger-*specific* peripheral mechanisms would mimic a shared
substrate without one existing. Cross-trigger comparisons that do not use one protocol risk
manufacturing apparent convergence from heterogeneous methods
(`topic:measurement-ascertainment-artifacts-in-pais`). If SFN is reliably present for one trigger but
**absent for another** under a single standardized protocol, this proposition fails and the finding
becomes trigger-specific. Conditional on P1 holding in ≥2 triggers.
