# Proposition: The small-fiber-neuropathy substrate recurs across distinct infectious triggers (PTLDS, long COVID, ME/CFS)

## Claim

The same peripheral small-fiber-neuropathy substrate (`proposition:0014`) recurs across **distinct
infectious triggers** — Borrelia/PTLDS, SARS-CoV-2/long COVID, and ME/CFS — with a comparable signature.
Subject = the SFN substrate; predicate = *recurs across*; object = multiple, etiologically distinct
post-infectious syndromes. This is an `empirical_regularity` supporting a **shared end-organ failure
mode reachable from many infections**, and it is the peripheral-lesion-level instantiation of the
project's umbrella conjecture (`hypothesis:0001-shared-dysregulated-attractor`): the convergence is
claimed at the level of a concrete structural lesion, not only at the symptom/review level.

## Evidence Summary

`literature_evidence`, **not yet coded as project evidence-lines** (`task:t049`). SFN / small-fiber
involvement is separately reported in long COVID, ME/CFS, and PTLDS — the empirical seed for convergence.
But no study in the corpus compares these triggers **head-to-head under one standardized protocol**, so
"the same substrate" is currently an aggregation of separate single-trigger reports, not a demonstrated
cross-trigger equivalence. Coded support is absent pending ingestion.

## Caveats

The convergence claim is vulnerable to the same deflationary rival that pressures `hypothesis:0001`: a
shared *symptom* (dysautonomia) reached by trigger-*specific* peripheral mechanisms would mimic a shared
substrate without one existing. Cross-trigger comparisons that do not use one protocol risk
manufacturing apparent convergence from heterogeneous methods
(`topic:measurement-ascertainment-artifacts-in-pais`). If SFN is reliably present for one trigger but
**absent for another** under a single standardized protocol, this proposition fails and the finding
becomes trigger-specific. Conditional on P1 holding in ≥2 triggers.
