# Proposition: Vascular, metabolic, and SFN axes are plausible downstream effectors of immune-state displacement

## Claim

At least three partially-separable **effector axes** plausibly transduce a displaced immune
state into symptoms: (i) **endothelial / vascular** dysfunction → impaired O₂
delivery/extraction; (ii) **metabolic / mitochondrial** dysfunction; and (iii) **autoimmune
small-fiber neuropathy** → peripheral dysautonomia. This is a **candidate mechanistic**
claim asserting that these axes are plausible and structurally downstream — *not* that any
one is necessary, dominant, or the sole path.

## Evidence Summary

Anchors, one per axis: `paper:Joseph2023` (invasive CPET: preload failure + impaired
peripheral O₂ extraction in ME/CFS and PASC — the vascular/O₂ limb); `paper:Appelman2024`
(long-COVID skeletal-muscle OXPHOS impairment worsening after provoked PEM — the metabolic
limb, cf. `proposition:0011`, `hypothesis:0006`); `paper:deSa2026` (passive-transfer
causal anchor for non-GPCR autoantibody-mediated SFN — the neuropathy limb,
`hypothesis:0007`). Each axis has independent support; what is **not** established is that
they are downstream of the *immune-state* node specifically rather than parallel primary
lesions — the same ordering ambiguity that `proposition:0039` targets.

## Caveats

`mechanistic_narrative` / `background`. These axes are already owned by dedicated
hypotheses (h0004 vascular, h0006 muscle-metabolic, h0007 SFN); this proposition only
records that the landscape routes them as **effectors** of the immune-state node. Their
being effectors-of-immune-state (vs. co-primary) is a directional assumption, not a
demonstrated ordering. Held deliberately plural — the effector set is a menu, not a
committed pathway.
